Niklas Khrypfs or Krebs, de Cusa or Cusanus, son of a fisherman, was born at Cues on the Mosel, educated at Deventer and at Padua, and after filling minor ecclesiastical positions in Germany, and serving with great distinction at the Basel Council, was sent by Pope Eugene IV on a special mission to Constantinople. In 1448 he was made cardinal. A good Greek scholar, he brought back from the East many important MSS., chiefly mathematical, the translation of which into Latin he supervised. At Padua Toscanelli influenced the direction of his studies. He was the most distinguished modern predecessor of Copernicus, and held that the earth was a star and that it moved :
> "Terra non potest esse fixa, sed movetur ut aliae stellae" (quoted by Heller, no. 5963, i, p. 216, from the Basel, 1565, ed.; cf. De docta ignorantia, bk. 2, ch. II, in no. 7466).
On his work in connection with the reform of the calendar (the 3rd tractate in this vol.) see Heller, *ibid.*, p. 218. He was one of the pioneers in modern mathematical physics, and his studies on measurements and specific gravity are of the first importance; see the dialogue De staticis experimentis at the end of no. 7466. In this he records the famous experiment, antedating Hales 200 years, of weighing earth and seeds, then the resulting plants, their ashes, and the earth in which they had grown.

Spiritually a man of wide vision, Nicholas was haunted by a dream of the possible union not alone of Latin and Greek Christians but of Mahometans, Jews and Gentiles. "Beneath all diversity of religious forms and ceremonies he descried one spirit, and in him is to be found the original of the saying, whose authorship has been so much disputed, 'Una est igitur religio et cultus omnium intellectu vigentium' [De pace fidei, leaf d 1]"; and he even suggested a Parliament of Religions for the establishment of religious concord (J. A. Smith, unpublished address; cf. also Webb, no. 6526, p. 349).

There is an extensive literature on De Cusa; see the list in no. 3500, p. 10, and several works noted on a flyleaf at the end of this incunabulum.

For two reproductions of 16th-century pictures of supposed scenes in De Cusa's life see S. Reinach, *Répertoire de peintures du moyen âge et de la renaissance*, t. ii, 1907, p. 637. One is in the Church of Neustift, the other in the Augustinian Convent at Brixen, De Cusa's diocese.

Pandam nunc que vobis dilectissimi fratribus ante promiseram circa facultatem mistice theologie Arbitror enim vos quos scio zelo dei duci dignos quibus hic thesaurus aperiatur. utique preciosus valde & maxime fecundus orans in primis mihi dare verbum supernum & sermonem omnipotentem. qui solum se ipsum pandere potest. ut pro captu vestro enarrare queam mirabilia que supra omnem sensibilem rationalem & intellectualem visum revelantur Conabor autem simplicissimo atque communissimo modo vos experimentaliter in sacratissimam obscuritatem manuducere. ubi dum eritis inaccessibilem lucem adesse sentientes quilibet ex se tentabit modo quo sibi a deo concedetur propius accedere : & hic pregustare quodam suavissimo libamine cenam illam eterne felicitatis ad quam vocati sumus in verbo vite per evangelium christi semper benedicti.
