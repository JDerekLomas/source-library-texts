# Preface.

## Kind Reader!

Since it is to be expected that, upon picking up and inspecting this book, you will desire some further account of it beyond what the title states: we are therefore inclined to serve your reasonable desire with this preface; and to report to you, within this narrow space, briefly and faithfully, concerning the causes, intentions, and the arrangement of this present <term>hymnbook</term> <note>original: "Gesang-Buch." This refers to the "Germantowner Gesang-Buch," a seminal collection for German-speaking settlers in colonial Pennsylvania.</note>.

I. As for the causes for the publication of this book, these shall be presented to you for Christian consideration under the following reflection on singing, since it is, after all, called a hymnbook.

That singing is praiseworthy is proven (a) from man’s glorious <term>creation</term> <note>original: "Schöpffung." The author argues that the physical ability to sing is a divine gift inherent in human biology.</note>, since his Creator has endowed him with the appropriate aptitude to produce a singing tone. It has pleased the highly praised, triune, almighty **GOD** (although He possesses and is the fullness of all delight within Himself in His eternal heavenly rest) according to His wise counsel in His love, to create man or human nature in such a glorious manner and state, that he, through divine preparation, is capable or receptive, so that the same—
