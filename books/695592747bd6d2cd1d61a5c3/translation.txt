# A THREE-LINE STANZA ON VOLUME II.

The World acts as a vocal Organ with ten registers;
As many beings as there are within it, so many are the poetic measures.
GOD is the Harmonizer <note>original: "Harmoftes," a Greek-derived term for one who joins or tunes parts into a whole</note>, in whom the Wisdom of the Father
Has arranged all things well, and the Spirit unites them in Love.
This love is harmony; by this love the World is bound together;
O leader <note>original: "ἐξαγὸς," likely referring to a guide or perhaps a critic of the work</note>, do you deny that this world belongs to the Deity?

## P R E F A C E.

<image-desc>Ornamental woodcut decorative initial 'I' depicting foliate patterns and scrolling vines within a square border.</image-desc>

*IN all arts and sciences, Practice and Theory seek a union so*
*intimate that if one should fail the other, both would deservedly*
*collapse; for practice does not maintain its reputation without Theory,*
*nor does Theory alone maintain its own without practice. This holds*
*true in other fields, but most especially in the faculty of Music;*
*for although the Theory of Music might be said to have achieved its*
*end in some way—namely, its own delight—content with the love*
*of truth alone: nevertheless, it can in no way happen that if a Musician*
*is destitute of all practice, he can perform anything worthy regarding*
*the said faculty. Hence, from my early youth, I have considered that*
*one must apply oneself equally to both—*
