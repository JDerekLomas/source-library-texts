# ¶ A Poem by the Divine and Enlightened Doctor Ramon Llull <note>original: "Raymundi Lulli doctoris illuminati". Llull (c. 1232–1316) was a Majorcan philosopher known as the "Enlightened Doctor" for his complex logical system.</note>
# To the most Reverend Father in Christ and Lord, Lord Francisco Jiménez,
# Cardinal of the Spains, etc.

Since for so long, most deserving Prelate, I might become better known through your assistance, being dear to you:
Hope for my teachings, since you bear your learned temples worthily encircled by the golden hat <note>The "galero," the distinctive wide-brimmed red hat of a Cardinal.</note>;
Since Toledo shines with you as its extraordinary shepherd, and the highest honor belongs to your religious devotion;
And since, as General Protector throughout Spain <note>original: "Hesperiam," a classical poetic name for the Spanish peninsula.</note>, you suppress the investigated acts of crafty crime by fire <note>A reference to Cisneros's role as Grand Inquisitor.</note>;
May you be the Grand Chancellor in the Spanish world, and may all righteous things applaud your hand;
Under your leadership, may the university at Alcalá <note>original: "Compluti." Cisneros founded the University of Alcalá (Complutense) in 1499.</note> rise with wondrous buildings, and may your undertakings be those of a great man;
Wealth, spirit, strength—everything finally entrusted to you—you yourself draw toward such holy uses:
I say, through all these blessings heaped upon you, O blessed one, through which you hold the weight of love for virtue:
I ask you, whom warring Spain, leader of the world, rejoices to have as Primate by your merits;
That you take up these alabaster jars <note>A metaphor for precious vessels containing spiritual perfumes or wisdom.</note>, breathing sacred vapors, as things worthy of you for your service.
Which, adorned with your titles and decorated with your insignias, your servant Proaza <note>Alonso de Proaza (1445–1519), a humanist scholar and editor of Llull's works.</note> dedicates to you with his whole heart.
(He stands by me as a guardian, skillful and a true minister: who, as a friend, offers his own efforts for our aid)
Accept the Art which I held by the gift of the powers above; accept the work forged in the mind of the Thunderer <note>A classical epithet for God/Jupiter, here referring to divine inspiration.</note>.
Accept that which shines with gems, which, full of divine power, contains within itself everything that can be known: I speak the truth.
Believe me, this Art was not fashioned by my own labor; it is not mine, but proceeded from the fountain of the true Jupiter <note>God.</note>.
If I were lying to you in this, so many good things would not be ready for my pens, the pens of an unjust writer.
Drive away the people hostile to me; take care of me: if you defend me under your wings, let the envious man bark and be jealous.
Religion is your custom; Spain has joined us to you: in your hands, these things will be the cause of favor for us.
You also, Maecenas <note>A famous Roman patron of the arts; here used as a title for the Cardinal.</note>, the specimen and indicator of virtue, you to whom we are believed to be beloved: show favor.
You, to whom such great power was granted on earth; you, whose deeds shine like the stars.
In you, our sighs of old finally see their end; you who are able to bring joys to my desires.
We pray that this "Inventive Art" <note>Llull's "Ars Inventiva Veritatis," a logical system designed to discover truth through the combination of concepts.</note> be committed to you:
I have written what I desire; let love dictate the rest.

->Nicolás de Pax of the Balearic Islands.<- <note>The scholar who likely provided this specific introductory material.</note>
