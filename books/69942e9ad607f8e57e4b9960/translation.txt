# PREFACE TO THE READER.

The work *On Rivers* <note>original: *Fluminibus*; referring to the treatise *De fluviis* attributed to Plutarch, often published alongside Psellus.</note> was produced through the care of Philippe-Jacques de Maussac <note>A 17th-century French scholar and jurist (1590–1650) known for editing Greek texts.</note>, who first published it in Greek, adding a translation and notes, at Toulouse in 1615, in octavo <note>A book size where each sheet is folded into eight leaves.</note>. The very skilled anatomist Adriaan van den Spiegel <note>original: *Adrianus Spigelius* (1578–1625); a Flemish physician famous for his work on the liver.</note> also translated this little book by Psellus into the Latin and Italian languages, as Leo Allatius <note>A prominent Greek-Catholic scholar and librarian at the Vatican (1586–1669).</note> reports in his *Diatribe*, chapter 47, page 31.

*Philippe-Jacques de Maussac first published Psellus's little book **On the Virtues of Stones** <note>original: *de Lapidum virtutibus*; a "lapidary" text describing the mystical and medicinal properties of gemstones.</note>, translated it into Latin, and corrected it; it was published at Toulouse in Greek and Latin in the year 1615, in octavo. My very dear friend Giacomo Filippo Tomasini <note>original: *Jacobus Philippus Thomasinus* (1595–1655); an Italian scholar and bishop.</note> informed me in his letter that this work is preserved to this day in the collection of Libérale Crema, a most excellent philosopher and physician of Treviso.
