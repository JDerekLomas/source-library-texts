### Folio 21

In the context of divinity, the god Shiva is called *Yeganeh* <note>original: "suyangalayā," likely a corruption of the Persian "Yegāneh" meaning unique/one, or "Yazdān" for God</note>. An angel is called *Firishta* <note>original: "phīrīgnaḥ," from the Persian "Firishta"</note>. Among the demonic beings, the various categories of ghosts, spirits, and flesh-eaters <term>bhūta-preta-piśāca</term> are known as *Khabis* and *Shaitan* <note>original: "khavīmaśetāne." "Khabis" refers to an impure spirit and "Shaitan" to the devil/Satan.</note>. 9. Regarding the nectar of immortality <term>amṛta</term>, they call it *Ab-i-hayat* <note>original: "āvīdayante," a Sanskritization of the Persian "Āb-i-hayāt," the Water of Life</note>.

In the matter of celestial vehicles <term>vimāna</term>, the word is *Javāza* <note>original: "javaśe"</note>. In the context of smoke, the term is used; and for fire <term>agni</term>, its flame or weapon is called *Shula* <note>original: "śikhāsālaha," likely referring to "Shu'la" for flame</note>. 10. Regarding food and offerings to the gods, <unclear>text obscured by stain</unclear>. In the context of the color red, it is *Jamal* <note>original: "jamāladaḥ"</note>. A demon <term>rākṣasa</term> is called *Adwar* <note>original: "ādovoraḥ"</note>. The king of the people is the *Sultan*. 

In the time of early morning light, the term is *Rushan* <note>original: "rūṣāmaḥ," from "Roshan" meaning bright</note>. For the evening twilight <term>sandhyā</term>, the word is *Saye* <note>original: "sāye," meaning shadow or dusk</note>. For the <insert><unclear>dark</unclear></insert> half of the month, the term is *Mah-i-siyah* <note>original: "māsrimohaḥ"</note>. For the calculation of years, the word is *Sal*. The time of total cosmic dissolution <term>pralaya</term> is known as *Qayamat* <note>original: "kyāmate," the Persian/Arabic term for the Day of Resurrection</note>. 13.

The delightful spring season <term>surabhi</term> is called *Nau-bahar* <note>original: "nobahāraḥ," meaning "New Spring"</note>. In the rainy season and at the end of the rains, <unclear>text obscured</unclear>. The heat of the summer <term>grīṣma</term> is called *Tabistan* <note>original: "tāpasnānā," a Sanskritization of "Tābistān"</note>. The cold season is *Zimistan* <note>original: "snājjīmīsnānaḥ," from "Zimistān"</note>. 14. 

For the autumn season <term>śarad</term>, the word is *Khazan*. A tablet is *Falak*. The sky <term>ākāśa</term> is called *Asman* <note>original: "vāsmāna"</note>. In the directions of the white sun and moon, the northern pole is *Utara*. 

Regarding the quality of light: the word *Wasl* <note>original: "vosala"</note> is used for illumination <term>prakāśa</term>. When there is an abundance of brilliance, the term for moonlight <term>jyotsnā</term> is used. For the "maker of frost," the moon <term>himakara</term>, the word is *Mah*. 

The essence of the milk of the autumn season is described as *Sarade*. Thus it is said that the planet Saturn <term>śanaiścara</term> is called *Zuhal* <note>original: "jorū," a corruption of "Zuhal"</note> among the stars.

### Folio 22

The shadow planet Rahu is called *Rah* <note>original: "rāho rāhaḥ"</note>; for Ketu, the term is *Zanab* <note>original: "keto janavaḥ," from the Persian/Arabic "Zanab" meaning tail</note>. For one who lives by death <term>mṛtyujīvī</term> <note>likely referring to a physician or an undertaker</note>, the terms are *Mu'alij* and *Khaishak* <note>original: "mome mikhīṣaka"</note>. In the context of the planets, Jupiter is mentioned, and for Venus <term>śukra</term>, the name is given. Regarding the day, time, and the clouds, the word for a wise man or a scholar <term>vidvān</term> is *Dana* or *Danish* <note>original: "dānādāniśca," from Persian "Dānā" (wise) and "Dānesh" (knowledge)</note>. Conversely, an ignorant person <term>ajñānī</term> is called *Nadan* <note>original: "nādānaḥ"</note>. One who is liberated or free <term>mukta</term> is known as *Khalasi* <note>original: "khalāsī"</note>.

<gloss>A</gloss> Regarding memory <term>smaraṇa</term>, the word *Yaad* <note>original: "yāda"</note> is used, while for forgetting or oblivion <term>vismaraṇa</term>, it is *Faramosh* <note>original: "pharāmogaḥ"</note>. Understanding, thought, news, and perception <note>representing the Sanskrit "buddhi"</note> are rendered as *Fahm*, *Fikr*, *Khabar*, and *Firasat* <note>original: "fahami phiki raṃ khivaraṃ phirāsate"</note>. In the context of joy <term>ānanda</term>, the term is *Khushali* <note>original: "khuśālī"</note>. A knower or a living soul is called *Zindar* <note>original: "nijāndār," likely from "Zinda-dār"</note>. For certainty or a proven principle <term>siddhānta</term>, the term is *Dalil* <note>original: "dalīla," meaning evidence or argument</note>.

For a dog, the word is *Kabal* <note>original: "kabalaṃ"</note>. In the context of compassion or mercy <term>kṛpā</term>, one uses *Mehr* and *Rahm* <note>original: "meharara ha ma"</note>. For enmity or a grudge <term>vaira</term>, the term is *Dushmani* <note>original: "duśmanī"</note>. Grief or sorrow <term>śoka</term> is called *Gham* <note>original: "gamaḥ"</note>. For that which is wonderful or a surprise <term>āścarya</term>, the word is *Ajab* <note>original: "ajaba"</note>. Regarding the feeling of fear <term>bhayānaka</term>, it is *Bim* <note>original: "vīme"</note>. 

The word for shame or modesty <term>lajjā</term> is *Sharm* <note>original: "śarma"</note>. Great anger or wrath <term>krodha</term> is called *Ghazab* <note>original: "gajave"</note>. In the case of regret or remorse <term>anutāpa</term>, the term is *Afsos* <note>original: "aphasosaḥ"</note>. A summary or clarification <term>prīto</term> is called *Khulasa* <note>original: "khulāsā"</note>. For stability or a firm decision <term>sthairya</term>, the name is *Karar* <note>original: "karāra"</note>. The heart or mind <term>citta</term> is called *Dil* <note>original: "dilnī"</note>, and courage or fortitude is *Himmat* <note>original: "himmata"</note>.

For worry or anxiety <term>cintā</term>, the word is *Andesha* <note>original: "andeśa ha"</note>. For work, desire, or a wish <term>kāma</term>, the terms *Arzu* <note>original: "āraja"</note> and *Mutarajjiz* are used. Deceit or fraud <term>kapata</term> is called *Makr* <note>original: "makaraḥ"</note>. Regarding the state of dreaming or hope and expectation <term>pratyāśā</term>, the word is *Umeed* <note>original: "umeda"</note>. Finally, for consolation or reassurance <term>āśvāsana</term>, the term is *Dilasa* <note>original: "dilāsā"</note>.

In the sense of **causing to cook** <term>pācayārthaka</term>, the term is **Me-patān-ad** <note>original: "mepatānad"; Persian *mī-pazānad*, he causes to cook</note>. For the past tense **he caused to cook**, the form **Patād** <note>Persian *pazānd*</note> is used, matching the Sanskrit **Apācayat**. These forms, along with others starting with **Ha**, should be recognized by those who are **discerning** <term>vicakṣaṇaiḥ</term>.

In the sense of **showing** or **causing to see** <term>darśayati</term>, the term is **Me-vīnān-ad** <note>original: "mevīnānad"; Persian *mī-bīnānad*, he causes to see</note>. Another word used in the sense of **showing** is **Namūd** <note>original: "namṛda"; Persian *namūd*, he showed/demonstrated</note>, which can be expressed in the Sanskrit perfect tense as **Darśayāmāsa**. The past form **he showed** is also given as **Vīnād**, corresponding to **Adarśayat**.

### Verbs of Weaving and Speaking

In the sense of **causing to weave** or **causing to sow** <term>vāpayati</term>, the term is **Me-vāyān-id** <note>original: "mevāyānid"; Persian *mī-bāfānad* (weave) or *vazānad* (blow/move)</note>. For the past tense **he caused to weave**, the form is **Vāyāi**.

In the sense of **causing to speak** or **causing to say** <term>goyān-arthe</term>, the term is **Me-goyān-id** <note>original: "megoyānid"; Persian *mī-gūyānad*, he causes to speak</note>.

### Causatives of Consumption and Movement
