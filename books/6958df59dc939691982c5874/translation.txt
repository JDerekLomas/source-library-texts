# CHARLES CLUSIUS
## TO ALL STUDENTS OF
## BOTANY.

<image-desc>Ornate decorative woodcut initial 'N' featuring scrolling botanical motifs within a square border.</image-desc>

Now at last, I am fulfilling the promise I made to you in my history of the plants of Spain <note>Clusius refers to his 1576 work, *Rariorum aliquot stirpium per Hispanias observatarum historia*</note>, undoubtedly later than many would have hoped, and later than I had intended myself. But many things often happen unexpectedly, by which our plans are disrupted and delays are introduced. Thus, occupied as I have been by various long travels since the year 1589, I have not been able until now to finish the history of the Pannonian <note>Pannonia was a province of the Roman Empire; in Clusius's time, it referred to the regions of modern-day Austria, Hungary, and surrounding areas</note> plants, which I began observing in the year 1574. I will say nothing of the adverse and rainy weather which frequently interrupted my efforts and, when I had reached the foothills of the Alps and other towering mountains, drove me back from climbing the highest peaks (where the plants I intended to observe had to be sought). For this very reason, I had decided to spend the entire previous summer surveying several of the highest mountain ridges—as much as the clear weather would allow—so that I might deliver a more complete history to you. However, warned by friends that most people were becoming impatient with this long delay, I preferred to bring it forth into the public eye, such as it is, rather than frustrate your expectations any longer.

# Preface

I am certain that this has been observed until now (at least in the writings I have read); in turn, I frankly profess that I have interspersed some things which have indeed been observed by others, but whose description was either mutilated, or whose origins did not correspond, or in which certain necessary characteristics were omitted.

SINCE, furthermore, in those travels of mine <note>The author, Carolus Clusius, was famous for his extensive botanical expeditions across Europe.</note> I observed several things, and also received illustrations and descriptions of certain others from friends which served our subject, it pleased me to bring them into their proper classes and insert them in convenient places, so that our history might be made so much more complete.

MOREOVER, I make passing mention of many others whose history has already been published by others long ago, because I discovered them growing in these neighboring provinces, although most other regions also abound with them.

BESIDES those plants <term>stirpium: plants or lineages of flora</term> whose history was handed down in the Appendix to the *Spanish Observations* <note>Referring to Clusius's earlier work, *Rariorum aliquot stirpium per Hispanias observatarum historia* (1576)</note>, I have rewoven the descriptions, made them more enlarged, and having inserted them everywhere in the appropriate places, I have brought them back into the second book, since it happened that I observed most things pertaining to their history more exactly in the intervening time.

# BY JUSTUS LIPSIUS
## A Eulogy concerning this book.

*You who alone have thoroughly known*
*The Flora <note>The Roman goddess of flowers and plants</note> of both the old world and the new,*
*And alone have known the Hygeia <note>The Greek goddess of health; here referring to medicinal plants</note> of both the old and the new;*
*You who have penetrated into the innermost recesses*
*Of Nature, my friend CLUSIUS:*
*You who alone grasp with your mind and reveal with your pen*
*What our world holds, and what the other world <note>Refers to the Americas/New World</note> possesses:*
*How much your Pannonian labors delight us!*
*Through the Pannonian lands <note>Pannonia was a Roman province covering parts of modern Hungary, Austria, and the Balkans</note>*
*You diligently gather a thousand ranks of herbs, a thousand flowers,*
*Things that must be read by us—*
*And not only read, but even seen—*
*Which you offer here in this brief little book,*
*By Jove, a learned and a good little book!*
*The reader who does not love and value this:*
*Does not love the Flora of the old world or the new,*
*Nor loves the Hygeia of the old world or the new.*

### A play upon the name of Clusius.

<margin>* By which name Apollo was called by the ancients, from the act of "opening." <note>original: "ab aperiendo." Lipsius is punning on the name Clusius (related to 'claudere', to close) and its opposite, 'aperire' (to open).</note></margin>
While you, Clusius, disclose all the secrets of Nature:
You should no longer be CLUSIUS <note>The "Closed One"</note> to me, but APERTA <note>The "Open One"</note>.

## CONCERNING THE CHERRY-LAUREL.
### CHAPTER I.

*As far as I know, the history of this tree has been set forth by no one; indeed, I believe it was not even known to the herbalists <note>Botanists of the 16th century were often called "herbarists" or "herbalists."</note> of our age. Since, however, it is of that kind which flourishes with evergreen foliage and is most elegant in appearance, it seems to rightfully claim the first place for itself in these commentaries of ours.*
