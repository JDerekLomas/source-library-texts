# D E D I C A T I O N.

*A hero, in the Emperor’s service, who, calm and upright,*
*Acquitted himself in armor, and showed that he carried his weapon*
*Not out of a desire for glory, but for the honor of the Empire,*
*As a terror to the wicked, and a refuge for the pious:*
*But when Caesar* <note>original: "Cezar"; here referring to the Roman Emperor in the legend of Saint Maurice</note>*, to appease Heathendom,*
*Ordered him to swing the censer for the idols,*
*And to serve the godless altar, according to Numa’s blind style* <note>original: "Numaes blinden stijl"; Numa Pompilius was the legendary second king of Rome, credited with establishing many pagan religious rites</note>,
*He offered up his life to the sword, and the axe,*
*And the horse’s hoof, and let it be entirely crushed to dust,*
*Also without setting himself against the court of Rome*
*With outward violence. Thus he gave, simply and honestly,*
*Like a crusader-hero* <note>original: "kruishelt"</note>, *to God and Caesar each their due,*
*With seven thousand soldiers, brave souls,*
*Who all fell patiently in one troop,*
*And won the noble martyr’s crown through a struggle*
*In which one suffers for the name of the great Savior.*
*So stood your bravery, always encouraged to strive*
*For the honor of the holy empire, even at the loss of life,*
*To support the Emperor in his right with your sword;*
*As your prudence guards the electoral authority,*
*Wields a free scepter, and defends Christ’s kingdom,*
*When the Elector* <term>Elector (Keurvorst): One of the German princes entitled to elect the Holy Roman Emperor.</term> *bestows favor upon you in his name;*
*Where LEOPOLD* <note>Leopold I (1640–1705), Holy Roman Emperor</note> *is hallowed, and, like*
*A golden morning sun and light, comes forth from Austria* <note>original: "Oostenrijck"</note>
*To nourish all that draws breath.*
*With what wisdom you knew how to smooth troubled minds,*
*Burning like a sea stirred by storms,*
*And gently snatch from Discord the prey*
*For which she flamed, so as not to leave Europe,*
*And so many souls, without hope*
*Of their preservation! Such a laudable deed*
*Follows the example of Maurice, who gave his life for God’s name.*
*The name JOHN* <note>original: "JOANNES"</note> *also does your highness no harm.*
*This name brings grace, and you shine through God’s grace,*

*Just as a prince and member of the blessed realm,*
*Which strikes the hereditary enemy’s host along the Danube’s flow,*
*And dictates the law to old Byzantium* <note>original: "Byzantium"; here referring to the Ottoman Empire, whose capital was Constantinople/Byzantium</note> *and its troops.*
*No less did this name honor you, when you, called with joy*
*By Elector FREDERICK, at the voice of FERDINAND,* <note>Frederick William, the "Great Elector" of Brandenburg, and Holy Roman Emperor Ferdinand III.</note>
*Were consecrated Grand Master of ancient Jerusalem,*
*And the Order of Saint John, in which the German lords,*
*Knights of the Cross, defend the rights of the hospital and Christ’s kingdom,*
*Under the shadow of God and His messenger of penance,* <note>original: "boetezant"; likely referring to John the Baptist, the patron saint of the Order, who preached repentance.</note>
*Upon which my Muse, with her heroic stride,*
*Gallops magnanimously, as if she, with the help of the choral hymns*
*Of heaven, could capture that light as required.*
*But it pleases me to see the Order of Saint John* <note>original: "heiligen Joan"</note>
*Arise, and its team of knights of the cross,*
*Rejoicing that Nassau, elevated to the Grand Mastership, / Bestows an even richer luster upon the first ancestor.*
*It was founded about six hundred years ago,*
*From distinct tongues* <note>original: "onderscheide tael"; referring to the "Langues," the administrative divisions of the Order of Saint John based on the languages spoken by the knights</note>, *for the service of the common good,*
*Chartered and expanded, to protect the faith of the cross* <note>original: "kruisgeloof"</note>
*From the encroachment of the Turk, and the yoke of Barbary.*
*It first planted the seat of war in Jerusalem,*
*By the authority of the holy church, upheld by the voice*
*Of all princes, and bound to its law and rules,*
*To be sealed in public by a martial vow.*
*The sword is hung at the knight's side,*
*To chain the hereditary enemy’s fury with might*
*Throughout his life, and to follow Christ’s*
*War troops wherever the head* <note>The Grand Master of the Order</note> *shall call him.*
*All ownership of property is strictly denied him,*
*And only the bare use for necessity is granted:*
*Just as marriage remains forbidden to the knight at all times,*
*To fight more unburdened for the faith of the cross,*
*And to turn Heathen unbelief away from God’s house.*
*The black mantle, and the white knight’s cross.*

# D E D I C A T I O N.

It fulfills its purpose and mark: yet trotting in God’s ranks,
So the armor and the red surcoat cover the loins.
Thus they shed their blood for many years in the field,
And repelled lawful, savage violence with force,
Out of compassion, to quench the rising flame,
Stoked by the son of Hagar, <note>original: "Agars zoon"; a biblical reference to Ishmael, traditionally used here to refer to the Ottoman Empire.</note> strengthened by Tartary and Russians,
And grown to the very peak of supreme command,
To endure, as our punishment, until it grieves God.

The imperial chronicles, come into our hands, report
How Germans, in the army of Christian heroic warriors,
In service of the Holy Sepulchre, acquitted themselves blow upon blow,
As a comfort to the brothers, and a terror to the enemy.
Thus they fought eastward: but when Heathendom in the North
Held the coasts, along the Baltic Sea, in extreme danger
With burning, scorching, murders, and shameful plundering,
The Order took the field, to cover Germany
Against such a heavy hailstorm with their cross-shields. <note>original: "kruis-rondas"; a rondas is a circular shield, here specifically one marked with the knight's cross.</note>
One saw the Weser stream, and Elbe, and Trave pull
Through Prussia, and the harvest of Livonia at the drum
Of Salza, <note>original: "Saltze"; referring to Hermann von Salza, the influential Grand Master of the Teutonic Knights who led the expansion into the Baltic.</note> to bring the unenlightened and raging Heathendom
To reason, which teaches them, with changed minds,
To offer their weapons to God and the standard of Jesus:
For watchful Rome had, in the face of envy,
Consecrated the German Knightly Order <note>The Teutonic Order.</note> out of zeal
With that Grand Master, to lead the campaign,
And to extend Christ's kingdom of the cross wide to the north.
It succeeded also all along the Baltic, when God gave His blessing;
It burned down the "church-forests," <note>original: "kerckwoudt"; referring to the sacred groves of pagan worship.</note> and broke down the bush-altars:
And Livonia, which, to the scorn of the Savior, girded the sword,
Voluntarily surrendered itself into the Order of Saint John:
After which Prince Albert in Brandenburg, <note>Albert of Brandenburg-Ansbach (1490–1568), the last Grand Master of the Teutonic Knights who converted to Lutheranism and became the first Duke of Prussia.</note> a head
Of the holy order, as a vassal prince, promised loyalty
To King Sigismund, <note>Sigismund I the Old, King of Poland.</note> for the fief of fruitful Prussia.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;What a mournful day it was, a day worthy of tears,
When the Ottoman host, enraged, by land and sea,
On foot and on horseback, came to curse Byzantium,
Launching assault after assault, and driving from seven directions
His "elephants" <note>Likely a metaphor for massive siege engines or the heavy, lumbering force of the sultan's army.</note> against the walls and towers,
With all his military might, proud of their crescent moon,
Seeking to extinguish the "mother-light"—the sun of the world <note>Byzantium/Constantinople was often envisioned as the light of the Christian East.</note>.
How that great city stood, like the fiery furnace of Mount Etna,
Engulfed in smoke and smog, fire and flame on every side;
Where lightning flashed upon the walls and thunder rolled along the ramparts.

Finally collapsed with a roar that made the earth tremble beneath,
Falling down, it filled the moat with stone and rubble,
And invited the enemy into the gaping void,
As if over a bridge made of ruins and fragments,
To rush through the breach and charge straight into the city.
Eight hundred knights of Latin and Greek descent,
All equally resolute and proud of spirit,
Reconciled to death for God's sake, took their place in the breach,
And cheered at the sound of drums and trumpets,
Amidst the storm of heavy cannons <term>cannon: originally "kartou," a large-caliber siege gun</term> and terrifying gunpowder <note>original: "donderkruit," literally "thunder-powder."</note>.
