# On the virtues and properties of the **Alkahest**.

## DISCUSSION
### THE TENTH.

The powers of the previously mentioned liquid, the **Alkahest** <term>Alkahest: In Paracelsian alchemy, a hypothetical "universal solvent" capable of reducing any body into its primary liquid form without destroying its essential components.</term>, must now be investigated more thoroughly. Its principal and most potent virtue is that it is a kind of universal solvent, through which all mixed things are liquefied, or dissected into various substances, or changed from their previous property into another.

However, I do not see by what reasoning it is claimed that it resolves all things into **prime matter** <term>prime matter: In Aristotelian and alchemical philosophy, the "materia prima" is the formless, fundamental substance that underlies all physical reality.</term> while the "power of the seeds" <note>original: "seminum potestate." For a twofold error seems to be committed here: first, because it is known by experience that it does not reduce anything—much less everything—into prime matter; and second, because the power of the seeds can in no way remain surviving in that which has been violently agitated by that solvent.

For the description of one so accurately fits the other that the distinction is scarcely discernible. 

This is because both works are carried out upon the same subject, as was said: both are pointed out by similar turns of phrase and names, such as: **Spirit** <note>original: "Spiritus"</note>, **Watery Fire** <note>original: "Ignis aqueus"</note>, **Fiery Water** <note>original: "Aqua ignea"</note>, **Universal Solvent** <note>original: "Universale solvens"</note>, and others of that kind. Both proceed by **fire**, both by **dissolving** <note>original: "solvendo"</note> and by **coagulating** <note>original: "coagulando" — the two-step cycle of "solve et coagula" (dissolve and combine) that was the foundation of alchemical practice.</note>. Both are equally long and tedious, and difficult in their investigation. In both, the underlying matter is brought to its na- <margin>turam</margin> <note>Catchword "turam" completes the word "naturam" (nature) on the following page.</note>
