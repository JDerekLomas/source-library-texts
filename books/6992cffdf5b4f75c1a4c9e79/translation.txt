### Manufacturing Methods for Western Self-Firing Firearms <gloss>By Ding Shoucun, Secretary of the Ministry of Revenue</gloss>

---

Chinese cannons and firearms typically utilize either paper fuses or priming powder, all of which must be ignited by a <term>slow-match<note>A length of cord or twine treated to burn slowly and consistently, used to ignite the powder in early firearms.</note></term>. There are two primary disadvantages to this system: first, in the frantic chaos of the battlefield, any slight negligence or error in the loading and firing process can lead to catastrophic accidents. Second, paper fuses are easily ruined by rain, while priming powder is often scattered by the wind; these methods are particularly difficult to manage during the dark of night.

For these reasons, the Westerners invented a "self-firing mechanism" <note>This refers to the flintlock or early percussion cap mechanisms which replaced the manual match-lighting process.</note>. It uses a screw to secure a piece of flint, with a steel plate serving as the cover for the ignition point, which produces fire when struck. 

The internal components consist of:
*   One large curved steel spring <note>the mainspring, which provides the force for the hammer</note>
*   One small curved steel spring <note>the sear spring</note>
*   A mechanism pad
*   Two interlocking slanted teeth <note>the notches or "bents" that hold the hammer at half-cock or full-cock</note>

The external components consist of:
*   A small steel striker
*   A small steel wheel
*   A "dragon head" <note>the hammer or cock of the gun</note>
*   A "duck bill" <note>the jaws of the hammer that grip the flint</note>
*   A screw
*   A trigger at the base of the mechanism

## Method for Manufacturing the Self-Firing Gun

To manufacture the <term>copper caps<note>original: *tong mao* (銅帽); percussion caps used for ignition.</note></term>, use thin sheets of <term>annealed red copper<note>original: *shu hong tong* (熟紅銅); copper that has been heated and cooled to make it malleable.</note></term>. First, construct a copper die-plate one and a half <term>fen<note>approximately 5 millimeters.</note></term> thick, one <term>cun<note>approximately 3.2 centimeters.</note></term> wide, and five *cun* long. Arrange twenty holes in a flat row, each hole having a diameter of about one *fen*. Inside the holes, densely carve fine threads as thin as hair. Place a copper anvil beneath the plate to serve as a support. Use a small copper <term>punch<note>original: *chu* (杵); literally "pestle," here used as a metalworking punch.</note></term> with a diameter slightly smaller than the holes. Place the red copper sheet over a hole, align the punch with it, and strike with a hammer. This will form one copper cap. After completing all twenty holes, remove the bottom support to release the caps.

Place a small amount of the <term>white powder<note>original: *bai yao* (白藥); the silver fulminate explosive produced in the previous step.</note></term> into each cap, then use the small punch to press it down firmly. Store these in a box. Once the copper caps and the explosive powder are prepared, there is a separate mechanical device used to hold and carry them for easy access; the details of this are found in the <italic>Supplementary Collection</italic>.

In manufacturing the gun, the size of the firearm may be according to standard patterns. However, it must feature a <term>threaded breech-plug<note>original: *luo si zhuan di* (螺絲轉底); literally "screw-turn bottom," referring to a threaded plug at the rear of the barrel for maintenance and structural integrity.</note></term>; otherwise, there will be no place to secure the firing mechanism and related components. 

The <term>nipple vent<note>original: *tu huo men* (凸火門); literally "protruding fire gate," the cone onto which the percussion cap is placed.</note></term> for transmitting the spark uses a hollow screw-tube. The <term>hammer<note>original: *huo long tou* (火龍頭); literally "fire dragon head," the cock or hammer of the lock.</note></term> that strikes the fire must have a <term>concave center<note>original: *ao xin* (凹心); a recessed face on the hammer.</note></term>. The depth of this recess should match the length of the copper cap. This design is intended to contain the blast and prevent fragments of the copper cap from flying out and injuring the shooter's hand upon ignition.

Once the **trigger mechanism** <note>original: "機括" (jikuo)</note> is securely set, the spring-rods will never drop prematurely, regardless of whether the device is tossed upward or plunged downward by the motion of the waves. This is a most ingenious design, as it ensures that those deploying the mines face absolutely no risk of accidental discharge.

Furthermore, the force exerted by the water bellows varies in graduated stages according to the depth of the water:

*   If the lever is submerged to a depth of **one foot**, the lifting force of the bellows is approximately **ten catties**.
*   If submerged to **three feet**, the lifting force reaches **thirty catties**.

The deeper the device is placed, the more powerful the bellows' force becomes. If it reaches a depth of one **zhang** <note>about 3.3 meters or 11 feet</note>, the force of the bellows is sufficient to lift a spring-rod set at **one hundred catties**.

### Instructions on the Use of Sinkers and Buoyancy

**Sinkers** <note>original: 墜 (zhui)</note> are constructed from lead or iron. Their size and weight may be determined by convenience; however, a large device should have a heavy sinker, while a small device requires a lighter one.

The sinkers are first stabilized around the four sides of the lower half of the **casing** <note>original: 槓 (gang); here referring to the main body or barrel of the naval mine</note> using ringed screws. The sinkers are only put into use at the moment the device enters the water, at which point they should be fastened tightly and evenly around the circumference. 

The reason they must be secured to the lower half of the casing is to ensure the device is bottom-heavy; this prevents the risk of the mine tilting or capsizing when tossed by waves. The reason the sinkers are only attached upon entering the water is to allow the casing and the weights to be transported separately, making the equipment light and easy to handle.

Generally, a wooden device is hollow; when it enters the water, its natural force is to float. Lead and iron are dense and heavy; when they enter the water, their natural force is to sink. If the **buoyancy** <note>original: 浮力 (fu li)</note> and the **sinking force** <note>original: 沈力 (chen li)</note> are perfectly balanced, the casing will merely sit level with the surface of the water. However, to ensure the casing actually begins to submerge, one must add an additional six or seven **qu** <note>original: 劬 (qu); a specific unit of weight or force measurement used in this manual</note> of sinking force.

Once a device begins to sink, it will inevitably continue until it reaches the water's bottom. The **bed-plate** <term>床板 (chuang ban): the rectangular baseplate or chassis that supports the internal components</term> is cast from pure copper. It measures five inches and eight-tenths in length, three inches and five-tenths in width, and eight-tenths in thickness <note>The measurements use traditional Chinese units: the *cun* (inch) and *fen* (tenth), where 10 *fen* equal 1 *cun*.</note>. At the head of the plate, there are two **ears** <term>耳 (er): protruding mounting lugs or brackets</term>, each six-tenths high, five-tenths wide, and nine-tenths long. These "ears" contain circular holes designed to receive the axle of the **lifting plate** <note>original: 揚板 (yang ban); previously referred to as the '揭板' (jie ban), this is the hinged component that initiates the firing sequence</note>.

Positioned one inch and four-tenths from the head, a **powder tube** <term>藥管 (yao guan): a horizontal conduit for the priming charge</term> passes crosswise through the assembly. This tube has a diameter of two-and-a-half tenths and is open on both the left and right sides. Along the "waist" (the center) of the powder tube, three holes are drilled in a row to accommodate the **fire towers** <term>火塔 (huo ta): the vertical ignition nipples or flash-points</term. 

Each end of the powder tube measures one inch in length with a diameter of six-tenths. These ends are designed to pass through the internal partition of the main barrel (or "bucket"), allowing the priming powder inside the tube to connect directly with the **main charge** <note>original: 大藥 (da yao); literally "great medicine," the standard term for the primary explosive propellant</note> located behind the partition. 

The assembly also features six small holes to receive the **spring-rods** <term>彈條 (tan tiao): flexible strips or pins used to provide tension or locking</term>, along with six matching screws. Finally, the main body of the plate is lined with eight large holes to receive the screws that secure the entire "bed" to the bottom of the **Thunder Bar** <term>雷槓 (lei gang): the heavy mechanical lever or striker that triggers the explosion</term>.
