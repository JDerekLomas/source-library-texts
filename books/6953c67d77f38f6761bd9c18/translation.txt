## TRANSLATOR’S PREFACE.

---

The *Ikhwanu-ṣ-ṣafā*, or “Brothers of Pure Friendship” (<note>literally, Brothers of Purity</note>), is the title of fifty-one Arabic treatises of considerable antiquity. These were the joint production of several authors—some sources say five, others ten—who lived and worked together in the closest intimacy and harmony; this shared bond is the origin of the work's title. Only one of these works—the one dealing with the contest between man and the animal kingdom regarding man's claim to supremacy—has, I believe, been translated into Urdu under the same title given to the collected fifty-one treatises in Arabic. Nevertheless, some of the remaining treatises are well worth translating as well.

The Urdu version of the *Ikhwanu-ṣ-ṣafā* has for some years held the honored position of a "test-book" for the first Civil and for the Military Interpreter’s Examination <note>These were standardized language proficiency exams for British colonial officials and officers in India</note>.

<summary>The translator introduces the "Ikhwanu-ṣ-ṣafā" as a collection of ancient Arabic treatises written by a cooperative of authors, noting that the specific section on the dispute between humans and animals is a standard text for linguistic examinations.</summary>

In undertaking this translation, I have steadily endeavored to keep two objects in view: 
1. To facilitate the study of the original; 
2. To produce a work which might be read and appreciated by those readers who possess no knowledge of Urdu, for the interest of the story itself. 

The text of the Urdu translator has therefore been rendered throughout as closely as is consistent with clarity and due regard to the idiom of the English language.

As an additional aid to accuracy, I have, in the course of translating, compared the Urdu with the original Arabic page by page throughout. This has not only afforded me a clear insight into the real meaning of many a doubtful passage, but it has enabled me to detect many important errors in the Urdu translation—errors which disfigure the work to a degree that makes it extremely desirable that it should be subjected to careful revision and correction.
