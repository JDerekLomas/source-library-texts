# THE MASTER

<margin>Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519)</margin>

->*“As a day well spent brings happy sleep, so a life well used brings happy death.”*<- <note>original: "Si come una giornata bene spesa dà lieto dormire, così una vita bene usata dà lieto morire." — A famous aphorism from Leonardo's notebooks.</note>

---

The sheets presented in this collection represent the pinnacle of the <term>sfumato</term> <note>A technique of blending colors and tones so subtly that there is no perceptible transition between them; literally "smoky."</note> technique and the psychological depth for which the Master is renowned. These studies of heads, captured with a precision that borders on the divine, were long held in the private collections of the British Crown.

<insert>Note: These reproductions were made using the latest heliogravure process of the time to ensure fidelity to the original chalk and ink.</insert>

By the grace of the Ministry of Public Instruction and Fine Arts, we are permitted to bring these "shadows of the soul" to the public eye. Leonardo did not merely draw a face; he mapped the internal movements of the mind <note>original: "i moti del mente"</note>.

<column-break/>

Whether depicting the grotesque features of a caricature or the sublime grace of a Madonna, the hand of the artist remains guided by a scientific rigor. To understand these sketches is to understand the very mechanics of human emotion.

We offer this volume to the scholars and dreamers alike, as a testament to the genius that flourished in the light of the Renaissance.

# THE NOTEBOOKS OF LEONARDO DA VINCI

## ON THE HUMAN FORM AND MOTION

->**A TREATISE ON ANATOMY**<-

<margin>Translated from the original Italian script</margin>

It is a common error among those who practice the art of drawing to believe that the beauty of the human figure lies solely in its outward appearance. However, as I have often observed, one cannot truly represent the <term>external accidents</term> <note>original: "accidenti esterni"; a philosophical term referring to the outward physical characteristics or movements of a body.</note> of the body without a profound understanding of the internal structures—the bones, the sinews, and the muscles—which provide the foundation for all movement.

<column-break/>

### OF THE MUSCLES AND SINEWS

The muscles must be drawn with great care, showing how they swell in action and soften in repose. <insert>Note: compare this to the tension of a crossbow string.</insert> Consider the following observations:

| Part of Body | Primary Function | Observation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Arm** | Lifting and Rotation | The <term>biceps</term> <note>original: "muscolo del braccio"</note> shortens as it pulls the forearm upward. |
| **The Leg** | Support and Locomotion | The weight of the entire trunk is distributed through the femur to the knee. |

<gloss>The soul moves the limbs through the mediation of the nerves.</gloss>

<note>Leonardo’s anatomical studies were revolutionary because he combined his artistic skill with actual dissections, a practice that was often restricted during the Renaissance.</note>

<note>This drawing is widely recognized as a preparatory study for the angel in Leonardo's masterpiece, *The Virgin of the Rocks*. It is executed in silverpoint, a technique using a silver-tipped rod on specially prepared paper that allows for no errors.</note>

<column-break/>

### DESCRIPTION OF THE WORK
The image depicts the head and shoulders of a young woman in a three-quarter view, her gaze cast downward in a gesture of serene contemplation. Her hair is styled in the delicate, flowing ringlets characteristic of the Florentine Renaissance, held in place by a thin headband. 

Leonardo utilizes <term>sfumato</term> <note>A technique of blending colors and tones so gradually that there is no perceptible transition between them, creating a "smoky" effect.</note> to achieve the soft transitions of light and shadow on her cheek and neck, giving the portrait a profound sense of psychological depth and atmosphere.

### On the Nature of Weight and Balance

The center of every weight <note>original: "centro d'ogni gravità"</note> rests upon the line that divides it in the middle, and if the weight is moved, the center of gravity moves with it. <insert>Every heavy body desires to descend toward the center of the world by the shortest path.</insert>

If a weight is suspended by a cord, the cord will align itself directly with the center of the earth. <margin>The line of direction <note>original: "linea del sito"</note> is the name given to the path from the center of the weight to the center of the world.</margin>

#### Observations on the Lever and Motion

The force <term>force: "forza," the active power applied to a machine</term> is to the resistance as the distance from the fulcrum is to the opposite distance. 

->**The Law of the Balance**<-

*   That part of the beam which is longer is the more powerful.
*   The weight that is further from the center of the scale appears heavier than it is in truth.
*   Motion is created by the inequality of weights.
