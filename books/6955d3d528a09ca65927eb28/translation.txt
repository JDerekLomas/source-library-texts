# THE
## PREFACE.

Having mentioned the motives that induced me to write this treatise and the goals I proposed for myself in the appropriate places within the following text, I think it is unnecessary to occupy the reader now with what he will encounter later. Indeed, I would judge it pointless to trouble others or myself with any kind of preface at all, were it not for the fact that I suspect this book will fall into the hands of some readers who—being unacquainted with the difficulty of attempts of this nature—will think it strange that I should publish anything about colors without providing a specific, comprehensive theory of them. 

However, I trust that intelligent and fair-minded readers will consider my position: that the stated design of this treatise is to deliver information that is historical <note>original: "Historical"; here meaning a factual record of observations and experiments rather than a chronological history</note> rather than...

# The Preface.

Dogmatic; and consequently, if I have added various new *speculative considerations and hints*, which perhaps may afford no small *assistance* toward the *framing* of a *solid and comprehensive hypothesis* <note>original: "Hypothesis"; a proposed explanation made on the basis of limited evidence as a starting point for further investigation</note>, I have done at least as much as I promised, or as the nature of my *undertaking required*. 

But there is another thing which, if it should be objected, I fear I should not be able so easily to answer: and that is, that in the following treatise (especially in the third part of it) the experiments might have been better *organized* <note>original: "Marshal'd"</note>, and some of them delivered in fewer words. For I must confess that this *essay* was written to a private friend, and that too by snatches, at several times and places, and (after my manner) on loose sheets. I often did not have everything I had already written with me when I was writing more; so, it should be no wonder if all the experiments are not arranged to the best advantage, and if some connections and sequences of them might easily have been improved. 

This is especially true since, having carelessly laid aside the loose papers for several years after they were written, I found some of those I counted upon to be very unseasonably missing when I came to put them together to send them to the *press* <note>The printing house</note>. To make any great change in the order of the rest was more than the printer’s urgency, and that of my own business <note>original: "avocations"</note>, would allow.
