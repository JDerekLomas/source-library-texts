### TO
## HAFIZ OF SHIRAZ

->Fate is a hand that possesses five fingers; when it desires to fulfill its purpose upon a man,<-
->It places two upon his eyes, another two upon his ears, and one upon his lips, saying: "Be silent!"<-
<note>original: "قضا دستيست پنج انگشت دارد..." This Persian couplet emphasizes the inescapable and silencing nature of destiny.</note>

*Thus said the Poet: "When Death comes to you,*
*All ye whose life-sand through the hour-glass slips,*
*He lays two fingers on your ears, and two*
*Upon your eyes he lays, one on your lips,*
*Whispering: Silence!" Although deaf thine ear,*
*Thine eye, my Hafiz <note>Hafiz (c. 1315–1390) was a Persian lyric poet whose collected works are regarded as a pinnacle of Persian literature.</note>, suffer Time's eclipse,*
*The songs thou sangest still all men may hear.*

*Songs of dead laughter, songs of love once hot,*
*Songs of a cup once flushed rose-red with wine,*
*Songs of a rose whose beauty is forgot,*
*A nightingale <note>In Persian poetry, the nightingale (bulbul) represents the lover longing for the rose (the beloved).</note> that piped hushed lays divine:*
*And still a graver music runs beneath*
*The tender love notes of those songs of thine,*
*Oh, Seeker of the keys of Life and Death!*

<summary>A poetic dedication to the 14th-century Persian poet Hafiz, reflecting on the themes of mortality, silence, and the enduring power of his verse despite the passage of time.</summary>

## DEDICATION

*While you were singing, the soft summer wind*
*That over Mosalla’s garden <note>A famous open-air place of prayer and recreation in Shiraz, often celebrated by the poet Hafiz.</note> blew, the stream*
*Of Ruknabad <note>A small stream near Shiraz, immortalized in Persian poetry for its clarity and beauty.</note> flowing where roses twined,*
*Carried your voice farther than you could dream.*
*To Isfahan and Baghdad’s Tartar horde,*
*Over waste and sea to Yezd and distant India;*
*Yes, even to the sunset they bore your word.*

*Behold we laugh, we warm ourselves at Love’s fire,*
*We thirst and scarce dare tell what wine we crave,*
*We lift our voices in Grief’s dark-robed choir;*
*Sing the wisdom that joy and sorrow gave!*
*If my poor rhymes held any of the heart’s lore,*
*Fresh wreaths were theirs to lay upon your grave—*
*Master and Poet, all was yours before!*

<summary>A poetic dedication addressed to the Persian poet Hafiz, acknowledging his enduring influence across the East and West and crediting him as the source of the author's inspiration.</summary>
