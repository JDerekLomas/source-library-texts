### That the citizens of the heavenly homeland are afflicted by the evils of the Babylonians as long as this world lasts is proven by many examples of Holy Scripture. Chapter IV.

By no means should we forget the word of Christ saying, “In the world you will have tribulation, but in me you will have peace.” <note>original: *In mundo pressuram habebitis, in me autem pacem*; a reference to the Gospel of John 16:33</note> The Babylonians are at odds with the heavenly citizens and pursue them even unto death; moreover, this conflict will endure for as long as we are surrounded by this mortal mass of flesh <note>A common theological metaphor for the physical body and its earthly desires</note>. Cain is still upon the earth, who kills the righteous Abel. Ham does not fear to mock <note>original: *suspendere naso*; literally "to hang from the nose," a classical Latin idiom for sneering or treating someone with contempt</note> his father. Esau displays with flashing eyes how much hatred he holds for his brother. The brothers of Joseph are unable to speak peacefully with him, and finally they sell him. The taskmasters appointed by Pharaoh afflict the children of Israel with many evils.
