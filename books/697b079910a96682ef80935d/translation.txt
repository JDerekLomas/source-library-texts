# AN
# INQUIRY
### INTO THE
## PATRIARCHAL and DRUIDICAL RELIGION, TEMPLES, etc.

-> Being the substance of some **LETTERS** to Sir *H. Jacob*, Baronet. <-

-> W H E R E I N, <-

-> The *primeval institution* and *universality* of the **CHRISTIAN SCHEME** is demonstrated; <-

-> The principles of the **PATRIARCHS** and **DRUIDS** are revealed and shown to correspond entirely with *each other*, and **BOTH** with the doctrines of *Christianity*; <-

-> The earliest *antiquities* of the **BRITISH ISLANDS** are explained; <-

-> A N D <-

-> The **SACRED STRUCTURES** of the **DRUIDS**, particularly those of **AVEBURY** <note>original: "Abiry"</note>, **STONEHENGE**, etc., are minutely described. <-

-> With an **INTRODUCTION** in defense of the various **HIEROGLYPHIC FIGURES** *described and displayed throughout this treatise*. <-

---

-> By **WILLIAM COOKE, M.A.** <-
-> Rector of *Oldbury* and *Didmarton* in *Gloucestershire*, Vicar of *Enford* in *Wiltshire*, and Chaplain to the Right Honorable *the Earl of Suffolk*. <-

---

-> Illustrated with **COPPER PLATES**. <-

> *The mind withdraws into pure and innocent places and enjoys sacred dwellings. Sufficient labor is spent neither in becoming acquainted with authors, nor in unfolding antiquity, nor in the knowledge of things, people, or times.* <note>original Latin: "Secedit animus in loca pura atque innocentia, fruiturque sedibus sacris. Nec in Auctoribus cognoscendis, nec in evolvenda Antiquitate, nec in Notitia vel rerum, vel hominum, vel temporum, satis operæ insumitur."</note> — Quintilian, *Dialogue on Orators*.

<summary>This is the title page of William Cooke's 1754 treatise, which argues that the ancient religion of the Druids and the biblical Patriarchs was fundamentally the same as Christianity. The book provides detailed descriptions of British archaeological sites like Stonehenge and Avebury to support this theory.</summary>
