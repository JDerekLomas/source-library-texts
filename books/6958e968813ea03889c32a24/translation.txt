ULYSSES ALDROVANDI <note>original: "VLYSSES ALDROVANDVS"; the famous naturalist of Bologna (1522–1605)</note> here brings forth his serpents to you, he who is by far the best among all natural philosophers <note>original: "Physiologorum"; here meaning those who study the nature of living things</note>. These are indeed serpents, yet they are not poisonous in their strike, nor lethal in their bite; they do not waste away the body with their spit or breath, nor are they fatal to the sight, such as those produced in the sandy deserts of Libya for the Africans. Instead, they are harmless, tame, and fruitful, because Wisdom has nourished them on the grass of Apollo <note>a metaphor for poetic and scientific inspiration</note> in the garden of the Muses. And although all of them, like the Scytale <note>a mythical snake with a skin like parchment or varied colors</note>, shine forth miraculously, clothed in many-shaped colors and a singular variety of facts and learning, they would nonetheless crawl in the dark upon the ground, stained by the mud of envy, did they not look toward you (Most Noble Prelate) as a Leader of the Muses <note>original: "Musageten"</note>, who does not disdain to cherish this family of soothing snakes with your patronage. The most learned author has long since sought to attract the great-hearted Lion of the Peretto family <note>a reference to the heraldic lion on the Peretti family coat of arms</note> with his literary services...

Indeed, we confess (most beneficent Prince) and openly profess, that from the flowing clouds of your liberality, which you pour into the bosoms of the needy and the deserving, we have proven that to be true in you which the ancients falsely proclaimed regarding Luernius, the leader of the Celts: namely, that the tracks of your chariot produce gold and benefits for mortals. Indeed, bound to you by the tightest bond of service, we are hardly able to explain in words—much less in reality—with what devotion and ardor we venerate the spirit of your divine virtue; in honor...

...to the sanctuary of [your] heart, already bringing to you the sacred headbands, the rods of office, and the purple robes in full measure. There are, I confess, leaders in your family **who, having been clothed with the Heavenly tiara on Earth, held scepters over nations and command over souls;** <note>This refers to ancestors who served as Popes or high-ranking prelates.</note> just as we celebrate their names, **so do we venerate their divine spirits.** But their ancient glory, which the **Christian World** recalls with grateful memory, receives a new brilliance from the radiance of your own heroic virtues today—a light that shall be darkened by no gloom of a stepmother-like fortune. <note>The author uses "stepmother fortune" to describe harsh or fickle fate.</note> It is glorious for descendants to have their cradles illuminated by the most ample titles of honor from the blood of their ancestors; yet it is far more glorious to graft one's own honors onto those of others, and by one's own merits to shine in the light of the age as an example to posterity. Your fortune, O most wise Prince, is in a high place because your virtue is in plain view; because both shine without ambition and scatter the seeds of a great soul, destined to take root in the soil of eternity as a trunk of solid glory. ## Summary of the Privilege of His Sacred Imperial Majesty

By the Privilege of His Sacred Imperial Majesty, it is provided that no one may reprint any of the Histories of Ulisse Aldrovandi of Bologna <note>original: "Vlyſsis Aldrouandi Bonon."; Aldrovandi (1522–1605) was a celebrated naturalist and physician from Bologna</note> which treat of Animals—and consequently also of Serpents and Dragons—either in whole or in part, without the will and express consent of the Author or his heirs. This restriction lasts for a period of ten years, beginning from the day the volume is completed. Furthermore, no one may sell copies printed elsewhere, under the penalty—whenever such a person is caught—of the forfeiture of all books and a fine of ten marks of pure gold. These terms are set forth more extensively in the Imperial diploma <term>diploma: a formal charter or official document granting a right or privilege</term> issued in the Royal Castle at Prague on the first day of April, in the year one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine.

Rudolf II, Emperor. <note>Rudolf II (1552–1612) was the Holy Roman Emperor known for his patronage of the arts and sciences.</note>

Rudolf Coraduz. <note>The Imperial Vice-Chancellor.</note>

->*By the personal mandate of His Sacred Imperial Majesty,*<-
->*Johann Barvitius.*<- <note>Johann Anton Barvitius, a high-ranking official and privy councillor in Rudolf's court.</note>

# BARTHOLOMEW
## AMBROSINI
->GREETINGS TO THE BENEVOLENT READER.<-

<image-desc>A large historiated woodcut initial 'H' featuring a seated figure in a landscape, possibly representing a scholar or a deity, within a rectangular frame.</image-desc> I believe it is acknowledged by everyone that man was born for the benefit of others. Therefore, I cannot help but assert that those who strive to hide away knowledge—acquired with the greatest zeal and produced through diligent labor—sin gravely; for they clearly abuse this noble gift of the mind. Conversely, those who are filled with the power of wonder and understanding deserve countless heralds of praise. Recognizing this divine gift of nature, they leave nothing untouched so that they may daily favor the cause of letters <note>The "cause of letters" refers to the advancement of scholarship and literature</note>. Among these men, the most illustrious and immortally famous Ulisse Aldrovandi <note>1522–1605, a celebrated Italian naturalist and physician</note> must, by right and merit, be counted. Distinguished by such a great and powerful gift from God, he was of no small help to the students of his age and to posterity through his teaching and writing (for it is in this way that the fellowship of civil life is fostered among men). Not only did he commit the works written by himself during his lifetime to the vital breezes <note>original: "vitalibus auris," a poetic way of saying he published them for the living world</note>, but he also bequeathed the engraved images of the works yet to be written to the Most Illustrious Senate of Bologna. He did this so that, under the protection and authority of such great men, all the images of natural things, adorned with their respective histories, might finally come into the hands of men. Thus, the curious reader will understand the number of books published by him through these briefly explained titles. First, he taught the nature of Insects, Crustaceans, and soft-bodied animals; then he published his Ornithology <term>Ornithology: the study of birds</term> divided into three volumes (while the books on Fish, as well as those on Solid-hoofed and Cloven-hoofed Quadrupeds, were posthumous). But thereafter, the care of composing the remaining works having been entrusted to me by the Most Illustrious and Prudent Senators of Bologna, two years ago I added the history of Digitated Quadrupeds <term>Digitated Quadrupeds: animals with separate toes or fingers, such as cats or dogs</term>, arranged with watchful labor, yet with the help of my brother Hyacinth; I added these to the images found in the Museum. Likewise, at present, I bring forth to the public the images of Serpents and Dragons, graphically adorned with descriptions, so that, God willing, I may be able to proceed to the publishing of not a few other works. Farewell, honest reader, and easily forgive any errors, as you may find they flowed not from the mind, but from a hurrying pen or from the carelessness of the Printer.

From the formless Chaos <note>original: "Ab informi Chao" - a classical reference to the state of the universe before creation</note>, the genius of Ulisse Aldrovandi and the study of the most excellent Mr. Bartolomeo Ambrosini have brought forth this treatise on Serpents and Dragons into its current form and appearance. This work has appeared to me to be truly filled with incredible erudition, properly fashioned with tireless labor, and most perfect in all its parts. In it, there is nothing to be changed, nothing to be deleted, nothing to be corrected or polished; it is judged, beyond all doubt and far from any flattery, to be worthy not of censorship, but of all praise. His lofty and commendable learning will not only suffice for practical use with great benefit and profit for students, but will even proceed toward the marvelous and the miraculous. This is the judgment I have made of so great a Doctor, whose pen (may the best God grant) may progress further day by day; meanwhile, I judge by the best right that the present work should be committed to the press, and I subscribe my name.

*I, Mr. Ottaviano Finati, Cleric Regular of St. Paul <note>The Barnabites</note>, and Rector of the Holy Penitentiary of Bologna, on behalf of the Most Eminent and Most Reverend Lord Cardinal Archbishop of Bologna.*

This work on Serpents and Dragons by the most illustrious Mr. Ulisse Aldrovandi, filled with every kind of learning and elaborated with the singular labor of the most excellent Bartolomeo Ambrosini, and most useful to all, I, the undersigned, have viewed most willingly.
