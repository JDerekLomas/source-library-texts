# THE SIDEREAL MESSENGER

<note>original: "Sidereus Nuncius"</note>

->Unfolding great and very wonderful sights, and proposing them to the consideration of every man, but especially of philosophers and astronomers; which have been observed by<-

->**GALILEO GALILEI**<-

->Patrician of Florence, Public Mathematician at the University of Padua,<-

->With the aid of a **SPYGLASS**<-
<note>original: "Perspicilli." At this time, the word "telescope" had not yet been coined; Galileo refers to his instrument as a "perspicillum" or "occhiale."</note>

->lately devised by him, in the surface of the Moon, in innumerable Fixed Stars, in the Milky Way, in Nebulous Stars, but especially in<-

->**FOUR PLANETS**<-

->Flying around the star of **JUPITER** at unequal intervals and periods with wonderful swiftness; which, unknown to anyone until this day, the author recently discovered for the first time, and determined to name the<-

->**MEDICEAN STARS**<-
<note>Galileo named the moons of Jupiter after his patrons, the Medici family, specifically the four brothers: Cosimo, Francesco, Carlo, and Lorenzo.</note>

<summary>The title page of Galileo's 1610 treatise announcing his revolutionary astronomical observations made with a telescope, including the mountains on the moon and the moons of Jupiter.</summary>

<meta>This page describes a commemorative engraving of a significant relic belonging to Galileo Galilei, specifically the broken objective lens from the telescope with which he discovered the moons of Jupiter.</meta>

# GALILEO'S BROKEN LENS.

->EXHIBITED IN THE LOAN COLLECTION OF SCIENTIFIC APPARATUS<-
->AT THE SOUTH KENSINGTON MUSEUM, 1876.<-

->*From a photograph*<-
->*of the Science and Art Department, South Kensington.*<-

<note>The image described is a highly decorative Baroque engraving. It depicts the actual cracked lens used by Galileo, now preserved as a scientific relic in an ornate frame.</note>

At the very top of the frame, a small circular crest supported by two cherubs contains the text: **THE MEDICEAN STARS** <note>original: "MEDICEA SIDERA"; this refers to the four largest moons of Jupiter, which Galileo named in honor of his patrons, the Medici family.</note>. Directly beneath this is a sunburst design with concentric rings inscribed with: **OFFSPRING, THE GREAT INCREASE OF JUPITER** <note>original: "SOBOLES MAGNVM IOVIS INCREM."</note> and **THE ILLUSTRIOUS CHILD OF THE GODS** <note>original: "CLARA DEVM NATVM."</note>.

The central cracked lens is held by metal clips and is surrounded by an inner oval frame containing various short phrases: **HERE BOTH** (left), **THE SPOTS** (top left) <note>A reference to sunspots, which Galileo also studied.</note>, **OF PHOEBUS AND** (top right) <note>Phoebus is a poetic name for the Sun.</note>, **OF JUPITER** (right), **THE STARS** (bottom right), **HE REVEALED** (bottom), and **THE UNSEEN** (bottom left).

At the base of the frame, an elaborate scroll features a four-line inscription:
**THE HEAVENS OPENED TO THE LYNX-LIKE MIND OF GALILEO** <note>The "Lynx-like mind" refers to the Accademia dei Lincei, or Academy of the Lynxes, the scientific society to which Galileo belonged.</note>
**BY THIS TINY MASS OF GLASS, HE SHOWED STARS NOT YET SEEN**
**RIGHTLY CALLED "MEDICEAN" BY THEIR DISCOVERER**
**FOR TRULY THE WISE MAN RULES EVEN THE STARS** <note>original: "SAPIENS NEMPE DOMINATVR ET ASTRIS."</note>

<summary>This page features an engraving of Galileo's original cracked telescope lens, presented in an ornate Baroque frame with Latin inscriptions celebrating his discovery of the Medicean stars (the moons of Jupiter).</summary>
<keywords>Galileo Galilei, Medicean Stars, Jupiter's moons, telescope lens, South Kensington Museum, scientific instruments, Accademia dei Lincei</keywords>
