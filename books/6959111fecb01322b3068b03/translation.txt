The present work was, among those of its kind, one of the few produced by the art of typography in Mexico during the course of the 16th century.

The National Museum, in proceeding to reprint it, has done so as much for it being a document that—with great originality—presents to us in a clear manner the state in which the natural sciences <note>The term "natural sciences" here refers to the study of biology, geology, and physics as understood in the early modern period.</note> were found in the immediate wake of the Spanish conquest, as for the fact that the known copies of it are extremely scarce.

It was written by Dr. Juan de Cárdenas, who, as he himself expresses, was a native of Constantina, in Andalusia <note>A region in southern Spain.</note>, where he first saw the light of day in the year 1563. He was a Professor of Vespers <note>The "Vespers" chair was an afternoon teaching position in colonial universities, second in rank to the morning "Prime" chair</note> at the University of Mexico, and as he relates in his work, he practiced his profession both in said Capital as well as in Compostela and Guadalajara. At the age of twenty-six he composed his work, which, due to various setbacks, he was unable to present in print until five years later. This is all we know regarding Dr. Cárdenas, for, due to a lack of documents, we have not even managed to ascertain the date of his death, which must have occurred in the early years of the 17th century.

The National Museum, desiring to enlighten the publication of this work with a study regarding its content, entrusted Mr. Dr. José Terrés with said task.
