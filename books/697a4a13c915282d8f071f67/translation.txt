## Counsel
**Whoever** does not observe the counsels at least mentally:
cannot observe the commandments. Chapter 47. <note>The "counsels" (evangelical counsels of poverty, chastity, and obedience) are paths to perfection, while "precepts" or commandments are the minimum requirements for all believers.</note>
**The** perfect observe the counsels in practice. Chapter 47.

## Consolation
**On** the deception of those who love God only for consolation: which they feel in that love. Chapters 60, 67, 68, 99. <note>original: "consolatione." In Catherine’s writing, this refers to "spiritual sweetness" or the emotional joy felt during prayer, which can becomes a distraction if sought for its own sake.</note>
**God** sometimes deprives the soul of consolation for its own benefit. Chapter 64.
**On** the deception of those: who do not help their neighbor in necessity so as not to lose their spiritual consolation and devotion. Chapter 70, Chapter 59.
**On** the deception of those: who have placed their affection in consolation and mental visions. Chapter 71, 106.
**Repetition** of the aforementioned consolations. Chapter 72.
**The** servants of God do not care for consolation in this world. Chapter 143.

## Contemplation.
**Everything** must be left behind when God sends some contemplation into the mind. Chapter 66. <note>Catherine teaches that even good works or planned prayers should be set aside if God grants a direct moment of contemplative union.</note>

## Contrition.
**Contrition** and love satisfy for the punishment: and for the guilt. Chapter 4. <note>In medieval theology, "guilt" (colpa) refers to the offense against God, while "punishment" (pena) refers to the debt of justice that must be paid through penance or Purgatory.</note>
**How** one must have contrition especially at one's death. Chapter 37.
**Everything** must be left behind to attend to the contrition that God gives. Chapter 76.
**When** contrition is valid without confession and when not. Chapter 75.

## Body.
**How** the body hinders the soul. Chapters 83, 84.

## Bodily (Exercises).
**Bodily** exercise satisfies neither for the punishment: nor for the guilt. Chapters 9, 11, 12. <note>original: "Exercitio corporal." Catherine emphasizes that external penance (like fasting) is a tool, but only the internal "affection" of the heart can truly satisfy for sin.</note>

# Repertory of Dialogues
## Correction
**Against** prelates <note>In the Catholic Church, a prelate is a high-ranking member of the clergy, such as a bishop or abbot, who has authority over others.</note> who do not correct their subjects. Chapter 118.
**Faulty** prelates cannot correct their subjects; and subjects do wrong by not accepting correction even if the prelates are wicked. Chapter 125.
**Prelates** who do not correct their subjects are the cause of all evils. Chapter 125.
**On** the correction by holy prelates toward their subjects. Chapter 119.
**How** one ought to correct their neighbor without false judgment. Chapter 102.

## Side
**For** many reasons, Christ opened his side on the cross. Chapter 75. <note>This refers to the wound in Jesus's side, which Catherine frequently describes as a refuge for the soul and a symbol of God's open heart.</note>

## Cruelty
**On** cruelty against one's neighbor. Chapter 6.

## Damnation
**The** damned cannot desire any good. Chapter 40.
**Their** punishment will increase on the day of judgment upon seeing the glory of the blessed. Chapter 42. <note>In medieval theology, the "pain of loss" was intensified for the damned by witnessing the joy they rejected.</note>

## Desires
**What** the desires of the righteous are. Chapter 2.
**Desires** of the perfect, and especially in suffering pain for Christ. Chapters 78, 79.
**How** much the desire to suffer pain pleases God. Chapter 5.
**How** it is necessary to walk through the virtues with desire. Chapter 54.
**One** walks with desires when the powers of the soul are united. Chapter 54. <note>The "powers of the soul" traditionally refer to the memory, the intellect, and the will.</note>
**According** to the soul's desire, God manifests Himself to it. Chapter 61.
**On** the desire for tears <note>original: "lachryme." In mystical tradition, the "gift of tears" is a physical manifestation of deep spiritual contrition or love.</note> and how much it pleases God. Chapter 91.
**On** the infinite desire of tears. Chapter 92.
**God** fulfills the holy desires of those who persevere in knocking at the door. Chapter 107. <note>A biblical reference to Matthew 7:7: "Knock and the door will be opened to you."</note>
**God** delays His gifts to make our desires grow. Chapter 42.
