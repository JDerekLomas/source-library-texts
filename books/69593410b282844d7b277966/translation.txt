### THE SECRETS
# OF ALL SECRETS
->THAT IS<-
## HIEROGLYPHICS
## EGYPTIAN-GREEK,
->Commonly not yet known,<-
->to demonstrate the origin of the false gods,<-
->goddesses, heroes, living creatures, and customs<-
->accepted as sacred among the ancients, derived<-
->from the single Egyptian Craft <note>original: "artificio", referring to the Great Work of alchemy</note>, which produced the golden<-
->medicine for mind and body. From this source, the many<-
->allegories of the poets, the fabulous narratives of<-
->writers, and the errors scattered throughout the entire<-
->circle of knowledge <note>original: "Encyclopaediam"</note> are revealed by the clearest<-
->light of truth, and each thing is restored<-
->to its proper tribe, explained in six<-
->books.<-
->By the Author<-
# MICHAEL MAIER, COUNT
->OF THE IMPERIAL PALACE, KNIGHT EXEMPT,<-
->Doctor of Philosophy and Medicine, and formerly<-

# SUMMARY OF THE INDIVIDUAL BOOKS
->*of this Treatise.*<-

BOOK 1. Concerning the Egyptian Hieroglyphic Gods: Osiris, Isis, Mercury, Vulcan, Typhon, etc.; and concerning the wondrous works of the Kings, the characteristics of individual living creatures, and other monuments pertaining to this subject. <note>The author views Egyptian mythology as a coded language for natural philosophy.</note>

BOOK 2. Concerning the allegories of the Greeks, especially the "golden" ones—such as the Golden Fleece of Jason, the Golden Apples of the Hesperides, etc.—denoting nothing other than the "golden medicine." <note>The "golden medicine" refers to the Philosopher's Stone or the Universal Panacea.</note>

BOOK 3. Concerning the fictitious genealogy of the gods and goddesses, which is Philosophical or Chemico-Medical in nature. <term>Philosophical: in this context, referring to Alchemy, often called the "Hermetic Philosophy."</term>

BOOK 4. Concerning the festivals and sacred rites, as well as the contests and games, established for the sake of this science.

BOOK 5. Concerning the Labors of Hercules, signifying the very same craftsmanship <note>original: "artificium"</note> of the Great Work.

BOOK 6. Concerning the Trojan expedition and the requirements without which Troy could not be captured, and the wanderings of Ulysses, pertaining to the same matter.
