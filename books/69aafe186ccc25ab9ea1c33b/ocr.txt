This is an administrative text from the **Ur III period** (Neo-Sumerian, ca. 21st century BC), likely originating from the city of **Umma**. It records the delivery of fresh apples to the palace. The tablet is a standard cushion-shaped clay tablet in good condition, featuring a vertical crack on both the obverse and reverse.

<notes>
- **Obv. 1, 3**: The fruit amounts are written in standard capacity measures: 1(barig) is a large horizontal wedge; 1(ban2) and 3(ban2) are represented by 1 and 3 vertical wedges respectively.
- **Obv. 2**: `ki-szum2-ma-ta` (from the onion field). In Umma, apples were frequently intercropped in onion gardens.
- **Rev. 3**: `iti szu-numun` is the 4th month of the Umma calendar.
- **Rev. 4-5**: The year formula `mu {d}szu-{d}suen lugal` corresponds to the first year of Šu-Suen (ca. 2037 BC). The name is split across two lines due to space constraints.
- **Museum Label**: The number "83.72" on the bottom edge is a modern inventory or auction lot number.
</notes>
