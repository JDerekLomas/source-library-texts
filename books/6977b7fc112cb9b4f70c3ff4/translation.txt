## On the Brothers of the R. C.
<note>original: "De Fratribus R. C." Referring to the *Fraternitas Rosae Crucis* or the Brotherhood of the Rosy Cross.</note>

Fortune afflicts these men through wars and through the sea,
Through wraths and snares, and through labors linked together,
Always to be exchanged for even heavier ones; old age itself,
Long expected, and desired with ill-omened prayers,
Subjects the body, so easily torn, to countless diseases.
None have a fixed home; they used to dwell in deep valleys,
In a secluded grove, and in a cave with overhanging rocks,
Amidst the grassy beds of riverbanks and the dens of wild beasts,
Under the burning sun, and beneath the axis of the freezing sky.
When the region of the heavens is corrupted, a wretched plague
Returns to the trees and the crops, a year of death.
They would surrender their sweet lives, or drag along their
Sickly bodies; then the Dog Star scorched the barren fields,
<note><term>Dog Star</term>: Sirius. In antiquity and the Renaissance, the "dog days" of summer when Sirius rose were associated with extreme heat, drought, and the spread of pestilence.</note>
The grasses withered, and the diseased grain denied them food.
Almighty Jupiter, who alone from the highest heaven
Moves all things, who surveys the affairs and cares of men,
Do you see these things? Or, Father, when you hurl your thunderbolts,
Do we tremble in vain? Do blind fires in the clouds
Terrify our minds and stir up empty rumblings?
Do we place our offerings upon your altars for nothing?
What are we building! What hope is there! Do we cherish an empty fame,
We, your own progeny, to whom you grant the citadel of heaven!
What of your holy faith? What of your ancient care for your own?
Great Father, what end do you finally grant to us, driven by so many waves!
Is this how you restore us, the Brothers, to your sovereignty,
<note>original: "sceptra." Literally "scepters," symbolizing divine authority or the successful completion of the Great Work.</note>
Now exhausted by every misfortune, and in want of all things?
