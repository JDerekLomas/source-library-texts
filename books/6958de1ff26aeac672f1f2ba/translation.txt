### SUMMARY OF PRIVILEGES.

It is provided by Royal privilege <note>A "privilege" was an early form of copyright granted by the sovereign to protect a printer's investment.</note> that no one other than Christopher Plantin <note>The most influential printer of the Northern Renaissance, based in Antwerp.</note> may print the book titled *History of Flowers and of Certain Fragrant Plants Used in Garlands, by Rembert Dodoens, Physician of Mechelen*, or sell copies printed elsewhere for the next six years, under penalty of confiscation of the books, as is more fully set forth in the letters issued at Brussels on December 2, 1567.

->De Langhe.<-

Indeed, concerning the origin and the discoverers of flowers and herbs, many fabulous tales are found among the Poets and other authors, both Greek and Latin <note>original: "tam Graecos, quàm Latinos"</note>, which would be long and exceedingly tedious to recount here. It is, however, very well known that gardens receive their greatest ornament, their greatest beauty, and their highest grace from these. For not from any topiary works or trellises <note>term: "perticalibus" refers to pole-supported structures or lattices used for climbing plants</note>, however artfully woven or adorned with colors, nor from shaded bowers or other garden works, does so much decoration come to them as from flowers and scented or garland-herbs <note>term: "coronariis" refers to plants specifically grown to be woven into crowns or wreaths</note>; without which, indeed, all garden works are destitute of ornament, grace, and pleasantness. Moreover, the pleasure and recreation of the mind derived from these can be nothing but most honorable. For they remind man of that which is beautiful and virtuous.
