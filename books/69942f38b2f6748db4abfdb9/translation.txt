He says again that God is a **lover of humanity** <note>original: φιλάνθρωπον (philanthrōpon) — a common epithet for God in Eastern Christian theology, emphasizing His benevolence toward mankind.</note>; and that the whole universe and the things held together within it are guided by **providence** for the benefit of the earth. He explains that the process of generation arises from the elements, and that everything occurring in this world happens in harmony and succession; from this, he says, death also happens to the body.

But Paul says that both death and life are held within him by the power of the **Spirit** <note>original: πνεύματος (pneumatos) — abbreviated in the text as a nomen sacrum. It refers to the Holy Spirit.</note>. For he says that the Spirit, through righteousness, gives life to the spirit within us; and again, that the Spirit has life in itself. By this same Spirit, he says, everyone shall be raised. For this mortal body of ours is given life in the Spirit.

Then, following this, he says again that God is in the Spirit, and the Son is in the Spirit, and the future blessings are said to be in the Spirit. We also await our very hope in the Spirit, and we bear the fruit of righteousness in the Spirit. He says that love, ascetic practice, and the virtues are all **spiritual** <note>original: πνευματικάς (pneumatikas) — meaning things pertaining to the Holy Spirit rather than just the human mind.</note>. 

And indeed, he says that the Lord God Himself, and through Him the Lord Jesus Christ, was present to the world even before the **dispensation** <term>dispensation: (original: οἰκονομίας / oikonomias) — the theological term for God's plan of salvation, specifically the incarnation of Christ.</term> of the flesh, being made manifest to all through the prophets. He says that the true light, our Lord Jesus Christ, appeared to us, bringing knowledge to those sitting in darkness; and we are taught the renewal of our nature through Christ’s presence. 

Just as Paul also shows these things similarly in his epistles; for that which was made manifest by grace shines the truth into all people. And again, he says that we are being renewed, that we possess the **first-fruits** <note>original: ἀπαρχήν (aparchēn) — a biblical term referring to the first portion of a harvest, used here to mean the beginning of the Spirit's work in a person.</note> of the Spirit, and that we confess our renewal through it. Our spiritual nature is made manifest to the **inner man** <note>original: ἔσω ἄνθρωπον (esō anthrōpon) — a Pauline concept referring to the soul or the spiritual core of a person.</note>. The future and the good things hoped for in the Spirit are also themselves made manifest in the Spirit. 

Furthermore, by receiving again the grace of the Spirit, He makes us worthy of the preaching and the promise in Christ Jesus our Lord. He says that Christ and our life are made manifest, and all spiritual things are hoped for in Him. He shows that Christ rose from death, and by His death, He conquered death; He abolished every rule and every authority, and death—the last enemy—is abolished, and immortality is handed over to all. Then, death shall be no more, and Christ shall appear as all in all.

Regarding the members of the physical body, he reminds us that one must seek to guard the body with great accuracy. For the Lord says that for the sake of the future resurrection and eternal life, we should worry about nothing more in this world, except to have hope in the Spirit. This does not mean seeking everything according to the value of that hope while being careless about the body's nourishment, but rather that we should set aside all excessive anxiety concerning the body. 

Concerning the use of the body's members within us, he says that God delivers all our members as **weapons of righteousness** <note>A direct reference to Romans 6:13.</note> to the Spirit. He says that the body is a **temple of the Holy Spirit**, and that all care should be given to guarding the body. He says we have the first-fruits of the Spirit and live on earth in hope, awaiting the **redemption** <term>redemption: (original: ἀπολύτρωσιν / apolytrōsin) — the final liberation of the physical body from decay at the end of time.</term> of our body. And all creation groans and travails together until now, expecting the glory of the children of God. All these things are spiritual—not according to what is seen by the eyes, but according to what is understood by the mind. We have great hope in the Spirit regarding these blessings, just as the Apostle Paul speaks concerning these things.
