The volume is held together by a white binding thread using a four-hole stitching pattern, a hallmark of Chinese bookmaking that allowed for durability and ease of handling.

### Ceremonial Protocols for the Reception of Foreign States Submitting and Dispatching Envoys to Present Tribute

**Preliminary Procedures**
Before the ceremony takes place, the **Office of Ceremonies** <note>The government bureau responsible for managing the logistics of imperial rituals and diplomatic receptions.</note> shall lead the foreign envoys to the **Tianjie Temple** <note>The Temple of the Heavenly Realm, a major Buddhist site in Nanjing used during the early Ming dynasty as a lodging and rehearsal space for foreign dignitaries.</note> to practice the required rituals for three days. A formal day will then be selected for the imperial audience.

**Preparation of the Hall (One Day Prior)**
One day before the audience, the **Directorate of Ceremonial** <note>A powerful agency of palace officials, often eunuchs, in charge of the Emperor's internal affairs.</note> shall arrange the **Imperial Throne** and an incense table in the center of the **Fengtian Hall** <term>Fengtian Hall: The Hall of Venerating Heaven, the primary ceremonial building in the Forbidden City where the most important state functions occurred.</term>. The **Chief Minister of the Seals** shall set the Seal Table in front of the **Imperial Throne**. 

**Arrangement of the Courtyard and Tribute**
The **Office of Ceremonies** shall set the Memorial Table to the north of the central path of the **Dan-chi** <term>Dan-chi: The "Vermilion Steps" or the grand red-paved courtyard and staircases leading up to the main hall.</term>. 

Additionally, in the exact center of the Hall, a station for the **Regional Tributes** <note>original: "fangwu" (方物), literally "local things," referring to specialized goods or products brought as tribute from the envoy's home country.</note> shall be set to the south of the table for memorials and letters. To the east and west of the central path, the attendants responsible for lifting the tribute tables shall be positioned to the left and right of the goods.

**Positioning of Personnel**
*   **Envoys:** Their positions shall be set to the east and west of the central path, located south of the tribute tables and facing north.
*   **Interpreters:** Their positions shall be set to the northwest of the envoys' stations, facing north.
*   **Civil and Military Officials:** Their standing positions for the audience shall be set at the north end of the **Dan-chi**, with the two wings facing each other from the east and west.
*   **Ceremonial Officials:** The officials responsible for receiving and announcing the memorials, those receiving and announcing the tribute goods, and the official tasked with unfolding the memorials, shall be positioned to the west of the Memorial Table, facing east.
*   **Official for Receiving Decrees:** This official shall be positioned inside the Hall, facing west.

Throughout the successive reigns of the dynasty, there have been slight variations in this ceremony. One day before the ceremony, officials from the **Directorate of Palace Attendants** arrange the **Imperial Throne** and the incense table in the **Hall of Supreme Harmony** <note>original: "Fengtian Dian"; the primary ceremonial hall of the Forbidden City during the early Ming dynasty</note> according to standard ritual. The **Seals Office** sets the Seal Table before the **Imperial Throne**. The **Office of Ritual Ceremonies** sets the Proclamation Table in front of the Seal Table. Five tables for the **Patent and Seal** <term>Cebao; the formal documents and seals that confirm a prince's rank and land grants</term> are placed in the center of the hall, and ten pavilions to hold the Patent and Seal are placed to the east on the **Crimson Steps** <note>Danbi; the elevated terrace and staircase leading to the main hall</note>.

The **Crown Prince’s** standing position is to the east of the **Imperial Throne**. The various **Princes** are positioned to perform their obeisance on the **Crimson Steps** and before the **Imperial Throne**, all facing north <note>facing the Emperor, who traditionally sits facing south</note>. All other arrangements, including the guards and ceremonial attendants, are identical to the ceremony for the investiture of the **Crown Prince** <note>literally "the Eastern Palace," a metonym for the heir apparent</note>.

Early on the day of the ceremony, the various attendants take their positions. The drums are beaten three times <note>known as the "Three Stricts," these drum sequences signaled the stages of preparation for the Emperor's appearance</note>. The Master of Ceremonies announces the "Middle Strict." Officials from the **Directorate of Imperial Regalia** request **His Majesty** to take his place in the **Hall of Prudent Conduct** <note>Jinshen Dian; a hall used by the Emperor for dressing and preparation</note>, dressed in his **Dragon Robe and Crown** <term>Gunmian; the highest level of sacrificial and ceremonial attire</term>.

A formal request is made for the **Crown Prince** to wait at the **Gate of Supreme Harmony** <note>Fengtian Men</note>, dressed in his ceremonial robes and crown. The **Imperial Prince** <note>the specific prince being invested</note> wears the **Far-Wandering Crown** <term>Yuanyou Guan; a distinctive crown with multiple ridges worn by princes and high-ranking nobles</term> and a **Crimson Gauze Robe**. The ushers then lead the civil and military officials, foreign envoys, monks, Taoist priests, and local elders to their designated places.

The **Imperial Prince** bows. <gloss>Music plays.</gloss> He rises.
