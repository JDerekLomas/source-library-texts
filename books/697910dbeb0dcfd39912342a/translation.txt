# On Proportion

Since I have now demonstrated the proportions for you—namely the man, the woman, and the child—in their divisions, I will also teach you how to bend and turn them in their primary parts in the simplest <note>The original phrase "auff das schlechtst" here means plainly or simply, rather than "poorly."</note> way, just as I have often mentioned before. If you pay close attention to the "death" <note>original: "deß todts," referring to an anatomical skeleton or flayed figure (écorché) used as a drawing reference.</note> that I made for the man's proportion, it will serve you very well in this bending and turning of the figures. I show you this only so that you know how to make the woman's limbs more lovely than the man's—although one also finds women who, by their nature, have limbs as coarse as a man's.
