## CHAPTER II.

->*Concerning the second kind of witches, the vain and superstitious, who are implicitly deceived by demons.*<-

**THE SECOND** kind of witches consists of those women who neither expressly invoke demons, nor wish or desire them to be present; their witchcraft is performed through vain and superstitious methods and rites. Their operations are carried out by incantation <note>original: "incantatione," used here for harming</note> to inflict injury, and by "pre-chanting" <note>original: "præcantatione," a term for charms used in folk healing</note> to heal. There is not just one way of performing witchcraft among them, but many and varied ways, according to how this genus is divided into many species. For enchanters or enchantresses labor with words and songs in profane rites: sometimes joining together herbs, sometimes characters <note>symbolic figures or sigils</note>, and sometimes strange, fabricated names without any meaning. Such was the author of the profane "Notary Art" <term>Ars Notoria: a medieval grimoire that claimed to grant knowledge through the contemplation of complex figures and prayers</term>, who dares to promise the knowledge of all arts through characters and a mixture of Greek, Hebrew, and Arabic names—or even names fabricated by chance or at will. **GEOMANCY** <note>Earth-divination</note> is named from the Greek *ge* <note>earth</note> and *manteia* <note>divination</note>, which is to say, divination of the earth. **CHIROMANCY** <note>Palmistry</note> is named from *cheir* <note>hand</note> and *manteia*, that is, divination of the hand. This is when a prophecy is provided by the inspection of the hands, through the consideration of the lines and signs within them.

**PAEDOMANCY** <note>Divination by children</note> is named from *pais* <note>child</note> and *manteia*, which is the divination of a boy. This is performed in many and various ways: sometimes in the entrails of children—a practice of the pagans who sacrificed boys to idols; sometimes in a child's vision, when spirits are summoned into a fingernail or elsewhere; and sometimes from the gestures and weeping of children.

**ORNEOMANCY** <note>Bird-divination</note> is named from *orneon* <note>bird</note> and *manteia*, which is the divination of a bird. In Latin this is otherwise called **Augury**, specifically when a prophecy is made from the song, movement, and flight of birds.

**PYTHIOMANCY** is named from *Pythios*, that is, Apollo, and *manteia*, which is the divination of Apollo. This was the kind once practiced by the Pythonesses <note>the Pythia or Delphic oracles</note>, who boasted that they possessed the spirit of Apollo within their bellies, through whom they would predict future events.

**GENESEOMANTIA** <note>original: "Geneseomantia"</note> is named from the Greek words for *birth* and *divination*, that is, the foretelling of a birth. It belongs to those who promise that they can predict, through the judgment of the stars and the figure of the heavens at the hour of birth, a person's entire life, character, fortune, and death.

**METROMANTIA** <note>original: "Metromantia"</note> is named from the Greek words for *measure* and *divination*, which is the divination of measurement. This is familiar today to certain old women who, by measuring a belt, a robe, a towel, or a thread by the length of the forearm <term>cubit: an ancient unit of length based on the forearm</term>, give prophecies concerning uncertain matters according to their craft.

**ARITHMOMANTIA** <note>original: "Arithmomantia"</note> is named from the Greek words for *number* and *divination*, that is, the divination of numbers. This occurs when a prophecy is sought from the numerical values of letters, hours, days, or names, as is done with the "Wheel of Pythagoras," the "Wheel of Apuleius," and those of other most vain men.

**MOLYBDOMANTIA** <note>original: "Molybdomantia"</note> is named from the Greek words for *lead* and *divination*, that is, the divination of lead. This is when, during the pouring of molten lead into cold water, monstrous shapes are observed therein, through which prophecies are given regarding witchcraft, robbery, theft, and various other things. Very similar to this is **Pinaciomantia**, which is performed by pouring wax or pitch into a small dish; it receives its name from the Greek word *pinakion*, which means a small dish or platter.

**ARUSPICIUM** <note>original: "Aruspicium"; here used broadly for the selection of auspicious times</note> is the divination performed by the selection of specific hours and days for beginning any business. Examples include the "elections" <term>elections: in astrology, choosing the most astrologically favorable moment to act</term> made by astronomers for travel, marriage, building, or putting on a new garment. And so it is applied to every individual act of men.
