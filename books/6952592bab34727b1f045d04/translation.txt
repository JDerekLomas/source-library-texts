# REASONS FOR THIS TREATISE.
## Chapter 1.

[Decorative initial Q] Since the whole purpose of our duty ought to tend toward this end—that just as servants under a master of the house, citizens under a ruler, cities under a province, and provinces under a kingdom—each person should diligently perform whatever contributes to the well-being of the head and the members of any community.

Now, the summit of all things consists in this: that everything should be directed toward the praise of the First Cause <note>original: "primæ causæ"; a philosophical and theological term referring to God as the ultimate source of all existence</note> and toward human use and knowledge. Therefore, it has seemed appropriate to descend to this branch of human learning, both because individuals are naturally moved by the rarity of things, as well as because this western world of ours <note>original: "orbis occiduus"</note> has such a fallen appetite for the knowledge of sincere truth that it can only be stirred by exotic treasures and delights hidden since almost the beginning of the world. 

The goal of this treatise of mine, although it is rooted in charity toward one's neighbor and in praise of Divine Wisdom, nevertheless—since under the pretext of peace and charity (for which Jesus, the author of peace, allowed himself to be destroyed so that he might leave peace in his place), those who strive for the Christian or Messianic <note>original: "Messihani"</note> name continually destroy that very peace—it seemed most fitting to bring forth from the shadows the histories of various nations and origins, as if they were the treasures of our own knowledge.

->**ON THE ORIGIN OF LANGUAGE AND SPEECH. Chapter 4.**<-

**I**t is well established that man differs from all other living creatures primarily in two ways: because he reasons and because he speaks. For this reason, Greece rightly called him a <term>logical animal</term> <note>original: "animal logicum." The Greek term "logos" carries a dual meaning that is central to this argument.</note>, since <term>logos</term> <note>original: "λόγος"</note> signifies speech or discourse no less than it signifies reason.

Having followed the reason and authority of nature and grace, I have already set forth—in a great many works—those matters which pertain to the highest peak of eternal reason and the law written in the minds of all people. First, I addressed this in *On the Harmony of the World* <note>original: "De orbis terræ concordia," Postel's most famous work (1544), which argued for the unification of all religions through reason.</note>, especially in the first and third books of that work. Then, I wrote of it in *On Nature and Grace Restored into One*, which is known by the name of the *Zohar* <note>The Zohar is the foundational work of Jewish mysticism (Kabbalah).
