### UNDER ROYAL PRIVILEGE.

By the privilege of **PHILIP**, King of Spain, etc., Duke of Brabant, etc., it is provided that no person, within the next four years, shall print the *History of Aromatics and Certain Simple Medicines Originating in the Indies* <note>original: "Historiam Aromatum & Simplicium aliquot Medicamentorum apud Indos nascentium"</note>, etc., nor import it if printed elsewhere, nor offer it for sale within any of the borders of his jurisdiction, without the consent of Christopher Plantin. Whoever shall do otherwise shall be punished by the confiscation of the books and a penalty to be paid to the Royal Treasury, as is more fully set forth in the diploma <note>A "diploma" here refers to the formal state document or charter granting the legal right.</note> itself.

->*Signed below,*<-

->I. de Witte.<-

## TO THE KIND READER.

Doctor Garcia de Orta <note>Garcia de Orta (c. 1501–1568) was a Portuguese Renaissance Sephardic Jewish physician and a pioneer of tropical medicine.</note>, who served as physician to the King's representative in India, wrote a book concerning those plants and aromatics <note>In this context, "aromatics" refers to spices and medicinal substances with strong scents.</note> which he observed with long care and diligent study among the Indians (where he practiced medicine for more than thirty years). However, yielding to the urgent requests of his friends, he wrote it in the Portuguese language, even though, as he himself relates, he could have written it in Latin.

For your benefit, kind Reader, I have rendered this work into Latin and condensed it into an Epitome <note>A summary or condensed version of a larger work.</note>, perhaps arranging the individual items in a slightly more convenient order than they were before, and even rejecting some things which did not seem to contribute much to the subject. For since our author assigned a separate dialogue to almost every individual simple <note>A "simple" is a medicinal plant or herb used on its own, rather than in a compound mixture.</note> and followed an alphabetical order, it was necessary for him to place many things inappropriately in their locations and to repeat most things, as usually happens in Dialogues.

Indeed, I undertook this labor all the more willingly because I have been greatly delighted by the study of botany <note>original: "rei herbariae"</note> almost from my early youth, and also because I believe that distinguished minds, being stirred by the reading of this book, will follow the example of our author.
