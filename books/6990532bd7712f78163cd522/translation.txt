If one considers the matter naturally and through observation, we see that general prediction is possible. First, through the greater and simpler forces, even the most unlearned people—and indeed even some irrational animals—perceive the annual differences of the seasons and the winds. For the Sun <note>original: ☉</note> is generally the cause of these. Second, those who are already accustomed to necessary observations, such as sailors, recognize the specific signs of storms and winds which occur frequently due to the configurations <term>aspects or geometric relationships</term> of the Moon <note>original: ☾</note> or the fixed stars relative to the Sun. However, because these observers often lack the expertise to accurately understand the times and places of these stars, or the cycles of the wandering stars <term>planets</term>—which also contribute significantly—it happens that they frequently fall into error.

What, then, prevents a person who has accurately grasped the movements of all the stars, both the Sun and the Moon, so that no configuration <note>original: συσχηματισμῶν</note> escapes their notice in terms of place or time; and who has grasped their general natures through continuous historical record <note>the tradition of recorded observations</note>; and who understands their productive powers—such as how the Sun warms and the Moon moistens, and likewise for the others—what prevents such a person from being able to discern, both scientifically and through successful conjecture, the specific quality resulting from the mixture of all these factors? 

Such a person could, for any given time, describe the specific state of the atmosphere <note>original: τοῦ περιέχοντος, literally "that which surrounds us"</note> based on the relationship of the phenomena at that time, stating that it will be warmer or more humid. Furthermore, they could perceive for each individual human the general quality of their temperament <note>original: ἰδιοσυγκρασίας</note> from the state of the atmosphere at the time of their formation—for instance, that the body will be of such a kind and the soul of such a kind. They could also predict occasional events by the fact that a certain state of the atmosphere is proportionate and favorable to a certain temperament for good health <note>original: εὐεξίαν</note>, while another state is disproportionate and leads to injury <note>original: κάκωσιν</note>.

*   The fractions of one-half and one-third applied to the diameter produce the **Quartile** (90°) and the **Sextile** (60°).
*   Regarding the superparticular ratios, if we take the sesquialter (3:2) and sesquitertian (4:3) ratios in relation to the one right angle of the **Quartile**:

We shall provide a reasoned account of this matter, and we shall not overlook a point that is truly worthy of attention: that it is only logical to establish the beginnings of the twelve signs <note>the zodiacal signs</note> from the equinoctial and tropical <note>solstitial</note> points. ### On Proper Faces, Chariots, and Thrones.

The familiarities of the stars <note>planets</note> and the twelve signs are, generally speaking, as described. A planet is said to be in its **"Proper Face"** <note>original: *idioprosōpos*</note> whenever it maintains the same configuration toward the Sun ☉ or the Moon ☽ that its own house <note>the sign it rules</note> maintains toward their houses. For example, Venus ♀ is in her proper face when she is at a sextile aspect <note>60 degrees</note> to the luminaries <note>the Sun and Moon</note>, provided she is in the evening position relative to the Sun and in the morning position relative to the Moon, in accordance with the original arrangement of the houses.

They are also said to be in their own **"Chariots"** <note>original: *lampēnais*</note> and **"Thrones"** <note>original: *thronois*</note> and such, whenever they happen to be associated with the places in which they are found by two or more of the aforementioned modes of familiarity. For it is then that their power is most increased in its activity, because the similarity and cooperation of the kindred familiarity of the signs makes them "shine" and "sit on their own thrones." 

Furthermore, they are said to **"rejoice"** <note>original: *chairein*</note> when, even if the surrounding signs do not have a direct familiarity with the planets themselves, they nonetheless have a familiarity with planets of the same sect <note>term: *hairesis*, the division of planets into day and night groups</note>; in this way, the sympathy arises from a distance. They share in a similar way in this likeness. Conversely, when they are found in alien places belonging to the opposite sect, much of their proper power is paralyzed, as the temperament resulting from the dissimilarity of the surrounding signs produces a different, mixed nature.

## On Applications and Separations

In general, the leading planets are said to be **applying** <note>original: "συναφῶν" (synaphon) — the movement of a faster planet toward an aspect with a slower one</note> to those that follow them. Those that follow are said to be **separating** <note>original: "ἀποῤῥοιῶν" (aporrhoion) — literally "flowing away" from an aspect</note> from those that lead, provided that the distance between them is not great. This is observed whether it happens bodily <note>by actual conjunction in the same degree</note> or according to one of the traditional configurations <note>the aspects: trine, square, etc.</note>. 

However, regarding the applications and separations of the bodies themselves, it is useful to observe their latitudes, so that we only accept those passages found on the same side of the ecliptic <note>the "middle circle" of the zodiac</note>. But regarding the configurations formed by aspect, such a distinction is superfluous, as all rays always travel toward the same point—that is, the center of the Earth—and meet similarly from every direction.

From all this, it is easy to see that the specific quality of each of the stars <note>the planets</note> must be examined based on their own natural temperament, and also the nature of the signs of the zodiac <note>literally "the twelve-parts"</note> that contain them, or even the configurations they make toward the Sun ☉ and the angles <note>the four pivot points of a chart: Ascendant, Midheaven, Descendant, and Imum Coeli</note>, according to the method we have set forth for all these things.

Their power is determined, first, by whether they are oriental <note>rising before the Sun</note> and increasing in their own motions—for then they are most powerful—or occidental <note>setting after the Sun</note> and decreasing in motion, for then they have a weaker energy. Secondly, it is determined by their position relative to the horizon. For when they are at the Midheaven <note>the highest point of the sky</note> or approaching the Midheaven, they are most effective. In the second degree of power are those on the horizon itself or approaching the horizon; and more so when on the eastern horizon <note>the Ascendant</note>, but less so when they are at the Midheaven below the earth <note>the northern angle</note> or otherwise configured with the rising place. If they are not positioned in any of these ways, they happen to be entirely powerless.

### On the Time of Predicted Events.

The second chapter, which concerns timing, deals with the periods in which portents <note>original: "ἐπισημασιῶν" - significant celestial signs or weather omens</note> manifest and the quantity of their duration; we shall investigate it in the following manner. Although eclipses occur at the same absolute time, they are not completed at the same seasonal hours in every inhabited region—this applies to both solar and lunar eclipses—nor do the magnitudes of the obscurations <note>original: "ἐπισκοτήσεων"</note> or the duration of their persistence appear equal in all places. 

First, for each relevant inhabited region, we shall arrange the "angles" <term>kentra: the four pivotal points of a chart—the Ascendant, Midheaven, Descendant, and Lower Midheaven</term> just as we do in a birth chart, based on the hour of the eclipse and the elevation of the pole <note>The latitude of the observer</note>. Next, we determine how many equinoctial hours the shadowing of the eclipse lasts in each relevant region. When these have been examined, for a solar eclipse, we shall assume the predicted effect lasts for as many years as the number of equinoctial hours found; for a lunar eclipse, the duration is measured in months.

The beginnings of the events and their general intensifications are observed from the relationship of the eclipse site to the angles. For if the site falls near the eastern horizon <note>original: "ἀπηλιωτικῷ ὁρίζοντι" - the Ascendant</note>, it signifies that the beginning of the event will occur during the first four-month period from the time of the eclipse, and its primary intensifications will occur during the first third of the total duration of the period. 

If it is near the meridian <note>The Midheaven</note>, the beginning is in the second four-month period and the intensification in the middle third. If it is near the western horizon <note>The Descendant</note>, the beginning is in the third four-month period and the intensification in the final third.

The specific abatements and intensifications of the events are determined from the intermediate syzygies <note>New and full moons occurring between major eclipses</note> when they fall upon the places that produce the cause or the places configured with them. They are also determined from other planetary movements: when the stars <note>planets</note> that produce the effect make their appearances, disappearances, stations, or acronychal risings <term>acronychal: rising in the east just as the sun sets in the west</term>, while being configured with the zodiacal signs that hold the cause. For when they are rising or stationary, they produce intensifications of the events; but when setting, or under the solar rays, or making acronychal appearances <note>original: "προηγήσεις" - here referring to retrogradation or preceding the sun</note>, they bring about an abatement of the predicted effects.

Having authority over both Jupiter <note>original: ♃</note> and the Moon <note>original: ☾</note>, if Saturn <note>original: ♄</note> is in a position of glory <note>meaning well-placed in the zodiac</note> in relation to both the world and the angles <note><term>angles</term>: the four most powerful points in a horoscope (Ascendant, Midheaven, Descendant, and Imum Coeli)</note>, he makes people who are fond of the body, strong-willed, deep thinkers, austere, single-minded, hardworking, commanding, prone to punishing, of many cities <note>perhaps meaning well-traveled or cosmopolitan</note>, fond of money, forceful, accumulators of treasure, and envious. But if Saturn is positioned without glory <note>poorly placed or weakened</note>, he makes them filthy, petty, indifferent, stubborn, malicious, cowardly, withdrawn, slanderous, lovers of solitude, prone to mourning, shameless, superstitious, fond of toil, heartless, treacherous toward their own kin, gloomy, and haters of their own bodies.

When Saturn is associated with Jupiter <note>original: ♃</note> in the manner set forth, if again in glorious dispositions, he makes people good, respectful of their elders, settled, well-intentioned, helpful, <insert>protective</insert>, critical, fond of possessions, magnanimous, generous, of good will, fond of their families, mild, intelligent, tolerant, and philosophical. But if in the opposite dispositions, he makes them tasteless, mad, easily startled by noise, superstitious, frequenters of temples, public confessors <note>those who publicly proclaim their sins</note>, suspicious, haters of children, friendless, secretive, lacking judgment, unfaithful, foolishly elegant, poisonous, hypocritical, obscure, ambitious, prone to regret, simple-minded, and yet likewise long-suffering.

<note>original: "ΚΛ. ΠΤΟΛ. ΤΕΤΡΑΒΙΒ." - This is the standard title for Ptolemy's four-book foundational work on astrology.</note>

...men who are modest, philosophical, faithful in their partnerships, stable, self-controlled, calculating, cautious, and prone to indignation and jealousy regarding suspicions about women. But under the opposite conditions <note>referring to when the planets are poorly positioned or "afflicted"</note>, they are lustful, lewd, doers of shameful acts, indifferent and impure in their sexual relations, unholy, plotters against female persons and especially their own kin, bold, gluttonous, prone to falling into vice, haters of beauty, fault-finders, slanderers, drunkards, greedy, counterfeiters, and those who behave lawlessly in their unions and are so treated by others. <margin>Mercury ☿ Saturn ♄</margin> When Mercury is associated with the planet Saturn, in honorable positions, he makes men resourceful, ambitious, seekers of legal knowledge, lovers of their fellow men, musical, participants in hidden and secret things <note>referring to occult or esoteric knowledge</note>, performers of wonders, wrestlers, those who live for the day, fortunate, administrators of affairs, shrewd, experienced, precise, simple-hearted, industrious, busy, and successful. But in the opposite positions, he makes them babblers, resentful, cruel in their souls, laborious, haters of their own path, envious, joyless, night-wanderers, intruders, traitors, unsympathetic, thieves, practitioners of magic, poisoners, forgers, unscrupulous, unsuccessful, and persistent.

<margin>Jupiter ♃</margin> When the star of Jupiter alone takes the lordship of the soul, in honorable positions, he makes men magnanimous, generous, god-fearing, honorable, pleasure-seeking, philanthropic, magnificent, free, just, high-minded, dignified, minding their own business, merciful, lovers of learning, beneficent, affectionate, and fit for leadership. But when he happens to be in the opposite position, he produces similar impressions in the souls, yet toward a lowlier, less distinguished, and less authoritative type. Instead of magnanimity, he produces lack of light; instead of piety, superstition; instead of modesty, cowardice; instead of dignity, conceit; instead of philanthropy, foolishness; instead of love of beauty, love of pleasure; instead of high-mindedness, laziness; instead of liberality, indifference, and other things similar to these.
