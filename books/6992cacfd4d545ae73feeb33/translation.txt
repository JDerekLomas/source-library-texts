...of all creatures, is it not the turtle?

-> **The Turtle** <-

*The Book of Documents* <note>original: *Shu* (書); likely referring to the *Shujing*, an ancient collection of rhetorical prose attributed to figures of antiquity</note> says: "The turtle is followed" <note>original: 龜從; a reference to divination where the turtle's shell crack patterns indicate the path to follow</note>—this is what is meant here. "Spirituality" <note>term: <term>Ling</term> (靈) - referring to the numinous or divine power of a creature</note> is the essence of virtue. The "turtle" signifies longevity; it is capable of illuminating matters from the distant past and future. When a King is neither biased nor partisan, and honors the elderly without neglecting old friends, then the **Divine Turtle** shall appear.

---

-> **The Turtle** <-

The **Divine Turtle** <note>term: <term>Linggui</term> (靈龜) - a mythological beast associated with wisdom</note> is the essence of the Black Deity of the North. When the King’s virtuous bounty <note>term: <term>Deze</term> (德澤) - the "moisture" of a ruler's kindness that allows the people to flourish</note> accumulates deeply and fishing and hunting follow the proper seasons, then the Divine Turtle shall appear. Once its colors are fully manifest, gold and jade will be found in abundance. Its upper part reflects the heavens, while its underside resembles the earth; its steady gait is like the mountains, and its movements follow the four directions. When it reaches three hundred years of age, it wanders upon lotus leaves <note>original: 褐葉; literally "brown leaves," but iconographically depicted as large lotus leaves</note>. At a thousand years, it transforms at the riverbanks; reaching a size of one foot and two inches, it can perceive life and death and clearly discern good and bad fortune. It is neither biased nor partisan, following only what is righteous.

---

-> **The Turtle** <-

The **Turtle Script** <note>term: <term>Guishu</term> (龜書) - divine symbols or "writing" found on a turtle's shell, often said to reveal the plans of Heaven</note> is a talisman of Heaven and Earth and the essence of Water. Why is this? Because the Earth and Heaven are connected by the rivers. When the King serves and obeys the Earth Goddess <note>original: 后土 (Houtu)</note> and receives the patterns of Heaven, [then the turtle appears]. Each year it wanders upon the lotus leaves; at a thousand years it transforms at the riverbanks. Reaching a size of one foot and two inches, it is able to perceive life and death.

# The Red Dragon carries the Chart to bestow it upon Emperor Yao

The *Spring and Autumn Annals: The Bud of the Primal Mandate* <note>original: *Chunqiu Yuanming Bao* (春秋元命苞); a Han-dynasty apocryphal text that interprets natural phenomena as political omens</note> says: "When Emperor Yao was wandering along the banks of the River, a Red Dragon emerged from the water carrying a Chart. The Chart was red and textured like fine silk. A casket of red jade served as its box, with a latch made of white jade. It was bound with yellow pearls and cords of dark jade. The inscription read: 'The Great Emperor of the Celestial August, in accordance with the divine, establishes this mandate.' It also said: 'To the Son of the Celestial Emperor, Yi Yao.' <note>note: 'Yi' (伊) was the ancestral surname of Emperor Yao</note> This Dragon Chart appeared when Yao first rose to power. The Grand Marshal <note>term: <term>Taiwei</term> (大尉/太尉) - one of the highest military officials in ancient China</note> and one hundred and twenty other officials opened and inspected it, later storing it away in the Great Forest." 
<gloss>The *Chart of Auspicious Omens* says: "The chart contained the forms of the Great River, the seas, mountains, rivers, hills, and marshes, as well as the signs of the thirty states. The *Book of Song: Treatise on Auspicious Signs* <note>original: *Song Shu Rui* (宋書瑞)</note> says: "The Red Dragon's River Chart is a tally from the Earth. # The Yellow Dragon carries the Chart to bestow it upon Emperor Shun

The *Pivot of the Dipper’s Movement* <note>original: *Chunqiu Yundoushu* (春秋運斗樞); a Han-dynasty astrological and prophetic text</note> says: "When Shun became the Son of Heaven, he traveled east on an inspection tour. In the middle of the month, he approached the waters to observe." <gloss>The annotation says: He approached the River to look out. This occurred in the seventh month or perhaps the [unclear] month.</gloss>

When he arrived at the river's edge, a vermillion creature carrying a Chart emerged and placed it before Shun. The Chart was held in a casket of yellow jade, shaped like a chest, three feet long, eight inches wide, and one inch thick. It was sealed on all four sides so they were joined together. On top was a latch. The seal was made of jade, the cord was of gold, and yellow fungi <note>term: <term>Lingzhi</term> (黃芝) - auspicious "mushrooms of immortality" used here as a ritual sealing wax</note> served as the sealing clay. At both ends was an inscription: "The Five Characters of the Seal of the Mandate of the Heavenly Yellow Emperor." The seal was three inches in length and width, and four-tenths of an inch deep, written in bird-script <note>term: <term>Niaowen</term> (鳥文) - an ancient, decorative style of calligraphy where characters resemble the shapes of birds</note>.

# The River Chart

When the Book of Words is spoken of, then the River Chart <term>original: *He Tu* (河圖); a mystical cosmogram of the universe said to have been revealed to ancient rulers</term> emerges. It is the tally of Heaven and Earth and the very essence of water. The River is the great arterial stream of the land. When a King respectfully follows the order of the earth and receives the Mandate of Heaven, the River and the Luo bring forth the Chart and the Writ. In ancient times, when the Yellow Emperor <note>a legendary ancestor and cultural hero of China</note> sat at Xuanhu by the Luo River, a phoenix arrived carrying a scroll in its beak. When Yao <note>one of the Three Sage-Kings known for his modesty and devotion to the people</note> sat in the middle of the River, a dragon emerged carrying the Chart on its back. Sages who immersed themselves in the River and the Luo or traveled there did so with great expectation. The Chart delineates the divisions and principles of the rivers, seas, mountains, streams, and states; the appearances and brilliance of people and things likewise arise within it.

# The Yellow Qiu

The Yellow Qiu <term>original: *Huangqiu* (黄虬); a hornless dragon associated with the center and the element of Earth</term> is another name [for this creature]. It is said that a dragon without horns is called a *qiu*; alternatively, it is the offspring of a *qiu* dragon. The Yellow Qiu is a miraculous omen for a human ruler. On its back, it carries a Jade Casket containing the records of the Jade Tablet. When a ruler aligns with the tallies and contracts <note>the divine proof of the Mandate of Heaven</note>, this omen appears in response.

Yellow is the color of the center <note>in the Five Phases theory, yellow represents the Earth and the central position of the Emperor</note>. It signifies that the ruler possesses the virtue of "central harmony," governing the six directions <note>North, South, East, West, Up, and Down—the entire universe</note> while residing at the center. The emergence of the Yellow Qiu symbolizes the great transformations wrought by the ruler.

The Jade Casket symbolizes that the sovereign possesses "jade-like virtue" <note>purity, endurance, and preciousness</note>. The act of the dragon carrying the casket on its back symbolizes the ruler’s enlightenment of the world under heaven. Carrying the Jade Tablet symbolizes that there are clear and brilliant patterns <note>orderly laws and culture</note>. The "Tablet" itself represents the ruler's sagely, brilliant, and cultured <term>original: *wen* (文); refers to civilization, writing, and refinement</term> virtue.

The reason the Writ emerges from the Luo River is the desire for the ruler's virtue to oversee the Luo, providing moistening grace and leveling the world like water that is inexhaustible. Just as water is perfectly level, so too should the ruler distribute equity to all under heaven.

# The Black Dragon

The Great Yu <note>the legendary founder of the Xia Dynasty, famed for his constant toil to control the Great Flood</note> received the omen of the Black Dragon, which allowed him to manage the waters and bring great peace. During the time of Emperor Xiaowu of the Song <note>reigned 453–464 CE</note>, a black dragon appeared. Emperor Wu said, "I am not worthy of this," for this was the same response given to Yu. By what virtue should one be judged? By following virtue, there is great governance. <gloss>it is at the side</gloss> It is peaceful. <gloss>one version says</gloss> A dragon of old appeared in the outskirts of Jiang.

Cai Chu <note>a historical figure or scholar cited here as an authority on omens</note> said: "The dragon is a creature of water. When the officials of water follow the correct path, the dragon appears. When the officials of water are in disorder <unclear>disorder</unclear> <gloss>disordered and chaotic</gloss>, then it does not appear."
