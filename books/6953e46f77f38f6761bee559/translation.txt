### RECENTLY HELD.

**A**bout ten months ago, a rumor reached our ears that a certain Belgian had fashioned a spyglass <note>original: "Perspicillum," the term used before "telescope" became standard</note>, by the benefit of which visible objects, though far removed from the eye of the observer, were distinctly perceived as if nearby; indeed, several experiences of this wonderful effect were being circulated, to which some gave credence, while others denied them. A few days later, the same was confirmed to me by letter from Paris <note>original: "Lutetia," the Roman name for Paris</note> by the noble Frenchman Jacques Badovere; this finally was the reason why I turned myself entirely to inquiring into the principles, as well as thinking out the means, by which I might arrive at the invention of a similar instrument; which, shortly thereafter, relying on the doctrine of refractions <note>the study of how light bends through different mediums</note>, I attained. First, I prepared for myself a leaden tube, in the ends of which I fitted two glass lenses, both flat on one side, but on the other side, I made one spherically convex and the other concave; then, moving my eye to the concave lens, I viewed objects as sufficiently large and near; for they appeared three times closer and nine times larger than when seen with the natural sight alone. Afterwards, I fashioned another more exact one for myself, which represented objects more than sixty times larger. Finally, sparing no labor and no expense, I reached the point where I constructed for myself an instrument so excellent, that things seen through it appear nearly a thousand times larger, and more than thirty times closer <note>original: "terdecupla ratione," literally a thirteen-fold ratio in linear terms, but here implying the magnification of the area</note>, than if they were viewed by the natural faculty alone. # SIDEREAL OBSERVATIONS

Let us first speak of the surface of the Moon which faces us. For the sake of easier understanding, I distinguish two parts: namely, one brighter and the other darker. The brighter part appears to surround and overflow the entire hemisphere, while the darker part infects the face itself like a certain cloud and renders it spotted. These somewhat dark and quite large spots are obvious to everyone, and every age has seen them; for this reason, we shall call them the **large** or **ancient** spots, to distinguish them from other spots smaller in size, but so thickly scattered that they sprinkle the entire Lunar surface, especially the brighter part. 

These, in fact, have been observed by no one before us. Although it is not yet possible to calculate their specific periods in numbers from these observations, we may at least declare certain things worthy of notice. First, since they sometimes follow and sometimes precede Jupiter at similar intervals, and are elongated from it toward both the east and the west by only the narrowest of diversions, and accompany it equally whether it is in retrograde or direct motion, no one can doubt that they complete their revolutions around it, while in the meantime they all together finish a twelve-year period around the center of the world <note>Jupiter takes approximately 12 years to orbit the Sun</note>. Furthermore, they revolve in unequal circles, which is clearly gathered from the fact that at their greatest distances from Jupiter, two planets were never seen joined together; yet near Jupiter, two, three, and sometimes all of them were found crowded together at once. It is further perceived that the revolutions of the planets describing narrower circles around Jupiter are faster; for the stars closer to Jupiter are more often seen in the east when the day before they appeared in the west, and vice versa. ...presents these stars, while they all together with Jupiter complete a great circuit around the Sun in a period of 12 years. Finally, we must not pass over the reason why it happens that the Medicean Stars <note>The four largest moons of Jupiter, named by Galileo in honor of the Medici family; today known as the Galilean moons.</note>, while completing their very narrow rotations around Jupiter, sometimes appear more than twice as large as themselves. We cannot seek the cause in earthly vapors <note>Atmospheric interference or "twinkling" caused by the Earth's air.</note>; for they appear increased or diminished in size while the mass of Jupiter and nearby fixed stars are seen to be unchanged. However, that they should approach or be distanced from the Earth at the perigee <note>The point in an orbit closest to the Earth.</note> or apogee <note>The point in an orbit farthest from the Earth.</note> of their revolution so as to provide the cause for such a great change seems altogether incredible; for a tight circular motion can in no way provide this. An oval motion (which in this case would be almost a straight line) is both incredible and seems in no way consistent with the appearances. 

What occurs to me in this matter I freely offer and submit to the judgment and criticism of those who philosophize correctly. It is well known that by the interference of earthly vapors, the Sun and Moon appear larger, but the fixed stars and planets appear smaller. Hence the great luminaries appear larger near the horizon, but the stars appear smaller and for the most part inconspicuous. They are diminished even more if those same vapors are bathed in light; therefore the stars appear quite thin during the day and during twilight; the Moon does not, as we also warned above. 

It is established, moreover, that not only the Earth but also the Moon has its own surrounding vaporous sphere, both from what we have said above and especially from those things which will be said more fully in our *System* <note>A reference to Galileo's planned larger work on the structure of the universe.</note>. We can also appropriately make the same judgment concerning the remaining planets; so that it seems not at all incredible to place around Jupiter a sphere denser than the rest of the ether, around which—just as the Moon moves around the sphere of the elements—the MEDICEAN Planets are led. By the interference of this sphere, they appear smaller when they are at their apogee, but when they are at their perigee, they appear larger through the removal or thinning of that same sphere. 

The shortness of time prevents me from proceeding further; let the kind reader expect more concerning these things shortly.
