# NOTICE TO THE READER.

Those who are acquainted with the principles and the truths of the Gospel are well aware that the natural state of the Righteous in this world is Persecution. If they chose to remain silent, or to speak only of vain and foolish things—even wicked and impious ones; if they wished to lead a pagan, worldly, and entirely vice-filled life, they would be left in peace without anyone saying or doing anything to them, except perhaps to flatter and praise them in their vanity. But as soon as one seeks to withdraw from evil, to speak the truth and practice it, they are exposed—as a prophet says—to plunder; they are hated, and even, if possible, put to death by the Priests <term>Sacrificateurs: literally "sacrificers," referring here to religious authorities who preserve the status quo through ritual or power rather than spirit</term> themselves, between the Temple and the Altar. <note>This is a biblical allusion to Matthew 23:35, referring to the martyrdom of Zechariah, used here to suggest that the most dangerous persecution often comes from within the church hierarchy.</note>

Examples of this are found everywhere and in every century. Those who have read the *Life of **Miss BOURIGNON*** <note>Antoinette Bourignon (1616–1680) was a Flemish mystic who claimed direct revelation from God. Her radical views led to her being hunted by both Catholic and Protestant authorities.</note> will see a notable example from our own time, which has perhaps had no equal—whether one considers the rage of her enemies, its duration, its malice, or the object of their animosity, who was merely a weak, solitary girl; or whether one looks at the cause, the persecutors themselves, or finally a thousand other rare and remarkable circumstances. The reading of the following letters may serve to illustrate these points, as well as those in the Second Part of the *Testimony of Truth* <note>original: "Temoignage de Verité"</note>, where her persecutions in **HOLSTEIN** <note>A region in Northern Germany where Bourignon attempted to establish a spiritual community and printing press, only to be driven out by Lutheran clergy.</note> are recounted, and where a Preface has been included that will exempt me from elaborating further here.

These letters have been arranged according to the chronological order of their dates because of their bearing on the history of her Life. Since her life was entirely one of persecutions, the principal periods of it could not fail to be marked here. The first letters, addressed to *Mr. de Cort* <note>Christian de Cort (d. 1669) was a Catholic priest and Superior of the Oratory in Mechelen. (Mademoiselle), referring to Antoinette Bourignon.</note> no less than those of that good man. He was targeted primarily for the love of the Truth that he had embraced; all other reasons were merely secondary motives or pretexts that moved his enemies to act as a consequence of this primary one. If one wishes to know the details of this more particularly, one need only read what is reported in the *History of the Life of Miss Bourignon*, as well as in *Innocence Recognized*, joined to the Second Part of the *Testimony of Truth*, and in the third part of *Light Born in Darkness*, from letter 31 to 35.
