## Cattail Rafts

The **Cattail Raft** <note>original: 蒲筏 (pufa); a specialized military float made from dried aquatic vegetation, valued for its buoyancy and the speed with which it could be assembled in the field</note> is constructed by taking cattails and bundling them into nine large cylinders. These bundles are arranged in alternating directions <note>staggering the thick and thin ends of the plants to ensure the raft remains level and structurally sound</note> and secured with ten rounds of binding, creating a shape resembling a bundle of spears.

The length of the raft should be determined according to the specific requirements of the crossing. If cattails are unavailable, **reeds** <note>original: 葦 (wei)</note> may be used as a substitute; both materials are effective for floating across waterways.

### The Method of Flying Cables

Regarding **Flying Cables** <term>Flying Cables</term> <note>original: 飛絙 (feiguan); a military engineering technique using tensioned ropes to facilitate water crossings for troops and gear</note>, the commander must recruit soldiers who are **skilled in swimming**. Alternatively, men may be instructed to tuck buoyant objects under their armpits to assist in floating as they swim, while tying a guide rope around their waists.

The procedure is as follows:
1.  The lead swimmers first cross the water to the opposite bank.
2.  Next, they use the guide rope to pull a thick, heavy cable across the expanse to both banks.
3.  Large pillars are then erected on each side to anchor the line.
4.  The cable is quickly pulled taut and secured.

Once the line is established, it allows the rest of the men to hold onto the **cable** <note>original: 絙 (guan); a thick rope or hawser made of twisted fibers or bamboo</note> and float across the water. Their **equipment** <note>original: 器械 (qixie); a general term for weaponry, armor, and military tools</note> is carried upon their heads to keep it dry and accessible. 

For the deployment of a large army, dozens of these crossing lanes can be established simultaneously to ensure a swift and orderly transit.

# Skin Boats

<term>Skin Boat</term> <note>original: 皮船 (pichuan); also known as a hide-covered coracle</note>

The **Skin Boat** is a specialized watercraft used for military and civilian transport across rivers and streams. These vessels are constructed by stretching **treated animal hides** over a lightweight wooden or bamboo frame, creating a waterproof hull that is exceptionally buoyant and easy to maneuver.

As seen in the accompanying illustration, these vessels were produced in multiple designs to suit different needs:

*   **Circular Skin Boats:** The top figure demonstrates a smaller, round vessel—often called a coracle—designed for a single navigator. Its shape allows it to spin and pivot easily in the water.
*   **Oval Skin Boats:** The lower figures navigate a larger, elongated version. This **oval-shaped design** <note>The elongated shape provides more stability and surface area, allowing for the transport of multiple personnel or heavier military equipment</note> is better suited for carrying larger loads across a current.

These boats were indispensable for military logistics because they could be disassembled and their hides folded, allowing an army to carry its fleet over mountains and through forests before reassembling them at the water's edge.

<note>The woodcut illustration reflects the practical application of these vessels, showing the rowers using long paddles to navigate. In a military context, these would be used for "silent crossings" where the splash of heavy wooden oars might alert the enemy.</note>

## The Wood Jar Raft
<term>muying</term> <note>original: 木罌; literally "wood-jar," referring to a raft built around wooden-framed ceramic buoyancy units</note>

The **Wood Jar Raft** is constructed by lashing together **ceramic urns and pots** <note>original: 甕缶 (wengfou); wide-mouthed earthenware vessels</note> to serve as a floating foundation. 

*   **Buoyancy and Capacity:** Each individual urn or pot is capable of supporting a weight of two *dan* <note>a traditional unit of weight; two *dan* is roughly equivalent to 120 kilograms or 265 pounds</note>, which provides sufficient strength to carry **one person**. 
*   **Assembly:** To form the raft, fifty or fewer of these jars are arranged in a group. They are hooked and linked together using **stout ropes**. 
*   **Structure:** Spears or long poles <note>original: 槍 (qiang)</note> are lashed across the top of the jars to create a stable framework. 
*   **Dimensions:** The resulting shape of the vessel is **long and rectangular**.

# Mechanical Rafts
<term>Xiefa</term> <note>original: 械筏; This refers to rafts constructed from standardized military equipment, specifically spears, rather than dedicated boat-building timber.</note>

**Mechanical Rafts** are constructed by taking **ten spears** and binding them into a single bundle. One such bundle possesses enough buoyancy to support the weight of **one person**. 

*   **Scale and Capacity:** As a standard rule, **five thousand spears** are used to create a single large raft. 
*   **Preparation:** The spears must have their **butt-spikes** <term>zun</term> <note>original: 鐏; the metal counterweight or spike at the base of a spear shaft</note> and blades removed. 
*   **Assembly:** The shafts are arranged in an overlapping pattern, similar to **fish scales** <note>original: 鱗次 (linci); implying a staggered, tight-knit arrangement for structural integrity</note>, and are lashed together both lengthwise and crosswise. A raft of this magnitude is capable of transporting **five hundred men**.
*   **Stabilization:** In some cases, **twenty floating bags** <note>original: 浮囊 (funang); typically made of inflated animal skins like goat or ox</note> are tied to both the left and right sides of the raft for increased stability.

## Crossing Procedures
To deploy the raft, swimmers or water-specialists are first sent to the opposite bank to erect **large pillars**. Two **thick hawsers** <term>guan</term> <note>original: 絙; heavy-duty ropes or cables</note> are then stretched across the river and secured to both banks to "sandwich" or guide the raft. 

**Wooden rings** are threaded onto these heavy cables and linked to the raft with connecting lines. A primary tow-rope is attached to the **bow of the raft**, allowing men on the far shore to pull it across. By using the cables as a fixed track, the risk of the raft drifting off course or the occupants drowning is eliminated.

*   **Buoyancy:** At each of the four corners, **four pig bladders** <note>original: 猪胞 (zhubao)</note> are used. The neck of each bladder is tied shut tightly to trap air, and the entire assembly is placed upon the water.

# Recipe for Manufacturing Gunpowder

To manufacture each batch of gunpowder, use five catties <note>A catty (jin) is a traditional unit of weight, roughly equivalent to 600 grams or 1.3 pounds.</note> of **saltpeter** <term>original: 硝 (xiao).</term>, one catty of **sulfur** <term>original: 黄 (huang).</term>, and one catty or more of **eggplant stalk ash** <term>original: 茄桿灰 (qie gan hui).</term>. Divide the mixture into three grinding troughs and ensure it is ground exactly five thousand, eight hundred times before being removed from the troughs.

The quality must be such that if a small amount is ignited in the palm of one's hand, the flash is so swift that the heat is not even felt. Where a standard recipe might require one catty of powder, only half a catty of this specific mixture is needed; because its explosive force is so great and its action so rapid, one must not use too much.

If eggplant ash is unavailable, **willow branches** <term>original: 柳條 (liu tiao).</term> may also be used, provided the bark and knots are removed. In the Southern regions, if neither willow nor eggplant is available, **Chinese cedar** <term>original: 杉 (shan).</term> or **dry rice stalks** <term>original: 槁 (gao).</term> are both acceptable substitutes.

### Recipe for Fuse Powder
To manufacture **fuse powder** <term>original: 火線藥 (huoxian yao).</term>, use one catty <note>Approx. 600 grams.</note> of **refined saltpeter**, three taels and six mace <note>A tael (liang) is 1/16th of a catty; a mace (qian) is 1/10th of a tael.</note> of **sulfur**, four taels and three mace of **willow ash** <note>Charcoal made from willow wood was preferred for its fast-burning properties.</note>, five mace of **eggplant ash**, five mace of **white arsenic** <term>original: 白砒 (baipi).</term>, and three mace of **camphor** <term>original: 朝腦 (zhaonao).</term>. 

Combine these ingredients according to the previously described method <note>Refers to the extensive grinding process detailed on page 10.</note>. Alternatively, one may use only the core ingredients mentioned earlier. This powder is suitable for both **running fuses** <note>Rapid-burning cords.</note> and **flat fuses**, though it should not be formed into granules.

### Recipe for Explosive Bomb Powder
If preparing a recipe for **explosive bomb powder** <term>original: 炸炮藥 (zhapao yao).</term>, the ratios for saltpeter and sulfur remain the same as above. However, reduce the amount of ash by five mace and add five mace of **white arsenic** and one mace of **camphor**. Mix the ingredients evenly using **high-quality distilled liquor**, then leave to dry in the sun.

### Recipe for Rockets ("Rising Fire")
If making **rockets** <term>original: 起火 (qihuo); literally "rising fire."</term>, reduce the amount of sulfur by one tael and two mace. The saltpeter, ash, white arsenic, and camphor remain in the same proportions as the fuse powder.

Sift the mixture repeatedly to combine thoroughly, then use high-quality distilled liquor to moisten it. Sun-dry the mixture, grind it once more, moisten again with distilled liquor, and sun-dry a final time. After grinding and sifting once more, the rocket powder is ready.

### Recipe for Fire-Arrow Powder
If making powder for **fire-arrows** <term>original: 箭藥 (jian yao).</term>, reduce the sulfur by one tael and one mace. Use four taels of **willow ash**. The saltpeter, white arsenic, and camphor remain in the same proportions as the fuse powder, along with five mace of **eggplant ash**.
