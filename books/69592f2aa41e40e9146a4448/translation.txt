# On the Art of Chiromancy

<note>original: "ciromancie"; the art of reading palms to determine character or fortune</note>

Since various authors have written various things concerning the art of **chiromancy**, I, wishing to bring clarity to what is confused and to leave behind what is doubtful or false, have compiled the present treatise. In this work, I shall treat the aforementioned art as plainly and lucidly as I am able, so that all things said concerning it may be brought into a certain order and more easily found. I shall conclude this treatise in eleven chapters.

First, it must be seen whether this art is permitted according to the law of God. It must be said that some indeed claim it to be illicit for this reason: because all **divination** <note>the practice of seeking knowledge of the future or the unknown by supernatural means</note> is illicit according to the law of God. Hence, in the Law, it was commanded by God: *you shall not suffer sorcerers and diviners to live upon the earth.* <note>A reference to Exodus 22:18 and Deuteronomy 18:10-12</note> Those who meddle in future things by predicting them through signs found in the hands seem to be such people; therefore, it appears to be illicit. This would make it like **pyromancy**, which is divination performed in fire, or **geomancy**, which is performed through markings made in the earth or in some other place such as a tablet or parchment, and likewise for many other sciences through which divination is performed, which are known to be rejected and condemned by the Church.

Others say that it is not illicit, because **Aristotle** <note>The Greek philosopher (384–322 BC), whose works were the foundation of medieval science</note> handed down the art of **physiognomy** <note>original: "phisonomie"; the practice of judging character from facial features</note>, through which he teaches how to judge what sort of man or woman one is in character by the lineaments found in the face and the shape of the face. Since, therefore, that art is without sin <note>original: "sine peccato"</note>, why should this one—through which judgment is given by the signs of the hand—not also be without sin? For the hand, after the face, is the most delicate part and the **"organ of organs"** <note>original: "organum organorum"; a famous Aristotelian definition of the hand from his work 'On the Soul' (De Anima)</note> according to the Philosopher <note>Aristotle</note> in the third book of *On the Soul* near the end; through which, it seems, a better judgment can be given. Likewise, the Philosopher in the third book of *On the History of Animals* makes mention of this art, saying: "If the lines of the hand are long, or if there are three cutting across the hollow of the hand or the palm, they signify length of life. Short lines, however, signify a shortness of life." Likewise—

Albertus, who was called "the Great" <note>Albertus Magnus (c. 1200–1280), a dominant medieval philosopher and theologian</note> and a philosopher, says that this [art] is comprised under **natural science** and under **philosophy**, although he considers this a matter of some doubt. It seems to me, moreover, that this science is lawful. If someone, by looking at the lines of the hand, predicts the character and natural inclinations of men—for example, that such a person who has such signs is naturally lustful or generous and the like—[it is lawful]. However, [it is not lawful] if such things are predicted to happen by necessity because of these signs. Through the signs of this art, it can be said of all others that this person is inclined toward such things, or toward certain actions which, if done, will lead to those ends, such as to being hanged and the like.

The method of judging by this art is that the hand should first be washed with warm water and rubbed until the skin is drawn out; then, once dried, it should be rubbed again. And according to the lines that appear sufficiently, and their colors, the judgment should be given. Likewise, you should know that a more certain judgment is given through manifest signs that concur or work together toward one conclusion. For a judgment given by a single sign, if other signs were contrary, would be of little value.

Likewise, you should know to give a more certain judgment by the principal and large lines rather than by other minute ones which are not so certified.
