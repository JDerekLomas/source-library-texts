# The Secret Treasure of Arnaldus de Villa Nova

->Here begins the treatise of Arnaldus de Villa Nova <note>Arnaldus de Villa Nova (c. 1240–1311) was a famous physician and alchemist.</note> concerning the preparation of the white water, which some call the "Secret Treasure of Natural Operators," etc.<-

Know, my son, that in this book we speak of our secret, and first I shall divide this book into six chapters. First, I shall say what the stone is. Second, I shall say why it is called "nature." Third, I shall say why it is called "animal" or "blood." Fourth, why it is called "herb." Fifth, I shall describe the operation—that is, the practical work—along with the causes and true understandings. Sixth, I shall describe the entire multiplication of weights, many things briefly and in summary. May our discourse prevail so that it may wisely teach those who might stray in the future. Know that this [teaching] of ours is not one of the [ordinary] hidden things of the philosophers. No one may arrive at this science unless he has first heard <term>Logic and First Philosophy</term> <note>Referring to the standard university curriculum of the Middle Ages, specifically Aristotelian physics and metaphysics.</note> and knows the entire nature of things and of the elements; otherwise, he would weary his soul and his body in vain.

Therefore, let him not believe that anything might happen for him through gold miraculously. For fools, possessing the books of the philosophers, speak in parables; they attempt [experiments] according to them and find nothing. But those who test them wisely possess our true <term>magistery</term> <note>The "mastery" or the completed alchemical process.</note> and shall profit. As Aristotle says in the *Book of Stones* <note>A work falsely attributed to Aristotle in the Middle Ages.</note> and Brother Roger <note>Likely referring to Roger Bacon, the 13th-century English friar and scientist.</note>: "It is a stone, and yet not a stone," which is found in every human, in plants, in mountains, and in

waters, and it is called "Whiteness" <note>original: *Albedo*</note>. Concerning this fact—that it is a stone—all proven physicians are also in agreement. And from these there were those who said that it is an animal called <term>rebis</term> <note>A Latin term meaning "double thing," representing the union of opposites (male/female, spirit/matter).</note>, and they spoke obscurely; and there were those who said it was the hair of men. And some said that it is an egg. But fools, not understanding the literal sense, have worked with <term>mumia</term> <note>A substance derived from embalmed bodies or bitumen, used in medicine and alchemy.</note>, with blood, with hair, with the *rebis*, with various alums and salts, and they found more than they understood <note>A sarcastic remark implying they created messes or accidents rather than the intended result.</note>.

<margin>d</margin> ## Chapter 1: What the Stone is

Know, my son, that our Stone <term>lapis noster: the "Philosopher's Stone," the goal of the alchemical Great Work</term>, as Hermes <note>Hermes Trismegistus, the legendary Hellenistic figure regarded as the father of alchemy.</note> said, is from nature. From this authority, many have been introduced to the knowledge of how this is, and there have been many expositors who explained it <margin>2</margin> and did not understand, and they spoke of what he said in such a great matter: that it is in animals; others in plants and herbs; others in salts <insert>and</insert> vitriol <note>original: "azodo," likely a variant of "azoc" or "azoth," often referring to mercury or mineral salts</note>. I shall tell you, my son, what our Stone is: Sun, Moon, and Earth <note>In alchemical code, Sun (Sol) refers to Gold, and Moon (Luna) refers to Silver.</note>. For these are our stones. These stones, however, are natural upon the earth and perform nothing by themselves, unless the industry of men administers to them. From these stones is made gold as true and silver as true as that made by nature. And it is said by the disciple to the master: "Surely, since the philosophers say that this is weaker than nature, we say that gold is made as true and silver as true from this as by nature." And the master says: "I shall tell you the first truth: We do not make it; rather, nature makes it when we administer to it." For our gold is not entirely artificial, but rather natural. And it is said, however, that it is triple in presence. For Hermes says that it is in such a great thing; and for just as nature is spiritual and invisible, so this is invisible and spiritual, since it recedes invisibly. And therefore it is called the fugitive spirit <term>spiritus fugitivus: a common term for Mercury (quicksilver) due to its volatility and tendency to evaporate</term>. And they are wandering, because in the world there is no other spirit through which it does not work what it works, because it is airy, which is a sign of perfection.
