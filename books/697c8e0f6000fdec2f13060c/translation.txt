# GEOMANCY.

**G**eomancy is one of the uncertain arts. It takes its name from the element *terra* <note>Latin: "earth"</note>, from *gē* or *gaia* <note>Greek: "earth"</note> and *manteia* <note>Greek: "prophecy" or "divination"</note>; from this comes *mantēs* <note>Greek: "seer" or "prophet"</note>, a diviner. A Geomancer or Geomanticus is a diviner who works from 16 points or figures. For Geomancy has 16 points or figures and belongs to the *astra terræ* <note>Latin: "stars of the earth"</note>, just as Pyromancy is a divining art from fire, Hydromancy is a divining art from water, and Chaomancy or Aeromancy is divination from the air.

As for what Theophrastus Paracelsus <note>Philippus Aureolus Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim (1493–1541), a famous Swiss physician, alchemist, and occultist whose writings heavily influenced Western esotericism.</note> thinks of these four arts, you can read in his book *Artes Præsagv* <note>likely referring to "De praesagiis" or "Prophetic Arts"</note>. He calls them *Sortilegia artes incertas* <note>Latin: "uncertain arts of casting lots"</note>; at one moment they are true, at another they are lies. He who will not trust in GOD, and instead questions and searches for future things, is kept away from divine matters through such *Artes incertas* <note>Latin: "uncertain arts"</note> by the lying spirits who invented and devised these arts. It all depends on the spirit. He who has a favorable spirit and will not trust GOD makes a good *Sortilegisten* <note>term: Sortilegist; a practitioner who seeks to determine the future by casting lots or random symbols.</note>. But he who has a lying spirit hits the mark once and fails nine times; for he is deceived by the spirit that did not rightly guide his hand and his chalk. Man does not understand it and does not know, but the spirits know all things; they cannot speak, therefore they speak through the points and often reveal to man what he desires, so that he might not trouble himself with GOD. Such and the like is what Theophrastus holds regarding Geomancy. Yet he speaks a little differently of it in *Sagaci Philosophia* <note>Latin: "The Wise Philosophy," referring to his work *Astronomia Magna*.</note>, where he examines it through nature. Whoever has the desire to consume his life and time in transient questions through this art may do so. I would rather go to the third school <note>In the context of Valentin Weigel’s mysticism, the "Third School" refers to the school of the Holy Spirit or internal divine knowledge, as opposed to the schools of worldly intellect or natural observation.</note> and learn all things that I am permitted to learn and know. It is free for everyone: he may live in nature and study transient arts, or he may walk in Grace, where full sufficiency, rest, peace, and eternal life are possessed.

The art of Geomancy has no certain ground nor foundation, and yet it often reveals certain things. It is not an art; it is a *Sortilegium* <note>Latin: "a casting of lots."</note>. Yet because man is an excellent creature under the sun, and through his mind can create for himself a neat *Ascendent* or heaven <note>term: Ascendant; the zodiacal sign rising on the eastern horizon at a specific moment, believed to influence personality and fate.</note>, it is also possible that his spirit may reveal many things through these four *Artes incertas* <note>Latin: "uncertain arts"</note>. ## **Geomancy.** <note>original: "Geomantia"</note>

Just as **Astrologers** <note>original: "Astrologi"</note> use the twelve houses of heaven for their **inquiries** <note>original: "Quæstionibus"</note>, from which they judge according to their nature and properties, so also the Geomancer has twelve **houses** <note>original: "Häuser"</note>. Beyond these, he has two **Witnesses** <note>original: "Testes"</note> who give testimony or agreement concerning the twelve houses, together with the **Judge** <note>original: "Judice"</note>, who is the 15th figure; he acts as a referee and **Judge** <note>original: "Judex"</note> who passes the verdict. This is the final figure which completes the **judgment** <note>original: "Judicium"</note>. However, if this 15th or final figure does not agree with any **House** <note>original: "Domo"</note>, then one must draw another **Judge** from the First and the Last <note>likely referring to the first and the specific house related to the question</note>, as the question requires and demands. For example, if someone asked about sickness or health, and the 15th or final figure did not match any **House**, one must seek and create a **Judge** from the First and the Sixth House <note>the 6th house traditionally governs illness</note>. All 15 or 16 **Points** <note>original: "Puncta"</note> take their origin in this way: with chalk, coal, or ink upon a tablet, paper, or table—or with the finger in sand—one makes roughly, by **fortuitous projection** <note>original: "fortuita projectione"; the traditional method of making random marks to be counted</note>, from the right hand toward the left, the first four **Points**.
