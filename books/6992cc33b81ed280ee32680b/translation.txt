These maps are an important example of how Chinese astronomical knowledge—which organizes the sky into different "mansions" and asterisms rather than Western constellations—was communicated to European audiences in the late 18th century.</meta>

<term>Planisphere</term>: A map of the celestial sphere or a portion of it, projected onto a flat surface, often featuring a rotating disk to show the visible stars at any given time.

<term>Celestial</term>: Relating to the sky or the heavens; in this context, the mapping of stars and planets.

<term>Asterisms</term>: Small groups of stars that form a pattern but are not necessarily part of the 88 modern constellations; Chinese astronomy is based on a vast system of these smaller patterns.

<summary>A set of two late 18th-century celestial maps by De Guignes the Younger, depicting the Northern and Southern Hemispheres according to Chinese astronomical tradition. These plates catalog stars by magnitude and utilize transliterated Chinese names for asterisms, serving as a bridge between Eastern and Western science.</summary>
