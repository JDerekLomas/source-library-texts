## Pancaratra Text
->*Sanjukta Gupta*<-

Otto Schrader in 1916 recommended the *Laksmi Tantra* for the study of Pancaratra <note>A significant tradition within Vaishnavism focused on temple worship and the five-fold manifestation of God.</note> philosophy. Among the vast number of Pancaratra Agamic <note>Scriptural texts considered divinely revealed.</note> texts, the *Laksmi Tantra* stands out because it deals almost exclusively with Laksmi, the divine creative impulse, intelligence, potency, potentiality, power, majesty, and speech. The focus of the text is on Pancaratra philosophy (including cosmogony <note>The branch of science or philosophy dealing with the origin of the universe.</note>) and the practice of yoga based on it, with its attendant Mantra Sastra <note>The science or sacred theory of mantras.</note>. It records the earliest Vaisnava <note>Relating to the worship of Vishnu.</note> speculation on the paradox of a Supreme God who is totally identified with Brahman <note>The ultimate, unchanging reality in Hindu philosophy.</note>, the unique and transcendent Conscious Reality, and is at the same time the creator of a dualistic universe which cannot be related to Him.

->*Arthur Avalon*<-

This work teaches the tantric rituals in great details and propounds the basic philosophy of Tantrism, revealing the best aspects of Saktism <note>The tradition focusing on the Goddess (Shakti) as the supreme power.</note>. The tantra also teaches the doctrine of duties, incorporating the laws of Manu <note>An ancient legal and social code.</note>, the *Bhagavadgita*, and sermons of the Buddha.

->*Arthur Avalon*<-

Divided into 36 Patalas <note>Chapters or sections of a manuscript.</note>, the book deals with the worship of Devatas <note>Deities or divine beings.</note> in the tantric way. As a special study, the reader is referred to the Patalas on the tantric Bhutasuddhi <note>The purification of the elements within the body.</note>, the worship of Tripura Vidya <note>A specific form of the Goddess and the knowledge associated with her.</note>, Japa <note>The meditative repetition of a mantra.</note>, Bija <note>Seed-syllables used in mantras.</note>, Yantra <note>Geometric diagrams used in meditation.</note>, Mudra <note>Symbolic hand gestures.</note>, and so forth which are the distinguishing marks of tantric ritual.

## PREFACE

Tantra is a major development in the evolution of Indian spiritual thought inasmuch as it represents a determined attempt at reconciling the apparent points of conflict among the different schools of philosophy that came up after the age of the Epics. Tantra seeks to synthesize the monism <note>The philosophical view that all is one</note> of the <term>Advaita</term> <note>The school of non-dualism</note> with the dualism of the <term>Samkhya</term> <note>A philosophy distinguishing between pure consciousness and matter</note>, enrich <term>Jnana</term> <note>Spiritual knowledge or wisdom</note> with the <term>rasa</term> <note>The "flavor" or essence of aesthetic and emotional experience</note> of <term>Bhakti</term> <note>Devotional love for the Divine</note>, join Nature to her Lord in the person of the human individual, and harmonize the claims of the Spirit with the rights of Matter. It recovers the lost heritage of the ancient <term>Veda</term> <note>The oldest scriptures of Hinduism</note> which lays emphasis upon the commonalty of man, Nature, and God and the equality of status between Mother Earth and Father Heaven. The principle of Tantra is to reject nothing that God has created, to utilize every means to raise the human consciousness to the Divine. Unlike many of the older systems, Tantra is highly rational in its approach; it asks for no faith in advance. It is a self-verifying science of the development of natural energies into their supernatural terms leading to a cosmic enjoyment of life in a spiritual consciousness. In a word, it is the highway of <term>mukti</term> <note>Spiritual liberation</note> and <term>bhukti</term> <note>Material or worldly enjoyment</note> in the highest sense.

The *<term>Kularnava</term>* <note>Literally "Ocean of the Kula tradition," a foundational Tantric text</note> is an important text in one of the traditions of Tantra with a pronouncedly practical bent of thought. It calls upon man to wake up to the rare privilege that has been given to him, e.g. human birth in which the being becomes conscious of himself and is offered a choice: a choice between stagnation and rapid progression towards his Godhead. # PREFACE

The seeker is asked to purify himself and told how to do it. In unambiguous terms he is told that a wine-drinker is different from the drinkers of ecstasy; the union of delight is between the ascending <term>Sakti</term> <note>The primordial cosmic energy or divine feminine power</note> and the presiding Lord above, and not between man and woman. In issuing these warnings, the adepts of the <term>Tantra</term> <note>A body of esoteric scriptures and practices focused on the expansion of consciousness</note> would seem to have anticipated the modern turn of mixing up sex with spirituality. It is a pity that a text like this has not received adequate publicity in the West where Tantra-enthusiasts are on the wrong track.

The *Kularnava* <note>The Kularnava Tantra, one of the most important texts of the Kaula school of Tantra</note> prescribes the modes of preparation for the high quest; it draws upon ethics, religion, philosophy, and yoga to elevate human life gradually to the level of godly life. It comprehends the multiple personality of man and provides for the healthy growth of his mental faculties, purification of his emotions and passions, and the orientation of his physical faculties through ritual, <term>japa</term> <note>The meditative repetition of a mantra or divine name</note>, <term>mantra</term> <note>Sacred sounds or syllables used as a tool for the mind</note>, and <term>upasana</term> <note>Systematic worship or "sitting near" the divine</note>. Who is fit for the path of Tantra? Who is competent to guide the novice on the "double-edged razor path"? What is the responsibility of a <term>Guru</term> <note>A spiritual teacher or dispeller of darkness</note> to a disciple? These and other relevant questions are raised and answered in a satisfying manner.

An attempt has been made in the following selections to present such contents as are eminently helpful to the needs of the awakened man of today. Portions relating to rituals, technicalities of special types of japa, etc. have been kept out of the treatment so as not to tax the aspirant of the present times who has little time or energies to devote himself to those practices, especially when it is possible to achieve the same results by more psychological means suited to the modern age and so elegantly presented in this text. Definitions of most of the important concepts and terms in the <term>Tantra Sastra</term> <note>The authoritative body of knowledge and scripture regarding Tantra</note>, as given in the text, will be found highly educative.

# INTRODUCTION

The Kularnava is a leading and perhaps the foremost <term>Tantra</term> <note>A category of sacred Hindu texts focusing on ritual, philosophy, and spiritual practice</note> of the <term>Kaula School</term> <note>A major tradition within Tantric Shaivism emphasizing the path of "Kula" or family/totality</note>. It enjoys a great reputation amongst works of its class and as such is constantly cited as an authority. The Chapters of the Text here published number seventeen and the verses total 2058 according as there are included or not verses 123-144 in the tenth Chapter. The <term>Colophon</term> <note>A concluding statement in a manuscript providing details about its authorship and production</note> however states that the portion here printed is only the fifth part of the whole <term>Tantra</term> consisting of 125,000 verses. Thus the <term>Colophon</term> of the first Chapter runs—"End of the first Chapter being the first portion of the fifth part entitled <term>Urdhvamnaya Tantra</term> <note>The "Upper Tradition" or "Upper Transmission" of the Tantric teachings</note> of the great Mystery and most excellent among <term>Agamas</term> <note>A collection of scriptures regarded as divinely revealed</note> containing 125,000 verses entitled the Kularnava Tantra." If this statement be correct I have not on enquiry been able to discover the whole work. Every <term>Manuscript</term> which I have come across contains the seventeen Chapters only here printed. Either then the rest of the book is lost or possibly exists under some different names. The work has been already twice printed and therefore would not in the ordinary course have found place in these texts according to the original design of the publication.
