The world is a small thing, unless it contains within it something for the whole world to seek. Certain sacred mysteries are not revealed all at once: Eleusis <note>The site of the Eleusinian Mysteries in ancient Greece, famous for secret religious rites where knowledge was revealed in stages to the "initiated."</note> keeps back things to show to those who return to visit. Nature does not reveal her mysteries all at once. We believe ourselves to be initiated: yet we linger only in her entryway <note>original: "vestibulo" — the porch or entrance hall of a Roman house or temple.</note>. Those secrets are not open indiscriminately, nor to everyone; they are withdrawn and shut away in the inner shrine. Of these, our age will see some things, and the age that follows us will see others. When, then, will these things be brought into our knowledge? Great discoveries come forth slowly, especially if our labor ceases.

You ask, what shall be the reward for this labor, than which there is none greater? To know Nature. For the study of this subject has nothing in it more beautiful—though it contains much that will be of future use—than the fact that it captivates man by its own magnificence, and is pursued not for profit, but for the sheer wonder of it.

->Seneca, *Natural Questions*<-
