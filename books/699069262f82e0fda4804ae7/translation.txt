### Preface

In the *Narada Samhita* composed by Narada, eighteen progenitors of the science of astrology are mentioned. Among them, the mention of the Sage Kashyapa is found.

> Brahma, Surya, Vashistha, Atri, Manu, Paulastya, Romasha,
> Marichi, Angira, Vyasa, Narada, Shaunaka, Bhrigu,
> Chyavana, Yavana, Garga, Kashyapa, and Parashara—
> these eighteen are the profound progenitors of the science of astrology.
<note>original Sanskrit: "Brahma Suryo Vashistho’trirmanuḥ Paulastyaromaśau / Marīchiraṅgirā Vyāso Nāradaḥ Śaunako Bhṛguḥ / Chyavano Yavano Gargaḥ Kaśyapaścha Parāśaraḥ / Aṣṭādaśaite gambhīrāḥ jyotiḥśāstra pravartakāḥ"</note>

->From the *Narada Samhita*<-

This text, the *Kashyapa Jataka* <note>A treatise on natal astrology attributed to Kashyapa</note>, should be considered as authored by the great sage Kashyapa. According to the Puranas and other ancient texts, it is known that many great seers belonging to the Kashyapa lineage existed at various times. All of them are famous individuals known by various names associated with Kashyapa; this same principle applies to the other sages as well.

According to the *Harivamsa Purana*, the original seven sages (Saptarishis) were considered the mind-born sons of Brahma.

> Marichi, Atri, the Lord Angira, Pulaha, Kratu,
> Pulastya, and Vashistha—these seven are the sons of Brahma.
<note>original Sanskrit: "Marīchi Ratrirbhagavānaṅgirāḥ Pulahaḥ Kratuḥ / Pulastyaścha Vaśiṣṭhaścha saptaite brahmaṇaḥ sutaḥ"</note>

->From the *Harivamsa Purana*<-

These are the primary sages; in their many subsequent generations, many other sages were born. The mention of Kashyapa appears as a fifth-generation descendant of the great sage Marichi. Kashyapa married the thirteen daughters of Daksha. From them, many races and species were produced. The names of Daksha's daughters are: 1. Aditi, 2. Diti, 3. Danu, 4. Kala, 5. Arishta, 6. Danayu, 7. Khasa, 8. Surabhi, 9. Vinata, 10. Tamra, 11. Muni, 12. Krodhavasha, and 13. Kadru.

The name of Kashyapa Prajapati <note>Prajapati refers to a lord of creation or progenitor</note> was Arishtanemi. Because he was the son of Kashyapa, he became famous by the names Kashyapa and Kashyapa. His residence was Mount Meru. This extends from Kashmir in the Himalayan mountains to Central Asia. According to the Puranas, because Meru was extracted from the sea, Mount Meru became "Kashyapa-meru." That same word gradually transformed and became '<term>Kashmir</term>' <note>The author suggests an etymological link between the Sage Kashyapa and the region of Kashmir</note>.

Kashyapa Brahma taught Ayurveda to the Ashwini Kumaras, Dharma Shastra (Law) to the Angirasas, Grammar to Brihaspati, and Natya Shastra (Dramaturgy) to Bharata Muni. He also learned the science of astrology. The sons born to Kashyapa Prajapati and Kadru are the Kadraveyas; they are the Kurds currently in the region between Iran and Iraq <note>This reflects a modern interpretive theory linking Puranic tribes to contemporary ethnic groups</note>.

These are the ones. Not only them, but the Nagas in Nagaland and the Sarps (from the land of Sarpya) in Poland are considered to be the descendants of Kadru and Kashyapa <note>In Hindu mythology, Kadru is the mother of all serpents and a wife of the sage Kashyapa</note>. In this manner, many sages became famous in the Puranas <note>Ancient Hindu texts containing mythology and history</note> under the name of Kashyapa.

The author of the present text was born into the lineage of the supreme Kashyapa. He authored many works. He composed the *Kashyapa Samhita*. He was an expert in <term>Kumara Tantra</term> (the treatment of pediatric diseases) and <term>Agada Tantra</term> (the science of toxicology/poisons). In the *Mahabharata*, in the history of King Parikshit, the mention of Kashyapa appears as a physician specializing in poisons.

The <term>Kashyapa Jataka</term> <note>A foundational text on horoscopy</note> has attained a prominent place among the texts of the science of astrology. Verses from his works are found in texts such as the *Hora Ratnam* and *Brihat Daivajna Ranjanam*. There are fourteen chapters in the *Kashyapa Jataka*. In the first two chapters, there is a discussion regarding the combinations of stars and planets and the span of life. From the third chapter to the fourteenth chapter, the effects of the planets when positioned in the twelve houses (starting from the Ascendant) are discussed. This type of astrological analysis is not available in other texts. This can be considered the unique specialty of this work. As if fitting the entire ocean into a single drop, he explained the positions of the planets in the twelve houses very extensively in just 260 verses. Because of this, <term>Daivajnas</term> (astrologers/soothsayers) who write horoscopes will find great benefit in the matter of predicting results. He declared his devotion to the sage Chyavana by stating that he is conveying these results according to the words of the great sage Chyavana.

By reading this book, kind-hearted readers will easily understand the results that occur when planets are in various positions. The translation of this book has been done according to the commentary of the great sage Abhaya Katyayana.

If this book provides even a little benefit to students of astrology and those interested in the science of astrology, I shall consider my efforts successful.

I humbly request that kind-hearted scholars point out any errors in the writing or printing of this book so that I may correct them in the next edition.

# The Horoscope of Kashyapa

->Chapter One<-

<margin>Auspicious Invocation</margin>

> Now, salutations to the Divine, who is self-abiding, eternal, and the destroyer of sins; to the Light of Consciousness who is beyond the divisions of sequential time. 1 <note>original: "Atha svasthāya devāya..." This verse invokes the supreme reality as pure light.</note>
> Salutations to the peaceful Radiance, who is unconditioned by space or time, whose form is infinite pure consciousness, and who is known only through one's own direct experience. 2
> Salutations to the sole Lord of the Universe, who is meditated upon by infinite names, who ordains all forms, and who is the true meaning expressed by all sacred mantras. 3
> Salutations to the Supreme Being, whose glory transcends speech and mind; who manifests as the three qualities, the eight qualities, and infinite qualities, yet remains fundamentally without attributes. 4 <note>The "three qualities" (triguna) refer to balance, activity, and inertia; the "eight qualities" (ashtaguna) often refer to the primary virtues of the soul in Vedic philosophy.</note>
> In whatever manner He is worshipped, wherever He is honored, whoever He may be—to that Divine Being, I offer my salutations. 5

Salutations to the Supreme Lord, who is the self-established essence, eternal, sinless, and the form of the Light of Consciousness which is devoid of sequential divisions. Salutations to the Supreme Lord who is beyond space and time, infinite, the essence of pure consciousness, the one who experiences Himself, the supremely peaceful, the radiant one, the wearer of many names, the one who possesses all forms, and the one who is the speaker of all mantras and the Master of the Universe.

Salutations to the Supreme Lord who, despite possessing the three qualities, the eight qualities, and infinite qualities, is nonetheless without attributes and formless, transcending both the mind and speech.

Salutations to the Supreme Lord who accepts any form of worship, who grants His vision anywhere, and who assumes any form.

### Description of Hora and Drekkana:

<margin>18</margin>
> <note>original Sanskrit: "Hora ravindvorojarkshe samabhe chandrasuryayo..."</note>
> The Hora belongs to the Sun and Moon; in odd signs, they are Sun and Moon; in even signs, Moon and Sun.

**Hora** — Half of a zodiac sign (15 degrees) is called a *Hora*. In odd-numbered signs <note>Aries, Gemini, etc.</note>, the first Hora (from 1 to 15 degrees) belongs to the Sun's sign, Leo; the second Hora (15 to 30 degrees) belongs to the Moon's sign, Cancer. In even-numbered signs <note>Taurus, Cancer, etc.</note>, the first Hora is the Moon's sign, Cancer, and the second Hora is the Sun's sign, Leo.

**Drekkana** — Dividing a zodiac sign into three parts <note>10 degrees each</note> results in a *Drekkana* <term>Drekkana: a decan or 10-degree subdivision of a zodiac sign</term>.

The first Drekkana consists of 10 degrees. In every sign, the lord of the first Drekkana is the ruler of that sign itself; for the second Drekkana (11 to 20 degrees), the ruler is the lord of the 5th sign from it; for the third Drekkana (21 to 30 degrees), the ruler is the lord of the 9th sign from it.

**Special Note :-** This period lasts for 7 1/2 *ghadiyas* <note>A traditional unit of time; 1 ghadiya equals 24 minutes</note> at the junction of the beginning and end of these stars. That is to say, a child born within three and three-quarters *ghadiyas* at the end of *Jyeshtha* or three and three-quarters *ghadiyas* at the start of *Mula* is considered born in *Abhukta Mula*. Otherwise, the ritual must certainly be performed before nine years pass. By doing so, the individual and their parents will be protected from the inauspicious results arising in life.

In the case of solar or lunar eclipses, if the birth occurs during an eclipsed sunrise <term>Grastodaya: the rising of a celestial body while already eclipsed</term>, the resulting effects will manifest late. In a total eclipse, the inauspicious results are felt in full. In a partial eclipse <term>Khandagrasa: a partial swallowing or eclipse</term>, the ill effects occur in proportion to the degree of the eclipse. 7. **Friendly Placement (Mitra-gata):** When any planet is naturally situated in the zodiac sign of its friend, it is considered to be in a "Friendly Sign."

8. **Friendly Sub-division (Mitra Navamansha-gata):** When a planet occupies the Navamansha division belonging to a friendly planet, it is considered to be in a "Friendly Sub-division."

9. **The Best Division (Vargottama):** This is determined by examining both the <term>Rashi</term> <note>the primary zodiac sign</note> and the <term>Navamansha</term> <note>the 1/9th division</note>. When a planet occupies the same sign in both the Rashi and the Navamansha, it is called <term>Vargottama</term> <note>literally "the best division," indicating great strength</note>. For example, if a planet in the sign of Aries is also in the Aries Navamansha, or in Taurus and the Taurus Navamansha, and so on for the other signs, it is Vargottama.

**For example:** If a planet in Aries is at 3 degrees and 5 minutes, it falls within the Aries Navamansha (which extends up to 3 degrees and 20 minutes in Aries), making it Vargottama. The others should be understood in the same manner.

10. **Inimical Sign Placement (Shatru-rashi Gata):** When a planet is situated in the sign of its natural enemy, it is considered to be in an "Inimical Sign." For example, Venus and Saturn are enemies of the Sun. Therefore, if the Sun is in Taurus, Libra, Capricorn, or Aquarius, it is in an inimical sign.

11. **Inimical Sub-division (Shatru Navamansha-gata):** When a planet occupies a Navamansha belonging to an enemy planet, it is considered to be in an "Inimical Sub-division."

# 12. Occupying One's Own Domain :- 

The Leo sign for the Sun, Cancer for the Moon, Aries and Scorpio for Mars, Gemini and Virgo for Mercury, Pisces and Sagittarius for Jupiter, Libra and Taurus for Venus, and Capricorn and Aquarius for Saturn are considered their <term>Svakshretra</term> <note>literally "own field" or "own domain"; the zodiac signs ruled by that specific planet</note>. Therefore, if any planet is situated in its own domain, it is considered to be in its own residence. Just like the own domain, being in one's own <term>Navamsha</term> <note>the ninth divisional chart used to determine the true strength of a planet</note> yields the same results.

## Auspicious and Inauspicious Results of the House Lord :-

> *The result is fruitful or fruitless based on the strength or weakness of the House Lord;*
> *If joined with a cruel planet, one should especially declare it fruitless.* **3**
> *Thus is the decision regarding auspicious results stated via the Lord of the House;*
> *Inauspiciousness is destroyed when he is strong, and increases when he is weak.* **4**
<note>original: "Saphalam viphalam taccha bhaveshasya balabalat..." - These Sanskrit verses outline the fundamental rule that the "Lord" of an astrological house determines if that area of life prospers.</note>

If the Lord of the House is strong, the results of that house will be successful. If the Lord of the House is weak, the results will be fruitless. The results of auspicious houses increase if the House Lord is strong and decrease if the House Lord is weak. In this manner, the fruit of a house prospers when the Lord is strong and declines when the Lord is weak.

## Special Note :- 

If the Lord of an auspicious house is a benefic planet, it becomes favorable. However, even if the Lord of an *inauspicious* house is weak, it results in good. This is because everyone desires the destruction of bad results and the increase of good results. <note>This is a nuanced point in astrology: a "bad" planet being weak is actually "good" for the person.</note>
