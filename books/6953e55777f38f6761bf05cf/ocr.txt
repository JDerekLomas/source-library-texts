<meta>Handwritten bibliographical notes on a flyleaf, likely by a 19th-century scholar or collector (possibly Sir William Stirling-Maxwell), concerning Andreas Vesalius’s 'De humani corporis fabrica'.</meta>

And. Vesalii de Corporis humani fabrica libri vii.
->Basileae. 1543.<-

First edition of this fine book. The woodcuts are by Johan Calcar, a pupil of Titian. : Brunet (ed. 1864) Vol V. p 1151.

Johan Stephan <insert>van</insert> Calcar or Calcar was b. circa 1500 in a town of that name in the Duchy of Cleve, & died about 1546. Vasari (Vita Firenze 1857, 10 vols in 8^vo. xiii p 150) speaks of him with high praise as a Fleming who had so completely caught the Italian style that his works would never have been ascribed to a Fleming. As a painter he was a successful imitator of his master Titian.

His portrait of And. Vesalius in this volume is so well painted that I <unclear>could not withhold it a</unclear> place in my "Examples of the Engraved Portraiture of the 16th Century", 1872, in which it appears at p 58.

An original, or at least an old portrait in oils of the <insert>same</insert> Vesalius, agreeing with this print, & ascribed to Titian is in the Hunterian Museum in Glasgow University.

The title page of this volume, representing Vesalius in his lecture-room, is also a work of very great interest. It is probable that many of the heads, as well as that of the Professor, are portraits. With the exception of this title, the blocks appear to have been used again in the edition of the book which was published at Basel, by the same publisher Ioan. Oporinus in 1555. The title-block in that edition is evidently a copy, & a very inferior hand, as may be seen by comparing, in the two, the head of Vesalius; the title-tablet, & the tablet at foot of page; the candle-stick & articles on the table near the Professor; the boy climbing on the side; & the man & dog on the other. This latter group has in the later edition, been increased but not improved by the addition of a goat.
