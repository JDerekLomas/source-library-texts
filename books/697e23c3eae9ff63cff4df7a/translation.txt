->**Decorative Marbled Paper**<-

This page features a full-page pattern of marbled paper, a decorative technique highly valued in historical bookbinding. The design consists of a deep reddish-purple base layer, adorned with intricate, hand-crafted swirls and "stone" spots in shades of blue, grey, and ochre.

<note>Marbling (<term>papyro-marmorata <note>A Latin term literally meaning "marble-like paper."</note></term>) was a specialized craft. To create this effect, a craftsman would float pigments on a bath of thickened liquid, manipulate the colors into patterns with combs or needles, and then carefully lay a sheet of paper onto the surface to "lift" the design.</note>

<note>In 17th-century manuscripts, such papers were used as endpapers (the leaves at the very beginning or end of a book) to protect the inner pages and to add a touch of luxury to the binding.</note>
