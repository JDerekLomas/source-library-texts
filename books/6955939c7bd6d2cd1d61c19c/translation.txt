# TO THE RESPECTABLE MAN
->LORD AGOSTINO FORNARI,<-
->Citizen of Genoa,<-

## HEINRICH CORNELIUS
->AGRIPPA VON NETTESHEIM,<-
->Sends Fondest Greetings.<-

<image-desc>A decorative woodcut initial 'C' featuring intertwined floral motifs and a central figure's face or mask.</image-desc>

As I was considering, most upright Agostino—you who, through your zeal for doing good, have made me a debtor perpetually bound to you in gratitude—with what devotion, what reverence, what pledge, or what industry of wit I might testify to this respect of my soul toward you, it was my intention to adorn you with some beautiful and great gift (since you are most worthy of the most beautiful and greatest things). I wished to offer you books from the innermost depths of both divine and human literature, rich in the integrity of their prose, the abundance of their subject matter, and the grave weight of their sentiments. 

But since I am far inferior in learning and wit to such an ambition, and follow far behind in eloquence of speech and elegance of language, I have nothing in the storehouse of my mind that I can offer except a notable ignorance. My very genius lies dejected by the indignation of my fortune, so much so that out of that very indignation I have almost been turned into a dog, like the Trojan Hecuba <note>In Greek mythology, Queen Hecuba was transformed into a dog after the fall of Troy due to her grief and rage.</note>. Having no strength left for speaking well, I remember nothing more than how to bite, to bark, to speak ill, and to rail. And so, being thus affected, I have written in these days a large enough volume to which I have given the title *On the Uncertainty and Vanity of the Sciences*, in which I have barked against that entire "War of the Giants" <note>original: "gigantomachiam." A metaphor for the arrogant struggle of human knowledge against divine truth.</note> of the sciences and arts; thus I have [attacked] all those [masters] of the sciences...

->DEDICATORY EPISTLE.<-

...and I have snapped with the most powerful bites at the most robust hunters of the arts, so that whenever I revisit the work itself, I marvel at myself for being such a dog in the form of a man—one who lacks nothing of the canine nature except for flattery, though that is a quality quite necessary for a courtier. Therefore, lest I seem a deserter of your respect while I am unable to bestow gifts that are worthy of you or fruitful treasures drawn from the sciences, I now offer you at least this **Cynic declamation** <note>original: "Cynicam declamationem"; a play on the word "Cynic," derived from the Greek word for "dog-like," referring to the school of philosophy that rejected worldly vanity.</note> from the same workshop of my ignorance and indignation as a proof of my respect for you. I present you with this gift; I establish this as dedicated to you, wishing to freely show the greatness of my soul toward you, and what a vigilant guardian, what a sharp-eyed scout, what a bold soldier, and what a magnanimous leader you have acquired in me. For while I announce the enemies from afar as if from a high watchtower, so that the citizens of divine letters may retreat to safer places, I then offer myself to be torn apart by all for the safety of everyone, fighting in the front line and at close quarters. By such an action, I am so far from fearing envy that I think gratitude is owed to me by all, since for the sake of the public good I act simultaneously as scout and soldier to announce and attack those things by which human safety is endangered; and furthermore, I offer myself as a leader to those who, exiting the labyrinth of human sciences, strive toward the citadel of truth. Receive now, therefore, most worshipful Augustine, this declamation, such as it is, and hold it as your own by right. For I know that if you love hunters and dogs, this cannot fail to be most pleasing to you. 

But I shall now transform from a dog into a crocodile, or a dragon, or some other fire-breathing serpent, and I will immediately finish my **Pyrography** <note>original: "Pyrographiam"; literally "fire-writing," likely referring to a lost or projected work on pyrotechnics or alchemy.</note> (a work such as this age has not yet seen, but which will be of use only to some famous destroyer of the world). Then I shall sprinkle myself with sacred water and wash in a living stream, so that at last, having cast off these fatal masks, I may return again into a man—lest by "Pythagorizing" <note>A reference to Pythagoras's doctrine of metempsychosis, or the migration of souls into different animal bodies.</note> too much and migrating into such various beasts, I should finally turn into a philosophizing ass, after the fashion of Lucian and Apuleius. But now, most happily fare thee well: and remember, when you read these writings of ours, that **to know nothing is the happiest life.**

# HEINRICH CORNELIUS AGRIPPA TO THE Reader.

**A**N will not this deed of mine, studious reader, seem to you magnanimous, over-bold, and almost Herculean—to take up arms against that entire **gigantomachy** <note>A war of giants; here referring to the massive, entrenched systems of academic knowledge.</note> of all sciences and arts, and to challenge all those most robust hunters of the sciences and arts into the arena? The haughtiness of the Doctors, the erudition of the Licentiates, the authority of the Masters, the attempts of the Bachelors, the zeal of all the scholastics, and the sedition of the mechanics will growl against me. If I overcome them, will it not be equal to or greater than striking the Nemean lion with a club, slaying the Lernaean hydra with fire, killing the Erymanthian boar, capturing the hind in the Maenalian grove that bears golden horns, piercing the Stymphalian birds in the clouds with arrows, crushing Antaeus within one's arms, fixing columns in the Ocean, conquering the triple-bodied Geryon, driving away the cattle, slaying the bull, overcoming Achelous in single combat, leading away the horses of Diomedes, dragging Cerberus bound in a chain, stealing the golden apples of the Hesperides, and more of that kind which were performed by Hercules with great labor and no less danger? For it is a matter of no less labor, and indeed of the greatest danger, to overcome these monsters of the universities and wrestling-schools. For I am not blind to how bloody a battle I must enter here at close quarters, and how full of danger this war is, since I am hedged in by an army of such powerful enemies. Alas, with how many engines of war they will attack me, how many...

# P R E F A C E

With what efforts will they attack me, with how many insults will they pursue me? First, the lice-ridden **Grammarians** will clamor, and with their etymologies they will graft a gouty name onto me from Agrippa <note>A reference to Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, whose work "De Incertitudine et Vanitate Scientiarum" likely inspired this satirical catalog of professions.</note>. Astonished **Poets**, in place of Momus <note>The Greek god of mockery and blame</note> or Aesop’s goat, will traduce me in their verses. The news-selling **Historiographers** will profane me even more than Pausanias and Herostratus. The loud-mouthed **Rhetoricians** will accuse me of high treason with angry eyes, terrible faces, thunderous voices, and dire gestures. The monstrous **Memoriographers** <note>Teachers of mnemonics or "art of memory"</note> will batter my brain with their phantoms. The combative **Dialecticians** will hurl innumerable weapons of syllogisms at me. The double-tongued **Sophists** will stop my mouth with the snares of their words and their insoluble riddles, as if a bridle had been thrown on me. The barbarian **Lullist** <note>A follower of Ramon Llull’s complex philosophical system</note> will drive my head mad with absurd words and solecisms. The impious **Mathematicians** will outlaw me from earth and sky. The atom-writing **Arithmeticians**, having stirred up moneylenders against me, will force me to an account of my debt. The stubborn **Gambler** will drive me to the noose. The fortune-telling **Pythagorean** will calculate unlucky numbers. The **Geomancer** <note>One who divines by dots or earth-markings</note> who marks points will cast figures of prison and sorrow. The many-toned **Musicians** will sing of me as a common street-tale, and with shrill shells and the discordant noises of shells, basins, and pans, they will deafen me more than is usual at the weddings of those marrying a second time; the pompous **Matrons** will exclude me from their dances, and the charming **Girls** will deny me kisses; the chattering **Maidservants** will mock me as a "dancing camel," and the **Actor** will play a foul tragedy upon me on the stage. The hundred-handed **Gladiator** will attack me on the right and left. The perplexed **Geometricians**, having cast triangles and squared circles like Gordian knots, will capture me in their entanglement. The vain **Optician** will paint or carve me as more deformed than an ape, or even Thersites <note>The ugliest man in the Greek army at Troy</note> himself. The wandering **Cosmographers** will exile me beyond the Sarmatians to the icy regions; the Daedalian **Architect** will secretly undermine me with impregnable machines and force me to wander, caught in unobservable labyrinths. The stygian **Miner** will condemn me to the ore-pits; the fatal **Astrologers** will threaten me with hanging, and by the unstable turning of the heavens, they will withhold favor from the gods above. The threatening **Diviners** will omen every evil; the troublesome **Physiognomist** will defame me as cold in love and weak of thigh. The raving **Metoposcopist** <note>One who tells character from forehead lines</note> will pronounce me a brain-sick ass; the prophetic **Chiromancer** <note>Palm reader</note> will divine everything unfavorably; the foreknowing **Soothsayer** will un-consecrate me with a gloomy omen. The portentous **Observer of Lightning** will send the avenging flames of Jupiter and the fires of the prophetic thunderbolt. The dark **Dream-interpreter** will terrify me with nocturnal ghosts. The raging **Prophet** will deceive me with an ambiguous oracle. The prodigious **Magicians**, like another Apuleius or Lucian, will transform me into an ass—not a golden one, but perhaps a dung-carrier.

The sacrilegious **Theurgist** <note>A practitioner of high or divine magic</note> will consecrate my head "to the crows" <note>original: "εις κόρακας" - a Greek idiom meaning "to the gallows" or "to hell"</note> or perhaps to the sewers. The contentious **Philosophers** will disagree with the most combative opinions. The **Pythagorean Peddlers** will make us migrate between the dog and the crocodile; the foul and biting **Cynics** will shut me in a tub or a tomb. The pestilential **Academics** will cry out that wives should be held in common. The gluttonous **Epicureans** will kill me with excess. The impious **Peripatetics** <note>Followers of Aristotle</note> will mortify the soul and exclude it from Paradise. The severe **Stoics**, having stripped away human affections, will turn me into a fern. The vain-talking **Metaphysicians**, as if from the Demogorgon’s chaos of things that are nowhere and never shall be, will never cease to overturn my mind with paradoxes. The **Ethical Censors** will report me to the assembly; the **Political Legislator** will forbid me from offices. The **Voluptuous Prince** will banish me from the court. The ambitious **Nobles** will drive me away from the public feasts. The senseless **People** will afflict me with insults in every street. The terrible **Tyrant** will shut me in Phalaris’s bull to be tortured. The factious **Oligarchs** will drive me into exile. The impetuous **Commoners**, that evil beast of many heads, will hurry me to destruction without a trial. Any afflicted **Republic** will accuse me of treason. The greedy **Priests** will forbid me from the altars. The masked **Monks** <note>original: "cuculliones" - hooded ones</note> and insulting hypocrites will harass me from the pulpit. The all-powerful **Popes** will reserve my sins for eternal fires. The lustful **Prostitutes** will threaten me with the "French Pox" <note>Syphilis</note>. The rapacious **Pimp** and the drunken **Bawd** will deliver my pockets of their money. The ulcerous **Beggars** will shut me out of the hospitals. The wandering **Pardon-mongers** will offer me "holy fire" and mad bites, and deprive me of their indulgences. The unfaithful **Steward** will run up my debt at the market. The blasphemous **Sailor** will drive me into Scyllas. The wicked **Merchant** will consume me, pawned by usury. The thieving **Tax-collector** will plunder my wages. The harsh **Farmers** will bar me from pleasant gardens. The idle **Shepherds** will vow me to the wolves. The wave-wandering **Fisherman** will cast a hidden hook. The shouting **Hunter** will set dogs and hawks upon me. The war-mighty **Soldier** will plunder me. The purple-clad **Nobles** will remove me from my rank. The cloaked **Heralds** will strip me of my ancestral images, and in the proclaimed knightly games, which they call tournaments, they will cry me out as a rustic tax-gatherer. The dung-eating **Physicians** will drench me with urine and dregs. Among whom the talkative **Logician-Physician**, by disputing about the disease, will withdraw the timely remedy. The reckless **Empiric** will expose me to the danger of death by a doubtful experiment. The deceptive **Methodist** <note>A school of medical thought, not the modern denomination</note>, by procrastinating the remedies, will prolong the sickness...

# PREFACE
# TO THE READER.

to a summary. Dirty pharmacists <note>original: "pharmacopolæ"</note> will drain me with enemas; emasculating surgeons will lie in wait for my teeth and testicles. Cruel anatomists <note>original: "anatomistæ"</note> will demand I be dissected. The filthy veterinarian will shut me in a stable and blind my eyes with the dust of the chariot-track. The deceitful dietician will finish me off with hunger. The thirsty cook will thrust some tasteless, offensive dish upon me. The prodigal alchemist <note>original: "alchumista"</note> will forbid me riches and banish me to his furnaces. Unconquerable Jurists <note>original: "Iuristæ"</note> will bury me under vast volumes of glosses <note>interpretive commentaries on law</note>. Haughty lawyers will charge me with high treason. Arrogant canonists <note>experts in Church law</note> will excommunicate me with dire curses. Litigious attorneys will intend six hundred slanders against me. The crafty procurator will collude with the adversary in a deserted cause. The wavering notary will sign a forgery. The inexorable judge will condemn the action and deny the "Apostles" (as they call them) of appeal <note>A legal term for the letters of appeal sent to a higher court</note>. The imperious chief scribe of the chancery will fail me with a decree. Obstinate theosophists will cry out "heresy!" or compel me to follow their idols. Supercilious "Masters of Ours" <note>A satirical title for university professors</note> will shout for a recantation <note>original: "Palinodiam"</note>, and the Atlantises of the Sorbonne <note>The theologians of the University of Paris, portrayed here as pillars holding up a false world</note> will proscribe me with great seals.

Do you see now, Reader, how many dangers I undergo? Yet there is hope of easily escaping these insults, if only you, patient for the truth and laying aside malice, approach these readings with a fair mind. Furthermore, I have that by which I may defend myself: the Word of God <note>original: "verbum Dei"</note>, which I, intrepid, shall oppose to these as a buckler and shield. And if I must die for Him, for whose sake I have willingly stirred up so many enemies against me, I shall die willingly before I desert Him. And I want you to know that I have written these things led not by hatred, nor ambition, nor deceit, nor error; neither sacrilegious greed nor the arrogance of a wicked mind drove me to this. Rather, the cause is both most just and most true: namely, that I see many people becoming so overgrown with human disciplines and sciences that they not only despise and scorn the eloquence of the Sacred Letters and the Canonical Scriptures of the Holy Spirit as rustic and ignorant (because they lack the ornaments of words, the strength of syllogisms, studied persuasions, and the foreign erudition of philosophers, but are instead established simply in the operation of virtue and naked faith), but they even pursue them with a kind of contempt. We see others also, though they seem more pious to themselves, who strive to prove and confirm the laws of Christ by the decree of philosophers, attributing more to them than to the holy Prophets, Evangelists, and Apostles of God—even though the latter are more than an octave <note>original: "dia pason" - a musical metaphor for a vast distance or total difference</note> apart from the former. Furthermore, in many and nearly all gymnasiums <note>schools or universities</note> a perverse custom and damnable habit has grown: they compel the students to be initiated by swearing an oath to Aristotle, or Boethius, or Thomas <note>Thomas Aquinas</note>, or Albert <note>Albertus Magnus</note>, or some

<column-break/>

other of their scholastics, that they will never oppose them; from whom, if anyone perceives a sense different by a broad fingernail <note>an idiom meaning "the slightest bit"</note>, they proclaim him a scandalous heretic <note>original: "hæreticum"</note>, offensive to pious ears, to be consumed by fire and flames.

Therefore, these so-rash giants and enemies of the Sacred Letters must be attacked; their camps and citadels must be stormed. It must be shown how great is the blindness of men who, with so many sciences and arts and so many masters and authors of each, always wander from the knowledge of the truth. How great also is the temerity, how arrogant the presumption, to prefer the schools of Philosophers to the Church of Christ, and to place the opinions of men before, or equal to, the Word of God. Finally, what an impious tyranny it is to imprison the talents of students to predefined authors, and to take away from disciples the freedom of investigating and following the truth. Since all these things are so manifest that they cannot be denied, pardon must be given if I am seen to speak more freely and perhaps with bitterness against some kinds of disciplines,

->A DECLAMATION.<-
->*On the sciences in general.* CHAPTER I.<-

It is an ancient opinion, and the nearly harmonious and unanimous sentiment of almost all who practice philosophy, by which they judge that any science whatsoever brings something of divinity to man himself, according to the capacity and value of each, so that these sciences can often carry men beyond the limits of humanity into the choirs of the blessed Gods. From this have come those various and innumerable praises of the sciences, by which every man strives to prefer those arts and disciplines—in which he has sharpened the powers of his intellect through long exercise—above all others, and to extol them even above the heavens themselves, in a speech no less ornate than it is long. 

But I, persuaded by reasons of a different kind, judge that nothing more ruinous, nothing more pestilential can happen to the life of men and the salvation of our souls than these very arts and these very sciences. Therefore, I believe we must proceed in reverse order: it is my opinion that the sciences themselves should not be extolled with such great proclamations, but should for the most part be disparaged. There is not one which lacks a just censure of reproach, nor again any which deserves praise from itself, unless it borrows it from the integrity of its possessor. I wish this opinion of mine to be received by you with such modesty that you do not think I am criticizing others who feel differently, nor that I am arrogating anything too insolently to myself. Therefore, you will grant me pardon for dissenting from the rest in this matter, until we begin to examine this opinion through each faculty of letters in order—not merely with common arguments taken from the surface of things, but with the firmest reasons drawn from the innermost guts of the matter; not with that argumentative eloquence of Demosthenes or Chrysippus, which...
