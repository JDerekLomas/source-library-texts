In those instances alone, the consideration of the direction of the "mouth" of the fire is required, not in other rituals such as the <term>Makha</term><note>A specific type of grand sacrificial festival.</note>. Some authors, however, argue that this consideration should apply even to the <term>Samskaras</term><note>Sacraments or rites of passage that "refine" or "perfect" an individual.</note> based on specific scriptural statements. Regarding the placement of the fire’s "mouth," this exception applies to those who maintain the sacred fire; here, the tradition of the learned elders is the ultimate authority.

### Now, the Procedural Guide

Before the day the ritual begins, on an auspicious day, the construction of the <term>Kunda</term><note>The consecrated fire pit.</note> and the <term>Mandapa</term><note>The ritual pavilion.</note> is prescribed. On the day preceding the start, for the attainment of ritual authority, one should perform a "supreme penance" <term>Prayaschitta</term><note>An act of atonement or purification to rectify errors or spiritual impurities.</note> and eat a meal consisting of non-meat flour porridge. On the day the ritual commences, for the purification of the body through the action, one should bathe in the Ganges using sesame seeds and <term>Amalaka</term><note>The Indian Gooseberry, traditionally used for ritual cleansing.</note>. Some follow the practice of anointing the ritual ground with cow dung, and then creating a <term>Svastika</term><note>An ancient solar symbol of well-being and auspiciousness.</note> design using rice flour or similar materials on the floor. Seats for the wife and the patron <term>Yajamana</term><note>The person who sponsors and pays for the ritual, and who receives its spiritual benefits.</note> should be laid out there, such as wooden stools.

The patron and his wife are then seated in their respective places. The scents, flowers, and other offerings are placed to the right. A vessel for washing the hands is placed to the left. Then follows the <term>Shanti Patha</term><note>Recitation of peace prayers from the Vedas to remove obstacles.</note>, the performance of auspicious acts, and the wearing of flower garlands around the neck. One then performs <term>Acamana</term><note>The ritual sipping of water for internal purification.</note> and <term>Pranayama</term><note>Regulated breathing exercises to steady the mind.</note>. With the "Om" syllable, one remembers the "Good-Faced" deity, Ganapati.
<margin>6 ||</margin>
After the remembrance of Ganesha, the "Main Intention" <term>Pradhana Sankalpa</term><note>A formal statement of intent made at the start of a ritual, declaring who is performing it, when, where, and for what purpose.</note> is made. Having recited the specific place and time, one says: "For the removal of all my sins and for the pleasure of the Supreme Lord, I shall worship through the <term>Devamukha Koti-homa</term><note>The 'Ten Million Oblations' ritual, one of the most extensive and powerful fire ceremonies.</note>." Or, one may say: "I shall perform the one-tenth portion of the Koti-homa."

If it is the "Hundred-Faced" ritual, then the term "Hundred-Faced" should be specifically mentioned. In a desire-based ritual <term>Kamya</term><note>A ritual performed for a specific material or personal result, such as health or prosperity.</note>, the specific desired outcome should be stated as described in the scriptures. Then follows the secondary intention for the specific acts of the ritual, and the intention for the worship of Ganesha. One should then perform the worship of Ganesha, the worship of the Mothers, the <term>Vasordhara</term><note>Literally the 'stream of wealth,' a rite where a continuous stream of clarified butter is poured into the fire.</note>, the <term>Pushyasvasti</term><note>A blessing for well-being.</note>, the ancestral rite for prosperity <term>Abhyudayika Shraddha</term>, and the declaration of the holy day.

Then follows the formal appointment <term>Varana</term> of the <term>Acharya</term><note>The head priest or preceptor who guides the entire ceremony.</note> and other priests. The mantra for appointing the Acharya is: "As the Supreme Priest Bṛhaspati was the Hotar-priest for Indra, so may you be the Acharya for me in this ritual." Thus, the Acharya is chosen. Just as all the Maruts were the priests for the king, so "you have been chosen by me as the Acharya in this sacrificial act." 

Then, the prayer to the appointed Acharya: "You are the path, the father, the mother; you are the teacher and the ultimate refuge. By your grace, O Brahmin-sage, may all my heart’s desires be fulfilled. For my liberation from distress and for my own spiritual peace, perform this incomparable sacrifice called the Koti-homa."

Next is the appointment of the <term>Brahma</term><note>The priest who sits silently and supervises the entire ritual to correct any errors through mental power.</note> priest. The mantra for this is: "As the four-faced Brahma is the grandfather of all the worlds, so may you, O best of the twice-born, be the Brahma in this sacrifice of mine."

Next is the appointment of the <term>Sadasya</term><note>A member of the ritual assembly who acts as an overseer or advisor.</note>. The appointment mantra is: "O Lord, knower of all Dharmas, best among all upholders of Dharma, be the member of this assembly in my extensive sacrifice, O you of noble vows." 

Then follows the counting of the homa-performers and the appointment of the twice-born <term>Brahmins</term> in that specific number. This is followed by the appointment of the eight door-keepers <term>Dwarapala</term> representing the Rigveda and other Vedas. In the same way, all the reciters <term>Japaka</term> are appointed.

### Investigation into the Birth of the Sacrificial Pit

As stated in the *Shanti-sara* <note>A compendium on peaceful/propitiatory rituals</note> and the *Kashyapa Samhita*: "Wherever the Sun is positioned among the constellations <term>Nakshatras</term>, one must calculate the positions of the Fire, Venus, and Saturn, as well as the Moon, Mars, and Jupiter. The positions of the shadow-planets Rahu and Ketu are specifically indicated to determine whether the 'face' of the ritual area is auspicious or inauspicious."

Thus it should be performed, according to the sages Gada and others. This applies to rituals performed for a specific desire <term>Kamya</term> and for life-cycle rites <term>Samskaras</term>, but <unclear>in certain cases</unclear> it is not an absolute rule. According to the Tantric teachings, the sages recall that if the "three-eyed" or "single-eyed" calculations are not used, it relates to the "nose" or entry point of the ritual.

Regarding the peace ritual <term>Shanti</term> performed during a divine sacrifice, the *Vishnu Dharmottara Purana* states: "Even if a cruel planet <note>A 'malefic' planet like Mars or Saturn</note> is positioned at the 'face' of the ritual, it may become auspicious through the proper movements. After performing the peace ritual, one should gift a cow to a family man who is observant of his duties. One should also fashion a figurine out of iron and cast it face-down."

The figurine is then treated with cow urine, honey, and other substances. <margin>G. 188</margin> Having placed it there and observed it, the sacrifice to the constellations <term>Nakshatra-homa</term> is prescribed.

Furthermore, certain alignments must be avoided by any means: Mercury in the eighth house, the Moon in the fourth house, Venus in the eighth house, or Mars in a specific placement. If Rahu is in the house of Dharma <note>the 9th house</note> while Jupiter is present, the performance of a sacrifice under such a sky leads to the destruction of sons, wives, and wealth. The conjunction of the lunar day and the weekday must be served... <note>text cuts off regarding the Vaidhriti yoga/alignment</note>
