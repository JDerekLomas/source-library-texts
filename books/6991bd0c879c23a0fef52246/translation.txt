# PREFACE.

To be fair to myself, I believe I should state that this book was prepared somewhat quickly from notes I used in some of my lectures. The lessons provided here are essentially the outlines of those lectures. In both the lectures and this book, my one and only purpose has been to acquaint the student with the means of developing and effectively using the mighty forces latent within them—<term>Personal Magnetism</term> <note>original: "Personal Magnetism"; in the early 20th century, this referred to a person's charisma or an unseen "force" that allowed them to influence others.</note> and <term>Psychic Influence</term> <note>original: "Psychic Influence"; the idea that one's thoughts or mental energy can directly affect the minds of others.</note>. To this end, I have sacrificed any claim to literary style or attempts to use elegant language.

I felt that I had a message to deliver, and I endeavored to deliver it promptly, clearly, and plainly, without any attempt at "fine writing." If a simple, everyday word seemed to express my thought, I used it. If a slang term or casual phrase seemed to fit, I included it.

I trust that my critics will spare themselves the trouble of pointing out my many defects of style and composition; I am fully aware of these things. I have made everything else secondary in my endeavor to make this work plain and practical. This is an explanation, not an apology.

With this understanding between us, I submit this little work to your kind consideration. While I am fully aware of its defects, I still feel that it will be helpful to some of the many who are endeavoring to overcome unfavorable environments; it may serve as a guide-post, pointing the way to better things. I feel that it will do its share of the work in removing <term>Fearthought</term> <note>original: "Fearthought"; a term popularized by the New Thought movement to describe the habit of worrying or dwelling on negative outcomes, viewed as the opposite of constructive "forethought."</note> from the minds of men, and in replacing "I CAN'T" with "I CAN and I WILL." I feel that it must do these things, for it contains within it the seeds of a mighty Truth.

W. W. A. <note>W. W. A.: William Walker Atkinson (1862–1932), a prolific writer and key figure in the New Thought movement.</note>

**CHICAGO, ILLINOIS, December 4, 1900.**
