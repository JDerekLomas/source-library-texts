# Five-Mile Fog

Take wood shavings and fry them in tung oil <term>Tung oil: A fast-drying oil pressed from the seeds of the tung tree, used in ancient China for waterproofing and as a highly flammable binder for incendiaries</term>. Mix these together and grind the mixture until it is extremely fine. Store the powder in cloth bags. When facing the enemy, each soldier should carry a measured amount. If you encounter a favorable tailwind, first pour the powder onto the ground and feign a retreat of over a hundred paces. Use a "running line" <note>original: 走線 (zouxian), a type of fuse used to ignite pyrotechnics from a distance</note> to set it alight. This will create a dense shroud of smoke and fog that obscures the area for five miles. Any men or horses who breathe in this vapor will suffer from ceaseless tears and nasal discharge, leading them to fall into such chaos that they kill one another. At this moment, charge forth with matchlocks <term>Matchlocks: Referred to as "Bird Guns" (niaoqiang) in the original Chinese, likely due to their accuracy or the bird-like shape of the firing mechanism</term> and elite cavalry to pursue and eliminate them. This is a vital tactic in the deployment of troops.

## White Cloud Divine Water

First, obtain genuine specimens of **Actinolite** <term>Actinolite (Yangqishi): A silicate mineral that forms needle-like crystals, used here as a physical irritant.</term> and **Tremolite** <term>Tremolite (Yinqishi): A mineral closely related to actinolite; in this context, the two are used together to create a fine, abrasive powder.</term>. Grind these two substances into a very fine powder. Place the powder in a porcelain basin with clear water to wash and purify it; gently allow the finest particles to float out with the water <note>This describes the process of levigation, where water is used to separate the finest particles from coarser grit.</note>. Once the sediment has settled, wash it again; repeat this process nine times. You must exert effort to ensure the resulting sediment is extremely fine and smooth. Sun-dry it until it resembles a fine face powder.

The powder is only fit for use if it possesses a brilliant, eye-catching luster. If it lacks brilliance or appears "spiritless," it must not be used. For every one catty <note>A catty (jin) is approximately 600 grams.</note> of this purified powder, soak it in high-quality *shaojiu* <term>Shaojiu: A potent distilled grain liquor.</term> and dry it in the sun. This cycle of soaking and sun-drying must be repeated seven times to reach the required standard. It is then ready for use.

**Mixing Method:** Take five catties of the prepared white powder and grind it finely; take four catties of **weathered lime** <term>Weathered lime (Fenghua shihui): Calcium hydroxide or slaked lime, which acts as a chemical irritant when it contacts moisture in the eyes or throat.</term> and sift it through a fine silk sieve.

Regarding the **Frankish Gun** (Folangji). If a mountain pass is defended by the enemy and is so well-fortified that it cannot be breached by ordinary means, use this weapon to attack it.

Therefore, it is essential to utilize an **iron gate** <note>An iron crossbar or locking mechanism used to secure the breech chamber firmly into the mother barrel during firing.</note> to ensure safety and performance.

Another point of excellence is found in the dual **sights**. <term>Sights: "Zhaoxing" (照星), literally "shining stars."</term> The rear handle should be positioned slightly lower so that it does not interfere with the gunner’s face when they are bracing the weapon to aim. To aim correctly, the gunner should squint with one eye, looking through the aperture of the **rear sight** to align it with the **front sight**. The aperture of the front sight must then be aligned directly with the intended target.

If the projectile and the **wad** <term>Wad: "Zima" (子馬), the wooden or paper plug used to seal the powder and projectile within the chamber to create pressure.</term> are both too large for the bore, the shot will be difficult to discharge. When it does finally exit, the recoil will be so immense that human strength will be unable to steady the weapon. Conversely, if the projectile is too small, it will fit too loosely in the bore, resulting in a weak and crooked flight path.

**Innovations in Loading and Ballistics**

In the past, using a **wooden sabot** <term>Wooden sabot: "Mu ma" (木馬), a wooden block or plug used to support the projectile in the barrel.</term> to push heavy shot often resulted in damage to the cannon and caused frequent delays in firing. With the current method, when applying the powder, it is no longer necessary to ram it down excessively hard, and the wooden sabot is discarded. One only needs lead balls that fit the diameter of the muzzle. 

Formerly, a flat-topped wooden tool was used as a **pusher** <term>Pusher: "Song zi" (送子), a specialized ramrod head used to seat the projectile.</term> to drive the shot home. This would flatten the lead ball upon impact; once deformed, the ball would not fly true or far. Now, we have manufactured an **iron concave-center pusher**. Because the tool is hollowed out in the center, when it pushes the shot into the mouth of the chamber, the edges are flush, but the body of the lead ball remains perfectly round. When fired, its exit is smooth and efficient. It can reach a range of over one *li* <note>Approx. 500 meters.</note>, with enough force to pierce through both men and horses.

**Mounting and Preparation**

Prepare the **ship's mount** <note>Original: "chuan duo" (船舵), literally "ship's rudder." In this context, it refers to the swivel mount or the steering-timber where the gun is positioned for naval defense.</note> by drilling a hole to accommodate an **iron pivot** <note>Original: "tie xin" (鐵信), an iron pin or mounting bolt.</note>. This allows the weapon to be moved up, down, left, and right with great ease and flexibility. 

**Standard Loading Procedure**

1.  First, complete the setting of the fuse line for the **breech-block** <term>Breech-block: "Zi chong" (子銃), the removable "child" chamber that holds the load.</term>.
2.  Use pre-measured bags of gunpowder, using exactly one bag per shot. 
3.  Take one bag and place it into the chamber. Use a wooden pusher to lightly pack it until firm, then cover it with a layer of paper.
4.  Place one lead shot into the mouth of the chamber. 
5.  Position the **iron concave-center pusher** against the face of the shot. Use an iron hammer to strike it firmly until it is flush with the mouth of the chamber. You must ensure the shot is not flattened; this is the ingenious advantage of the concave center.
