# To the English Reader.

Jakob Böhme <note>original: "Jacob Behm"</note> writes here concerning the highest point of controversy, which is predestination. This topic has been left without a satisfactory resolution by any writer since it first arose. Each individual party contends for and against <note>original: "pro & con"</note> the issue, still relying on the texts of Scripture brought to support their own side, despite all the debates that have been held between them. Both the scriptural texts and that great mystery remain undecided. 

The principal cause of this may be the apparent opposition between the texts cited by both sides; one party assumes that the texts on their side should overrule <note>original: "oversway"</note> the meaning of those on which the opposing party bases its arguments. However, the texts must necessarily be acknowledged as most true, and the truth in every opinion cannot be contrary to the Scriptures—even if the parties are unable to reconcile the meaning of those texts or their own opinions, or to see how far both sides are true. Consequently, they remain at a wide distance from one another in their affections.

<margin>\* Böhme’s Preface to Election, verse 9. <br> $^a$ This Book of Election, Chapter 13, verse 68 to the end of the book.</margin>

But this author does not dispute at all. He desires only to discuss and offer his understanding and the basis for interpreting the texts on both sides, answering the objections of human reason. He aims to manifest his knowledge for the purpose of joining <note>original: "Conjoyning"</note>, uniting, and reconciling all parties in love, despite their differing and various levels of knowledge.

And he has performed this here and there in all his writings, which explain the basis of under- <note>The page ends on the catchword "under-", likely leading into "understanding" on the following page.</note>

<summary>In this preface, the editor introduces Jakob Böhme's approach to the controversial topic of predestination, arguing that Böhme seeks to reconcile opposing theological factions through love and spiritual understanding rather than through traditional academic dispute.</summary>
