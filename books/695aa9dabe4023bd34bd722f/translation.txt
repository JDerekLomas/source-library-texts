->PREFACE<-

I have entirely omitted the book called the *Geoponica* from this edition, as it arose from an error <note>original: "ex errore ortus sit"; the editor refers to a scholarly misattribution</note> (see the *Festschrift* dedicated to Moritz Cantor on the occasion of his eightieth birthday, Leipzig 1909, p. 118 ff.); the excerpts from Hero’s works which it contains have been collated in their proper places. I have not even included the *Geodaesia* <note>A treatise on land measurement</note>, which offers nothing but thin excerpts from Hero’s *Geometry*; in the introduction to Volume V, I shall diligently describe its manuscripts and indicate their contents. I omitted Didymus because I discovered that another scholar is occupied with him. On the other hand, I have added certain metrological fragments (p. 402, 26 ff.) found in my manuscripts, which Hultsch <note>Friedrich Hultsch, a noted 19th-century metrologist</note> had placed among the remains of the *Metrological Writers* (I fr. 5, 95, 81). The Constantinople manuscript (see Vol. III p. VII ff.) supplied many unpublished additions; I possess a photographic reproduction of it through the kindness of Hermann Schöne. I have taken care to reproduce most of its figures <note>diagrams or illustrations</note> because of the antiquity of the manuscript; almost all those provided by the remaining manuscripts I have omitted as useless for understanding the author's words. Their style can be sufficiently understood both from those I included as specimens along with Hultsch, and from the Constantinople examples.
Therefore, I have used these manuscripts:

## DEFINITIONS.

This short work, which I do not hesitate to attribute to Hero, has been handed down to us as the first part of a mathematical collection which some unknown Byzantine scholar, perhaps in the 11th century, had excerpted from various authors (1–132 *Definitions*, 133 from Hero’s *Geometry*, 134 from Euclid’s *Elements*, 135 from Geminus, 136–137 from Proclus’s *Commentary on Elements I*—or rather from some collection of Euclidean scholia <note>marginal ancient or medieval commentaries</note>—138 from Anatolius and Theon of Smyrna), other parts of which are also preserved separately in other manuscripts. I shall begin with those that provide the entire collection.
