# To the READER.

The purpose of this Preface is not to flatter the Reader into a high opinion of either these *Discourses* or their *Author* (the *Discourses* will best demonstrate what they are, and as for the *Author*, his own works will praise him); rather, it is only to give a clear and plain account of what concerns *this edition*. Additionally, I wish to observe something concerning the *Discourses* themselves and their *Author* which may be necessary for the reader to know.

<margin>Regarding this edition.</margin>
The papers now published I received from the Author’s executor, Mr. *Samuel Cradock* (then a Fellow of *Emmanuel College*, now Rector of *North Cadbury* in *Somerset*). I thought his generosity to the public in sharing these treasures was worthy of being gratefully remembered here first of all.

Having taken a general look at these and some other papers (several of which were loose and scattered, not being written by the Author in any bound book), my first care was to collect those that were of the same kind <note>original: "Homogeneal"</note> and related to the same discourse. I also took care to note where any new additional matter was to be inserted. (For the Author, whose mind was a rich and fruitful soil—a bountiful and ever-bubbling fountain—would sometimes add further thoughts and considerations to what he had previously delivered in public. I found this employment sufficiently perplexed and toilsome at first; but by reading over the manuscripts more than once, I got through those difficulties and dispatched that first trouble. I am well assured that the severed parts, as well as the additional considerations, are now brought to their due and proper places where the author himself would have disposed them if he had transcribed his papers.

And now I found that I stood in need of more hands and eyes than my own for the fair transcribing of the papers (which were otherwise impossible to be printed), as also for the examining of the material quotations in this volume. In this labor, I had the assistance of some friends to whom the memory of the author was very precious. As for some short allusions and expressions borrowed out of ancient *authors*, serving rather for *ornament* than *support* of the matter in hand, there seemed to be less need of being solicitous about all of them. But for the other testimonies, which are many and weighty, there were but few (though some possibly among such a number of quotations might escape notice) that were not examined. I am sure that this labor was not unnecessary or in vain, however wearisome it was, especially where the *authors*, or the specific places in the *authors' works*, were not mentioned.

And then, for the sake of those readers whose education had not acquainted them with some of the languages in which many of the testimonies were represented (being otherwise men of good accomplishments and capable of receiving the designed benefit of these papers), it seemed expedient to render the *Latin*, but especially the *Hebrew* and *Greek* quotations, into *English* <note>original: "English;"</note>
