### Chapter Four: Reducing the Width

This chapter deals with instances where a field’s <term>length</term> <note>original: 縱, *zong*</note> is increased while its <term>width</term> <note>original: 廣, *guang*</note> is decreased; hence it is titled "Reducing the Width" <note>original: 少廣, *Shao Guang*. This is a classical category in Chinese mathematics, dating back to the Han Dynasty, primarily dealing with finding the side of a shape when the area is known—effectively division and square roots.</note>. It follows the principle of reversing the calculations used in field measurement. One uses the "Square Method" to divide the <term>area</term> <note>original: 積, *ji*</note> to find the side of a square, and the "Circle Method" to divide the area to find the dimensions of a circle. The essential steps for <term>extracting square roots</term> <note>original: 開平方, *kai pingfang*</note> and circular roots are often perceived as complex and tedious for beginners. Therefore, I have provided a simplified and clear explanation in the notes below.

#### Song of Summing Length and Width <gloss>Same as the method of square root extraction with a subtracted linear term</gloss>

> When length and width are summed but their individual values are unknown,
> Multiply the area by four, ensuring no error is shown.
> Square the sum of the sides and subtract the fourfold area;
> Extract the root of the remainder to find the difference, clear as a mirror.

> Halve the result, and the long side is revealed to you.
