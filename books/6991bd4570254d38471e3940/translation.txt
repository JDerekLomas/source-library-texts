It is wrong to believe that it belongs only to the <term>Learned<gloss>Translated from "Savans," this refers to the established scientific and academic elite of the late 18th century, particularly those in the Royal Academy of Sciences and the Faculty of Medicine.</gloss></term> to pronounce upon the merit and reality of a discovery. Rarely can a learned man—that is to say, a man who has collected and compared many ideas found before him—understand a man of genius who announces to him an order of new truths.

The mind has its habits just like the heart, and the mind renounces its habits no more than the heart does.
