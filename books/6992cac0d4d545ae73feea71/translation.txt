## ○ Discourse on the Origins of the Five Elements: Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth

Respectfully we hear: Before Heaven and Earth were distinguished, the state was called "Primordial Chaos." Before the Creative and Receptive forces <note>original: "Qian and Kun," representing the fundamental male/female or active/passive principles of the universe</note> were divided, it was named the "Embryonic Radiance." The sun, moon, stars, and constellations had not yet been born; the forces of Yin and Yang and the seasons of cold and heat had not yet been divided. 

Above, there were no rain or dew, no wind or clouds, no frost or snow, and no thunder—it was nothing but a dim and dusky gloom. Below, there were no plants or trees, no mountains or rivers, no birds or beasts, and no human people—it was nothing but a dark and hazy obscurity. At this time, a single Breath <term>Qi: The vital energy or life force that composes all things in the universe</term> coiled and knotted in the center. Thus:

The Supreme Easiness produced Water. <gloss>The state before energy existed is called Supreme Easiness.</gloss>
The Supreme Beginning produced Fire. <gloss>The state where energy existed but form did not is called Supreme Beginning.</gloss>
The Supreme Origin produced Wood. <gloss>The state where form existed but substance did not is called Supreme Origin.</gloss>
The Supreme Simplicity produced Metal. <gloss>The state where substance existed but physical bodies did not is called Supreme Simplicity.</gloss>
The Supreme Ultimate produced Earth. <gloss>The state where substance was fully formed is called the Supreme Ultimate.</gloss>

The five grains were not yet cultivated; people drank the blood of animals and ate their raw fur and feathers. Their reputations were vast and drifting, and their joy was simple and boundless. # Discourse on the Associations of the Ten Heavenly Stems and Twelve Earthly Branches

**Question:** At that time, when Da Rao Shi <note>A minister of the Yellow Emperor credited with inventing the sexagenary cycle of timekeeping</note> first created the *Jiazi* <term>Jiazi: The sexagenary cycle, a system of 60 terms used to number years, months, days, and hours in traditional Chinese chronometry</term>, how did he determine the designations for Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth <term>Wuxing: The Five Elements or Phases that represent the fundamental forces of the universe</term>?

**Answer:** In the beginning, the Ten Primordials—the Ten Heavenly Stems—were assigned as follows: *Jia* and *Yi* belong to Wood; *Bing* and *Ding* belong to Fire; *Wu* and *Ji* belong to Earth; *Geng* and *Xin* belong to Metal; and *Ren* and *Gui* belong to Water. These are the associations of the Ten Heavenly Stems.

As for the Earthly Branches: *Yin* and *Mao* belong to Wood; *Si* and *Wu* belong to Fire; *Shen* and *You* belong to Metal; *Hai* and *Zi* belong to Water; and *Chen*, *Xu*, *Chou*, and *Wei* belong to Earth. These are the associations of the Twelve Earthly Branches.

**Further Question:** Since the Sages placed the Wood elements of *Jia, Yi, Yin,* and *Mao* in the East; the Fire elements of *Bing, Ding, Si,* and *Wu* in the South; the Metal elements of *Geng, Xin, Shen,* and *You* in the West; the Water elements of *Ren, Gui, Hai,* and *Zi* in the North; the Earth elements of *Wu* and *Ji* in the Center; and the branches *Chen, Xu, Chou,* and *Wei* scattered across the Four Corners <term>Siwei: The four intermediate directions—Northeast, Southeast, Northwest, and Southwest—which serve as transitions between the primary compass points</term>—what is the reasoning behind this?

**Answer:** It is said that in the East, there is the deity Tai Hao <note>original: "Taihao," the "Great Brilliant One," often identified with Fuxi, the cultural hero who created the Eight Trigrams and taught humanity how to fish and hunt</note>. He rides upon the *Zhen* <note>The trigram in the I Ching representing Thunder, signifying the spark of life and the beginning of movement</note> and holds the compass. He governs the Spring and presides over benevolence, gentle winds, and harmonious energy. This is the season when all living things must emerge and grow; therefore, Wood resides here. This is why *Jia, Yi, Yin,* and *Mao* are categorized together in the East.

In the South, there is the deity Yan Di <term>The Flame Emperor: A legendary ruler and divine farmer in Chinese mythology who taught the art of agriculture and herbal medicine</term>. He rides upon the *Li* <note>The trigram in the I Ching representing Fire, light, and clarity</note> and holds the scales. He governs the Summer and presides over intense heat and scorching energy. This is the season when all living things reach full growth and nourishment; therefore, Fire resides here. This is why *Bing, Ding, Si,* and *Wu* are categorized together in the South.

### The Centrality of the Earth Element

The text says: Thus, Wood, Fire, Metal, and Water occupy the four cardinal directions and follow the four seasons. Only the Earth stems, *Wu* and *Ji*, reside in the center, while the Earth branches—*Chen, Xu, Chou,* and *Wei*—are scattered across the "Four Dimensions" <term>Siwei: The four intermediate directions (Northeast, Southeast, Southwest, Northwest) which act as transitions between seasons</term>. Why is this?

The answer is: In the Center, there is the god, the Yellow Emperor <note>Huangdi, the most celebrated of the Five Emperors and the progenitor of Chinese civilization</note>, who rides upon the *Kun* trigram <term>Kun: The trigram representing Earth and the receptive principle</term>. He holds the marking line <note>original: "繩" (sheng) — a carpenter's plumb line or cord used for measurement and rectification</note> and governs the Earth below. Furthermore, Wood, Fire, Metal, and Water can none of them exist without Earth. Therefore, the stems *Wu* and *Ji* are placed in the center, and the branches *Chen, Xu, Chou,* and *Wei* are scattered among the Four Dimensions, so that each element may obtain its necessary support.

These "Three Powers" <term>Sancai: The triad of Heaven, Earth, and Humanity</term> cannot be lacking.
