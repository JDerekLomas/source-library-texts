There are also prosperous lands and fortified cities located on high, treacherous terrain. Situations such as these occur frequently in the world. If one only begins to plan when already thirsty, how could it ever be in time? The best strategy is to constantly store water from rain and snow; this can be used to prevent such desperate straits. ## I. Preparation
<term>ju</term> <note>original: "具". The first of the nine essential matters, referring to the gathering and sorting of materials.</note>

The materials for a **reservoir** <term>shuiku</term> are six in total, used for construction, for covering, and for plastering. There are three types of materials used for construction and covering: **square stones** (ashlar), **bricks** <term>zhuanpi</term>, and **stone pebbles** (cobbles). There are three types of materials used for plastering: **lime** <term>shihui</term>, **sand**, and **crushed tile shards**. 

When the three plastering materials are combined, it is called **"Three-Component Mortar"** <term>sanhe zhi hui</term>. If the sand or the tile shards are omitted, it is called "Two-Component Mortar."

The limestone used for firing into lime may be either blue or white; it should have a dense texture and a lustrous appearance. If the texture is porous, the lime will not be "sticky" <note>original: "昵" (ni). Refers to the adhesive quality or plasticity of the mortar</note> and will fail to adhere properly. 

In the firing process, one may use wood or coal. The fire must not be extinguished for two and a half days for the transformation to be complete. 

**The Method for Testing the Lime:**
First, take a single stone and weigh it. Place it among the other stones to be fired. Once the process is finished and the stone is removed, weigh it again. The stone should have lost one-third of its original weight <note>This weight loss occurs because the intense heat drives off carbon dioxide from the limestone (calcium carbonate), converting it into quicklime (calcium oxide).</note>.

## First: The Boiling Test

Take clear water and place it in a clean vessel to boil thoroughly. Pour the boiled water into a white porcelain bowl and wait for it to settle. If sand or earth appears at the bottom, the quality of this water is poor. High-quality water leaves no dregs or sediment. Furthermore, good water makes food easy to cook and soften.

## Second: The Sun Test

Place clear water in a white porcelain vessel and set it under the sun, allowing the sunlight to shine directly into the water. Look into the path of the light; if there are particles of dust or fine, thread-like fibers resembling drifting vapors, the "Qi" <note>original: "氣" (qi); here referring to the internal essence or vital energy of the water, which indicates its purity</note> of this water is bad. The essence of good water is perfectly clear and transparent, reaching all the way to the bottom without obstruction.

### Third: The Taste Test

Water is a fundamental element <note>original: "元行" (yuanxing); referring to the Five Phases or Elements of traditional Chinese cosmology. As a primary element, water in its purest state is believed to be neutral.</note>. Fundamental elements possess no flavor. That which has no flavor is "true water." Any flavor present is the result of external substances combining with it. When testing water, "blandness" <note>referring to a lack of distinct mineral or organic flavor</note> is the primary standard. Water with a pleasant taste is secondary, while water with a foul taste is considered inferior.

### Fourth: The Weight Test

When there are various sources of water and you wish to distinguish the superior from the inferior, take a single vessel and weigh each sample by pouring them into it one after another. The lightest water is the highest quality <note>Historically, "light" water was associated with purity and health, whereas "heavy" water was thought to contain excessive minerals or salts that could lead to illness.</note>.

### Fifth: The Paper and Silk Test

Another method: Use paper or a type of silk fabric that is a lustrous, pure white. Dip the material into the water and then allow it to dry. The water that leaves no visible trace or stain is of the highest quality.

### 5. Water-Powered Multiple Mill

To build this, one must separately construct a **vertical wheel** <term>豎輪 (shulun): a vertical water wheel, often undershot or breastshot, used to capture the kinetic energy of flowing water.</term> to be struck by the water. Located above is a **horizontal wheel** <term>臥輪 (wolun): a horizontal gear or turbine-like wheel that sits parallel to the ground.</term> driving one millstone. At the end of its shaft is another wheel, fitted with **wooden teeth** <note>original: "木齒" (muchi); these function as gear teeth to transfer power between perpendicular shafts.</note> around its circumference to drive a second millstone on the side. 

Once the water is diverted and poured into the flume to strike the water wheel, both the upper and the side millstones rotate together with the wheel. This hydraulic machine is ingeniously distinct and superior to a single mill, as this vertical wheel is connected to two mills simultaneously.

Furthermore, there is a version involving **two boats** placed side-by-side. Four pillars are erected upon them, with a roof made of thatch and bamboo. A millstone is placed on each boat. These are moored with **ropes and cables** in the middle of a swift current. At the bows of the boats, wooden boards are inserted diagonally to channel the water toward the wheel. **Iron anchors** <term>鐵爪 (tiezhua): literally "iron claws"; heavy multi-pronged anchors used to stabilize the floating mills against the current.</term> are cast to ensure the boats do not drift or tilt. 

The water strikes the vertical wheel, and its axle extends the full length across the boats to drive both mills. Should a flood occur, the boats can be moved closer to the shore or relocated as needed. Compared to other designs, this is known as a **"flexible mill"** <note>original: "活法磨" (huofamo); referring to its portability and adaptability to changing water levels.</note>. Those who seek to promote the public interest should evaluate and utilize this design.
