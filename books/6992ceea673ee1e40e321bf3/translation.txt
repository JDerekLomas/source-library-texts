Gui E <note>A prominent Ming Dynasty official and scholar known for his works on geography and administration.</note> said: Guangdong is the ancient land of the Hundred Yue <note>A collective term for various non-Han ethnic groups who inhabited Southern China and Northern Vietnam in antiquity.</note>. From Nanxiong <gloss>which connects south to Lianzhou; Nanxiong and Lianzhou are both prefectural seats</gloss>, one can head toward the regions of Jing and Wu <gloss>referring to the provinces of Huguang and Jiangxi</gloss>. While these goods were originally intended for the livelihood of the local people, imperial eunuchs <note>High-ranking palace officials sent by the Emperor to supervise trade or collect tribute</note> have arrived several times to claim them.

# Wuzhou Prefecture

According to the "Tribute of Yu," this region belongs to the territory of Jingzhou <note>The "Tribute of Yu" (Yu Gong) is a chapter in the *Book of Documents* that describes the Nine Provinces of ancient China. Jingzhou was one of these traditional divisions.</note>. In celestial astrology, it falls under the "Field Allocation" of the Ox and Maiden lunar mansions <note>original: "Niu Nü Fenye." Traditional Chinese geography linked specific regions on earth to constellations in the sky to interpret omens and fate.</note>. It governs one sub-prefecture and nine counties. 

Its borders extend:
* East to the boundary of Fengchuan County in Zhaoqing Prefecture, Guangdong, a distance of 30 *li* <term>li: a traditional Chinese unit of distance, approximately 500 meters or 1/3 of a mile.</term>.
* West to the boundary of Nanping County in Xunzhou Prefecture, a distance of 245 *li*.
* South to the boundary of Xinyi County in Gaozhou Prefecture, Guangdong, a distance of 180 *li*.
* North to the boundary of He County in Pingle Prefecture, a distance of 270 *li*.

From the prefectural seat, the distance to the Capital <note>Referring to Beijing, the northern capital of the Ming dynasty.</note> is 8,262 *li*, with a travel allowance of 126 days. The distance to Nanjing <note>The secondary southern capital of the Ming dynasty.</note> is 5,095 *li*.
