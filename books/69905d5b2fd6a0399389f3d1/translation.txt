## FOREWORD

This small book (the result of reflection and experience) is not intended to be a complete study <note>original: "exhaustive treatise"</note> of the much-written-about subject of the power of thought. It is meant to suggest ideas rather than provide a detailed explanation. Its goal is to encourage men and women to discover and understand the truth that—

> “They themselves are the makers of themselves”

by virtue of the thoughts they choose and encourage. The mind is the master weaver of both the inner garment of character and the outer garment of circumstance. Though people may have woven their lives in ignorance and pain until now <note>original: "hitherto"</note>, they may now weave in clarity <note>original: "enlightenment"</note> and happiness.

James Allen

*Broad Park Avenue,*
*Ilfracombe,*
*England.*

## THOUGHT AND CHARACTER

THE <term>aphorism</term> <note>aphorism: a short, concise statement expressing a general truth or principle</note>, “As a man <note>original: "thinketh"</note> thinks in his heart, so is he,” not only embraces the whole of a man’s being, but is so comprehensive that it reaches out to every condition and circumstance of his life. A man is literally what *he thinks*, his character being the complete sum of all his thoughts.

Just as a plant springs from—and could not exist without—the seed, so every act of a man springs from the hidden seeds of thought, and could not have appeared without them. This applies equally to those acts called <term>spontaneous</term> <note>spontaneous: performed or occurring as a result of a sudden inner impulse, without premeditation</note> and <term>unpremeditated</term> <note>unpremeditated: not thought out or planned beforehand</note> as it does to those which are deliberately executed.

Action is the blossom of thought, and joy and suffering are its fruits; in this way, a man gathers <note>original: "garner"</note> the sweet and bitter <term>fruitage</term> <note>fruitage: the product or result of an action, much like a harvest of fruit</note> of his own <term>husbandry</term> <note>husbandry: the management and cultivation of resources, like a farmer tending to his crops</note>.

## EFFECT OF THOUGHT ON CIRCUMSTANCES

[ 11 ]

A person’s mind can be compared to a garden, which may be intelligently cultivated or allowed to grow wild. But whether it is cultivated or neglected, it must, and will, produce growth. If no useful seeds are planted in it, then an abundance of useless weed seeds will fall into it and will continue to produce more of their own kind.

Just as a gardener tends to their plot—keeping it free from weeds and growing the flowers and fruits they desire—so too can a person tend the garden of their mind. They can weed out all wrong, useless, and impure thoughts while cultivating the "flowers and fruits" of right, useful, and pure thoughts toward perfection. By following this process, a person eventually discovers that they are the master gardener of their soul and the director of their own life. They also reveal the laws of thought within themselves and understand, with ever-increasing accuracy, how the forces of thought and elements of the mind operate in shaping their character, circumstances, and destiny.
