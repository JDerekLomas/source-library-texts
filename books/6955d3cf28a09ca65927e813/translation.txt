<margin>These are two of the most important devotional poems in the Tamil Sri Vaishnava tradition, typically recited during the auspicious month of Margazhi (December–January).</margin>

<term>Tiruppalli Eluchi</term> <note>Literally "The Sacred Awakening from the Couch." A genre of hymns sung to wake a deity—in this case, Lord Ranganatha of Srirangam—symbolizing the awakening of the soul's devotion.</note>

<term>Tiruppavai</term> <note>A collection of thirty stanzas credited to the female poet-saint Andal. It describes a young woman's vow (pavai) to reach the Divine, blending pastoral imagery with deep spiritual longing.</note>

->TO<-

## The Right Honourable

# The Lord Viscount Shannon.

->MY LORD,<-

Such was the merit of the Honourable Mr. *Boyle*, such were his abilities and public spirit, that, perhaps, no man ever deserved a greater, or bore a fairer character. The honor he reflects upon his noble family and your Lordship is worthy of the honor he derives from it. Never were a polite education and a philosophical genius improved more to the advantage of mankind, to private satisfaction, and to true glory, than by the great Mr. *Boyle*. He stands as a noble example for the imitation of men of plentiful fortunes and a philosophical taste <note>In this context, "philosophical" refers to "natural philosophy," what we now call science.</note> in studying their own and the public advantage.

# The DEDICATION.

And from where do such bright examples more frequently arise, or from where can they with more justice be expected, than from your Lordship's noble family? Your family inherits the virtues that have rendered the name of Mr. Boyle <note>Robert Boyle (1627–1691), a founding figure of modern chemistry and a leader of the Scientific Revolution.</note> so deservedly famous.

The two former volumes of the abridgment of his incomparable philosophical writings were put under the protection of very able judges. This third volume now begs your Lordship's acceptance, so that the entire work may be submitted to those who know how to set a just value upon philosophical merit, and who have a natural and tender regard for the original author.

I am,

->May it please your Lordship,<-

->Your Lordship's most humble,<-

->most obedient,<-

->And most devoted Servant,<-

->Peter Shaw.<- <note>Peter Shaw (1694–1763) was a physician and chemist who popularized Boyle’s work through these abridgments.</note>
