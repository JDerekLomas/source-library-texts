*<term>Occultism</term> <note>The study of hidden or "occult" spiritual realities</note> has, for several years now, conquered an important place in the minds of many contemporary researchers. From all of this emerges a particular doctrine which, curiously enough, can be very well allied with our contemporary scientific theories and, what is more, can help science to clear away some of the chaos of the still-unexplained facts of Nature.*

->THE TRI-UNITY — CORRESPONDENCES AND ANALOGY — THE ASTRAL.<-

History records that the greatest thinkers of antiquity born in our West went to complete their education in the Egyptian mysteries.

The Science taught by the guardians of these mysteries is known by various names: Occult Science, Hermeticism, Magic, Occultism, Esotericism, etc., etc.

Identical in its principles everywhere, this code of instruction constitutes the traditional Science of the Magi, which we generally call: *Occultism*. This science embraced the theory and practice of a great number of phenomena, of which only a small part today constitutes the domain of magnetism or so-called spiritist evocations. These practices, contained within the study of <term>Psychurgy</term> <note>The art of soul-manipulation or directing psychic forces</note>, formed—let us note well—only a small part of Occult Science, which further comprised three great divisions: <term>Theurgy</term> <note>Divine work; the practice of rituals intended to invoke the presence of gods or spirits</note>, <term>Magic</term>, and <term>Alchemy</term>.

The study of Occultism is vital from two points of view: it illuminates the past in an entirely new light and allows the historian to take up antiquity in a form still little known. On the other hand, this study presents the contemporary experimentalist with a synthetic system of affirmations to be verified by science, and ideas regarding forces that are still little known—forces of Nature or of Man to be verified by observation.

The use of <term>analogy</term> <note>The principle of "as above, so below," suggesting a structural relationship between different levels of reality</note>, the characteristic method of occultism, and its application to our contemporary sciences or our modern conceptions of Art and Sociology, allows an entirely new light to be shed on the most apparently insoluble problems.

## CHAPTER ONE

### § 1. — THE MICROCOSM OR MAN

**R**OTHING appears more complicated at first glance than the human being. How can one analyze all the details of the anatomical and physiological makeup of this being, without even mentioning his psychological makeup?

Esotericism <note>The study of hidden or inner knowledge, as opposed to "exoteric" or public knowledge.</note> seeks synthesis everywhere and leaves the study of details to the powerful efforts of the analytical sciences. Let us see if it is possible to synthetically determine the principles constituting the human being.

Generally, all the organs constituting this human being appear to us in a full period of action. All of this functions, stirs, and manifests to us under a thousand aspects, and it is only with the greatest difficulty that one can determine the few causes through the multiplicity of effects.

But now evening has come; the limbs give way, the eyes close, the external world no longer has any influence on the human being, and he himself no longer has any influence on the external world: he sleeps. Let us take advantage of this sleep to begin our study.

Man sleeps, and yet his arteries beat, his heart functions, and the blood circulates; his digestive organs continue their work, and his lungs inhale and exhale the vivifying air rhythmically. During this sleep, what we call "man" is capable of neither movement, nor sensation, nor thought; he can neither love, nor hate, nor be happy, nor

But the man awakens. Something more is added to the three previous forces. The limbs, which were at rest, begin to move; the face becomes animated, and the eyes open; the human being who was lying down stands up and speaks. A new life is about to begin, while the organic life continues its action mechanically.

The principle that has just appeared differs essentially from the previous principles: it has its own specific organs of action in the body (striated fiber organs <note>These are voluntary muscles, such as those in the arms and legs, which we control consciously</note>); it has a special nervous system; it uses the body as a worker uses a tool, or as a mechanic uses a locomotive: it governs all those centers and peripheral organs that were at rest just a moment ago. This principle, we call the **Conscious Spirit**.

Let us return to our artist and his statuette.

Suppose that the matter, overcome by labor, has yielded to the impulses of the hand that kneads it, and that the statuette is finished.

What, in short, is this statuette? It is a physical image of the idea that the artist has in his brain. The hand has performed the office of a mold in which the matter has been shaped; and this is so true that, if an accident breaks the clay statuette, the artist will find the original form still existing in his brain and will be able to remake a new statuette—a more or less perfect image of the idea that serves as the model.

There exists, however, a means of preventing the loss of the statuette as soon as it is finished: that is to cast a mold of this statuette. Through the mold, one obtains a negative of the thing to be reproduced, such that the matter which comes out of the mold will always manifest the primitive form, without the artist ever having to intervene.

This is why we strongly advise all our readers who are not yet familiar with these questions to first study the Spiritist <note>Spiritism (French: "spiritisme") refers specifically to the movement codified by Allan Kardec, which emphasizes communication with spirits and reincarnation.</note> theory and to practice Spiritism by means of all the mediums they may have at their disposal.

And if Spiritism even seems to them to be the total expression of truth—if this essentially consoling doctrine suffices for their aspirations—let them take care not to seek anything else.

Spiritism teaches, in effect, the ternary constitution <note>The "threefold" nature of man, usually defined in these circles as Body, Soul (or Perispirit), and Spirit.</note> of the human being; the state of the Spirit in the astral plane is well described by the doctrine of "erraticity" <note>A term used in Spiritism to describe the state of souls between incarnations.</note>; the law of reincarnation with all its social consequences is well explained; and a member of the ancient Hermetic university of Egypt would recognize in this simple and consoling doctrine the preliminaries of all initiation.

The contemporary philosopher would search in vain, it is true, within Spiritism for a theodicy <note>A vindication of divine goodness in the face of evil.</note>, a cosmogony <note>A theory regarding the origin of the universe.</note>, or even an original metaphysics; but Spiritism affects such an intense love for experimentation and such a contempt for all metaphysical doctrine—even scientific ones—that the philosopher has nothing to say.

Therefore, let us repeat it again: always begin with Spiritism, and if this doctrine fully answers your aspirations, stay there. We are not sectarians claiming exclusive possession of the truth; we are independent seekers, and every sincere conviction deserves our respect.

If, however, the constant action of "Spirits" in the production of these phenomena does not seem as obvious to you as people claim; if you notice close analogies between the communications obtained and the intellect of the medium; if your studies, conducted according to the principles of positivism <note>A philosophical system that recognizes only that which can be scientifically verified or logically proved.</note>, lead you to study the relationship between hypnotism and the Spiritist facts you observe—then approach occultism, and take account of the theories it puts forward for the explanation of these still-strange facts.

### THE "THEOSOPHICAL SOCIETY"

If we strongly advise our readers to begin their studies without fear through spiritualist phenomena and theories, it is because they will find themselves in the presence of researchers whose sincerity generally cannot be doubted.

# THE INDEPENDENT GROUP FOR ESOTERIC STUDIES

Nearly three years ago (November 1889), the Independent Group for Esoteric Studies was founded. The goal was as follows:

1. The impartial study—outside of any academy or clericalism <note>The author emphasizes independence from both official scientific institutions and established religious authorities.</note>—of the scientific, artistic, and social data hidden at the heart of all symbolisms, all cults, and all traditions.

2. The scientific study, through experimentation and observation, of the forces of nature and man that are still unknown (spiritist, hypnotic, magical, and theurgic <note>Theurgy: a form of high magic intended to invoke divine or celestial powers.</note> phenomena).

3. The grouping of all scattered elements with a view to the struggle against the despairing doctrines of materialism and atheism.

No entry fee and no dues are requested from members. Subscribers to one of the journals published by the Group are entitled to join the study circles upon their request.

At the present time (1892), the Group, originally founded in a small office, possesses in Paris, at 29 RUE DE TRÉVISE, a lecture hall and a reading room annexed to a bookstore, the *Bookstore of the Marvelous* <note>original: "Librairie du Merveilleux"</note>, specially dedicated to the sale and publishing of spiritualist works. Furthermore, the Group counts 96 experimental groups, branches, and correspondents, both in Paris and in the provinces and abroad. It is the only spiritualist society in France that has been able to establish such a regularly organized grouping.

Work continues in Paris within commissions (closed study groups), each dealing with a specific question.

Reports are published in the *Veil of Isis* <note>original: "Voile d'Isis"</note>, the weekly organ of the Group.

In addition to this *Veil of Isis* (Editor-in-chief: Julien Lejay; editorial secretary: L. Mauchel), the Group possesses *Initiation* <note>original: "l'Initiation"</note>, a monthly review directed by Papus <note>The pseudonym of Gérard Encausse, a highly influential French occultist.</note>; *Psyche*, a monthly review of Art and literature (Editor-in-chief: Emile Michelet; editorial secretary: Augustin Chaboseau); and *The Light of Paris*, a weekly (director: Miss A. de Wolska).

# CONCLUSION

In recent times, occultism <note>The study of hidden or supernatural powers and the laws governing the universe.</note> has been the target of passionate attacks both in France and abroad. Certain authors, who are either poorly educated or ill-informed, have sought to deny the antiquity and the unchanging nature of the esoteric tradition throughout the ages; others, relying on secondary details, have sought to find contradictions by looking at different schools of thought.

However, we have made every effort to abandon the polemics so dear to superficial minds. We therefore refer the aforementioned authors to the three fundamental points of **Occult Science: Tri-Unity, Analogy, and the Invisible World**, and to the citations taken from the most diverse eras and from the most varied authors, from the *Zend Avesta* <note>The primary collection of religious texts of Zoroastrianism.</note> to *Wronski* <note>Józef Maria Hoene-Wroński (1776–1853), a Polish messianic philosopher and mathematician.</note>. This response, in fact, will be worth more than all the polemics and discussions regarding the relationships that unite contemporary Occultism to the ancient initiations.

On the other hand, it has been believed that our century held the monopoly on the theories and practices uniting visible beings to the invisible world.

The theory of modern phenomena—such as suggestion, incarnations, materializations, and intelligent responses through raps <note>A reference to "spirit rapping" or table-turning common in 19th-century spiritualism.</note>, etc., etc.—which was already provided in the 16th century, will sufficiently refute this assertion.

Finally, the return to the serious study of extraordinary facts relating to this order of ideas, and the search for theories that are more scientific than sentimental, will lead the researcher, we are convinced, to take a greater interest in this old "Science of the Magi" <note>A term for the ancient wisdom-tradition of the Zoroastrian priests or "Magians," often used to describe high magic.</note> which is so little known.

To all of you who, trusting in the future, seek an ideal other than the gold coin; to all of you who, tired of positivism <note>A philosophical system that recognizes only that which can be scientifically verified or logically proved, rejecting metaphysics.</note> as I once was, believe in the omnipotence of human Reason assisted by Intuition; to you, noble artists, young scientists, and future leaders of men, I make an appeal in the name of Morality which is fading, of Science which is misunderstood, and of the Ideal which is being materialized. Let us react against the narrow conceptions of materialism and clericalism <note>The exercise of excessive political power by the clergy.</note>; let us reflect on the profound transformation taking place in our societies and let us know how—if the events being prepared are to be considerable—to raise our souls to the height of such events.
