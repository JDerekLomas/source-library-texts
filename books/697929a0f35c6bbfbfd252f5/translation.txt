# TO THE READER.

You have here, friend reader, that most noble part of medicine, in which no bitter juice is painfully forced upon the patient, nor does a crude mixture of substances serve as a remedy for the human body. Rather, its purer and more secret power—like a little soul <term>animula: a "soul-let" or quintessence, the distilled essence of a substance</term> drawn forth from materials by art—winds itself into our spirits and makes life more vigorous. 

This matter was truly unknown for a long time to the common "little doctors" <note>mediculis: a slightly derogatory term for unlearned physicians</note> of our age, and even longer desired by the common people who stood in need of its help. Although first discovered by the most illustrious physicians and developed through laborious processes, this medicine was once familiar only to princes, Caesars, kings, and dukes through the industry of their personal doctors. Now, by the kindness of the Great God, Ulstadius <note>Philip Ulstad, a 16th-century Nuremberg physician and alchemist</note> has taken care through this work that it should become both healthful and available to the common people. 

Let others praise the noble help of Theriac <note>A famous ancient "universal" antidote made of dozens of ingredients, including viper flesh</note> against the most cruel sicknesses; let others cry out with wonderful proclamations for the medicine of Mithridates <note>Mithridatium, a complex preventive antidote named after King Mithridates VI of Pontus</note> as "protective remedies" <note>original Greek: ἀλεξητήρια (alexētēria), meaning substances that ward off poison or disease</note> against a harvest of afflictions. To be sure, these are never praised enough for their own abilities; but because they are composed everywhere of crude, heavy matter, they drive away no diseases other than those of the body. 

But who will not clearly approve of this work? For it is most effective not only for driving away the diseases of the body, but once these are driven out, it is equally capable of increasing the spirit and wonderfully prolonging life, whereby...

### Sends many greetings. <note>original: *S. D. P.*, abbreviation for *Salutem Dicit Plurimam*</note>

IF you are in good health, it is well; I indeed, by the 
kindness of God, am in the best of health, Felix, most 
happy <note>A play on words; the name *Felix* means "happy" or "lucky" in Latin</note> of all men. I have always intended in my mind
what I might be able to return to you for the count-
less favors you have bestowed upon me, for I know that
ingratitude is detested by all more than any other vice. 
Therefore, it came to mind to dedicate to you this 
small gift of literature, such as it is—gathered by my 
own diligence from the reading of various authors—
and to bring it into the light under the protection of 
your name; I have no doubt that you will receive it 
with a favorable mind. Although it is quite brief 
and succinct, I nevertheless do not doubt that it will 
delight you greatly, both because it is a repository, as 
it were, of new and old medicines, and because it 
contains many philosophical, medical, and alchemical 
matters written very obscurely by John of Rupescissa, 
Raymond Lull, Arnald of Villanova, and Albertus 
Magnus—those supreme seekers of the secret things 
of Nature <note>These four men were the most famous medieval authorities on alchemy and the "quintessence" or distilled alcohol</note>. Indeed, they wrote so obscurely that 
these things can be understood by few, or rather almost 
none, in our age. This must be because people have, 
up until now, seen nothing or very little of the old 
teachings and books. For these works seem to have 
come into the hands of only a few...
