When the WALDENSIANS <note>The Waldensians are a medieval Christian movement that faced severe persecution and eventually joined the Protestant Reformation.</note>, in the thirty-first year of this current century <note>1731</note>, were supported in their oppression by the generous charity of the Sovereigns of our Fatherland, and a General Collection was held in our Provinces for their benefit, many were eager to know these Ancient Confessors of the Faith more closely. This moved me to investigate their origins and further fates; from this was born the description of *their history until the end of the 12th Christian century.* The volume which contained this report I published in the year 1732: with the intention, if I did not lack time, inclination, and ability, to continue that history until the *Reformation*, and subsequently until our own *age*. This was the reason why I presented the published work in the form of a *First Volume*;

<header>PREFACE CONCERNING</header>

the; but it has since often troubled me that I had not let this *literary work* <note>original: "Pennevrugt," literally "fruit of the pen."</note> stand on its own: because not long after publication, through an excess of worldly and ecclesiastical duties, and through a lack of sufficient documents, I began to see that such a task was too extensive <note>original: "van te langen adem," literally "of too long a breath."</note>. I would have continued it, however, had the necessary supply of books and writings relative to this been brought to me (as I had hoped), or if they had been obtainable by me. Two *Waldensian candidates* <note>Student ministers who have completed their theological studies but are not yet ordained.</note>, who had studied here in this country (if my memory does not fail me), did me the favor, truth be told, between the appearance of my *History* and the year 1738, after a mutual conversation, of giving me a list of the number of their congregations and their members; the former of which then numbered thirteen (in the three valleys of *Lucerne*, *Perosa*, and *St. Martin*) <note>These are the three "Waldensian Valleys" in the Piedmont region of the Italian Alps.</note>, and the latter, all counted together, did not amount to 8,000 (*). I remember nothing further of this

(*) In the *Valley of Lucerne*: they had them at *Angrogna* with 1,000, at *San Giovanni* <note>Dutch: "St. Jean."</note> with 1,300, at *Torre Pellice* <note>Dutch: "La Tour."</note> with 1,100, at *Villar Pellice* with 1,200, at *Bobbio Pellice* <note>Dutch: "Bobi."</note> with 1,200, at *Prarostino* & *Roccapiatta* with 900, and at *Rorà* with 200 members. In the *Perosa Valley* there were congregations: at *San Germano*, at *Pramollo*, and at *Pomaretto*, for which the number of members was not stated. In the *Valley of St. Martin*, they had them at *Villasecca* <note>Dutch: "Ville Seche."</note> with 500 members; also at *Maneille* & *Macel*, and at *Prali* & *Rodoretto*, without mention of the total number of members.
