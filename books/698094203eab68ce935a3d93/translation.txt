## ¶ To the excellent man, Lord Hieronymus Ebner, Senator of Nuremberg. Johann von Staupitz, servant of God and of the reformed Augustinian family <note>The "Augustinian family" refers to the Order of Hermits of St. Augustine, specifically the observant branch to which both Staupitz and Martin Luther belonged.</note>—would that he were not useless—wishes happiness and that he may rule healthfully in the fear of God.

¶ My love, which I have held for many years for your illustrious homeland, O upright man, has often instilled in me a desire to serve the people of Nuremberg at some time. And finally, in this sacred Advent <note>The liturgical season of preparation before Christmas.</note>, it was brought about by God's disposition that by preaching Christ to them, I sought to clarify the fulfillment of eternal predestination. Compelled by the devotion of my listeners, though somewhat unwillingly, I have compiled what I preached into one little book, so that the pious reader—whom I would ask to do this diligently—might more freely correct it and return me to the truth if he finds anything said in error or without sufficient counsel.

During my preaching, many people, and indeed the most honorable citizens, introduced themselves into my friendship, as they are most refined; their friendship cannot be anything but welcome and pleasant to me. But the brotherhood I have established with you gave me the reason to dedicate these nocturnal labors <note>original: "lucubrationes." ## 55
¶ Firstly, the contract between Christ and the Christian is far different from that between a husband and a wife. A contract between husband and wife requires that each gives themselves to the other; that is, that the man belongs to the woman and the woman to the man through free consent. The husband has power over the woman’s body, and the woman over the husband’s body, but not over the spirit. He should hold her not as a slave, but as a partner <note>original: 'sociam' — a companion or peer</note> and a helpmate for procreation. Therefore, the power of spouses over one another is limited, not absolute.

## 56
¶ The contract between Christ and the Church is consummated and is of this nature: "I take you as my own; I take you for myself; I take you into myself." And conversely, the Church (or the soul) says to Christ: "I take You as my own; I take You for myself; I take You into myself." Thus, Christ may say: "The Christian is mine; the Christian is for me; the Christian is I." And the bride says: "Christ is mine; Christ is for me; Christ is I."

## 57
¶ The first of these statements is common to our human marriages, but the other two transcend it. By virtue of the first, the Christian possesses whatever a bridegroom owes a bride by the right of marriage. By virtue of the second, the Christian possesses all the benefits that can come to them from Christ, with absolutely nothing excluded. By virtue of the third, the Christian possesses Christ just as they possess themselves.
