Suddenly, I have felt the urge to publish the extremely important and quite indispensable treatise *on the fire of the magi* <note>original Latin: *de igne magorum*</note> in exactly the same manner as I did the *Philosophical Furnace*. Admittedly, this was a most tedious and laborious task, which truly could not be performed with haste but required great attention, though some might perhaps consider it an easy matter. One had to take great care that not a single little word escaped which served to clarify the subject, and that no superfluous word was inserted in its place that might have broken the continuity.

I can therefore assert with confidence that I have examined and tested every single word, without exception, to prevent even the slightest corruption—which would be more than mere arrogance. Hardly anyone from the <term>golden lineage</term> <note>a term used by alchemists to refer to the "Sons of Art" or the enlightened community of practitioners</note> will discover an error when comparing this new edition with the old ones, unless they were to force useless wordplay upon it, or perhaps take offense at the fact that many old words are still retained within the context, which I have left standing by deliberate design. 

In a word, the reader need not fear that through my laborious <term>remelting</term> <note>original: *Umschmelzung*; the editor uses a metallurgical metaphor to describe the process of refining and "re-casting" the old, crude text into a modern, polished form</note>—which many have long desired—they might lose one <term>secret</term> <note>original: *Geheimniß*; referring to the hidden alchemical truths or "arcana" within the text</note> or another; not at all!
