Then the <term>calumny</term> <note>Slander or malicious misrepresentation.</note>, scolding, reviling, and condemning will begin, and poor **Theophrastus** <note>Paracelsus.</note> will have to bear the brunt of it, as if he had kept the truth hidden. I ask these critics that they might consider that **Theophrastus** speaks according to the custom of the <term>Cabalists</term> <note>Practitioners of a mystical tradition; here referring to the use of coded language and symbols.</note>, the natural magicians <note>original: "magorum naturalium"</note>, and all philosophers <note>original: "philosophorum"</note>. He does not always mean what the literal letter indicates, and he desires to have disciples <note>original: "discipulos"</note> for his <term>Arcana</term> <note>Secrets or hidden mysteries, specifically alchemical medicines.</note> who are practiced in those arts which he presents in the *Paragranum* <note>One of Paracelsus’s major works, defining the four pillars of medicine: Philosophy, Astronomy, Alchemy, and Virtue.</note>. From this it follows that whoever knows nothing of the aforementioned arts will accomplish little of value in **Theophrastus’s** <term>Arcana</term>, because he does not understand what he should do or leave undone, or whether he should take "white" or "black." However, that he brought to light the **secrets** <note>original: "secreta"</note> and **mysteries of nature** <note>original: "Arcana naturæ"</note>—as they were in use among the ancient fathers, Jews, and heathens, through which they maintained long life and the body’s health—somewhat **obscurely** <note>original: "obscurè"</note>, so that not every peasant should understand them, but rather **Magically**, so that they remain with those who would be worthy of them: this cannot be held against him by any impartial person.
