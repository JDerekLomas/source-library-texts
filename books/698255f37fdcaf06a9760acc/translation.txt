# THE EDITOR’S INTRODUCTORY PREFACE.

<image-desc>A decorative woodcut drop-cap initial 'C' containing ornate foliage and floral patterns.</image-desc>
When the horrendous plague of the Pestilence was spreading everywhere through a lamented and already sufficiently exhausted Germany, and was laying low in great numbers everywhere the bodies of wretched mortals—already worn out by so many and such great calamities of war—or rather, corpses that were still barely breathing, and when it indeed utterly spurned all medical treatment: I myself, along with others, wished to withdraw for a while from the Academy of the Lindens <note>original: "Academo Tiliarum"; this refers to the University of Leipzig, as "Lipsia" (Leipzig) was traditionally associated with the Slavic word for linden tree.</note> and sought to catch freer and purer air in my native salt-works. <note>original: "patriis Salinis"; Rivinus was born in Halle, a city famous for its salt springs.</note> For thus, with my beloved Ovid <note>original: "NASONE"; Publius Ovidius Naso, the Roman poet.</note> from Book VII of the *Metamorphoses* (the passage where a most accurate description of the pestilence is provided, along with its physical as well as metaphysical causes), I publicly proclaimed:

> While the evil seemed mortal, and the harmful
> Cause of such a great slaughter lay hidden, it was fought with art. <note>original: "arte"; referring to the medical arts or skill.</note>
> The destruction surpassed the aid, which lay defeated.
> Now the healer is gone, and the savage slaughter
