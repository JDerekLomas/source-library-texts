# Preface to the Yangming Interpretive Diagrams

The <term>Grand Historian<gloss>Referring to Sima Qian (c. 145–86 BCE), the author of the *Records of the Grand Historian*.</gloss></term> remarked that practitioners of the School of Yin and Yang are often excessively restricted by taboos and prohibitions. How true these words are! This state of affairs exists because those who study the arts of Yin and Yang <note>original: "阴阳家" (Yinyang jia), referring to practitioners of cosmology, astrology, and divination.</note> do not grasp the underlying logic, and those who compile books on these subjects do not edit or categorize the material correctly. 

If one were to follow every rule presented in these texts, the resulting contradictions would create an unbearable burden of restraint. Yet, if one were to discard these practices entirely, the clear and manifest evidence of good and bad fortune would prove impossible to deny. 

What, then, is the proper course of action? My view is that one must weigh the relative importance, vitality, and decay of these influences to decide what to adopt and what to set aside. Avoid the "great and urgent" influences; ignore the "small and trivial" ones. Follow that which accords with logic, and discard that which contradicts it. It is as simple as that.

For instance, influences such as <term>The Grand Duke Jupiter<gloss>Taisui; a powerful deity/influence corresponding to the planet Jupiter's cycle.</gloss></term>, the "Sword Edge" meeting malevolent stars, or the "Great Monthly Establishment" belong to the "great and urgent" category and ought to be avoided. Conversely, influences like the "Silkworm Room," the "Great Lunar," or "Flowing Wealth" are "small and trivial"; these can be overlooked, as it is not necessary to seek perfect alignment with every single minor factor. 

Principles such as the auspicious or inauspicious positions of the year, the <term>Flying Stars of the Nine Palaces<gloss>Jiugong Feibai; a divination system tracking shifting cosmic energies across a grid.</gloss></term>, the flourishing or decay of <term>Spirits and Malevolent Influences<gloss>Shen-sha; various auspicious and inauspicious stars used in Chinese astrology.</gloss></term>, and the punishments or clashes within the "Hanging Palaces"—all of these are established with profound intent and should be strictly followed. 

However, miscellaneous celestial stars, vulgar and absurd folk sayings, and fabricated rules intended only to make fools of the ignorant—

When discussing the principles of Yin and Yang, once one understands what to adopt and what to discard, one must further apply a "living method" <note>original: "活法" (huofa), referring to flexible application rather than rigid adherence to rules.</note> from within one's own mind. 

Consider, for example, <term>the Metal Deity<gloss>Jinshen; a malevolent astrological influence associated with the element of metal and the season of autumn.</gloss></term>. Its power governs the autumn, and its "birth" <note>In the "Twelve Life Stages" theory of Chinese element cycles.</note> occurs in the <term>Snake<gloss>Si; the sixth of the Twelve Earthly Branches.</gloss></term> stage. During the peak of autumn, or when it occupies the directions of the Snake, Rooster, or Ox, misfortune is surely unavoidable. However, if one acts during the winter or spring—months when the influence lacks vital energy—its power is divided and its momentum lost. In such cases, it may not cause deep harm. 

Based on this, we can see that the "living method" rests with the individual; other matters can be inferred by this same logic. Thus it is said: "Where can one find a person of keen insight to discuss these matters with?" To find a mysterious harmony among the <term>Nine Schools of Thought<gloss>The various philosophical branches of ancient China, including Confucianism, Daoism, and the School of Yin-Yang.</gloss></term> was the subtle intent of the Grand Historian. May those who obtain this book view it in this light; perhaps then it will be of benefit.

My family has loved "geography" <note>here meaning geomancy or Feng Shui</note> for generations and has amassed a vast collection of texts on the subject. Regarding the mnemonics and songs of Yin and Yang, opinions are often divided and confused; one person says "yes" while another says "no." Where is the student to find the truth? 

It came to my great-uncle, the Elder Fuxin. He hailed from a line of scholars and pursued the imperial examinations. During his life, he meticulously researched books on astronomy and geography. His collection was immense and his revisions extensive; he cross-referenced differences and similarities to analyze signs of good and bad fortune.

He categorized them by school and type, and only then did standards begin to emerge. In his later years, he annotated a volume called the *Interpretive Search for the Sun and Light* <note>original: "阳明按索" (Yangming Ansuo), likely the title of this manual.</note>, considering it a family heirloom never to be shown to the unworthy. 

In the turmoil of the <term>Jiawu year<gloss>1354 CE, a period of significant rebellion and warfare at the end of the Yuan Dynasty.</gloss></term>, he preemptively hid the finest of these books in a cellar. ## On Selecting Directions within the Inner Formation

Within these maps, the <term>astral influences<gloss>Xingsha; a collective term for the stars and energetic forces governing a location.</gloss></term> of various directions are color-coded: red characters indicate auspiciousness, while black characters signify malevolence. For example, if you intend to perform work in the **Mao direction** <note>The direction of East (90°), associated with the Rabbit in the Chinese zodiac.</note>:

First, consult the **Earthly Branch Year Map** to see if the **Mao direction** is marked with a red character. Next, check the **Three Yuan White Stars** <note>Favorable flying stars in traditional numerology.</note> and the **Heavenly Stem Year Map**. If the charts show no conflict with the <term>Absolute Malevolence of Yin and Yang<gloss>Yinyang Desha; specific highly dangerous astrological alignments.</gloss></term>, you may proceed. Then, look at both the Branch and Stem formations to determine which specific month the **Mao direction** aligns with a red auspicious star; only then is it considered usable. 

You must also verify which specific year the auspicious star belongs to, as one cannot simply assume a generic "good" star is lucky in every instance. If the Year Map lacks a red character, or if the **Mao direction** shows a black malevolent star and also conflicts with your own <term>natal malevolence<gloss>Mingsha; harmful stars determined by one's specific birth date.</gloss></term>, you must search the Branch and Stem formations for a month where the **Mao direction** *does* have a red auspicious star. In such cases, only small-scale renovations or minor works may be undertaken.

If the **Stem Formation** aligns with "Noble People," "Heavenly or Monthly Virtue," the "Golden Path," or "Stem Virtue," and the **Branch Formation** aligns with the "Three Whites," "Nine Purples," "Sovereign Emperor," "Great Record," or the inner layers of "Monthly Virtue," "Heavenly Way," "Human Way," or "Vital Energy" <note>original: 生炁 (Shengqi)</note>, it is considered highly auspicious. This is especially true if it aligns with a "Special Point."

### On Shifting and Replacing

The <term>Method of the Nine Palaces<gloss>Jiu Gong; a 3x3 grid used in ancient Chinese numerology and astrology to track the movement of cosmic energies.</gloss></term> traces its ancestry to the **Lo Shu** <note>The "Scroll of the River Luo," an ancient magic square where the sum of every row, column, and diagonal is fifteen.</note>; it is the natural principle of Heaven and Earth. By pairing it with the **Three Eras** <note>A 180-year cycle divided into 60-year units.</note> and the **Nine Stars**, and dividing them into the two hidden cycles of <term>Yin and Yang<gloss>The two opposing yet complementary forces of the universe.</gloss></term>, the stars "fly" through the grid in Shifting and Replacing patterns. Through this, fortune and misfortune are fully revealed. 

The classics say: "Shifting the Palaces is the vehicle for the astral influences." All stars and malevolent forces move according to the shifting of these palaces. The creation, destruction, punishment, and clashing of energies are all understood through these shifts. The subtlety of <term>Fixing Directions<gloss>Xiufang; the practice of choosing the right compass direction for building or repairs.</gloss></term> and selection reaches its peak here. The method is as follows: take the **Month Governor** <note>The specific energy governing the month of the intended action.</note> and place it in the Central Palace, then rotate it through the eight directions. Observe

<column-break/>

the arrival of the "Host" and "Guest" stars on the upper and lower levels, noting their mutual creation or destruction and whether they bring luck or harm. Next, calculate the **Replacement Palace**: take the <term>Sexagenary Cycle sign<gloss>Jiazi; the 60-year/day/month cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches.</gloss></term> corresponding to that month, place it in the Central Palace, and distribute it forward through the Nine Palaces. Look for creation or destruction as described above.

For example, in the 4th month of a **Jia-Ji year** <note>Years ending in the Heavenly Stems 'Jia' or 'Ji'.</note>, the Replacement corresponds to the **Ding-Chou** sign. Place this in the center; the hidden **Geng-Chen** sign then reaches the **Gen direction** <note>Northeast</note>. Since Metal and Earth create one another, this is auspicious. 

Furthermore, regarding the shifting and replacement of the Nine Palaces across the Three Eras: suppose in the 3rd month of a **Jia-Zi year** in the **Upper Era**, one works in the **Qian direction** <note>Northwest</note>. The **1-White Star** governs that year; when placed in the center, the **2-Black Star** shifts to the Northwest—this is inauspicious. In that same 3rd month, the **6-White Star** governs the month; this shifts the **7-Red Star** to the Northwest. This is called the <term>Clashing Swords Massacre<gloss>Jiaojian Sha; an alignment of the 6 and 7 stars believed to cause conflict, surgery, or legal trouble.</gloss></term> and is malevolent. 

Next, calculate the Replacement Palace. The 3rd month belongs to the **Xun direction** <note>Southeast</note>; the replacement matches the **5-Yellow Star**. Placing the 5-Yellow in the center and distributing the stars, the **6-White Star** returns to the Northwest direction. This is called **"Returning to the Palace,"** a state where all malevolent influences are suppressed. Generally, an auspicious Shifting Palace is the highest priority, followed by the Replacement Palace. It is even more wonderful if the shifting and replacement stars create each other or the original position. If the shifting is auspicious but the replacement is malevolent, the direction may still be used.

## On the Stars and Malevolent Influences within the Formations

The <term>Two Formations of Stems and Branches<gloss>Zhi Gan Er Ju; the organizational systems based on the Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches used to map time and space.</gloss></term> are each divided into inner and outer layers. In the inner layer, there are twenty-four positions, with each star or <term>malevolent influence<gloss>Xing Sha; literally "Stars and Kills," these are symbolic energies in Chinese astrology that can be auspicious or inauspicious.</gloss></term> occupying one position. In the outer layer, there are eight palaces, with each star or malevolence occupying three positions. For example, in the first month of a *Jia* or *Ji* year, the diagram shows the **Great Month Construction** <note>A powerful monthly astrological deity.</note> in the **Gen Palace** <note>The Northeast direction.</note>; thus, the three positions of *Chou*, *Gen*, and *Yin* are all inauspicious. Other cases follow this logic. Furthermore, regarding the "Shifting Palaces" of the outer layer: items like the *Jia-Zi* sequence and the Nine Stars are written in **large characters** to indicate they are "Shifting Palaces," while those written in *small characters* are "Replacement Palaces." For all stars and malevolences, one must examine the small characters below each position to see which specific year they apply to. For instance, in a "Stem Formation" for the first month of a *Jia* or *Ji* year, if the characters under the Great Month Construction in the Gen Palace say "Jia Year," then all six *Jia* years are inauspicious, while the *Ji* years are not. Whenever it says "Until the preceding [point]," it refers to the Winter...

<column-break/>

In the Two Formations of Stems and Branches, twenty-four positions are distributed among the <term>Eight Palaces<gloss>Ba Gong; the eight directions of the compass associated with the Bagua or Eight Trigrams.</gloss></term>, with each palace governing three positions. For example, the **Qian Palace** governs *Xu* and *Hai*. The **Zi position** <note>North</note> governs *Ren* and *Gui*. Furthermore, in the four "cardinal" palaces—*Zi* (North), *Wu* (South), *Mao* (East), and *You* (West)—the <term>Five Phases<gloss>Wu Xing; the elemental energies of Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water.</gloss></term> are uniform, making them easy to use for construction or planning. For instance, in the palace governing *Zi, Ren, and Gui*, all three positions belong to Water. However, in the four "corner" palaces—**Qian** (Northwest), **Kun** (Southwest), **Gen** (Northeast), and **Xun** (Southeast)—the Five Phases differ at each position, which must be carefully observed. For example, the **Qian Palace** governs *Xu, Qian,* and *Hai*. *Xu* belongs to Earth, *Qian* belongs to Metal, and *Hai* belongs to Water. If a person has a "Fire destiny," performing work in the *Xu* position is auspicious because Fire and Earth generate one another. However, working in *Qian* or *Hai* would be inauspicious, as those elements conflict with Fire. Other palaces follow this principle, as do the theories regarding the lines of the hexagrams.

## On the Selection and Rejection of Stars and Malevolent Influences

Generally, if a **Noble Star** is in a <term>Yang Cycle<gloss>Yang Dun; the period of increasing light from the Winter Solstice to the Summer Solstice.</gloss></term> but encounters a "Yin Noble," or is in a **Yin Cycle** but encounters a "Yang Noble," it lacks power; such instances are not listed here. When the **Three White and Nine Purple Stars** <note>The "Flying Stars" used in Feng Shui to calculate the luck of a specific area.</note> arrive at a direction and conflict with it, or carry malevolence and misfortune, they are written in **black ink** to indicate they are inauspicious. 

In cases where auspicious and inauspicious influences share a palace, if the malevolence is of a type that "must not be offended," one must abandon the good and avoid the bad. However, if the malevolent influence is "light," one may set aside the bad and utilize the good—the **Heavenly and Monthly Virtues** <note>Highly auspicious stars that can neutralize minor harms.</note> are examples of this. The **Tai Sui** <note>The Grand Duke Jupiter, the most important yearly deity.</note> and the **Sword Edge** are considered malevolent; if they are light, they are not listed, or they are marked over with **vermilion** <note>Red ink.</note>. They must only be avoided if they are not to be offended. If they are extremely inauspicious, they are fully listed. The "Life-Star Malevolence" is inauspicious in the years of *Ding, Jia,* and *Bing*; outside of the three *Jia* years, it is not inauspicious and thus not listed. Other stars follow this rule.

### On Directions and Locations

Whenever residents of provincial capitals or counties engage in construction, if they are separated from the site by a main street or a house wall, the proximity does no harm. If working in a specific direction, one only fears the Year or Month <term>Punishing<gloss>Xing; a specific type of astrological clash or "penalty" between signs.</gloss></term> the Birth Fate of the individual or the direction itself.
However, if the state is building watchtowers, halls, or official courier stations, they must strictly avoid the Year or Month clashing with inauspicious stars. Officials should be especially cautious of this.
