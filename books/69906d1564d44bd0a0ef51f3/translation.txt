Having bowed to the forms of Brahma, Shiva <note>original: "Isha"</note>, the Moon, Indra, the Sun, Fire, and the Vasus <note>A group of elemental deities in Hindu cosmology.</note>, and having heard the sacred tradition, Maheshvara composes the *Vajrashata* <note>The "Hundred Thunderbolts," likely referring to powerful astrological rules.</note> for the success of daily religious and practical affairs. || 1 ||

**Commentary:**
Brahma is the well-known creator. Shiva <term>Isha</term> is Mahadeva. The Moon is the "Maker of Night." Indra is the well-known king of gods. The Sun is the "Maker of Day." Fire is the "Maker of Heat." The Vasus and others refer to the fierce planetary deities. Having bowed to these deities, whose form is the very essence of the sacrifice, for the success of practical affairs—specifically for the fulfillment of the duties of the twice-born <note>The *dvijati*, or the three higher social classes in traditional Indian society, for whom rites of passage are mandatory.</note> such as marriage and the sacred thread ceremony <term>upanayana</term>—the teacher named Maheshvara composes the work entitled the *Vajrashata*. || 1 ||

The technical terms for the year, season, month, day, solar day, lunar fortnight, lunar day, and the specific names of the yogas, karanas, zodiac signs, and lunar mansions—starting from the ascendant <term>lagna</term>—are well-known to the world. Their specific meanings should be understood through the traditions of those who know them. || 2 ||

**The meaning of this is:**
The Years <term>Samvatsara</term> are the sixty years beginning with *Prabhava*. The Seasons <term>Ritu</term> are the six beginning with the cool season <term>Shishira</term>. The Solstices <term>Ayana</term> are the southern and northern paths of the sun. The Months <term>Masa</term> are the twelve beginning with *Chaitra* <note>Roughly March-April.</note>. The Lords of the Day <term>Dineshwara</term> are the seven days of the week. The Lunar Days <term>Tithi</term> are the fifteen beginning with the first day of the fortnight <term>Pratipada</term>. The Yogas <note>A mathematical combination of the positions of the Sun and Moon.</note> are the twenty-seven beginning with *Vishkumbha*. The Karanas <note>Half of a lunar day.</note> are the eleven beginning with *Bava*.

The yogas <note>astrological combinations of the sun and moon</note> known as Vyaghata, Vajra, Ganda, and Atiganda are considered particularly harsh. Regarding the first yoga, Vishkambha, its first three ghatikas are avoided <term>varjya</term>. Likewise, for Vyaghata and Vajra, the first nine ghatikas are avoided. For Ganda and Atiganda, the first six ghatikas of each must be abandoned. In the constellation of Mula, the first five ghatikas are avoided. Between Ashlesha and Magha, which are ruled by the Serpents <note>original: "sarpa"</note> and the Ancestors <note>original: "pitru"</note> respectively, the three ghatikas at the junction <term>sandhi</term> must be avoided by those seeking success. By combining the lunar day <term>tithi</term> with the constellation and other factors, then performing the calculation—multiplying by seven and dividing by twenty—the remaining count determines the specific results. In the yogas of Vishkambha and others, the wise avoid the "mouths" or beginnings of these periods for all auspicious works, even if they are otherwise favorable to the lords of the gods. || 5 || 

From the Sun's constellation and the day's constellation, in sequence, the 15th, 8th, 10th, 19th, 18th, 21st, 22nd, 25th, 7th, 23rd, and 24th are the yogas known as "Shadasha-vakra" and others which must be avoided. Furthermore, the combination of three lunar days in a single solar day <note>a rare and inauspicious occurrence</note> is to be abandoned.

As stated in the authorities: when a planet has recently risen, it is in a state of "infancy"; when it is about to set, it is in "old age." Jupiter <note>original: "jiva"</note> and Venus <note>original: "bhrigu"</note> are considered "infants" for a period of half a month <note>fifteen days</note> after they rise. Other authorities suggest this state of infancy or old age lasts for ten days, while still others claim it lasts seven nights. **॥ 20 ॥** 

Regarding Venus: When it sets in the eastern direction and then rises in the western direction, rituals such as marriages and vows <term>vrata-dana</term> should be avoided during the transition. This is due to the fault of "infancy." 

Similarly, when it sets in the western direction and rises in the eastern direction, the five days following this should be avoided for auspicious works. These three conditions of "infancy" are to be shunned. In the same way, when Venus rises after setting in the west, certain lunar mansions <note>specifically "Paibham" or the constellation Ashlesha</note> must be avoided. According to the view of the sage Bhargava, when Jupiter and Venus are in these states, all rituals involving vows and gifts must be avoided. According to other opinions, a period of seven days should be abandoned for such activities. 

The ritual of **shaving and hair-cutting** <term>kshaura</term>—which includes the grooming of hair, beard, teeth, and nails—should be performed under the following lunar mansions: Ashvini, Shravana, Magha, Mrigashira, Dhanishta, Jyeshtha, Hasta, Revati, Swati, Chitra, Punarvasu, Shatabhisha, Pushya, and the three "Uttara" constellations. It should be done during the day. One should avoid performing this on the days of the Sun, Saturn, or Mars <note>Sunday, Saturday, and Tuesday</note>, and avoid the nighttime and the twilight hours. **॥ 21 ॥** 

The constellations Ashvini, Shravana, Magha, Dhanishta, Jyeshtha, Hasta, Revati, Swati, Chitra, Punarvasu, Shatabhisha, and Pushya are favorable for hair-cutting. The wise state that hair-cutting <term>kshaura</term> should not be done on the ninth lunar day. It is well-said by the learned that for the acceptance of an initiation <term>diksha</term> or for release from bondage, the timing must be excellent, particularly for Brahmins, looking for all auspicious signs in the planetary hours <term>hora</term>. **॥ 22 ॥** 

Hair-cutting must not be performed: immediately after eating; immediately after an oil massage; on the day a month-long fast or vow is completed; upon entering a village; or when traveling between different watches of the day <note>Yama: a three-hour period</note>. Specifically, one should not shave on the ninth day counting from the day an oil massage was first taken. ### Thus ends the Chapter on Lunar Transits

From the moment the Sun enters a new sign <term>Sankranti; the passage of the Sun from one zodiac sign to the next</term>, sixteen *nadi* <note>a unit of time equal to 24 minutes</note> before and after are considered significant. For the transit into **Cancer** <term>Karkata</term>, thirty units are remembered. The difference in the months is explained hereafter. **॥ 21 ॥**

### Now, the Determination of Solar Transits

From sunrise, twenty-six units are considered extremely subtle. According to the opinion of the sages, one should avoid those twenty-six and instead take the sixteen *ghatikas* <note>equivalent to a nadi, 24 minutes</note> before and after the transit as the auspicious time. In the transit of **Cancer**, the *ghatikas* before the transit are meritorious. In others, thirty, or for the sign of **Aries** <term>Mesha</term>, forty *ghatikas* are considered meritorious.

Furthermore, a lunar month that is devoid of a solar transit is known as an **intercalary month** <term>Malimlucha or Adhimasa; an "unclean" extra month added to the lunar calendar to keep it in sync with the solar seasons</term>. It should be avoided for all auspicious rituals. In a year where an intercalary month occurs, the two-month period associated with it is avoided for sacred acts. If a month is devoid of a solar transit, it is clearly an intercalary month. A month with two solar transits is called a **lost month** <term>Kshaya Masa; a rare event where the lunar month is shorter than the solar transit, resulting in a month being "skipped"</term>. This may occur once in a great while. Within a year, typically after three intercalary months, two such months may be seen.
