**Fire** is generated by the number two and completed at seven; **Wood** is generated by three and completed at eight; **Metal** is generated by four and completed at nine; **Earth** is generated by five and completed at ten. Summing these together yields the number fifty-five <note>This refers to the "River Map" (He Tu) numerology, where the sum of the "generating" numbers (1-5) and "completion" numbers (6-10) of the Five Elements equals 55.</note>. Only the *Suwen* <note>The *Suwen* (素問), or "Basic Questions," is a foundational text of the *Yellow Emperor’s Inner Canon*.</note> of the **Yellow Emperor** states that while **Earth's** generating number is five, its completion number is also five. 

This is because **Water**, **Fire**, **Wood**, and **Metal** all rely upon **Earth** to reach their completion, whereas **Earth** relies on nothing else; therefore, it remains uniquely as a single five. One can see the logic of this when it is illustrated in a diagram. To create such a diagram: place **Wood** in the East, **Metal** in the West, **Fire** in the South, **Water** in the North, and **Earth** in the Center. 

The four directions each take their own generating numbers and combine them with the **Earth** at the center to form their completion numbers. However, **Earth** occupies its own central position and has no other Earth to combine with; thus, it naturally remains only at the number five. This is because **Earth** does not need to wait for "Earth" to reach completion. Summing the numbers of the Five Elements in this manner results in fifty, which is the **Number of the Great Expansion** <note>original: 大衍之數 (Dàyǎn zhī shù). # Volume Seven

The method of **calculating with yarrow stalks** <note>original: *shishi* (揲蓍), the ancient practice of manipulating stalks of the yarrow plant to generate the hexagrams of the *I Ching* or *Book of Changes*</note> employs forty-nine stalks. When these stalks are gathered together, they act as a single unit, and the forty-nine are hidden within that "one." When they are scattered for use, they appear as forty-nine, and the "one" is hidden within the forty-nine. 

This "one" represents the **Tao** <note>The Way; the ultimate, indivisible principle of the universe</note>. If you describe it as "Nothingness," the one is nevertheless present; if you describe it as "Being," it cannot be physically grasped. The forty-nine stalks represent **Function** <note>original: *yong* (用), the manifest application or movement of the world</note>. In a state of stillness, they return to the one; in a state of motion, one perceives only their function. The "one" remains among them, yet it cannot be seized. This is what is meant by the phrase: "The **Number of the Great Expansion** <term>Dayan zhi shu (大衍之數): A foundational numerological concept representing the total possibilities of the universe, traditionally numbered at fifty</term> is fifty, but its practical use is forty-nine."

Those in the world who discuss numerology generally grasp only its "rough traces" <note>the visible, mechanical, or surface-level patterns</note>. Yet, numbers possess aspects so subtle that they cannot be understood by mere **skillful calculation** <note>original: *qiaoli* (巧歷), referring to the technical work of mathematicians or calendar-makers</note>. If even these mechanical traces are difficult to master, then when it comes to the state of **being moved and thereby penetrating all causes under heaven** <note>original: *gan er sui tong* (感而遂通); a classic expression describing how a sage intuitively understands the world's complexities without conscious effort</note>, physical traces play no part at all. This is why the **spirit of foreknowledge** <note>the divine or intuitive power of predicting the future</note> cannot be easily sought through visible traces—and certainly not by grasping only the most superficial of them.
