# Preface.

The word <term>Jyotisham</term> <note>Astrology/Astronomy</note> means the knowledge of 'Jyoti' or light. This light is the very same radiance that is the cause of creation, preservation, and destruction. In English, the word **Astrology** is derived from the roots *Astro* (star) and *Logos* (logic or reason). It is our common experience that when the full moon rises, the tides in the ocean swell, and conversely, on the day of the new moon, the ocean's strength diminishes. We also observe that for many suffering from illnesses, their ailments worsen on the new moon, and deaths frequently occur on those nights. From this, we can understand that the Moon possesses certain specific powers regarding the Earth and the living beings residing upon it. It is a sight known to us all that sunlight is absolutely essential for the protection of life, and that when there is an excess or absence of that sunlight, various transformations occur in living beings. If these two spheres among the <term>Navagrahas</term> <note>The Nine Planets/Heavenly Bodies of Indian astronomy</note>—the Sun and the Moon—have such authority over the world and its inhabitants, could not the other planets also possess similar influences? If they have such powers that touch everyone in general, could these planetary spheres not also possess specific powers that affect each individual in distinct ways? If these questions become the subject of our reflection, there is certainly no way for us to lack faith in Astrology.

Western scientists such as 'Dante', 'Pythagoras', 'Bacon', Kepler, and 'Democritus' were believers in the science of light <note>original: "Jyothishshasthram" - referring here to the combined study of astronomy and astrology</note> just like the Eastern scholars.

Among the Hindu sages, Parasara and Jaimini were the ones who achieved great mastery in <term>Jyotishshastra</term><note>The traditional Indian system of astronomy and astrology</note>. It was based on the schools of thought established by Parasara that many subsequent authors composed numerous astronomical and astrological texts. Most of Jaimini's works have unfortunately been lost to us. Today, only a rare text titled "Jaimini Sutras" remains in circulation under his name. Two works written by Varahamihira, titled *Brihat Jataka* and *Brihat Samhita*, are considered invaluable treasures by scholars of this science. Similarly, the writings of Garga, Satya, Chavya, Devacharya, and Venkatesha are regarded as foundational texts by many practitioners.

Those who have studied this science correctly are capable of accurately and precisely understanding every event destined to happen to an individual from birth until death, as well as all the changing conditions of their life from the moment of their beginning. However, most people practicing as astrologers today have not attained true proficiency or experience in this science. It is a subject that one might not fully master even with a lifetime of effort. Even with the things one has learned, it is not easy for anyone to examine a <term>Jataka</term><note>A birth chart or horoscope</note> subtly and completely, while correctly synthesizing the various positions of the planets. Most practitioners are only able to conduct a horoscope reading based on the limited things they happen to know. Because of all this, it is common for astrologers to make mistakes. ### The Celestial Sphere and the Planetary Orbs.

In the beginningless and endless celestial sphere, among the numerous orbs that exist, the Earth where we reside is but one single orb. All these orbs are bound by a force of attraction to the solar orb, and they all perpetually revolve around the Sun. Among the seven planets <term>Saptagrahas; the seven traditional celestial bodies used in ancient astronomy</term>, the planet situated at the greatest distance is Saturn. Jupiter, which is the largest among the planetary orbs, is situated below Saturn. Situated below Jupiter is Mars. After Mars comes the Sun <note>The text here describes a specific vertical ordering of the heavens common in traditional Indian cosmographies</note>. The Earth is situated 93,000,000 <term>Nazhika; a traditional unit of measurement, though here likely used to represent miles in a modern context</term> below the Sun. The Moon is a satellite that continuously revolves around the terrestrial orb. The Moon is 210,000 miles distant from the Earth. The planets situated below the Earth are Venus, followed by Mercury. All planets have their own distinct paths of travel. Through those paths of travel, they all circumambulate the Sun. The paths of motion for each planet are called their respective orbital spheres <term>Kakshyamandalam; the orbital circle or plane of a planet</term>. The Moon, which orbits the Earth, stays in each zodiac sign for approximately $2\frac{1}{4}$ days. During the first $2\frac{1}{4}$ days, covering the lunar mansions of Ashvati, Bharani, and a quarter of Karthika, the Moon resides in the sign of Aries. Following this, for the next $2\frac{1}{4}$ days, it moves into Taurus, and so on in sequence until the final $2\frac{1}{4}$ days when it resides in Pisces, completing one full revolution around the Earth. The significance of the term "Aries-portion" <note>original: "Medakooru"</note> for Ashvati, Bharani, and a quarter of Karthika is that during these $2\frac{1}{4}$ days, the Moon is positioned in the sign of Aries. Therefore, by looking at one's birth star <note>The 'Nakshatra' or lunar mansion</note>, one can understand which zodiac sign the Moon was occupying at the time of birth. # Chapter 2

## The Ascendant Sign (Lagna)

The 24-hour period we call a day is divided into 60 *nazhikas*. One *nazhika* consists of 60 *vinazhikas*. A *nazhika* is equivalent to 24 minutes. A *vinazhika* is <gloss>2/5</gloss> of a minute <note>equivalent to 24 seconds</note>. Within these 60 *nazhikas*, the Earth completes one full rotation on its axis. To understand how the positions and movements of the planets and stars in the celestial sphere are going to shape the life of a child born on Earth, one must contemplate the relationship between the child's location on the Earth's surface at the moment of birth and the positions of the planetary spheres in the heavens at that exact time.

<note>The following technical terms appear in the original text: Rahu and Ketu, Planets, Zodiac Sign (Rashi), Ascendant (Lagna), Nazhika (unit of time), Vinazhika (sub-unit of time), Terrestrial Globe, House Results, and Divisional Status.</note>

The duration of each <term>Rasi</term><note>A Rasi is a sign of the zodiac, representing a 30-degree arc of the ecliptic.</note> varies in every geographical region according to its Longitude and Latitude. However, in Malabar, the following durations are generally observed for each sign:

<note>Nazhika and Vinazhika are traditional units of time. 1 Nazhika equals 24 minutes; 1 Vinazhika equals 24 seconds.</note>

In every month, the first sign to rise at dawn is the sign corresponding to that month. For example, on the 1st of the month of Aquarius (<italic>Kumbham</italic>), the sign of Aquarius begins at sunrise. However, by the 2nd of Aquarius, one-thirtieth (1/30) of the total duration of that sign is subtracted, and only the remaining portion exists after sunrise (<term>Udayalparam</term><note>Literally "after sunrise," referring to the duration of a zodiac sign remaining after the sun has risen.</note>). On the 3rd of Aquarius, <gloss>2/30</gloss> parts are subtracted, and on the 4th, <gloss>3/30</gloss> parts are subtracted, leaving only the remainder after sunrise. Following this progression, on the 30th of Aquarius, <gloss>29/30</gloss> parts of the sign will have passed before sunrise, leaving only a small fraction remaining.
