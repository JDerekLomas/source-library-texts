### Introduction.

To every human being, two ways are presented here on earth: Namely, the way of God in Christ, and the way of the world in the Devil. Whichever way he chooses to go, he must struggle, fight, and suffer—yet with a very different outcome.

If he walks the way of God in Christ, he must fight, struggle, and suffer according to the outer man <note>The "outer man" refers to the physical body and worldly existence, as opposed to the "inner man" or spirit.</note>, but inwardly he has peace, and hereafter eternal life.

If he walks the way of the world in the Devil, he must also suffer much; inwardly he has no rest or peace, while outwardly he has a brief pleasure and joy, and hereafter eternal torment, martyrdom, and damnation.

But so that one may walk the way of God in Christ toward eternal joy and salvation <term>Seeligkeit: blessedness or the state of being saved</term>, one can be guided there through three means:

# Practice / by which our heart is guided upward to God.

### III. Through the Key of David / which is a practice by which one learns to look above oneself to the cause of the tree in the midst of Paradise / that is / what Adam and Christ are. <note>The "Key of David" is a biblical reference to spiritual authority and revelation (Isaiah 22:22; Revelation 3:7). Here, it serves as a metaphor for unlocking the mystery of the human condition—the tension between our fallen nature (Adam) and our redeemed nature (Christ).</note>

The first part or means teaches one to consider the fundamental reason why Christ gave and commanded the Lord’s Prayer <term>Vater Unser: The "Our Father" or Lord's Prayer</term>, from which one learns that the gracious God has given all things and knows them before we pray, and that we must awaken ourselves and not God with our prayer and shouting. And that both Adam and Christ are within us with their qualities, and that all earthly and heavenly wisdom lies hidden within us, like a great treasure in the field <note>A reference to the Parable of the Hidden Treasure in Matthew 13:44.</note>, which no one can obtain unless he prays, unless he seeks, and unless he knocks.

Take for yourself seven weeks, early in the morning and in the evening, also often in the night if you cannot sleep, for such meditations, which you should practice every time before you pray your Lord's Prayer.

1. That God knows everything beforehand better, what is necessary for us, before we ask.
2. That God calls, lures, drives, and admonishes all people to pray, and also promises a certain hearing <note>Meaning a guaranteed response or "hearing" of the prayer by God.</note>.

It prescribes a seven-week cycle of meditation focused on the Lord's Prayer and the belief that God knows human needs before they are voiced.</summary>
