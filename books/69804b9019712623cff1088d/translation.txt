I would have lacked neither comfort, nor you a proper funeral. O calamity, do you thus complete this disastrous year, which laid my Rachel from her childbed onto her bier; <note>Rachel, Jacob’s beloved wife and Joseph’s mother, died giving birth to Benjamin.</note> which saw my bed defiled by my firstborn; <note>A reference to Reuben, Jacob's eldest son, who slept with his father's concubine, Bilhah.</note> my little daughter violated; <note>Dinah, Jacob’s daughter, who was abducted and raped by Shechem.</note> my sons, mad with rage and revenge, committing that murder upon Hamor and his city; <note>In retaliation for Dinah’s rape, Jacob’s sons Simeon and Levi massacred the men of Shechem (the city of King Hamor).</note> and the entire Hebrew race slandered and stained, with significant danger to our property, our blood, and our lives.

**Joseph**, led into **Egypt** by **Midianites** and **Ishmaelites**, and sold to **Potiphar**, King **Pharaoh’s** chamberlain and master of the household, is set free through his lord’s favor, raised in arts and sciences, and finally placed in the supreme management of the house. He is remarkably blessed by God under the supervision of this young man, whose wisdom and virtue—outstanding in a beautiful and well-formed body—so enticed the **Mistress of the House** <note>Potiphar's wife, often unnamed in the Bible but called Zuleika in other traditions.</note> that she often sought to lure that God-fearing and most chaste soul into unchastity. However, being rejected as steadfastly as he was piously, and spying an opportunity on a certain feast day, she pressed and persisted in her shameful desire, even grabbing him by his garment: whereupon he, fleeing out of the house, left the garment behind with her. She then, calling the whole household together, accused the **Hebrew** of violence and violation; this was so believed by her husband that he cast the accused into the King’s prison.
