## Embodying the Way
<gloss>Its form cannot be seen; its laws are immense; its application is eternal. Thus, the Sage achieves governance by purifying his heart and spirit.</gloss>

The Way <term>Tao: the fundamental, unnamable process of the universe</term> that can be spoken of is not the eternal Way. The name that can be named is not the eternal Name. The Nameless is the origin of Heaven and Earth; the Named is the mother of the ten thousand things <note>A traditional Chinese phrase referring to the infinite variety of all things in existence</note>.

Therefore, by being constantly without desire, one may observe its subtle essence. By constantly having desire, one may observe its outward manifestations <note>Original: 徼 (jiào), meaning boundaries, limits, or visible effects</note>. These two arise from the same source but differ in name. Together they are called the Mystery—mystery upon mystery, the gateway to all wonders.

## Emptiness and Non-existence
<note>Chapter 23. This text emphasizes acting in accordance with the natural cycles of the universe.</note>

To speak little is natural. A whirlwind does not last all morning; a sudden rain does not last all day. Who causes these? Heaven and Earth. ### Greed and Loss

The people are starving because those above them consume too much in tax-grain; this is why they starve. The people are difficult to govern because those above them interfere with too much "doing" <term>wu-wei|the Taoist principle of "non-action" or effortless action, suggesting rulers should lead by following nature rather than through over-regulation</term>; this is why they are hard to govern. The people treat death lightly because they are so consumed by the pursuit of a lavish life; this is why they treat death lightly. Indeed, only those who do not strive for the sake of living are wiser than those who value life too highly.

### A Warning Against Strength

When humans are born, they are supple and weak; when they die, they are stiff and strong. When the ten thousand things <note>a phrase meaning all of creation</note>, such as grass and trees, are born, they are tender and fragile; when they die, they are withered and dry. Therefore, the stiff and strong are the companions of death, while the supple and weak are the companions of life.

### Natural Action

One who is brave in daring will be killed; one who is brave in not daring will survive. Of these two, one is beneficial and the other is harmful. Who knows the reason why Heaven <note>original: "天" (Tian), referring to the divine natural order</note> hates what it hates? Even the Sage <note>a realized being who lives in harmony with the Way</note> finds this difficult to understand. The Way of Heaven does not compete, yet it is skilled at winning; it does not speak, yet it is skilled at responding; it does not summon, yet things come to it of their own accord; it is calm, yet it is skilled at planning. The net of Heaven is vast; its mesh is wide, yet nothing is lost.

# The True Scripture of the Primordial Word by Guan Yinzi
## Chapter One: The One Universe

Guan Yinzi <note>Guan Yinzi is the traditional name for Yin Xi, the legendary Gatekeeper of the Hangu Pass who, according to tradition, persuaded Laozi to write the Daodejing.</note> says: It is not that the <term>Tao</term> <note>The Way; the ultimate, nameless reality and the source of all existence in Taoist thought.</note> cannot be spoken of; rather, that which cannot be spoken of *is* the Tao. It is not that the Tao cannot be thought of; rather, that which cannot be thought of *is* the Tao. The things of Heaven flow forth in a violent, surging torrent, while the affairs of humanity are a tangled mess. We spin in circles through endless "ifs," struggling in deep anxiety. In our haste, we chase things that seem real but are not. We fight over them, distance ourselves from them, curse them, spit at them, discard them, or demand them. Speaking of the Tao is like trying to blow away a shadow; thinking of it is like trying to carve a shape into dust. Even the wisdom of sages creates only more delusion, and the spirits themselves do not recognize it. Because it cannot be acted upon, attained, measured, or divided, it is variously called "Heaven," "Fate," "Spirit," or "The Mystery." All of these combined are called the Tao.

It is said: The Way of the Sage is the Mandate of Heaven; it is not that the Sage can dictate the Way himself. The Virtue of the Sage is a correspondence with the seasons; it is not that the Sage can dictate Virtue himself. The affairs of the Sage are human actions; it is not that the Sage can dictate affairs himself. Therefore, the Sage does not "possess" the Way, does not "possess" Virtue, and does not "possess" affairs. <note>The text argues that the Sage is a vessel for natural laws rather than an ego-driven creator of them.</note>
It is said: The Sage knows the "self" is without a self, therefore he harmonizes it with <term>Benevolence</term> <note>original: "ren" (仁); a central virtue implying universal humaneness and compassion</note>. He knows that affairs are without a self, therefore he balances them with <term>Righteousness</term> <note>original: "yi" (義); the sense of duty or doing what is situationally appropriate</note>. He knows the mind is without a self, therefore he restrains it with <term>Propriety</term> <note>original: "li" (禮); traditional ritual, decorum, and social harmony</note>. He knows that knowledge is without a self, therefore he illuminates it with <term>Wisdom</term>. He knows that words are without a self, therefore he maintains them with <term>Trustworthiness</term>.
It is said: The Way of the Sage might use Benevolence as benevolence, or use Righteousness as benevolence, or use Propriety, Wisdom, or Trustworthiness as benevolence. While Benevolence, Righteousness, Propriety, Wisdom, and Trustworthiness each lack the other four, the Sage unifies

<column-break/>

them. Since no thing can be excluded from this unity, he encompasses them with Emptiness. Since nothing in the world can make things difficult for him, he acts with Ease. Since nothing in the world can obstruct him, he adapts through <term>Expediency</term> <note>original: "quan" (權); the ability to weigh circumstances and adapt principles flexibly</note>. By using this to center the world, he can establish Rituals. By using this to harmonize the world, he can create Music. By using this to make the world impartial, he can manage resources. By using this to protect the world, he can defend against insults. By using this to follow the world's nature, he can establish Laws. By using this to observe the world, he can create tools. The Sage does not use his "individual self" to rule the world, but uses the world to rule the world. The world attributes the credit to the Sage, but the Sage leaves the credit to the world. This is why the rule of the legendary kings <term>Yao, Shun, Yu, and Tang</term> <note>The Four Sage Kings: semi-mythical rulers revered as icons of perfect, selfless governance</note> was called <term>Nature</term> <note>original: "ziran" (自然); literally "self-so," meaning things happening spontaneously and naturally without forced effort</note> by all the people.

# True Scripture of the Mysterious Lady

Using this to become one with all things, one can enter water or fire without harm. Only those who possess the Way can accomplish this; they are also capable of performing such feats but choose not to do so. <unclear>Is it not a pity? I truly fear that this Way is gradually being corrupted.</unclear>
It is said that human power can seize the <term>Creative Transformation (Zaohua)</term> <note>The natural formative powers of the universe</note> of Heaven and Earth. For example: raising thunder in winter, creating ice in summer, making a corpse walk, causing a withered tree to bloom, capturing ghosts within a bean, fishing from a mere cup, opening a door that is only painted, or speaking with earthen spirits. All these are achieved through pure <term>Qi</term> <note>The vital breath or energy that flows through all things</note>; therefore, one can transform the ten thousand things.
Today’s passions and desires never cease, which is also the work of Qi. However, Qi as a substance has its moments of gathering and its moments of dispersing. The way I circulate Qi has fundamentally never gathered nor dispersed. That which gathers is subject to birth; that which disperses is subject to death. That which has never gathered nor dispersed knows neither birth nor death. Guests come and go, but the post house remains as it always was.

For one whom external things cannot weigh down, the body becomes light; such a person can ride the phoenix or the crane. He rode the wind and traveled, soaring with a cool and light skill, returning only after fifteen days. Regarding the pursuit of good fortune, he was never anxious or hurried. Yet, although he escaped the necessity of walking on the ground, he still <note>The philosopher Liezi could fly, but he was still limited because he required the wind to carry him.</note> depended on something. 

But as for one who mounts the true essence of Heaven and Earth, and rides the changes of the <term>Six Breaths (Liu Qi)</term> <note>These are the elemental forces of nature: yin, yang, wind, rain, dark, and light.</note> to wander through the Boundless—what would such a person need to depend on? Therefore, it is said: The <term>Perfect Man (Zhiren)</term> has no self; the <term>Spirit Man (Shenren)</term> has no concern for achievement; the <term>Sage (Shengren)</term> seeks no fame.

The Emperor Yao, wishing to cede the empire to the hermit Xu You, said: "When the sun and moon have come out, if the torches are not extinguished, is it not difficult for them to provide light? When the seasonal rains have fallen, if we continue to irrigate the fields, is it not a wasted effort? While you stand ready, Master, the world would be well-ordered, yet I still occupy the throne. I see my own deficiencies; I beg to turn the empire over to you."

Xu You replied: "You govern the world, and the world is already well-governed. If I were to take your place, would I be doing it for a 'name'? A name is but the guest of reality..."

...like clouds in the sky. When it circles upward on a whirlwind like a ram's horn, rising ninety thousand miles, it cuts through the clouds and mist and bears the blue sky on its back. Only then does it set its sights on the south, preparing to journey to the Southern Abyss. 

A <term>quail (Chiyan)</term> mocked the bird, saying: "Where does he think he is going? This is the distinction between the small and the great. 

Thus, those whose knowledge is sufficient for a single office, whose conduct is suited to a single neighborhood, or whose virtue matches the needs of a single ruler so that they are trusted by a whole country, view themselves in much the same way as that quail.
