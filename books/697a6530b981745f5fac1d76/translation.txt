# Of Curious Matters.

**A**s Marin read through these **questions** <note>original: "Quæstiones"</note>, he spoke to Simplicius: these questions are curious, and it appears from them that Master Simplicius has not only done much in **Hermetic Philosophy** <note>original: "Philosophia Hermetica"; the study of alchemy and the spiritual transformation of matter.</note> and the **knowledge of the mineral kingdom** <note>original: "cognitione Regni mineralis"</note>, but must also have read diligently; to such an extent that one could quite easily discern why he had crafted these questions. Without doubt, he would have reflected partly on **Flamel** <note>Nicolas Flamel (c. 1330–1418), a famous French figure often associated in legend with the discovery of the Philosopher’s Stone.</note>, **Chortalasseus** <note>Johann Grasshoff (d. 1623), an alchemist who wrote under the pseudonym Chortolasseus; his works focused on the "Golden Age" of alchemy and the transformation of metals.</note>, and partly on **Sendivogius** <note>Michael Sendivogius (1566–1636), a Polish alchemist and chemist who proposed that a "life force" existed in the air, later identified as oxygen.</note>. For as much as he remembered of these authors, they all aimed at the **mineral kingdom** <note>original: "Regnum minerale"</note>, and especially Chortalasseus and others named the **Saturnine Ore** <term>Minera Saturnina: In the language of alchemy, "Saturn" refers to lead.
