# Introduction

Nowadays, as the poverty of the inhabitants of India increases day by day, various types of diseases are also significantly on the rise. However, alongside this, for the purpose of removing disease, the Western medical system is also expanding greatly. This has reached such a point that nowadays, one would hardly find a household or village where one or two people with a complete or partial knowledge of English <note>original: "angreji," referring here to Western or British-style medicine</note> medicine cannot be found.

Within this English medical system, two types of treatment are primary: Allopathic and Homeopathic. Among these, while Allopathy is promoted everywhere in India, Homeopathy does not have the same level of reach. It must be written with great sadness that, except for the land of Bengal <note>The author refers to "Bang desh"; Bengal was the primary hub for the early adoption and spread of Homeopathy in India</note>, the promotion of Homeopathy in other regions remains virtually non-existent even today.

Yet, for this disease-stricken land of India, Homeopathic treatment is as useful as any, and it is a fact proven in every way that no other treatment is quite so beneficial. Specifically, the Allopathic school of treatment is as difficult to manage as its medicines are filled with toxic reactions <term>poisonous effects or medicinal side-effects</term>, the use of which is never free from the risk of sudden disaster.

However, the Homeopathic system of medicine is not like this. Its cost is low, the medicines are simple, and the system of treatment is easy to follow. So much so that even a person with a basic education can easily treat simple ailments with the help of a book. By providing relief from the suffering <term>salvation or liberation from pain</term> of neighbors, family members, and the poor and miserable, one can attain unprecedented fame and merit. A special quality of this system is also that if the medicine is correctly prescribed <term>judgment or selection of a remedy</term>, then even if it does not do good, it will certainly cause no harm.

<meta>Continuing from the introduction where the author argued that Homeopathy is more affordable and safer than Allopathy for the Indian public, this page traces the specific history of the Lahiri Company’s publications and the transition of their medical manuals from Bengali to Hindi.</meta>

Nowadays, in the land of Bengal <note>original: "Vanga desh"</note>, alongside the expansion of the Homeopathic medical system, many medical texts have also been published. The late Jagdish Chandra Lahiri, the renowned founder of the famous Lahiri Company, was the pioneer of this movement. He was the first to establish a major dispensary in the capital city of Calcutta and authored various medical works in the Bengali language. He specifically created a book titled *Home Treatment* <note>original: "Grahachikitsa," a manual for domestic medical care</note> for the householders of Bengal. With its help, any householder can easily provide Homeopathic remedies for common illnesses and see beneficial results.

Gradually, as branch dispensaries of the aforementioned company were established in the cities of Patna and Bankipur in the Bihar region, as well as in the holy district of Mathura in the United Provinces <note>The United Provinces was a region in British India, roughly corresponding to modern-day Uttar Pradesh</note>, a Hindi translation of the Bengali *Home Treatment* was published from the Bankipur branch to promote this medical system in those areas. However, that initial translation was based only on the very first edition of the book.

Since then, four more editions of the Bengali *Home Treatment* have been released, each progressively incorporating new information. Because the sections on treating common ailments were expanded so extensively, the size of the book grew significantly. The stock of the old printed Hindi *Home Treatment* was also completely sold out about a year ago. Now, this second Hindi edition—translated from the fifth Bengali edition—is being printed. When compared to the older versions, it would not be an exaggeration to call this a brand-new book.

Diseases that are complex or fatal <term>saunghatik: refers to dangerous, life-threatening, or "critical point" illnesses</term> have been carefully omitted from this volume. This is because it is not appropriate for the intended audience of this book <note>referring to laypeople/householders</note> to attempt the treatment of such severe medical conditions.

# Temperature Instrument (Thermometer)

This instrument is made of glass; its tube contains mercury <note>original: "para"</note> which rises when it encounters heat. Fever is identified through this device. The method for using it is as follows: the specific part of the tube where the mercury is collected should be held pressed firmly in the armpit <note>original: "bagal"</note> for five minutes. By doing this, the mercury will rise. There are markings on the glass; the large marks are called "degrees" <note>original: "digree"</note>, and the small marks represent one-fifth <note>0.2</note> of a degree.

The normal temperature of the body is 98.4 or 98.5 degrees. When a fever <note>original: "jwar"</note> occurs, the temperature rises, and this is detected by the instrument. If the temperature falls below 95 degrees, there is a danger to life; however, the natural temperature of some individuals has been observed to be as low as 97 degrees. In cases of cholera <note>original: "visuchika"; a bacterial disease causing severe dehydration and drop in body temperature</note>, the temperature has been seen to drop to 95. Just as a low temperature is a cause for alarm, so too is a high temperature; 107 degrees or higher is extremely dangerous. Before applying the temperature instrument, the mercury should be brought down to the 98-degree mark. The way to do this is to hold the upper part of the instrument in your fist and give it a sharp jerk <note>original: "jhatka"</note> to make the mercury descend. It should be applied only after resetting it to 98 in this manner. It must be handled with great care, as there is a constant risk of the glass breaking.
