## The Use of the Philosophical <term>Athanor</term> for this Work.
<note>An Athanor is a specialized furnace designed by alchemists to provide a steady, uniform heat over long periods without needing constant attention.</note>

There are three spheres in the Athanor: the first is the largest and is solid; the middle one is perforated at the top so that the steam may pass through; the third is made of oak wood, in which the <term>putrefaction</term> is carried out by the steam-fire.
<note>Putrefaction (German: *Fäulung*) is the essential first stage of the "Great Work," where the raw material is broken down and "dies" to be reborn.</note>
In the first sphere, there should be enough water, which, when it evaporates, must be replaced with more warm water. This stage of putrefaction is completed in forty or forty-five days, during which the blackness, known as the <term>Raven’s Head</term>, usually appears.
<note>Original: *Raben-Haupt*. This symbolizes the *nigredo* stage, where the matter turns deep black, signifying the successful beginning of the transformation.</note>
When the putrefaction is complete, remove the wooden sphere, as the water is no longer needed. Place it instead in the perforated sphere, which is filled with ashes; the degree of the fire should be so hot that it does not hurt the finger. Then, within 50 days, various colors will appear, which are called the <term>Peacock’s Tail</term>, and finally the Green color alone.
<note>Original: *Pfauen-Schwanz*. This refers to the *cauda pavonis* stage, an iridescent phase where the material displays many colors before settling into a single hue.</note>

After this, take the glass and place it in the largest sphere filled with sand, so that the sand covers the sealed glass with its material. Close the <term>Athanor</term>, stoke the fire underneath to a heat that a finger cannot endure; thus, within 50 days, it will become entirely white.
<note>The text here uses "Atkanor," a likely typographical error for Athanor.</note>
Continue with this degree of fire until the material turns yellow, which will happen within 30 or at most 50 days.
