# CONCERNING ST. DIONYSIUS
<note>original: "De D. Dionysio"</note>
## Testimonies of the ancients regarding the Areopagite.
<note>Dionysius was a member of the Athenian high court (the Areopagus) who, according to the Book of Acts, was converted by St. Paul. This page gathers quotes from later saints to prove he was a real and authoritative figure.</note>

---

*Ambrose, Letter 82, Book 10.*
<note>St. Ambrose (c. 339–397 AD) was the Bishop of Milan and one of the most influential figures in the early Western Church.</note>
<image-desc>Decorative drop cap 'A' enclosed in a square border of small scrollwork ornaments.</image-desc>
**S**peaking of those whom *Blessed Paul* converted, he says: "The Apostle did not depart from this group without the gift of grace; for even Dionysius the Areopagite, along with his wife Damaris and many others, believed. Thus, those gatherings of the most learned and eloquent men demonstrated, by the example of those who believed, that they had been conquered by a simple discourse." <note>original: "disputatione"; here referring to Paul's sermon at Mars Hill in Athens.</note> *Similar things are said by Eusebius <note>The "Father of Church History" (c. 260–339 AD).</note> in the History of the Church, Book 3, Chapter 4; and by Nicephorus in Book 2. The writings of Dionysius are cited by Sophronius of Jerusalem to Sergius of Constantinople; by Pope Agatho to the Emperor Constantine; by Gregory of Nazianzus in his sermon on the Nativity; by John of Damascus in 'On the Faith,' Book 1, Chapter 12; and by others, whom we shall list in the following letter.*

—we already owe much to the generosity, care, and sleepless efforts of the noble and illustrious Lord **Albert Fugger** <note>The Fuggers were a dominant European banking and mercantile family based in Augsburg; Albert Fugger (1516–1575) was a significant patron of learning and a collector of rare manuscripts.</note>, which he devotes to restoring the monuments <note>original: "monimenta," meaning the preserved writings or intellectual legacies</note> of the finest and most especially pious writers. It is because of him that this divine work of *Mystical Theology* <term>Mystical Theology: A branch of Christian theology, attributed here to Dionysius the Areopagite, that seeks to understand the "hidden" or "mystical" nature of God through prayerful experience rather than just logic.</term>, together with its commentary, now sees the light of day once more. I might, therefore, have deservedly dedicated this second edition to him; nevertheless, with his kind permission, it seemed right for good reasons to dedicate it instead to your Highness.

For since the first edition was favored with a patron from among your predecessors, the Princes and Bishops of Augsburg, I judged it most fitting that this second edition—which is certainly not inferior, and perhaps even better than the first—should find no other patron than a Prince, a Bishop, and a leader of Augsburg. It should be granted to none other than the successor of that previous patron after this interval of time: a successor, I say, not only in rank, but also in virtue and learning. **Johann Eck** <note>Johann Eck was a major figure of the Counter-Reformation; he had prepared the earlier edition of this work for Bishop Christopher von Stadion.</note> prophesied and prayed for this for him at the very beginning of his bishopric—
