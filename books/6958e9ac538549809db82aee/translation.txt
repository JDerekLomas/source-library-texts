## Coptis (Goldthread)
<gloss>Top Grade in the Divine Husbandman's Classic</gloss>
<gloss>Explanatory Names</gloss> King’s Thread <gloss>from the Divine Husbandman's Classic</gloss>; Branch Thread <gloss>from the Treatise on Medicinal Properties</gloss>. Shizhen says: The root is long and has <insert>sprouts</insert> that connect like beads, and the color is yellow, hence the name <term>Huanglian</term> <note>literally "Yellow Successive-Beads"</note>. <gloss>The Miscellaneous Records state:</gloss> Coptis grows in the river valleys of Wuyang, the commanderies of Shu <note>modern Sichuan</note>, and the sunny side of Mount Tai. Roots are gathered in the second and eighth months. Hongjing says: Wuyang is in Jianping. Today, the best roots are shaped like connected beads. The sprouts resemble mustard greens or shepherd's purse. A single stem produces three leaves, standing about a foot high. It does not wither in the depths of winter. The flowers are yellow. Those from the Jiangzuo region have high nodes like connected beads; those from Ezhou have lower nodes that do not connect. Currently, those from the Qin region, Hangzhou, and Liuzhou are considered excellent. Song says: Today it is also found in the Jianghu, Jing, and Kui prefectures. The variety from Xuancheng, which has nine nodes and is hard and heavy—clinking when struck together—is superior. Those from Shiqian are next, followed by those from Dongyang, Shezhou, and Chuzhou. The sprouts are about a foot high with leaves like sweet chrysanthemum. It flowers in the fourth month with yellow blossoms and fruits in the sixth month, resembling small slices. Regarding the Jiangzuo variety, the roots are yellow and beaded, the sprouts survive winter, and the leaves resemble small chicken-tail ferns. It flowers in the first month with fine, pale white to yellowish spikes. The roots are firm enough to harvest in the sixth or seventh month. Qin says: The variety from the Shu Road is thick and large with an extremely bitter taste; it is best for treating thirst. The Jiangdong variety has nodes like beads and is excellent for treating dysentery. The Lizhou variety is even better. Shizhen says: In the late Han dynasty, Li Dangzhi’s herbal only took the yellow, fat, and firm roots from Shu as the best. In the Tang dynasty, the Lizhou variety was superior. Today, although it grows in both Wu and Shu, the variety from Yazhou and Meizhou is considered the finest medicine. The rise and fall of medicinal sources varies like this. Generally, there are two types: one has thick roots without hairs, with bead-like growths shaped like eagle or chicken claws, firm and deep yellow; the other has no beads, many hairs, a hollow center, and a lighter yellow color. Each has its appropriate use.

<margin>Processing the Roots</margin> Xiao says: To use it, wipe away the "fleshy hairs" with a cloth. Soak in rice-sour water for one full day <note>approx. 24 hours</note>, filter out, and roast dry over a willow-wood fire. Shizhen says: The five viscera and six bowels all possess "fire" <note>pathogenic heat/energy</note>. When balanced, there is health; when agitated, there is disease. Thus, there are theories of "Sovereign Fire" and "Ministerial Fire," but in reality, they are of one essence. Coptis enters the Hand Lesser Yin Heart Meridian and is the primary medicine for governing fire. To treat fire in its "home" organ <note>the Heart</note>, use it raw. To treat excess fire in the Liver and Gallbladder, stir-fry it after soaking in pig fat juice. To treat Lung fire, stir-fry with vinegar. To treat fire in the Upper Burner <note>chest area</note>, stir-fry with wine. To treat fire in the Middle Burner <note>stomach area</note>, stir-fry with ginger juice. To treat fire in the Lower Burner <note>pelvic area</note>, stir-fry with salt water or Glauber's salt. To treat damp-heat fire in the Qi level, stir-fry after soaking in a decoction of medicinal Evodia. To treat "hidden fire" within blood masses, stir-fry with dry lacquer powder. To treat fire from food stagnation, stir-fry with yellow earth. These various methods do not merely serve as "guides" for the medicine; rather, acrid heat can restrain its bitter cold, and salty cold can restrain its drying nature. The practitioner must weigh these options carefully.

<margin>Flavor and Nature</margin> Bitter, cold, non-toxic. <gloss>The Miscellaneous Records state:</gloss> Slightly cold. <gloss>Pu says:</gloss> Shennong, the Yellow Emperor, Lei Gong, and Tong Jun say it is non-toxic. Li Dangzhi says it is "lesser cold." <gloss>Zhicai says:</gloss> Scutellaria, Dragon Bone, and Gypsum serve as its "messengers" <note>assisting herbs</note>. It dislikes Chrysanthemum, Silene, White Dittany, Genkwa, and Silkworm. It fears Coltsfoot and Achyranthes. It overcomes Aconite and detoxifies Croton seed. <gloss>Quan says:</gloss> Avoid pork; it dislikes cold water. <gloss>Xiao says:</gloss> If taking ten ounces of this medicine, one must not eat pork. If taken for three years, one must never eat pork for the rest of one's life. Shizhen says: Taoist books say eating Coptis with pork causes diarrhea, yet medical formulas include "Pig Stomach Coptis Pills" and "Hog Gallbladder Coptis Pills." Is it possible to avoid the meat but not the internal organs?

<margin>Primary Indications</margin> Heat-related eye pain, injury to the corners of the eyes, tearing, and improving vision. Intestinal sores, abdominal pain, and dysentery. Women’s vaginal swelling and pain. Long-term use prevents forgetfulness. <gloss>Divine Husbandman's Classic</gloss>. Cold or heat in the five viscera, chronic diarrhea with pus and blood. Stops "wasting-thirst" <note>diabetes-like symptoms</note>, treats great fright, removes water retention, benefits the bones, harmonizes the stomach, thickens the intestines, and stops abdominal pain. <gloss>Miscellaneous Records</gloss>. Treats the "Five Labors and Seven Injuries," boosts Qi, stops heart and abdominal pain, palpitations, and irritability. Moistens the Heart and Lungs, promotes muscle growth, stops bleeding, treats seasonal heat diseases, stops night sweats, and treats various skin sores and toxic swellings. Made into pills, it treats childhood malnutrition and kills parasites. <gloss>Daming</gloss>. Settles Liver Qi, treats toxic heat sores and fistulas, irritability in the heart, nausea, and the urge to vomit. Treats fullness and oppression below the heart. Governs heart disease where energy is rebellious and overflowing, and accumulations in the heart. <gloss>Zangqi</gloss> <gloss>Zhen Quan</gloss>

# **Goldthread (Huanglian)** <gloss>Haogu says: Remove the core. Apply to malignant blood sores. Antidote for drug overdoses. Treats poisoning from calomel <note>mercurous chloride</note> at the crown of the head and the "bean-sized" points on the feet. Shizhen introduces Yuansu’s teachings, saying:</gloss>

The nature of Goldthread is cold and its flavor is bitter. Both its essence and aroma are potent; it can both ascend and descend, representing the "Yang within Yin." It enters the Hand Lesser Yin <note>Heart</note> Meridian. It has six primary uses: first, to drain fire from the Heart; second, to remove damp-heat from the Middle Burner <note>the digestive region</note>; third, it is essential for all types of sores; fourth, to dispel wind-dampness; fifth, for sudden outbreaks of red eyes; and sixth, to stop bleeding in the middle part of the body. Zhang Zhongjing treated nine types of "stuffiness below the heart" <note>epigastric distension</note>, and his five types of "Heart-Draining Decoctions" all utilized it. <gloss>Huo Wuyi says</gloss>: Bitterness enters the Heart, and cold overcomes heat. Goldthread possesses the ultimate bitterness among bitter things; its coldness guides away the "empty heat" <note>deficiency heat</note> below the heart. If it meets sweetness, it moves; if it meets bitterness, it settles. The bitterness of Goldthread and Reed Pith is used to settle this condition. <gloss>Haogu says</gloss>: Goldthread is bitter and drying. Bitterness enters the Heart, and the ultimate result is dryness. To drain the Heart is actually to drain the Spleen. This is "draining the son when the mother organ is in excess" <note>a Five Elements theory principle where the Heart is the mother of the Spleen</note>. <gloss>Zhenheng says</gloss>: Goldthread removes damp-heat from the Middle Burner and drains Heart fire. However, if the Spleen and Stomach Qi are too weak to transport nutrients, Scutellaria <note>Huangqin</note> should be used instead. In the past, I stir-fried it with pig gall juice and assisted it with Gentian <note>Longdancao</note> to powerfully drain fire from the Liver and Gallbladder. For dysentery where the stomach opening is hot and the patient cannot eat, use a decoction of Goldthread and Ginseng. Sip it throughout the day; if vomited, force yourself to drink again. If even one sip passes the throat, recovery is possible. <gloss>Liu Wansu says</gloss>: Ancient formulas regarded Goldthread as the supreme treatment for dysentery. The proper treatment for dysentery requires pungent, bitter, and cold medicines. Pungency can disperse and open up stagnant clusters; bitterness can dry dampness; cold can overcome heat and balance the Qi. While most bitter-cold drugs cause purging, only Goldthread and Phellodendron <note>Huangbo</note> are cold yet drying in nature, able to lower fire and remove dampness to stop diarrhea and dysentery. Thus, it is the "sovereign" <note>lead ingredient</note> for treating dysentery. <gloss>Zongshi says</gloss>: People today often use Goldthread for dysentery, strictly adhering to the principle of "bitterness dries dampness." But commoners, seeing only intestinal weakness and leakage with a hint of blood, use it without considering the balance of cold and heat. They only wish to stop the dysentery, which often leads to dangerous exhaustion. If the Qi is robust at the onset of illness with high heat and bloody stools, it stops upon taking it, and the full dose is unnecessary. For those who are weak and cold, be cautious and do not use it lightly. <gloss>Gao says</gloss>: All pain, itching, and sores belong to Heart fire. For all sores, Goldthread and Angelica <note>Danggui</note> should be the sovereign ingredients, with Licorice and Scutellaria as assistants. For unbearable sudden redness and swelling of the eyes, Goldthread and Angelica should be infused in wine and decocted. Furthermore, for undigested food and epigastric fullness, one must use Goldthread and Bitter Orange <note>Zhishi</note>. <gloss>Song says</gloss>: There are many formulas using Goldthread for the eyes, but the "Sheep Liver Pill" is particularly marvelous. I now instruct physicians to wash the eyes using equal parts Goldthread, Angelica, and Peony, decocted in snow water or sweet spring water. Wash while hot; if it cools, reheat it. This is extremely beneficial for the eyes. For "wind-toxin" red eyes where other medicines have failed, this is invariably miraculous. Eye diseases are caused by blood stagnation; therefore, blood-moving herbs are combined with Goldthread. Blood moves when it encounters heat, which is why the wash is used hot. <gloss>Han Mao says</gloss>: For diseases of the "five portions," Goldthread is the primary agent. It does not only drain Heart fire; this is why it is ranked alongside Scutellaria and Phellodendron. For those with eye ailments, soak it in human milk and steam it, then use as drops or take internally. Use it raw as the sovereign, assisted by a little Cinnamon <note>Guan gui</note>. Boil it a hundred times, add honey, and take on an empty stomach; this can cause the Heart and Kidney to communicate in an instant. Added to "Five-Ling Powder," it treats nocturnal emissions. When stir-fried with yellow earth, ginger juice, wine, and honey as the sovereign, with Rangoon Creeper <note>Shijunzi</note> as the minister, and White Peony, wine-processed Ophiopogon <note>Maidong</note>, and Aucklandia <note>Muxiang</note> as assistants, it treats the "five types of malnutrition" in children. Stir-fried with Evodia <note>Zhuyu</note> and equal parts Aucklandia, with double the amount of raw Rhubarb, made into water pills, it treats the "five types of dysentery." These are all examples of the proper method of formulating prescriptions. <gloss>Shizhen says</gloss>: Goldthread is an essential medicine for treating the eyes and dysentery. Ancient formulas like the "Fragrant Goldthread Pill" use Goldthread and Aucklandia. "Ginger-Goldthread Powder" uses Dried Ginger and Goldthread. "Transformation Pill" uses Goldthread and Evodia. "Ginger-Yellow Powder" uses Goldthread and Fresh Ginger. For "wasting and thirst" <note>diabetes-like symptoms</note>, use wine-steamed Goldthread. For "hidden summer heat," use wine-boiled Goldthread. For blood in the stool, use Goldthread and Garlic. For Liver fire, use Goldthread and Evodia. For mouth sores, use Goldthread and Asarum <note>Xixin</note>. These all pair one cold with one hot, one Yin with one Yang—using cold because of heat, or heat because of cold. Sovereign and minister assist each other, Yin and Yang complement each other; this is the pinnacle of the art of formulation. Thus, they achieve success without the harm of one-sided excess. <gloss>Hongjing says</gloss>: Common formulas often use Goldthread for dysentery and thirst. Daoist formulas use it for longevity. <gloss>Wei says</gloss>: Wang Wei of the Liu Song Dynasty wrote a "Praise of Goldthread":

> Goldthread’s flavor is bitter, left and right supporting each other. It cuts through cold and cleanses summer heat, extending life and lightening the body.

The scriptures say: In the past, when the Emperor used flying pellets, he could no longer walk, until he heard of this. Furthermore, Jiang Hai of the Liang Dynasty wrote a "Hymn to Goldthread":

> Goldthread is the supreme herb, second only to Cinnabar. It guards the walls and wards off evil, granting long life and eternal vision. Like a mottled dragon traversing the sky, or a tamed horse leaving the earth. Soaring with grace, following the Way brings profit.

<gloss>Shizhen says</gloss>: Neither the *Classic* <note>Shennong Bencao Jing</note> nor the *Additional Records* mention that long-term consumption of Goldthread leads to immortality. Only Tao Hongjing mentions that Daoist formulas use it for longevity and becoming a transcendent. Records state that Feng Junda and Heixue Gong both took Goldthread for fifty or ten years and became immortals. However, I believe Goldthread is a medicine of extreme bitterness and extreme cold, used to lower fire and dry dampness. Once the illness is cured, one should stop. How can it be taken for a long time? That would allow the "energy of execution" <note>the harsh, killing cold of autumn/winter</note> to constantly operate, thereby damaging the generative and harmonious Qi of life. The *Basic Questions* <note>Suwen</note> records Qibo saying: "The five flavors enter the stomach, each returning to what it prefers. Attacking for a long time increases the Qi; this is the natural transformation of things. If the Qi increases for too long, it follows the course of Heaven." Wang Bing’s commentary says: Sour enters the Liver as warmth, bitter enters the Heart as heat, pungent enters the Lung as coolness, salty enters the Kidney as cold, and sweet enters the Spleen as ultimate Yin. The four energies are combined, and each increases its flavor and benefits its Qi. Thus, each follows the Qi of its home organ. Therefore, long-term consumption of Goldthread or Sophora <note>Kushen</note> actually results in *heat*. This is because it transforms into fire. The same applies to other flavors. Over time, the Qi of an organ becomes excessively dominant, leading to the exhaustion of others and the path to sudden death. This is why those who abstain from grain and take elixirs do not die suddenly—because they do not have the one-sided assistance of the five flavors. Furthermore, Qin Guan wrote to Qiao Xisheng regarding Goldthread: "I hear you are taking Goldthread for an eye ailment, even after ten ounces you have not stopped. This is likely unacceptable. Medical classics state that long-term use of Goldthread or Sophora actually causes heat. Although it is extremely cold, its flavor is extremely bitter; upon entering the stomach, it first returns to..."

# Compendium of Materia Medica: Volume 13, Herbs
## [Section on Coptis chinensis / Goldthread]

If the heart remains in a state of distress for a long time without ceasing, the "Heart Fire" <note>In Traditional Chinese Medicine, the Heart is associated with the Fire element; "Heart Fire" refers to inflammatory or over-active states</note> becomes overly dominant, resulting in heat. This is the underlying principle. Furthermore, eye diseases are rooted in "Liver Heat." The Liver and Heart stand in a "Mother and Son" relationship <note>The Five Elements theory: Wood (Liver) fuels Fire (Heart)</note>; if the Heart is fire, the Liver is also fire. The Kidney, however, is a solitary organ of Water. When a person suffers from a condition where one part Water cannot overcome two parts Fire, how can they long endure bitter medicines? Such medicines cause the Heart to become overly dominant in its specific nature. To use fire to save fire—how can that be acceptable? 

Qin Yueren <note>The legendary physician Bian Que</note> wrote his book based on the theories of the Duke of Wang, expanding upon them in detail. In our own Ming Dynasty, Prince Duan of Jing suffered from many "fire" illnesses. His physicians ordered him to take "Golden Flower Pills," a cooling medicine consisting of four ingredients including Coptis. He took these for several years, but the fire only grew more intense, eventually leading to internal cataracts and loss of sight. Observing this, we see that bitter, cold medicines not only prevent a person from achieving longevity, but if taken for a long time, they cause the vital energy to become unbalanced. This imbalance is the very reason for a premature death. We should <gloss>formerly page 22</gloss> take the words of the *Suwen* <note>The Basic Questions of the Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon</note> as our law. The claims in Master Tao’s Daoist books regarding long-term consumption for immortality are entirely mistaken. These errors harm people most severely; physicians must be aware of this. Yang Shiying stated: Coptis is able to remove "evil blood" from the orifices of the heart.

<insert>Excess Heat in the Heart Meridian</insert> Use "Heart-Purging Decoction" (Xiexin Tang). Use seven *qian* <note>approx. 26 grams</note> of Coptis, one and a half cups of water. Boil down to one cup. Take warm, far from mealtimes. Reduce the dose for children. <gloss>From: Formulas from the Imperial Grace Formulary</gloss>

<insert>Sudden Heat and Heart Pain</insert> Eight *qian* of Coptis, chopped. Decocted in water. <note>From: Secret Essentials from the Outer Terrace</note>

# Baikal Skullcap (Huang Qin) <gloss>Middle Grade</gloss>

<gloss>Aliases: Rotten Intestine, Golden Scutellaria, Internal Intestine, Hollow, Carved Stone, Lady Gui, Medicine</gloss> <gloss>Aliases: Carved, Page, Wu, Bitter, Overseer, Master, Record</gloss> **Those with solid interiors are "Larval Scutellaria" (Ziqin)** <gloss>Striped Scutellaria, Network</gloss> **"Supporting House Scutellaria"** <gloss>Tang Dynasty; Tao Hongjing says they are round</gloss>
Those that are broken are called "Old Scutellaria" (Suqin). Because their centers are all decayed, they are named "Rotten Intestine." Shizhen <note>Li Shizhen</note> says: The character for *Qin* in the *Shuowen Jiezi* dictionary is written as "yellow herb," referring to its color. Some say *Qin* means *Qian* (dark/black), as the old roots are often hollow, yellow outside and black inside. This is what is now called "Piece Scutellaria" (Pianqin). It has names like "Rotten Intestine" because of its decayed core. "Larval Scutellaria" (Ziqin) refers to the new roots which are mostly solid; these are what are now called "Striped Scutellaria" (Tiaoqin). Some say Western Scutellaria is mostly hollow and deep in color, while Northern Scutellaria is mostly solid and deep. <gloss>The "Separate Records" says: Scutellaria grows in the valleys of Zigui and Yuanju. Collect the roots on the third day of the third month and dry in the shade.</gloss> <gloss>Tao Hongjing says:</gloss> Zigui belongs to Jianping Prefecture. Now the best comes from Pengcheng. Pengzhou also has it. Only those that are deep in color and firm are good. **Huang** <gloss>Collection: Common formulas use it often. Taoists do not require it. Feng says: Those now from Yizhou, Kuochuan, and Jingzhou are excellent. Those with solid exteriors are also good and called "Pure Tail Scutellaria."</gloss> **Su Song says:** Nowadays, it is found in all nearby prefectures of Sichuan, Hedong, and Shaanxi. The seedlings are over a foot long. The stems are as thick as chopsticks. The leaves grow in clusters from the ground on four sides, resembling Gromwell. It grows about a foot high. Some have single stems. The leaves are slender, long, and green, growing in opposite pairs. In the sixth month, it blooms with purple flowers. The root is like Anemarrhena, four to five inches long. Collect the roots in the second and eighth months and dry in the sun. Wu Pu’s Materia Medica says: In the second month, it grows reddish-yellow leaves in opposite pairs on four sides. The stem is hollow or square, three to four feet high. In the fourth month, the flowers are purplish-red. In the sixth month, the seeds are black and the root is yellow. Collect from the second to the ninth month.
