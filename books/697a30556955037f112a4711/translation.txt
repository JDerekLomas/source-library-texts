## THE
# Translator's PREFACE.

The following work <note>original: "Piece"</note> is an account of the *Rosicrucian Doctrine of Spirits*.

Monsieur Bayle † informs us that it was published in Paris by the famous Abbot de Villars in the year 1670. He adds that some have been of the opinion that *The Count de Gabalis* was based on two Italian chemical letters written by Borri; others affirm that Borri took the main ideas in his letters from this work. However, Monsieur Bayle leaves the debate over this point to those who have a deeper scholarly interest <note>original: "critically curious"</note>.

The present revival of this book was inspired by *The Rape of the Lock*; in the dedication of that poem, Mr. Pope gave his opinion that the best account he knew of the Rosicrucian system is in this treatise <note>original: "Tract"</note>. We do not doubt that this will be a sufficient recommendation of it to the public.

The following is a *new translation* from the *Paris edition*, which is now very hard to find. Some *notes have been interspersed* to better explain several passages and authors mentioned in the text.

He died in the Castle of Sant' Angelo <note>The Castel Sant'Angelo in Rome served as a papal fortress and prison.</note> in 1695 at 79 years old.
