# THE
## Translator’s PREFACE.

*The following work is an account of the* Rosicrucian Doctrine of Spirits.
*Mr.* Bayle <note>Pierre Bayle (1647–1706) was a prominent French philosopher known for his "Historical and Critical Dictionary," a massive work that influenced many Enlightenment thinkers.</note> *informs us that it was published in* Paris *by the celebrated Abbot* de Villars <note>Henri de Montfaucon de Villars (1635–1673), a French priest and author whose satirical take on the occult eventually led to his mysterious murder.</note>, *in the year 1670. He adds that some have been of the opinion that* The Count de Gabalis <note>original: "Le Comte de Gabalis"</note> *was originally based upon two* Italian Chemical Letters *written by* Borri; *others affirm that* Borri *took the main ideas in his letters from this work. However, Mr.* Bayle *leaves the debate of this point to those who are more curious about critical details.*
*The current revival of it was inspired by* The Rape of the Lock <note>Alexander Pope’s famous 1712 mock-epic poem. Pope used the "elemental spirits" from this book (sylphs, gnomes, etc.) as the supernatural "machinery" of his poem.</note>; *in the dedication of that poem, Mr.* Pope *has given his opinion that the best account he knew of the* Rosicrucian system *is in this* treatise <note>original: "Tract"</note>. *We do not doubt that this will be a sufficient recommendation of it to the* public.
*The following is a new translation from the* Paris *edition, which is now very difficult to find; there are some notes interspersed to better illustrate several passages and the authors referred to.*

This sect is of German origin <note>original: "Extraction"</note> and was originally styled *Rose-Croix* or *Rosicrucians*; they were also called the *Enlightened*, *Immortal*, and *Invisible*. This name was given to a specific fraternity or secret group <note>original: "Cabal"</note> that appeared in Germany at the beginning of the 17th century <note>original: "XVIIth Age"</note>. Those who are admitted into it, called the *Brethren* or *Rosicrucians*, swear fidelity, promise secrecy, write enigmatically (or in coded characters), and obligate themselves to observe the laws of that society. This society has as its goal the re-establishment of all academic disciplines and sciences—especially *medicine* <note>original: "Physick"</note> which, according to their view, is misunderstood and poorly practiced.
