## Now, the rules for the results of <term>Planetary Transits</term> are described
<note>original: "Atha gocaraphalavidhirucyate". *Gochara* refers to the transit of planets through the zodiac relative to the position of the Moon at the time of a person's birth.</note>

When the **Sun** <note>original: "Dinakara," meaning "The Day-Maker."</note> is in the house of birth (1st), he causes the loss of one's place or residence; in the second, he causes fear; in the third <note>original: "Duschikya," a technical term for the 3rd house.</note>, he expands <margin>sar</margin> wealth; in the fourth <note>original: "Hibuka," a term derived from Greek *hypogeion*, referring to the 4th house/lower midheaven.</note>, he grants the loss of honor; in the fifth, he creates misery; in the sixth, he is a destroyer of enemies; in the seventh, he is a destroyer of wealth; in the eighth, he causes pain to a person; and in the ninth, he causes the loss of one's radiance or luster. 
<margin>Dharma 1</margin> <note>original: "Dharma" is the 9th house; the "1" likely marks the end of a section or a specific verse.</note> 
When the Sun enters the house of action (10th), he is the producer of success in work; in the house of gain (11th), he provides much wealth and property; and when staying in the house of expenditure (12th), he grants great calamity arising from the loss of wealth. 
<margin>the Sun 2</margin>

Now, the **Moon** <note>original: "Chandra" or "Himaghu," the "Cool-Rayed One."</note>: In the house of birth, the Moon [indicates prosperity], but in the second, he shows the loss of wealth; in the third house, he gives property; in the fourth, he causes diseases of the stomach; in the fifth house <note>original: "Tanaya-griha," the house of children.</note>, he causes the failure of one's work; in the sixth, he brings gain of wealth; in the seventh <note>original: "Dyuna," the house of partnership.</note>, he brings wealth along with a wife; in the eighth, even though he is the Moon, he brings death <note>The 8th house is traditionally associated with longevity and death.</note>. 3. The ninth house causes fear of the King <note>In historical contexts, this often refers to legal trouble or government authorities.</note>; having arrived <gloss>m</gloss> <note>The gloss "m" likely corrects the Sanskrit grammar of the verb.</note> in the tenth, he brings attainment; in the eleventh, he creates happiness; and in the house of expenditure (12th), he brings sickness and the destruction of wealth. 4.

Now, **Mars** <note>original: "Kuja" or "Kshonī-sūnu," the "Son of the Earth."</note>: Arriving in the first house, the Son of the Earth creates fear arising from enemies; in the house of wealth (2nd), he consumes wealth; in the third, he is the giver of [wealth]; in the "abyss" (4th) <note>original: "Patala," the 4th house.</note>, he causes fear from enemies; <unclear>residing</unclear> in the fifth, or in the house of enemies (6th), he creates wealth and sickness; in the house of passion (7th) <note>original: "Madana," the 7th house.</note>, 5. he generates obstacles to one's life-force; staying in the house of death (8th), the Earth-born Mars indicates excessive body pain; staying in the ninth, he is auspicious; staying in the tenth or in the house of gain (11th), he brings great profit; and arriving in the house of expenditure (12th), he causes the destruction of wealth and health through disease. 6.

Now, the **Knower** <note>original: "Jna," an epithet for **Mercury** (Budha).</note>: Mercury in the first indicates fear; in the house of wealth (2nd), he brings imprisonment or wealth, yet accompanied by fear of enemies; in the third or fourth, he becomes the giver of unfavorable results; in the house of children (5th), he grants a place amongst enemies; in the house of passion (7th), he causes various kinds of physical calamities. 7. In the eighth, the Son of the Moon <note>original: "Shashi-suta," another name for Mercury.</note> causes an increase in wealth; in the house of righteousness (9th), he causes great bodily pain; in the house of action (10th), he brings happiness and wealth to one's children; in the house of income (11th) [he gives gain]; and in the house of expenditure (12th), he brings the destruction of wealth. 8. **Jupiter** <note>original: "Guru," the Preceptor of the Gods, associated with wisdom and expansion.</note>: When in the house of birth (1st), he causes fear; in the house of wealth (2nd), he brings about incomparable riches. In the third, he indicates distress and pain, and in the fourth, the disappearance of wealth. In the place of sons (5th), he grants happiness, yet in the house of enemies (6th), he causes sickness. In the seventh <note>original: "Dyuna," the house of partnerships and marriage.</note>, he brings worship by kings; however, in the house of death (8th), he causes the destruction of accumulated wealth. In the house of merit (9th), he causes the increase of wealth; in the tenth, the "Teacher of the Gods" <note>original: "Surapujya," a title for Jupiter.</note> creates affection. In the house of gain (11th), he gives status and riches, but when standing in the twelfth, he brings physical pain to a person. 10 

Now, **Venus** <note>original: "Bhrigu" or "Shukra," the planet of beauty, luxury, and art.</note>: In the birth house, the son of Bhrigu destroys enemies; in the second house, he gives wealth. In the third house, he grants sons; in the fourth, he brings an intellect focused on enemies <note>original: "ribuddhi," possibly meaning a strategic mind or anxiety regarding rivals.</note>. In the seventh <note>original: "Madane," the house of passion.</note>, he bestows sorrow, but in the eighth, he is a giver of wealth. 11 

In the house of merit (9th), he generates various types of fine garments; staying in the tenth, Venus is a bringer of happiness. When situated in the house of gain (11th), he accumulates wealth, and even when gone to the house of expenditure (12th), he facilitates the arrival of money. 12 

Now, **Saturn** <note>original: "Shani" or "Dinakara-tanaye," meaning "The Son of the Sun." Saturn is traditionally viewed as a stern, karmic influence.</note>: When entering the birth sign, the Son of the Sun causes confusion of the mind. In the second, there is mental distress caused by the theft of wealth. In the third, there is the gain of riches. In the bottom of the sky <note>original: "Patala," the 4th house/nadir.</note>, there is an intellect plagued by enemies; in the house of children (5th), there is the destruction of sons, servants, and wealth. In the sixth place, he generates the gain of wealth; in the seventh, it is a path of connecting with faults or conflicts. 13 

In the eighth, he causes physical pain; in the ninth, the loss of wealth; and in the house of action (10th), he brings ill-will and depression. In the eleventh <note>original: "Upantya," the house of gains.</note>, he gives wealth and eliminates misfortune; thus the eloquent speak. 14 

Now, **Rahu** <note>original: The North Node of the Moon, a shadow planet representing obsession and sudden change.</note>: Rahu in the birth house causes fear, quarrels, and the loss of fortune and honor; he also brings mental confusion and then great happiness <margin>Kam 1</margin>. He gives fear of kings and the loss of wealth, as well as the heat of sorrow and conflict. For men, he causes the quick destruction of wealth. The sages **Garga** and others declare that the results for **Ketu** <note>the South Node of the Moon.</note> should be spoken of as identical to Rahu. 15 

This concludes the **Planetary Transits**. <note>original: "Iti Graha Gocharam."</note>
