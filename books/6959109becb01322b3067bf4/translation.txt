# PREFACE

The **Cape of Good Hope** is said to be that furthest corner of **Africa**, which extends as far as 33 degrees, 35 minutes latitude and 35 degrees, 2 minutes longitude, according to the *Observations* of **Mr. de la Caille** <note>Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille (1713–1762) was a French astronomer who famously mapped the stars of the southern hemisphere from the Cape.</note>, in the southern hemisphere.

This promontory was first discovered by **Bartholomew Dias**, a *Portuguese* explorer, around the year 1487; it was visited again several years later by *Vasco de Gama*. The *Dutch* <note>original: "Belgæ"</note>, however, after a century and a half had passed, fixed their settlements here—the first of all *Europeans* to do so—following the advice of the Surgeon **van Riebeck** <note>Jan van Riebeeck, who established the Dutch East India Company colony in 1652</note>. ## THE SOIL.

The SOIL here, as in all of Africa, is arid and generally sterile, sandy, clay-like, and not a little mountainous.

Deep sand, often shifting, covers all the maritime coasts; in the spring time these are mostly inundated by the winter rain, but in summer they are parched, and destitute of almost all water and inhabitants.
