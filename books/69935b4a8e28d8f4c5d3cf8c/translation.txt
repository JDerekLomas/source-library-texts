# Aitareya Upanishad

May my speech be established in my mind; may my mind be established in my speech. O Self-luminous One, reveal Yourself to me. May you both <note>referring to mind and speech</note> be the carriers of the Veda for me. May what I have heard not depart from me. I join day and night through this study. I shall speak the <term>Rita</term> <note>the cosmic or natural order</note>. I shall speak the truth. 
May That protect me. May That protect the teacher. May That protect me. May That protect the teacher, protect the teacher.
->Om Peace, Peace, Peace!<-

In the beginning, this universe was the Self <term>Atman</term> <note>The Supreme Soul or Singular Consciousness</note> alone. There was nothing else whatsoever that blinked. He thought: "Let me now create the worlds." 1

He created these worlds: the high waters (<term>ambhas</term>), the light-rays (<term>marichi</term>), the mortal realm (<term>mara</term>), and the lower waters (<term>apas</term>). That "high water" is beyond the heavens; the sky is its support. The "light-rays" are the atmosphere. The "mortal realm" is the earth. What is beneath it are the "lower waters." 2

He thought: "These are the worlds. Now, let me create the guardians of the worlds." From the waters themselves, He drew forth the Person <term>Purusha</term> <note>The cosmic, archetypal human form</note> and gave him shape. 3

He brooded over him <note>used heat/austerity, known as tapas</note>. As He was brooding over him, a mouth burst forth, just like an egg. From the mouth came speech, and from speech, Fire. The nostrils burst forth; from the nostrils came the breath, and from the breath, the Air. The eyes burst forth; from the eyes came sight, and from sight, the Sun. The ears burst forth; from the ears came hearing, and from hearing, the Directions of Space. The skin burst forth; from the skin came hair, and from the hair, the plants and trees. The heart burst forth; from the heart came the mind, and from the mind, the Moon. The navel burst forth; from the navel came the downward breath (<term>apana</term>), and from the downward breath, Death. The generative organ burst forth; from the organ came seed, and from the seed, the Waters. 4

->Thus ends the first section. 1<-

These deities, once created, fell into this great ocean <note>symbolizing the vast, chaotic state of existence before being housed in bodies</note>. He subjected that Person to hunger and thirst. The deities said to Him: "Find us a dwelling place where we may be established and eat food." 1

He brought a cow to them. They said: "Indeed, this is not enough for us." 2

He brought a man <term>Purusha</term> <note>The human form, seen here as the crown of creation</note> to them. They said, "Well done, indeed!" <note>original: "sukrtam bata". Fire, becoming speech, entered the mouth. Air, becoming breath, entered the nostrils. The Sun, becoming sight, entered the eyes. The Directions, becoming hearing, entered the ears. Herbs and trees, becoming hairs, entered the skin. The Moon, becoming the mind, entered the heart. Death, becoming the out-breath <term>apana</term>, entered the navel. The Waters, becoming semen, entered the generative organ. 4

Hunger and Thirst said to Him <note>the Creator</note>, "Assign a place for us." He said to them, "I assign you a place within these very deities; I make you sharers with them." Therefore, to whatever deity an offering is made, Hunger and Thirst become sharers in it. <note>This explains why human physiological needs like hunger are inextricably linked to our sensory and vital functions.</note> 5

He thought, "Here now are the worlds and the guardians of the worlds. Let me create food for them." 1

He brooded over the waters <note>used the heat of consciousness/austerity</note>. From the waters thus brooded over, a form was born. That form which was born is indeed food. 2

Having been created, this food tried to run away. He <note>the first embodied being</note> tried to seize it with speech. He was not able to seize it with speech. If he had seized it with speech, then by merely uttering the name of food, one would have been satisfied. 3

He tried to seize it with the breath. He was not able to seize it with the breath. If he had seized it with the breath, then by merely smelling food, one would have been satisfied. 4

In man, indeed, this <note>the Self</note> first becomes an embryo. That which is the seed <term>retas</term> is the strength and vigor <term>tejas</term> gathered from all the limbs of the body. One bears this self within one's own self. When he pours this into a woman, he causes it to be born. This is his first birth. 1

It becomes one with the woman’s self, just as her own limb does. Therefore, it does not hurt her. She nourishes this self of his that has entered into her. 2

She, the nourisher, must be nourished. The woman bears the embryo. In the period before the child's birth, the father nourishes it; by nourishing the child from the moment of birth onward, he is actually nourishing his own self, for the continuation of these worlds. For thus are these worlds continued. This is his second birth. 3

This self <note>the son</note>, being the representative of the father, is substituted for him for the performance of pious deeds. Then his other self <note>the father</note>, having performed his duties and reached the end of his lifespan, departs from this world. Departing from here, he is born again. This is his third birth. 4

Concerning this, it was said by the Seer <term>Rishi</term>:
"While I was yet in the womb, I knew all the births of the gods. A hundred iron citadels <note>original: "pur ayasir"; a metaphor for the constraints of the physical body and previous lives</note> held me down, but like a hawk, I forced my way out with speed." Vamadeva spoke this even while lying in the womb. 5

He <note>Vamadeva</note>, knowing this, ascended aloft after the destruction of the body. In that heavenly world <term>svarga</term>, he obtained all desires and became immortal—yes, he became immortal. 6

May you both <note>referring to speech and mind</note> be the <term>carriers</term> <note>original: "anisthah"; literally the two pins of an axle that keep the wheels of knowledge moving</note> of the **Veda** <note>the sacred knowledge of the scriptures</note> to me. May what I have heard not depart from me. I shall join together day and night through this study. I shall speak of the **Divine Law** <note>original: "rtam"; the inherent order of the universe</note>; I shall speak of the **Truth**.
>
> May That protect me; may That protect the speaker. May It protect me; may It protect the speaker—protect the speaker.
>
->ॐ Peace! Peace! Peace!<-

In the beginning, this universe was the <term>Atman</term> <note>the Supreme Self or Soul</note> alone. There was nothing else whatsoever that winked <note>original: "mishat"; refers to any living or moving thing</note>. He thought: "Let me now create the worlds." 1

He created these worlds: **Ambhas** <note>the celestial waters beyond the sky</note>, **Marici** <note>the region of light or the atmosphere</note>, **Mara** <note>the earth, the realm of mortals</note>, and **Apas** <note>the subterranean waters</note>. That which is beyond the heavens is *Ambhas*; the heavens are its support. The atmosphere is *Marici*. The earth is *Mara*. The waters that are below it are *Apas*. 2

He thought: "These are the worlds. Now, let me create the guardians of the worlds." From the waters themselves, He drew forth the <term>Purusha</term> <note>the Primal Person or Cosmic Being</note> and gave him a shape. 3

He brooded over him <note>original: "abhyatapat"; used here as a creative heat or intense contemplation</note>. As he was being brooded over, a mouth burst forth, much like an egg. From the mouth came **speech**, and from speech, **Fire** <note>original: "Agni"</note>. Two nostrils burst forth; from the nostrils came the **breath**, and from the breath, **Air** <note>original: "Vayu"</note>. Two eyes burst forth; from the eyes came **sight**, and from sight, the **Sun** <note>original: "Aditya"</note>. Two ears burst forth; from the ears came **hearing**, and from hearing, the **Quarters of Space** <note>the cardinal directions</note>. The skin burst forth; from the skin came **hairs**, and from the hairs, **herbs and trees**. The heart burst forth; from the heart came the **mind**, and from the mind, the **Moon**. The navel burst forth; from the navel came the **down-breath** <note>original: "apana"</note>, and from the down-breath, **Death**. The generative organ burst forth; from the organ came **seed**, and from the seed, **Water**. 4

->Thus ends the First Section<-

Those deities, once created, fell into this great salty ocean <note>symbolizing the cycle of worldly existence</note>. He <note>the Creator</note> subjected the Person to hunger and thirst. Then those deities said to Him: "Find for us a dwelling place in which we may rest and eat food." 1

He brought a cow to them. They said: "Indeed, this is not enough for us." 2
