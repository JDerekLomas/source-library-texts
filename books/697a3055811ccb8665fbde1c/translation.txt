## Preface.

This little philosophical treatise! Dear favorable reader, came to me from a good friend who received it many years ago from an old library. He requested that—since in these troubled times <note>Likely referring to the social and academic disruption of the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648).</note> almost all studies lie dormant—the noble **Chemistry** <note>original: "Chymia"</note> should not be extinguished, but rather, for the special benefit and comfort of the **Medical and Chemical Commonwealth**, I should commit it to the press. For whatever they seek in all of nature and desire to learn, they shall find in this little treatise. It provides instruction that, above all things, they should begin such work with the fear of God, true repentance, the practice of Christian love, fasting and prayer, sobriety and moderation; for this little treatise, being full of Christian parables and exhortations, may well be called **CHEMICAL THEOLOGY**.

And so that the Christian- and art-loving artist <note>The "artist" here refers to the alchemist or practitioner of the "Hermetic Art."</note> may better and more clearly understand what is contained herein, that same person has attached to the end of this treatise a sketch of the **secret Furnace of the World, bearing an image of the World itself** <note>original: "Mundi præfererens imaginem ipsisfimi"</note> (of which only brief, dark, and piecemeal descriptions are found in the first volume of the *Theatrum Chemicum* <note>A massive compendium of alchemical writings first published in 1602.</note> regarding the **Art of Metallic Transformation** <note>original: "de arte metallorũ Metamorphof."</note> on pages 36 and 221, and elsewhere in many philosophical books). This is intended to encourage you to further contemplate these high matters. For to the inexperienced, it is quite impossible and unbelievable what noble medicines—as God's wonderful gifts—can be brought about and united within it, such as Air, Water, Fire, Salt, Oil, Spirits of Wine, or even the common **Spirit of the World** <note>original: "Spiritum mundi"; a hypothetical universal essence thought to permeate all matter.</note> into a stone, salt, powder, or earth. This is not to say that one must necessarily have such a motivating instrument in the **Philosophical Work** <note>The process of creating the Philosopher's Stone.</note>; no, for even without it, those with great and sweet ingenuity know well how to reduce and unite **pure things with pure things** <note>original: "pura puris"</note> through an **amicable conjunction, not a hostile or destructive one** <note>original: "conjũctione amicabili non inimicabili vel deftructiva"</note> into a new, clear, transparent **body**, and to bring it into a **Palingenesis** <term>Palingenesis: A term for rebirth or reconstruction, often used in alchemy to describe the process of a substance being destroyed and then "resurrected" in a more perfect form.</term> and rebirth. However, what those others can scarcely bring to an end in one, two, or more years, they can circulate and accomplish in this **mystic furnace** <note>original: "furno miftyco"</note> in one month or six weeks, thereby gaining time without special effort. This has never been brought to the light of day so clearly by any philosopher since the world began. May you be content with this and reflect and strive after these things yourself in the fear of God; then I do not doubt you will find and hit the right mark. It might also happen, if the dear God grants grace, peace, and a healthy body, that these great secrets may be further elucidated and described in another, larger treatise. God be with you.

# CHAPTER I.

He who walks in the fear of God and acts accordingly, his praise remains forever; therefore, rightly, no wisdom, knowledge, or art is or can be true which does not have and take its origin from the fear of God. ### To Momus and Zoilus <note>Momus was the Greek god of mockery and Zoilus was a grammarian known as a "scourge" of Homer; together they represent cynical, nitpicking critics.</note>.

Many a clever-clogs and know-it-all might well pull apart this little treatise in a "masterly" fashion—or even, since the noble, true <term>Chemistry</term> <note>original: "Chymia"</note> is not known to many, despise and destroy it to the highest degree. This book was published in open print like a farewell gift, intended solely for the God-fearing lovers of the Art, out of a true heart, so they may learn, recognize, and follow God's glorious, wondrous works and rich blessings, to find special comfort in these troubled times, and to nourish themselves honestly through the work of their own hands.

Yet such a critic cannot erase his own shame and mockery, for while he might know how to extract "white water," oil, and salt from herbs—separating the pure from the impure—he does not have enough wit in his little brain <note>original: "cerebell"</note> to know how to reduce and unite these three elements again into a clear, spiritual body. This body would then be a living, penetrating medicine, or <term>Universal Cure</term> <note>original: "Panacea"</note>, quite pleasant in taste and light in weight, to be taken in whatever medium one wishes; it works as if by a miracle, to the honor, praise, and glory of God, and for the benefit of one's neighbor.

Since he does not know even this small thing, how then does he intend to produce from <term>plants</term> <note>original: "Vegetabilibus"</note>—according to how each is subject to its own <term>Star</term> <note>original: "Astro", referring to the astrological influence believed to govern specific plants.</note>—by means of <term>decay</term> <note>original: "putrefactione", a vital stage in alchemy where matter is broken down to be reborn.</note> alone, all kinds of metals, both fixed and non-fixed <note>original: "tam fixa, quam non fixa". "Fixed" metals like gold withstand fire; "non-fixed" metals are volatile or easily changed.</note>, even better than Nature herself? This is true, truly true. But, there is no disputing those who deny first principles <note>original: "Sed, contra negantes principia, non est disputandum." A common Latin philosophical maxim.</note>. To this they simply say, "where is the proof?" Yes, indeed—as if roasted pigeons would just fly into one's mouth! <note>A German idiom for expecting success or rewards without putting in any effort.</note>
