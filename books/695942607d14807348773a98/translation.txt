## PREFACE TO THE SECOND EDITION.

It has been at the earnest wish of many students, members of the Theosophical Society, and of my Hermetic brothers <note>original: "fratres"</note> of the Order of the <term>G.D.</term> <note>The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, a late 19th-century magical society.</note>, that I have made a revised translation of the “<term>Sepher Yetzirah</term>” <note>The "Book of Formation," one of the oldest and most important books of Jewish mysticism (Kabbalah).</note>. In this edition, I have followed most closely the Hebrew version of Joannes Stephanus Rittangelius, which was printed in 1642 at Amsterdam. I have abandoned many of the Latinized renderings which had been adopted in the First Edition from the works of Postellus and Pistorius.

The Notes have been expanded, and it is hoped that they explain and illustrate difficult passages, making this complex <note>original: "abstruse"</note> treatise more understandable than it has been found until now.

->W. W. W., F.T.S.<- <note>William Wynn Westcott, Fellow of the Theosophical Society.</note>

## INTRODUCTION.

---

The "Sepher Yetzirah," <note>original: "Sepher Yetzirah," meaning "Book of Formation"</note> or "Book of Formation," is perhaps the oldest philosophical treatise still in existence written in the Hebrew language. The great interest shown in recent years regarding the Hebrew Kabbalah <note>The Kabbalah is a traditional system of Jewish mysticism and self-knowledge</note> and its related schools of thought has led me to translate this volume from the original Hebrew texts, comparing them with the Latin versions produced by medieval scholars. Three important books of the "Zohar," or "Splendor"—which serves as the primary storehouse of Kabbalistic teaching—have been translated into English for the first time by the skillful and learned Kabbalist MacGregor Mathers, my fellow student in the occult sciences. This English translation of the "Sepher Yetzirah" is an almost essential companion to those even more difficult and complex discussions; indeed, the two books explain one another.

Although its name means "The Book of Formation," the "Sepher Yetzirah" is not a narrative of Creation in the sense of a replacement for the Book of Genesis. Instead, it is a very ancient and educational philosophical treatise on one specific perspective regarding the origin of the universe and humanity—a perspective that is both ancient and characteristically Hebrew. This method of organizing the processes of origin into a system that is simultaneously alphabetical and numerical is a feature found only among Semitic authors.

Hebrew tradition assigns the doctrines of the oldest portions of the *Zohar* <note>The "Zohar" or "Book of Splendor" is the foundational work in the literature of Jewish mystical thought known as Kabbalah.</note> to a date before the building of the Second Temple. However, Rabbi Simeon ben Jochai, who lived during the reign of the Emperor Titus (70–80 <term>A.D.</term>), is considered to have been the first to commit these teachings to writing. Furthermore, Rabbi Moses de Leon of Guadalajara, Spain, who died in 1305, certainly reproduced and published the *Zohar*.

Ginsburg <note>Christian David Ginsburg (1831–1914), a noted scholar of the Masorah and Kabbalah.</note>, speaking of the Zoharic doctrines of the *Ain Suph* <note>original: "Ain Suph" — The Infinite or Boundless; the divine origin of all existence.</note>, says that they were unknown until the 13th century. However, he does not deny the great antiquity of the *Sepher Yetzirah* <note>The "Book of Formation," one of the earliest surviving books on Jewish mysticism.</note>, in which it will be noticed the *Ain Suph Aur* <note>original: "Ain Suph Aur" — The Limitless Light.</note> and *Ain Suph* are not mentioned.

I suggest, however, that this omission is no proof that the doctrines of the Limitless Light and the Infinite did not then exist. It is a reasonable supposition that the *Sepher Yetzirah* was the volume assigned to the Yetziratic World (the third of the four Kabbalistic Worlds of Emanation). Meanwhile, the *Asch Metzareph* <note>The "Purifying Fire," a Kabbalistic-alchemical text.</note> is concerned with the Assiatic World—the fourth or lowest World of Shells—and is clearly an alchemical treatise. Again, the *Siphra Dzenioutha* <note>The "Book of Concealed Mystery."</note> may be fittingly considered an Aziluthic work, treating of the Emanations of Deity alone. There was doubtless a fourth work assigned to the World of Briah (the second type of world), but I have not been able to identify this treatise.

Both the Babylonian and the Jerusalem Talmuds refer to the *Sepher Yetzirah*. Their treatise named *Sanhedrin* <note>A section of the Talmud dealing with law and the legal assembly.</note> certainly mentions the "Book of Formation" and another similar work. Rashi <note>The famous medieval French rabbi and commentator (1040–1105).</note>, in his commentary on the treatise *Erubin*, considers this a reliable historical notice. This work, then, or a similar predecessor, is at least as old as 200 <term>A.D.</term>

Other positive historical notices are those of Saadjah Gaon, who died in 940 <term>A.D.</term>, and Judah Halevi, who lived around 1150 <term>A.D.</term>

Both of these Hebrew classics speak of it as a very ancient work.

The most generally accepted modern opinion is that the author was Rabbi Akiba, who lived in the time of the Emperor Hadrian, around 120 A.D.

Graetz, however, assigns it to early <term>Gnostic</term> <note>A diverse group of early religious movements that emphasized secret spiritual knowledge.</note> times, in the third or fourth century. Zunz speaks of it as post-<term>Talmudical</term> <note>Written after the completion of the Talmud, the central text of Rabbinic Judaism.</note> and belonging to the <term>Geonic period</term> <note>The era of the Jewish spiritual leaders and scholars in Babylonia, roughly 600 to 1000 A.D.</note> from 700 to 800 A.D. Rubinsohn, in the "Sacred Library" <note>original: "Bibliotheca Sacra"</note>, speaks of this latter idea as having no real basis.

The <term>Talmuds</term> <note>The collections of Jewish law and tradition.</note> were first collected into a concrete whole and printed in Venice in 1520 A.D.

The "Zohar" <note>The foundational work of Jewish mysticism, or Kabbalah.</note> was first printed in Mantua in 1558, again in Cremona in 1560, and at Lublin in 1623; a fourth edition was published by Knorr von Rosenroth at Sulzbach in 1684. Some parts are not very ancient, since some versions mention the Crusades.

Six surviving Hebrew editions of the "Sepher Yetzirah" were collected and printed at Lemberg in 1680. The oldest of these six <term>recensions</term> <note>A revised or critical version of a text.</note> was that of Saadjah Gaon.

Commentaries by Judah Halevi and by Eben Ezra, from the 12th century, are also known.

There are now to be found in the best libraries several Latin versions, namely: that of Guillaume Postel <note>original: "Gulielmus Postellus"</note>, 1552, Paris; one by Johann Pistorius in his "Volume of the Cabalistic Art" <note>original: "Artis Cabalisticæ Tomus"</note>, 1587, Basel; and a third by Joannes Stephanus Rittangelius, 1642, Amsterdam. This last version provides both the Hebrew and Latin texts, and also includes the "Thirty-Two Paths" as a supplement.

There is also a good German translation by Johann Friedrich von Meyer, dated 1830. Quite recently, and since the completion of my translation, my attention has been drawn to a version by Isidor Kalisch, in which he has reproduced many of the valuable annotations of Meyer. The edition which I now offer is fundamentally that of the ancient Hebrew <term>codices</term> <note>Manuscript volumes of ancient texts.</note> translated into English, and compared with the Latin versions of Pistorius, Postel, and Rittangelius.

“*Commended to writing with the deepest understanding.*” <note>original Latin: profundissimis sensibus literis commendatus.</note> — Johannes Pistorius. <note>Johannes Pistorius (1546–1608) was a German physician and theologian who published a famous collection of Kabbalistic texts.</note>

The “Sepher Yetzirah” <note>Hebrew: "Book of Formation"</note> consists of six chapters, containing 33 paragraphs distributed among them in this manner: the first has 12, followed by 5, 5, 4, 3, and 4.

The oldest title includes an addition: “The Letters of our Father Abraham” or “ascribed to the patriarch Abraham.” It is described as such by many medieval authorities, but this origin is undoubtedly legendary. However, it is perhaps no more improbable than the supposed authorship of the “Book of Enoch,” mentioned by Saint Jude in the Bible and rescued in modern times from the wilderness of Ethiopia by the great traveler James Bruce.

In essence, the work was likely the crystallization of centuries of tradition by a single writer. It has been expanded from time to time by later authors who also revised it. Some of these additions, which were rejected even by medieval students, I have not included in the text at all. I present in this volume only the undisputed kernel of this occult nut—a work upon which many great authorities (Hebrew, German, Jesuit, and others) have written long commentaries, yet have failed to explain satisfactorily.

I find Kalisch <note>Isidor Kalisch (1816–1886), a prominent rabbi and scholar who produced an English translation of the Sepher Yetzirah in 1877.</note> speaking of these commentaries, saying:

> “They contain nothing but a medley of arbitrary explanations, sophistical distortions of scriptural verses, astrological notions, oriental superstitions, a metaphysical jargon, a poor knowledge of physics, and not a correct elucidation of this ancient book.”

Kalisch, however, was not an occultist. These commentaries are so extensive that they require years of study, and I feel no hesitation in confessing that my researches into them have been only superficial.

For convenience of study, I have placed the Notes in a separate section at the end of the work, and I have provided a short definition of the subject matter of each chapter.

## CHAPTER II.

**SECTION 1.** The twenty-two sounds and letters are the foundation of all things. They consist of three "mothers," seven "doubles," and twelve "simples" <note>These categories refer to the phonetic properties of the Hebrew alphabet and their corresponding astrological or elemental associations.</note>. The Three Mothers are *Aleph* (א), *Mem* (מ), and *Shin* (ש); they represent Air, Water, and Fire. Water is silent <note>The sound of Mem is a closed-lip hum.</note>, Fire is sibilant <note>The sound of Shin is a hissing "sh" sound.</note>, and Air—derived from the Spirit—is like the tongue of a balance standing between these opposites. It maintains equilibrium, reconciling and mediating between them.

2. He has formed, weighed, and composed with these twenty-two letters every soul, and the soul of everything that shall ever exist in the future.

3. These twenty-two sounds or letters are formed by the voice, impressed upon the air, and audibly modified in five places: in the throat, in the mouth, by the tongue, through the teeth, and by the lips <margin>The five phonetic classifications of Hebrew letters: gutturals, palatals, linguals, dentals, and labials.</margin>.

4. These twenty-two letters, which are the foundation of all things, He arranged as if upon a sphere with two hundred and thirty-one gates. This sphere may be rotated forward or backward, whether for good or for evil; from the good comes true pleasure, and from evil comes nothing but torment.

5. For He showed the combination of these letters, each with the other: *Aleph* with all, and all with *Aleph*; *Beth* with all, and all with *Beth*. Thus, by combining all the letters together in pairs, the two hundred and thirty-one gates of knowledge are produced <note>This refers to the mathematical combinations of the 22 letters taken two at a time (22 × 21 / 2 = 231).</note>.

6. And from the non-existent <note>original: *Ayin*, meaning "nothingness" or the primordial void.</note> He made "Something" <note>original: *Yesh*, meaning "existence" or "substance."</note>; He created all forms of speech and everything that has been produced. From the empty void He made the material world, and from the inert earth He brought forth everything that has life. He hewed, as it were, vast columns out of the intangible air, and by the power of His Name made every creature and everything that exists. The production of all things from the twenty-two letters is the proof that they are all merely parts of one single body <note>This suggests a monistic view where all of creation is a unified whole emerging from the divine alphabet.</note>.

## CHAPTER III.

SECTION 1. The foundation of all others is composed of the Three Mothers: Aleph, Mem, and Shin. They resemble a balance; on one side are the guilty, on the other side are the purified, and Aleph—representing the Air—is like the tongue of the balance standing between them. <note>The "tongue" refers to the central pointer of a scale that determines equilibrium.</note>

2. The Three Mothers—Aleph, Mem, and Shin—are a great mystery, most wonderful and deeply hidden, sealed as if with six rings. From them proceed Air, Fire, and Water, which divide into male and female forces. These Three Mothers are the foundation; from them spring three "Fathers," <note>In Kabbalistic thought, the "Fathers" usually refer to the primary elements or planetary forces derived from the Mothers.</note> and from these have proceeded all things that exist in the world.

3. The Three Mothers in the world are Aleph, Mem, and Shin. The heavens were produced from Fire; the earth was produced from Water; and the Air, derived from the Spirit, acts as a reconciler between the Fire and the Water.

4. The Three Mothers—Aleph, Mem, and Shin, representing Fire, Water, and Air—are revealed in the Year. From fire came heat; from water came cold; and from air was produced the temperate state, acting again as a mediator between them. The Three Mothers—Aleph, Mem, and Shin—are also found in Man: from fire the head was formed; from water the belly was formed; and from air the chest was formed, placed once more as a mediator between the others.

5. These Three Mothers He produced, designed, and combined; and He sealed them as the three mothers in the Universe, in the Year, and in Man—both male and female. He caused the letter Aleph to rule over the Air and crowned it; then, combining it with the others, He sealed it as Air in the World, as the temperate climate of the Year, and as the chest in Man.

## CHAPTER IV.

SECTION 1. The Seven Double Letters—Beth, Gimel, Daleth, Kaph, Pe, Resh, and Tau—each have two sounds associated with them. They are linked to Life, Peace, Wisdom, Riches, Grace, Fertility, and Power. The two sounds of each letter are the hard and the soft—the aspirated and the softened <note>original: "the aspirated and the softened," referring to the dagesh (a dot in the letter) which changes the pronunciation in Hebrew</note>. They are called "Double" because each letter represents a contrast or a reversal; thus: Life and Death; Peace and War (Evil); Wisdom and Folly; Riches and Poverty; Grace and Indignation; Fertility and Solitude (Desolation); Power and Servitude.

2. These Seven Double Letters point out seven directions: Above, Below, East, West, North, South, and the Palace of Holiness in the center of them, which sustains all things.

3. These Seven Double Letters He designed, produced, and combined; with them, He formed the Planets of this Universe, the Days of the Week, and the Gates of the Soul (the sensory openings) in Man. From these Seven, He has produced the Seven Heavens, the Seven Earths, and the Seven Sabbaths. For this reason, He has loved and blessed the number Seven more than all things under Heaven (His Throne).

4. Two letters produce two houses <note>original: "houses," meaning permutations or combinations of letters</note>; three form six; four form twenty-four; five form one hundred and twenty; six form seven hundred and twenty; seven form five thousand and forty; and beyond this, their numbers increase so much that the mouth can hardly speak them, nor the ear hear the total of them. So now, behold: the Stars of the Universe (Planets) are Seven: the Sun, Venus, Mercury, the Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, and Mars. The Seven are also the Seven Days of Creation and the Seven Gateways of the Soul of Man—the two eyes, the two ears, the mouth, and the two nostrils. Thus, with the Seven are formed the seven heavens, the seven earths, and the seven periods of time; and so has He preferred the number Seven above all things under Heaven.

SECTION I. The Twelve Simple Letters are *Heh*, *Vav*, *Zayin*, *Cheth*, *Teth*, *Yod*, *Lamed*, *Nun*, *Samech*, *Ayin*, *Tzaddi*, and *Qoph*<gloss>43</gloss>: they are the foundations of these twelve properties: Sight, Hearing, Smell, Speech, Taste, Sexual Love, Work, Movement, Anger, Mirth, Imagination<gloss>44</gloss>, and Sleep. ## CHAPTER VI.

SECTION 1. There are three Fathers and their generations, seven conquerors and their armies, and twelve boundaries of the Universe. See now, the faithful witnesses of these words are the Universe, the Year, and Man. The group of twelve <note>original: "dodecad"</note>, the group of seven <note>original: "heptad"</note>, and the group of three <note>original: "triad"</note> each have their provinces. Above is the Celestial Dragon, *Teli* <note>original: "T L I"; likely referring to the constellation Draco or a cosmic axis</note>, below is the World, and lastly, there is the heart of Man. The Three are Water, Air, and Fire; Fire is above, Water is below, and Air acts as the mediator <note>original: "conciliating"</note> between them. The sign of these things is that Fire sustains and evaporates the waters; the letter *Mem* is silent, *Shin* is hissing, and *Aleph* is the Mediator, acting as a friend placed between them.

2. The Celestial Dragon, *Teli*, is placed over the universe like a king upon a throne; the revolution of the year is like a king over his dominion; the heart of man is like a king in warfare. Moreover, He made all things to correspond with one another; and God <note>original: "Elohim"</note> set good over against evil. He made good things from good, and evil things from evil: with the good He tested the evil, and with the evil did He try the good. Happiness is reserved for the good, and misery is kept for the wicked.

## “SEPHER YETZIRAH” <note>original: "Book of Formation"</note>

**IT** is of considerable importance for a clear understanding of this occult <note>original: "Occult"; meaning hidden or esoteric knowledge</note> treatise that the whole work be read through before any commentary is made. This allows the general idea of the various chapters to become a single, concrete concept in the mind. A separate consideration of the individual parts should follow this general grasp of the subject; otherwise, much confusion may result.

This book may be considered an allegorical parallel between the idealism of numbers and letters and the various parts of the universe. It sheds much light on many mystic forms and ceremonies still in existence—notably upon Freemasonry, the Tarot, and the later Kabbalah—and it is a great aid to understanding the astro-theosophic <note>systems combining astrology and divine wisdom</note> schemes of the Rosicrucians. To obtain the full value of this treatise, it should be studied alongside Hermetic rituals, the “Isiac Tablet” <note>an elaborate bronze tablet of Egyptian imagery influential in Renaissance occultism</note>, and a complete set of Tarot designs.

Note that the oldest manuscript <note>original: "MSS."</note> copies of the “Sepher Yetzirah” have no vowel points; the latest editions do have them. The system of points used in writing Hebrew was not perfected until the seventh century, and even then, it was not in constant use. Ginsburg <note>Christian David Ginsburg, a famous 19th-century Masoretic scholar</note> asserts that the system of vowel pointing was invented by a Rabbi Mocha in Palestine around 570 A.D., who designed it to assist his pupils. However, Isaac Myer <note>a prominent 19th-century American Kabbalist</note> states that there are undoubted traces of pointing in Hebrew manuscripts of the second century.

### CHAPTER I.
