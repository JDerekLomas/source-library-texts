## Narrative and Illustrated Description of Sichuan

Gui E <note>Gui E (1478–1531) was a prominent Ming Dynasty scholar-official and Grand Secretary known for his expertise in geography and administrative reform.</note> states: Sichuan is the territory of ancient Liangzhou <note>One of the Nine Provinces of ancient China, traditionally associated with the western regions.</note>. Jiange <gloss>namely Jianmen Pass, located on the northern border of Baoning</gloss> serves as the fortified stronghold of the "cloud-reaching" plank roads <term>Plank roads: Timber paths built into cliffside faces, essential for traversing the rugged mountains of Sichuan.</term>. Qutang <gloss>the gorge rocks are located in Kui Prefecture</gloss> locks the flow of the Ba Gorges. The region is bordered by the "Fan" tribes <note>original: "番族" (Fanzu), historically referring to Tibetan or other non-Han peoples to the west.</note> <gloss>forming an obstruction to the west</gloss> and by the "Man" tribal departments <note>original: "蠻部" (Manbu), a general term for indigenous ethnic groups in the south and southwest.</note> <gloss>referring to Dongchuan, Wumeng, Wusa, and Mangbu</gloss>. The mountains and rivers bind the land like a collar and sash, forming a natural protective fence. Thus, throughout history, ambitious men...

### Surveying XI (Continued)

If this region is held as a separate stronghold, bandit armies dare not cast their eyes westward; the land is fertile and the mountain passes are so well-defended that preparations are complete. However, if internal treachery arises, the terrain becomes a hindrance: "suspending carriages and binding horses" <term>Suspended carriages and bound horses: A classical idiom describing mountain paths so steep and narrow that carriages must be hoisted by ropes and horses' legs must be wrapped to prevent slipping.</term>. In such conditions, forces are unable to reach one another, making it difficult to suppress sudden uprisings. Furthermore, imperial princes are stationed here, and heavy garrisons of troops are sent to the frontiers; all their provisions and supplies are drawn from this land.

Moreover, the city of Songpan is an isolated outpost situated deep within the territories of the "Fan" <note>original: "番" (Fan), referring to Tibetan tribes.</note>. Its "throat" <note>A vital supply line.</note> depends entirely on Longzhou <gloss>the Pacification Commission</gloss>. Transporting supplies over a thousand *li* is frequently blocked by the "Fan" and "Man" tribes. This represents the hidden danger of the Western Ba region.

Regarding the tribes of Wusa and Mangbu <gloss>now known as Zhenxiong</gloss> <gloss>comprising four Military-Civilian Prefectures</gloss>: although their borders interlock with ours like dog’s teeth and they depend on our favor for their very breath, they maintain internal factions. They frequently engage in skirmishes and local warfare. Furthermore, they share a "lips and teeth" <note>A metaphor for close interdependence—if the lips are gone, the teeth get cold.</note> relationship with Xuzhou <gloss>Xuzhou</gloss> and Luzhou <gloss>Luzhou</gloss>. The essential strategy here is to pacify them according to their own customs and to win over their local leaders.

Then there is Diaomen <gloss>a place name in Tianquan Liufan</gloss>, which serves as a controlling "arm" over the various tribes, and Bozhou <gloss>the Pacification Commission</gloss>, whose wealth is stored across four provinces <gloss>Bozhou is a territory bordering Huguang, Yunnan, and Guizhou</gloss>...

<summary>This page details the logistical vulnerabilities of Sichuan, specifically the difficulty of supplying isolated outposts like Songpan and the necessity of managing the diplomatic relations with the surrounding ethnic tribes of Wusa, Mangbu, and Bozhou.</summary>

- **Jianchang:** An area in modern-day southern Sichuan (centered around Xichang), historically a strategic gateway to Yunnan.
- **Six Commanderies:** The military administrative units (Wei) governing the Jianchang area.
- **Geographical Regions:** A general term for the lands under discussion.
- **Commoners and Frontier Tribes:** The diverse ethnic makeup of the border population.
- **Geography:** The study of the earth's features and administrative divisions.

There are four Military-Civilian Prefectures <note>term: Military-Civilian Prefecture (Junmin Fu) — specialized administrative regions in border areas where military and civilian governance were combined to manage diverse populations</note>: **Dongchuan**, **Wumeng**, **Wusa**, and **Zhenxiong**. In total, the province consists of nine Prefectures, four Military-Civilian Prefectures, twenty-one Sub-prefectures, one hundred and ten Counties, one Pacification Commission, one Pacification Office, and ten Chief’s Offices.
