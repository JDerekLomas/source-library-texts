### BY THE SAME WRITER

# THE HIDDEN CHURCH OF THE HOLY GRAIL

## **Its Legends and Symbolism, considered in their Affinity with certain Mysteries of Initiation and other Traces of a Secret Tradition in Christian Times**

->Large demy octavo <note>A standard book size, approximately 5.5 by 8.75 inches</note>, 734 pages. **Price 12s. 6d. net**<-

### SOME OPINIONS OF THE PRESS

> "The high romance of the Grail, which inspired Hawker <note>Robert Stephen Hawker (1803–1875), poet and mystic</note>, which inspired Tennyson... has caused the well-known mystic and student of mysticism, Mr. A. E. Waite, to devote upwards of seven hundred pages to a treatise on 'The Hidden Church of the Holy Grail.' ... The book is one which no lover of romance, of our old literature, of the Celtic spirit, or, more particularly, of the Grail Legend, can afford to leave unread."—*Nation*.

> "Mr. Waite shows himself a brother of the literary craft by all possible tests. He has learning in many languages; he knows whatever is extant regarding his subject in print, as well as the record of its manuscripts. And his own style (whether we like or dislike it) is opulent with innumerable lights and jewels from alchemists, liturgies, old romance, secret orders of initiation, and other recondite <note>original: "recondite" — obscure or little-known</note> sources not easily unlocked by the explorer. . . . He is valiant, wise, and self-controlled. It is not for me to give one who stands outside the Roman Church an imprimatur <note>original: "imprimatur" — official approval or license to print, usually from the Catholic Church</note>; but I accept his conclusion gladly: 'The Holy Grail is the Catholic Quest drawn into romance.'"—The Rev. William Barry, in the *Bookman*.

# OPINIONS OF THE PRESS—continued

> "Mr. Waite's work is so learned and so subtly wide-ranging that it cannot help but recommend itself to a certain class of thoughtful readers."—*Scotsman*.

> "A lengthy, elaborate investigation by a writer who is a learned authority on the history of mysticism."—*Times*.

> "Mr. A. E. Waite's marvelous, scholarly, mystical exposition of the legend of the Holy Grail <note>original: "Graal"</note>. . . Mr. Waite's wonderful exposition of the truth which underlay the old-world legend of the Sacred Quest."—*Review of Reviews*.

> "Mr. Waite has found a subject after his own heart, one that gives full scope for his remarkable knowledge of the byways of mysticism. . . He brings to the execution of his task a rare mastery of the whole history and literature of the subject, and a deep insight into its hidden spiritual aspects."—*Glasgow Herald*.

> "Mr. Waite is known to all students of mysticism—and happily they are an increasing group—as one of the few competent writers on this fascinating subject. . . All his work bears the mark of knowledge gained from the actual sources, not from other men's views about those sources. . . Though, undoubtedly, much of the esoteric <note>intended for or likely to be understood by only a small number of people with a specialized knowledge</note> teaching has escaped at least one reviewer, he desires to record his deep gratitude to Mr. Waite for his latest volume. . . To those who are initiated even to a slight degree, we commend Mr. Waite's account of the secret knowledge which he so lavishly places within their reach."—*Irish Times*.

> "Perhaps the most important and effective treatise upon Christian mysticism yet published. . . From now on, all consideration of the Grail literature—whether of that which is unknown or that which is yet untraced—is destined to be judged by the standard of Mr. Waite's interpretation."—*Occult Review*.

> "The book is not only a comprehensive summary of the Holy Grail on its legendary or historical side, but also deals exhaustively with its mystical aspect."—*Light*.

> "A principal part of Mr. Waite's task—admirably carried out—has been to give a long and elaborate analysis of all these romances. . . '*The Hidden Church of the Holy Grail*' will be indispensable to all students of the greatest romance-cycle in the world."—*T.P.'s Weekly*.

## OPINIONS OF THE PRESS—continued

> “A book which, when known, will appeal more strongly to thinking Masons who believe in the mysteries of the Craft than any recent publication. It is the quest for the withdrawn secret, the search for the Lost Word, which is the very soul and meaning of Masonry, as it is and has been of all occult schools and of all real mystics. . . . This author stands pre-eminent among mystic writers for clearness, coherency, and a profound spirituality. . . . We can only yield to the enchantment <note>original: "witchery"</note> of his theme and style.”—*American Tyler-Keystone*.

> “Mr. A. E. Waite is one of the most profound and gifted among modern students of mysticism and a poet of fine imaginative power; his works in verse and prose fill many volumes and include some of the most brilliant and scholarly contributions that have been made in recent years to occult literature.”—*Aberdeen Free Press*.

> “A learned and fascinating inquiry into the mystery that lies embedded beneath the medieval <note>original: "mediæval"</note> romances of the Holy Grail <note>original: "Graal"</note>. The labor and research which its pages display are enormous. The author is equally at home in folklore, Masonry, Rosicrucianism, and medieval theology.”—*Saturday Review*.

> “No one who knows Mr. Waite will question his fitness to deal with any subject of a mystical nature, and anyone who reads his book will soon be convinced that, in addition to his wide grasp of the main principle, he has a very wide and extensive knowledge of the literature of the subject. . . . Thus Mr. Waite’s equipment is complete.”—*Seeker*.

> “This exhaustive study of the Grail literature—Welsh, English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese—will prove of permanent value to the ordinary student of the subject. Never before has so complete a summary appeared, not only of the great medieval Quest legends, but of modern commentaries and explanations <note>original: "elucidations"</note>. Mr. Waite’s work is especially important for the careful judgment that he passes on these attempted interpretations. . . . He himself derives the legend from some consciousness at the very heart of Catholicity <note>In this context, "Catholicity" refers to the universal, historical Christian tradition rather than specifically the Roman Catholic Church.</note>, only differing from orthodox Christianity in that the latter, while retaining the outer symbols of the mysteries of the faith, has lost, or half let slip, something of their inner significance. . . . This inward meaning . . . Mr. Waite devotes all his real gifts of mystical phrasing to draw out for us.”—*Guardian*.

# OPINIONS OF THE PRESS—continued

> “In Mr. Waite’s case, there cannot be the shadow of a doubt about how students of our literature will receive his book. As a presentation of everything known concerning the legends of the Holy Grail <note>original: "Graal"</note>, whether here or on the Continent, it is almost appallingly complete. . . . If readers are not attracted by his main argument . . , they may find agreeable instruction and noble entertainment in reading the clever and admirably written summary of the ancient texts which Mr. Waite supplies. . . . Mr. Waite’s doctrine is at least poetic; it has an element of divinity that lends it grace.”—*Yorkshire Post*.

> “His learning is extraordinary, and his experience in this kind of inquiry is equally so. His style is also a noble one, not at all in the modern fashion. . . . With great subtlety and fairness, he prepares us to believe that the confusion and obscurity of the tales arose from an imperfect understanding of something deeper, and that 'something' is exactly what he is searching for.”—*Daily Chronicle*.

> “Mr. A. E. Waite recently produced a highly complex treatise, strengthened by immense scholarly learning and hidden <term>occult</term> lore, in his ‘Hidden Church of the Holy Grail.’”—Mr. ERNEST RHYS, in the *Manchester Guardian*.

> “Mr. Waite has enriched the literature of the Holy Grail with a volume of investigation which, for its information alone, deserves a place in the library of any student or scholar. . . . It is a masterly work in its way . . . one which no lover of the beautiful Quest Legend can afford to neglect.”—*New Age*.

> “He has written a work of great value that cannot be ignored by any serious student of the delightful romances and legends that have been woven around the mystery of the Holy Grail.”—*Quest*.

> “To the rare reader who has found traces of an undying tradition—a Hidden Church or Wisdom—this book will be a true revelation. . . . Perhaps no other living man is as well-suited as Mr. Waite to approach this subject. . . . Our author combines the grasp of scholarship with a sympathetic attitude and a deep knowledge of hidden things. . . . We can do little more than emphasize the value of this volume, while pointing out why it holds special interest for Freemasons. Again and again throughout the work, one encounters references to Masonic traditions that are so illuminating they will truly startle the informed reader. . . . We could provide many more extracts and quotations, every one of which would throw light upon real Masonry—upon that which underlies and gives life to all Masonic forms and ceremonies.”—*American Freemason*.
