<meta>This page continues the technical exposition of the Surya Siddhanta (The Sun Treatise). Having introduced the seeker Maya and the divine source of this knowledge on the previous page, the text now shifts from narrative to the precise mathematical divisions of time that govern the Hindu cosmos.</meta>

Time is said to be of two kinds: the first is "gross" or measurable (<term>Martta</term>), and the second is "subtle" or immeasurable (<term>Amurtta</term>). (10)
<note>original: "मर्त्तश्चामूर्त्त उच्यते" — This distinction separates the time we can perceive and calculate from the infinitesimal or infinite time that lies beyond human measurement.</note>

The measurable time is that which begins with the breath (<term>Prana</term>), while the immeasurable begins with the "atom" (<term>Truti</term>). Six breaths are said to make one <term>Vinadi</term>; sixty of those <term>Vinadis</term> are known as a <term>Nadika</term>. (11)
<note>A Prana (breath) is roughly 4 seconds. A Vinadi is 24 seconds, and a Nadika (or Ghati) is 24 minutes.</note>

Sixty <term>Nadikas</term> constitute a "starry" day and night (<term>Nakshatra Ahoratra</term>). Thirty of these days make a month. A "civil" month (<term>Savana</term>) is likewise defined by thirty sunrises. (12)
<note>A "starry" or sidereal day is the time it takes for the earth to rotate relative to the distant stars, rather than the sun.</note>

A lunar month (<term>Aindava</term>) is measured by lunar days (<term>Tithis</term>), while a solar month (<term>Saura</term>) is determined by the Sun’s entry into a zodiac sign (<term>Samkranti</term>). Twelve months are called a year; this is also known as a "Day of the Gods." (13)

The Gods and the Demons (<term>Asuras</term>) live at the North and South poles respectively; therefore, their day and night are the reverse of one another. Six times sixty (360) of these divine days make one year of the Gods. (14)

Twelve thousand of these divine years are called a "Cycle of Four Ages" (<term>Caturyuga</term>). In the count of solar years, this consists of four million, three hundred and twenty thousand years. (15) <gloss>4,320,000</gloss>
<note>original: "चतुर्युगमुदाहृतं" — This refers to the Mahayuga, the great cycle encompassing the Krita, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali ages.</note>

The "twilights" and "dawn-junctions" (<term>Sandhya</term> and <term>Sandhyamsha</term>) must be included in this <term>Caturyuga</term>. The arrangement of the four ages—the <term>Krita</term> and the others—is determined by the four quarters of Virtue (<term>Dharma</term>). (16)
<note>Virtue is imagined as a bull standing on four legs in the first age, losing one leg in each subsequent age.</note>

The tenth part of a <term>Caturyuga</term>, multiplied by four, three, two, and one, gives the length of the <term>Krita</term>, <term>Treta</term>, <term>Dvapara</term>, and <term>Kali</term> ages respectively. A sixth part of each age constitutes its own dawn and twilight junctions. (17)

Seventy-one cycles of these ages are called a <term>Manvantara</term> (the age of a Manu). At its conclusion, a transition period (<term>Sandhi</term>) is declared, which is the length of a <term>Krita</term> age and takes the form of a Great Flood (<term>Jalaplaba</term>). (18)
<note>original: "जलप्नब" — This refers to the periodic dissolution or deluge that cleanses the world between the reigns of different Manus.</note>

In one <term>Kalpa</term> (an Aeon), there are fourteen such <term>Manus</term> along with their transition periods. At the very beginning of the <term>Kalpa</term>, there is a fifteenth transition period, which is also the length of a <term>Krita</term> age. (19)

Thus, a <term>Kalpa</term> is made of one thousand cycles of four ages; it is the force that brings about the destruction of all beings. This <term>Kalpa</term> is called a "Day of Brahma." His Night is said to be of the same duration. (20)

His total lifespan is one hundred years, calculated by these days and nights. One half of his life has already passed; of the remaining half, this is the first <term>Kalpa</term>. (21)
<note>The text places the current moment in the "Varaha Kalpa," the first day of the 51st year of Brahma's life.</note>

### On the Rising and Setting of Planets

Now, the knowledge of the rising and setting of planets is proclaimed; their forms are overcome by the rays of the Sun <note>original: "Divakara," literally 'the Day-maker'</note> because of their small brilliance. **1**

When their position is slightly greater than the Sun's, **Jupiter** <note>original: "Jiva"</note>, **Mars** <note>original: "Kuja"</note>, and **Saturn** <note>original: "Arkaja," son of the Sun</note> set in the west. When their position is less than the Sun's <note>original: "Vivasvat"</note>, the **Moon**, **Mercury**, and **Venus** set in the east. When it is greater, these fast-moving bodies rise in the west. **3**

To determine the setting in the west or the rising in the east, one should calculate the positions of the Sun and the planet at the time of sunset or sunrise respectively. These are divided by sixty... <unclear>verification</unclear> <unclear>required</unclear>... the sum of the difference of those ascendants is produced. **5**

### Chapter 14: On the Measures of Time

There are nine measures of time: the measure of Brahma <note>the Creator</note>, the measure of the Gods <term>daiva</term>, the measure of the Ancestors <term>pitrya</term>, the measure of Prajapati <note>the Lord of Creatures</note>, the measure of Jupiter <term>guru</term>, as well as Solar <term>saura</term>, Civil <term>savana</term>, Lunar <term>chandra</term>, and Sidereal <term>arksha</term>. 1.

Among these, four are used in daily practical affairs: the Solar, Lunar, Sidereal, and Civil measures. The measure of Jupiter is used to determine the sixty-year cycle, while the others are not used for daily reckoning. 2.

The length of day and night, the "eighty-six-mouths" <term>shadashitimukha</term> <note>specific points in the solar year used for calculations</note>, the solstices <term>ayana</term>, the equinoxes <term>vishuva</term>, and the auspicious times of the Sun's transit into a new sign <term>sankranti</term> are all determined by the Solar measure. 3.
