Webster <note>original: "Webster" refers to Noah Webster, whose dictionary was the standard authority on the English language in the 19th century.</note> in his definition of Spirit says, "Spirit is life or intelligence conceived of entirely apart from physical embodiment. God is Love. We cannot see love, nor grasp any comprehension of what love is, except when love is clothed with a body. All the love there is in the universe is God. # THINKING
## SECOND LESSON

In the first lesson, we learned that the real substance within everything we see is God. We learned that all things are one and the same Spirit appearing in different degrees of manifestation. All the various forms of life are simply the same one Life emerging from the invisible into visible forms. All the intelligence and wisdom in the world is God acting as Wisdom in various degrees of manifestation. Finally, all the love that people feel and express to others is just a small amount, so to speak, of God as Love becoming visible through the human form.

Now, when we say there is only one Mind in the entire universe, and that this is the Mind which is God, some people will immediately understand. Having followed the first lesson and recognized God as the one Life, one Spirit, and one Power expressing itself in various degrees through people and things, they will say, "Yes, that is all clear."

But someone else will ask, "If God is the only Mind that exists, then how can I think wrong thoughts, or any thoughts other than God's thoughts?"

<header>Second Lesson</header>

The connection between the Universal Mind and our own individual minds is one of the most difficult things to express in words, but once it dawns upon you, it is easily understood.

In reality, there is only one Mind (or Spirit, which is life, intelligence, etc.) in the universe; and yet, there is a sense in which we are individual or separate—a sense in which we possess free will and are not merely puppets.

A human being is composed of spirit, soul, and body. Spirit is our central, unchanging **"I,"** the part of us that has never changed since infancy and will never change throughout all eternity. That which Christian Scientists <note>A religious movement founded by Mary Baker Eddy in the 19th century, emphasizing the spiritual nature of reality and the power of prayer for healing.</note> call the "mortal mind" is the realm of the intellect, where we perform conscious thinking and exercise our free will. This part of our being is in a constant process of change.

In our descent, or our springing forth from God into the material world, the spirit is the innermost part—closest to God; the soul is the "clothing," so to speak, of the spirit; and the body is the external clothing of the soul. Yet, in reality, all are one, making up the whole person—just as steam is at the center, water is next, and ice is the external form, all being the same substance in different degrees of condensation. In thinking of ourselves, we must not separate spirit, soul, and body, but rather hold all as one if we would be

We "fell" <note>The "Fall of Man" is interpreted here not as a historical event of sin, but as a shift in human awareness from the spiritual to the physical world.</note> by allowing our consciousness to descend into the external or more material part of ourselves.

"Mortal mind"—the term so frequently used and so distracting to many—is the "error consciousness," which gathers its information through the five senses from the outside world. Never let it be an hour of obligation <note>original: "bondage"</note>, but always one of restfulness.

Some, having realized the calm and power that come from daily meditation, have made the mistake of withdrawing themselves entirely from the world so that they may give their entire time to meditation. This is asceticism <note>The practice of severe self-discipline and abstention from all forms of indulgence, often for religious reasons.</note>, which is neither wise nor profitable.

The Nazarene <note>Jesus of Nazareth</note>, who was our noblest example of the perfect life, went daily apart from the world only so that he might return to it with renewed spiritual power. So we go apart into the stillness of the Divine Presence so that we may come forth into the world of everyday life with a new inspiration, and increased courage and power for activity and for overcoming obstacles.

“We talk to God—that is prayer; God talks to us—that is inspiration,” says Lyman Abbott <note>Lyman Abbott (1835–1922) was a prominent American theologian and editor known for adapting Christian theology to modern ideas.</note>. We go apart to become still, so that new life, new inspiration, new power of thought, and new supplies from the Source <note>original: "Fountainhead"</note> may flow in; and then we come forth to share it all with those around us, so that they too may be lifted up. Disharmony <note>original: "Inharmony"</note> cannot remain in

any home where even one member of the family daily practices this hour of the presence of God; the renewed filling of the soul with peace and harmony results so surely in the continuous outflow of peace and harmony into the entire surroundings.

Meekness does not mean being submissive <note>original: "servility"</note> or foolish <note>original: "imbecility"</note>, but it means a spirit which could stand before a Pilate <note>A reference to Pontius Pilate, the Roman official who presided over the trial of Jesus; here used as a symbol of false judgment.</note> of false accusation, “opening not his mouth.” No one is so grand or so Godlike as the person who, because he knows the truth of Being <note>The ultimate reality or divine nature of existence.</note>, can stand meekly and calmly <note>original: "imperturbed"</note> before the false accusations of the mortal mind <note>A term used to describe the limited human intellect and ego, which is often seen as being in opposition to Divine Truth.</note>. “*Your gentleness has made me great.*” <note>original: "Thy gentleness hath made me great" — Psalm 18:35.</note>

# Twelfth Lesson

If you feel superior to anything any of God’s children may do or suffer, you have not yet learned how to forgive.

The very pain you suffer, and the very failure to demonstrate <note>In New Thought, "to demonstrate" means to successfully apply spiritual principles to bring about a desired physical or life result.</note> success in matters that touch your life deeply, may be based on this very spirit of resentment <note>original: "unforgiveness"</note> which you harbor toward the world in general. Resolve to put it away.

Do not be enslaved by false beliefs about your circumstances or environment. No matter how evil any circumstances may seem, nor how much it may seem that some other person is at the source of our sorrow or trouble, God—who is good and good alone—is *real* there when you call His law into action <note>original: "expression"</note>.

If we have the courage to persist in seeing only God in everything, “even the anger of man” shall be consistently turned to our advantage. Joseph, in speaking of the action of his brothers in selling him into slavery, said, “As for you, you thought evil against me; but God meant it for good.” “*All things* work together for *good* to those who love God,” or to those who recognize only God. All things! And the very circumstances in your life that seem like torturing, heartbreaking evils will turn to joy before your eyes if you will steadfastly refuse to see anything but God in them.

# Bondage or Liberty, Which?

It is perfectly natural for the human soul to seek to escape from its troubles by running away from present environments or planning some change on the material level <note>original: "plane"</note>. Such methods of escape are absolutely useless and foolish. “Useless is the help of man” or mortal effort.

There is no permanent or real *outward* way of escape from miseries or circumstances; everything must come from *within*.

The words, “God is my defense and deliverance,” held to in the silence until they become part of your very being, will deliver you out of the hands and the arguments of the sharpest lawyer in the world.

The real inner consciousness of the truth that “The Lord is my shepherd, I shall not want,” will supply *all* my needs more surely and far more generously than any human hand can.

The ultimate aim of every soul should be to reach the consciousness of an indwelling God. And then, in all external matters, affirm *deliverance* through and by this Divine One. There should not be a frantic running about of the human self <note>original: "mortal"</note>, making human efforts to aid the Divine, but a calm, restful, unwavering *trust* in All-Wisdom and All-Power within to accomplish the thing desired.
