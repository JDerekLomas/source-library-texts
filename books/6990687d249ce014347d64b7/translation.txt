# S I G N S   O F   D E A T H .

In this world, among those born as human beings, when they sleep after quenching their hunger, they see many kinds of dreams. Because they wake up without understanding the results of these dreams, I praise the feet of Ganesha <note>original: "Ganapathy," the elephant-headed god of wisdom and the remover of obstacles</note>, the son of Shiva <note>original: "Ishan," described here as the one who bears the water (the Ganges) on his holy head</note>. To ensure that no confusion arises while explaining these results, may the Sage-Teacher <note>original: "Guru Muni," likely referring to the sage Agastya, the legendary patron of Tamil grammar, medicine, and divination</note> who granted these arts also be our protection. 1

O son, I tell you to fix your mind on this, so that you may know the time when death approaches. Do not harbor arrogance, do not speak vain words, do not think meanly of others, and do not seek to bring blame upon yourself. When you are walking along a path, if your "fire-shadow" <note>original: "vanni nizhal," likely referring to the aura or the shimmering heat-shadow cast by the body</note> is not visible to you, death is coming for you, man. Do not wander about with a boastful mouth; instead, search for the way to salvation within a year. 2

Look here, if you hold a mirror in a dream and do not see your head reflected in it; or if you see a base person <note>original: "neesan"</note> come and seize an ascetic <note>original: "Pandaram"</note> by the hair and drag him away; or if you see monkeys, red dogs, tigers, or wicked demons <note>original: "Rakshasas"</note> daily—who, O man, can stay on this earth for more than a year? There is no doubt in this world that the messenger of the God of Death <note>original: "Naman"</note> has been sent. 3

If you are looking for signs, do not search the whole town. When you are walking down a street and the running sun appears to your eyes as a dim light or like a blurry halo, then the "dance" you perform <note>an idiom for one's life on earth</note> will last only ten months. Truly, death is near. Do not wander about pridefully thinking of your house, children, or wife; instead, look to the sacred scriptures <note>original: "Vedas"</note>. 4

When you walk upon the earth, if it appears to your sight as if your shadow has two heads, then death awaits you in eight months. If you see a monkey or a donkey inside your house in a shifting dream, life lasts only six months. If an owl, a dog, and a pig—all three—chase you and bark or howl, then only one month remains. 5

O human born through great penance, listen. These are the omens and dreams that appear like the designs written by the Creator <note>original: "Birman" or Brahma</note> upon the skull. Do not be afraid; listen further. Whatever you see...

Behold this sacred knowledge <note>original: "Vedam"</note> spoken by the planet Jupiter <note>original: "Vyazha Bhagavan," also known as Brihaspati, the preceptor of the gods</note>. **7**

Do not think of this lightly, dear child, but listen. If one bathes by dipping into a river or a pond, and before taking a cloth to wipe themselves dry, the chest begins to burn like a kiln, then listen, my father: for that man, death will come on the fifteenth day. I say this with authority; know it well. Do not think of this as mere talk, do not seek out bad deeds, and listen now to another evil dream. **8**

Listen, my father: if you dream of applying oil to yourself, taking a fibers for bathing <note>original: "arappu," a herbal soap-nut powder</note> in your hand, and picking up red-colored flowers; and if a dark-skinned man <note>likely an agent of Yama</note> and you both dress in red garments and walk toward the South, you will meet death on the tenth day. This word spoken by the Great Masters <note>original: "Nadhakkal," referring to the Siddha masters</note> shall not prove false. Listen, my father: only fools will call this a lie, and tomorrow they shall reside in the pit of hell. **9**

Come, listen to another dream. While there is a fire in a prosperous house, if the wick of a steadily burning lamp appears as if it has been cut into pieces, O son; and if all the beautiful flames fall downward and the lamp-stand remains broken like a headless torso, then death will come directly on the fifth day. For the fools who do not believe, the seven hells await. **10**

That hell is for the sinners, my father. Now listen to another evil dream. Among the birds of ruin, if a kite or a crow pecks at your face, O young one, then death will surround you on the fourth day. Do not think of this as a small matter; you cannot escape it. Do not speak with vain pride saying "I will live for many more days." Listen further. **11**

Listen more: if you look at your own eyes, my father, and from your two knees down to your feet, you see yourself as having stumps instead of legs, then we declare that death comes in just two days. This is what the god Subramanya <note>the deity Murugan</note> instructed to the sage Agastya; what Bhishma told the virtuous Yudhishthira <note>original: "Dharmar"</note>, and what Yudhishthira told his brother Arjuna. **12**

Do not forget the word of Righteousness <note>Dharma</note>. Do not become intoxicated by praising yourself. O men of action <note>original: "Karmigal"</note>, listen to one more dream. If a dark woman with disheveled hair comes near you laughing and talking flirtatiously, and suddenly climbs upon your shoulders and screams like a buffalo—if you survive for two more days after seeing this, then you are God <note>original: "Ishan"</note> himself. <note>The implication is that survival is impossible for a mortal; the buffalo is the traditional mount of Yama, the God of Death.</note>

If one sees an elephant, a herd of horses, hears stories of righteousness <note>original: "Dharma kathai"</note>, sees a flower grove, a conch, a lamp, the sun, or the moon in a dream; or if one sees the sun’s rays, a deer, the primary gods, mountains, or even simple churned buttermilk—these twelve are not in vain, they are truly auspicious. I shall explain them in detail, listen closely. Listen: if you climb trees like the Fig <note>original: "Atthi"</note>, the Tamarind, the Sugarcane, or the Punnai <note>the Mastwood tree, *Calophyllum inophyllum*</note> when they are laden with fruit; or if you see a scorpion, or a good serpent <note>original: "Sarpam," usually referring to a cobra</note> dancing clearly with its hood spread; or if you see a swarm of ants or see yourself walking among them—know that your benefits will increase day by day. Even seeing excrement <note>original: "malam"</note> in a dream is surprisingly a good omen. **15**

If you see a just rain falling, or if you wear fine new clothes; if you see women dressed in pure white garments, or if you encounter kings; if a dark-eyed woman <note>original: "Maivizhiyal," literally one whose eyes are lined with kohl</note> speaks to you, or if you see yourself walking up a mountain path; if you hear or read the story of the Five <note>original: "Aivar kathai," referring to the Pandavas of the Mahabharata</note>—you shall attain the comforts and happiness of a king. **16**

But if a man comes to call you in the world of dreams and pours oil on a hairless, bald head; or if you ride on the back of a monkey and travel swiftly; or if you step upon a corpse that has fallen devoid of its life-force; or if you carry a burden of grass or firewood upon your head—if you see these things appearing favorably in your dream, I tell you, your body will be afflicted by a wasting disease <note>original: "Satrathee," likely referring to a type of consumption or chronic illness</note>. **17**

If you travel North accompanied by a dark-eyed woman, or purchase turmeric, betel leaves, and areca nuts; or if you dream of your hand reaching up to touch the very sky—you shall become a king ruling the world surrounded by the seas. If you see tender coconuts, young fruits, or sugarcane, a son will be born to you without any defects. If a young man enters a grove and plucks flowers, a daughter will be born; there is no doubt in this. **18**

If you see a crow in your dream, wealth will depart. If you see a pig, it signifies great distress and poverty. Seeing a buffalo or a dog is an ill omen. It is not good to dream of consorting with a widow; your life will be ruined day by day. If you see yourself renouncing domestic life to become a wandering mendicant <note>original: "Andi"</note>, what can I say? Your worldly pleasures will vanish. **19**

If you see the house where you reside catching fire and burning, gold will soon "smile" in your hands <note>meaning you will acquire wealth</note>. If you see yourself hunting in the forest, dear one, vehicles and conveyances will come to you. If you see the sky collapsing and falling, great sorrow and weeping will come, but it will eventually pass. It is a defect to dream of performing a marriage <note>original: "Vivaham"</note>; it will result in doubts and anxieties settling in your mind. **20**

# THE SCIENCE OF DREAMS.

## The Kummi.
<note>A "Kummi" is a traditional Tamil folk song rhythm, often performed with rhythmic clapping. The verses here follow this meter to make the omens easier to memorize.</note>

If you see shoes in your dream—or if you wear them on your feet and walk—all worries will vanish from your mind, and you shall enjoy happiness like the king of a city. **1**

If you see yourself becoming a wandering ascetic <note>original: "Andi," a religious mendicant</note>—or visiting any temple—or even if you see your beloved relatives acting as enemies—even lost possessions will be recovered. **2**

If you see those who went to distant lands coming back home—walking while wearing wet clothes <note>original: "Eera thuni," which can symbolize grief or funeral rites</note>—how can I describe the horror that such a dream foretells? **3**

If you dream of traveling to unseen lands by sea—in a small boat or a steamship—and you have such a dream during the day, everything your hand touches will turn to gold. **4**

To fall into filth <note>original: "Naragu," referring to excrement or sewage</note>—to have your clothes stained by that filth—or to see your whole body smeared in it—it means that the long-afflicting Saturn <note>original: "Sani," the planet associated with cycles of misfortune</note> has finally left you. **5**

If arrows, pots, spears, or knives—come dancing toward you—or if you see the commands of troublesome demons <note>original: "Rakshathar"</note>—know that death shall arrive at that very hour. **6**

If you see your clothes catching fire—or the great forest burning—you will come to know the true character and wisdom of the women in your family; there is no doubt about it. **7**

If you see a priest <note>original: "Anthanan," traditionally a Brahmin</note> perish and die—or an elephant stumble and fall—it is certain that death will come to someone older than you. **8**

If you dream of going to the holy city of Kashi <note>Varanasi</note> and returning—or of wandering in a great forest—by the grace of the Lord <note>original: "Ishan," a name for Shiva</note>, you will live forever as an immortal <note>original: "Chiranjeevi"</note> without any lack. **9**

The code of righteousness <note>original: "Dharma Shastra"</note>—that the great teacher Bhishma told to King Yudhishthira <note>original: "Daruman," the eldest Pandava</note>—was studied by Munisami of Sirumanavur, who recited it so that it may be available to everyone. **10**

Monday and Saturday—both are forbidden toward the East;
To go toward the South, Thursday is not favorable;
Likewise, a journey to the North,
My dear friend, Tuesday and Wednesday are forbidden;
To travel West from the place where you reside,
O brother, Friday is truly, truly ill-omened;
These directions are the **Trident in the hand of Lord Shiva**;
If you must travel on an unfavorable day, listen to the secret.

(Explanation:) Traveling toward the East on Monday or Saturday, the North on Tuesday or Wednesday, the South on Thursday, and the West on Friday is prohibited, as these directions represent the "Trident of Shiva" <note>original: "Sivan-kai-choolam," a metaphorical way of saying one is walking directly into the points of a spear</note> facing the traveler. However, if an urgent journey arises on such a day, there is a method to proceed. One must depart during the **Ambrosial Hour** <term>Amrita Yoga Kadigai: A specific auspicious time interval calculated based on the alignment of the day's star and the day itself, believed to neutralize negative influences</term> which occurs for three and three-quarters of a *naazhigai* <note>A traditional unit of time; one naazhigai is approximately 24 minutes</note>.

## Venba.

Depart for the North at dawn—for the prosperous East at noon;
For the South, as the sun begins to set—and for the West
At the stroke of midnight—then you need not look for the day's omens;
Know this and rejoice, for there shall be no dawn of sorrow.

(Explanation:) If one must travel North, they should do so within a quarter-measure of time after sunrise. If traveling East, one should depart when the sun is directly overhead like an umbrella <note>i.e., high noon</note>. For the South, the time is just before or just after the sun sinks into the western sea <note>sunset</note>. If one must go West, the time is midnight. For those who travel at these specific times, they may proceed with certainty and truth regardless of what day of the week it is; no দোষ <note>original: "Dosha," meaning a spiritual flaw or astrological blemish</note> shall befall them.

(Explanation:) Milk, fruit, cow dung, a cow, tender coconut, a maiden, two Brahmins, a palanquin, water pots, a mirror coated with quicksilver, mango leaves, and a basket of manure are described as auspicious omens by the elders.

(Explanation:) Saplings of flowering plants, lotus flowers, water, a crowd of people returning after eating at a feast, a white fly-whisk <term>Chamarai: A ceremonial whisk made from a yak's tail, used in temples and for royalty</term>, snacks, and bundles of seedlings for grain—know that these were declared good by the ancestors. ## Signs that should appear on the Left

> The forest-dwelling buffalo, the pig, the bear, the monkey, and the musk shrew <note>original: "munjur" - the Asian house shrew, often associated with Ganesha</note>,
> The running dog, the cat, the monitor lizard, and the mongoose,
> The ram of the flock, the leech, or whoever it may be—
> If they come towards your **left side**, it is very auspicious.
> 
> Considering the results of walking in the four directions of the land,
> Combining the power of those coming from the right, the left, and the front,

---

## Dining.

Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday are good. It is not advisable on other days.

## Wearing New Clothes.

Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday are the suitable times to wear new <term>Kodi: Fresh, unwashed, or newly purchased cloth, typically worn for the first time on auspicious occasions</term> garments.

---

## Sleeping.

If you sleep with your head placed toward the East, you will gain wealth. Facing the South, your lifespan and riches will increase. Facing the West, your fame will grow. Facing the North leads to death <note>original: "mrityu" - a Sanskrit term for death or the deity of death</note>. When in your own home, sleep with your head to the South; in your mother-in-law's house, to the East; and while traveling, to the West. The head should never be placed toward the Northern direction.

---

## Shaving and Haircutting.

One should not undergo shaving <note>original: "kshauram" - ritual or routine hair cutting and shaving</note> during the months of Aadi <note>mid-July to mid-August</note>, Purattasi <note>mid-September to mid-October</note>, Margazhi <note>mid-December to mid-January</note>, and Maasi <note>mid-February to mid-March</note>, nor on Sundays, Tuesdays, or Saturdays.

# THE EFFECTS OF LIZARD FALLS

O son, who has inquired about the details of dreams,
Listen as I tell you: if a lizard falls upon the **head**,
A conflict will soon arise in your life.
Do not hesitate; if it falls upon the **face**,
You will soon have the sight of many relatives.
Listen, help from kings will come if it falls on the **eyebrows**;
Wealth in hand will be lost if it falls on the **upper lip**;
<margin>1</margin>My dear, on the **lower lip**, it brings great profit.

Great profit and long life come from the **right ear**;
It is profit in trade if it falls on the **left ear**, my friend.
On the **chin**, expect punishment from the ruler.
On the **knees**, you will find comfort and joy—this will not fail.
On the **hair**, there is great fear; on the **eyes**,
It will cause you to leave your current place for another.
You shall have a union with another woman
<margin>2</margin>If it falls on the hard-to-describe **left shoulder**.

Health is granted if it falls on one’s **right shoulder**;
Upon the **breasts**, a sinful event <note>original: "paba sambavam" - an occurrence of sin or an ill-omened event</note> will happen.
Gold and wealth shall arrive if it falls on the **stomach**;
It brings about a journey, though it is considered an ill-omen.
If it falls distinctly upon the **throat**,
We declare that your enemies will be destroyed.
On the **female genitals**, it brings wealth; on the **male genitals**,
<margin>3</margin>The books of the elders say it signifies poverty <term>Daridram: A state of extreme poverty or misfortune</term>.
