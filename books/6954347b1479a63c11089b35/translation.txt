# Editor’s Preface to the Third Edition.¹)

When I received the request from the publishers of the *Philosophical Library* to produce a new edition of *Kant’s Critique of Judgment*, and accordingly had to closely examine the previous edition (by *v. Kirchmann*), the necessity of a radical restructuring became immediately clear to me. This applied to both 1. the treatment of the text and 2. the factual explanation <note>The editor refers to the scholarly commentary and annotations provided alongside the primary text</note>, to which the publisher also agreed without hesitation.

*Kirchmann’s* text was almost entirely a simple reprint of the second *Hartenstein* edition (from 1867). This may have been meritorious for its time: however, measured by the standards of modern textual criticism <term>Textual criticism: the scholarly process of reconstructing the original wording of a text by comparing different versions and editions</term>, it can only be described as thoroughly outdated. Just one example: while *Rosenkranz* was so little acquainted with the textual history of the work he himself edited that he believed *Kant* had never "undertaken an internal change" to the text, *Hartenstein* did indeed know of the differences between the first and second original editions (cf. our *Introduction* p. XII f. regarding this), but he claimed—and *Kirchmann* followed him blindly in this—that the third was only a simple (*Kirchmann*: unchanged) reprint of the second. In reality, the agreement...

The two more recent special editions, by *Kehrbach* (Reclam, 1878) and *Benno Erdmann* (*L. Voß*, 1880, 2nd ed. 1884), are, by contrast, familiar with the facts of the matter. Nevertheless, *Kehrbach* strangely takes the first edition as his basis, despite its deficiencies admitted by *Kant* himself (see below); furthermore, his listing of variants is incomplete, and the only four text improvements designated by him as new are all already to be found in older editions. On the other hand, *Erdmann’s* edition (just like his edition of the *Critique of Pure Reason* <note>original: "Kritik der reinen Vernunft"; Kant's foundational work on epistemology</note>) is distinguished by extraordinary correctness and care: vanishingly few printing errors, a large number of genuine improvements (the majority of which we have included in our edition, while the others, insofar as they are noteworthy, are noted below the text), in addition to an instructive introduction regarding the realization of *Kant’s* work and individual related substantive questions, and finally an extensive "Appendix on Textual Revision" (48 pages) produced with philological meticulousness <term>philological meticulousness: the scholarly practice of comparing different versions of a text to ensure accuracy</term>.

I, too, have turned my attention to the latter. The reader will find the fruit of my efforts in a number of further textual changes (see the list below on p. VIII), in which I believe I see an improvement of the text. Furthermore, I have noted all improvements by others that seemed of any significance (most of them stem from *Erdmann*). Departing from *Kehrbach* (1st ed.) and *Erdmann* (2nd ed.), I have based the text on the third edition, as it was the last original edition printed during *Kant’s* lifetime—
