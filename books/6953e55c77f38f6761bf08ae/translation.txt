# An Introductory Preface.

<margin>The trials and observations I had since made.</margin>

What became of my papers, I mention elsewhere in a preface where I complain of their loss. But since I wrote *that*, I found many sheets that belonged to the subjects I am now about to discuss. Therefore, seeing that I had in my hands as much of the first dialogue as was required to state the case and serve as an introduction—both to the conference between *Carneades* and *Eleutherius* <note>Carneades and Eleutherius are fictional characters used by the author to represent different philosophical viewpoints.</note>, as well as to some other dialogues which for certain reasons are not published here—I resolved to supply, as well as I could, the contents of a paper belonging to the second of the following discourses. I could not possibly retrieve that paper, even though it was the most important of them all. 

And having once more tested the opinion of *friends* (though not the same ones as before) about this imperfect work, I found their reaction such that I was willing to comply with their desires: not only that it should be published, but that it should be published as soon as conveniently possible. I had indeed, all along the dialogues, spoken of myself as a third person. Because these discourses were among the first treatises that I ventured to write long ago on *philosophical* <note>original: "Philosophical"; in this context, it refers to "natural philosophy" or what we now call science.</note> matters, I had reason to desire, like the painter, to *hide behind the canvas* <note>original: "latere pone tabulam"</note> and hear what men would say of them before I owned myself to be their author.
