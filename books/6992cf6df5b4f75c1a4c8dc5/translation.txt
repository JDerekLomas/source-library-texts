In the *Short Records of the Martyr Emperor* by Wen Bing <note>Wen Bing (1609–1669) was a Ming loyalist historian; "Martyr Emperor" refers to the Chongzhen Emperor, the last ruler of the Ming dynasty.</note>, it is recorded that at the beginning of the Chongzhen era (circa 1628), Catholicism first began to circulate widely. Xu Guangqi <note>A famous Chinese scholar-official and scientist who was one of the most influential converts to Christianity in Chinese history.</note> of Shanghai was a member of this religion. When Xu entered the central government, he vigorously promoted its teachings. Consequently, many bronze Buddhist statues maintained in the palaces and government offices were completely destroyed and smashed. 

By the time Prince Daoling <note>The Emperor's infant son, Zhu Cijiong.</note> became terminally ill, the Emperor went to visit him. The Prince spoke, saying, "The Lady of the Nine Lotuses <note>A reference to the Empress Dowager Li, the Emperor's grandmother, who was a devout Buddhist and posthumously associated with this deity.</note> is standing in the air; she is recounting the destruction of the Three Jewels <note>The "Three Jewels" (Triratna) refers to the core of Buddhist faith: the Buddha, the Dharma (teachings), and the Sangha (community).</note> and the harsh treatment of the Marquis of Wuqing..." Having finished these words, the Prince passed away. The Emperor was struck with great fear, but there was nothing he could do.

In the Catholic church of the capital, there were two Westerners who presided over the faith: Niccolo Longobardo <note>Original: 龍華民 (Lóng Huámín), an Italian Jesuit priest.</note> and Johann Adam Schall von Bell <note>Original: 湯若望 (Tāng Ruòwàng), a German Jesuit and astronomer who became a high-ranking official in China.</note>. Those who converted to their religion were first commanded to bring their household Buddhist statues to the church. In front of the Catholic hall, there was a stone pillar and a stone pond. As soon as the converts brought their statues, they would smash them against the pillar and discard the fragments into the pond. This was established as a standard practice for all.

A man with a **curly beard** <note>A common Qing-era description for Westerners, referring to the Jesuits' facial hair</note> sits and **plays a keyboard instrument** <note>Original: *guqin*. While this usually refers to the Chinese zither, here it describes the console of a pipe organ or harpsichord</note>. When he plays, the sounds of reed pipes, flutes, stone chimes, bamboo flutes, bells, drums, and brass cymbals are all present—not a single one is missing. 

The method for this involves constructing a wooden frame atop a lofted structure. Dozens of **lead pipes** are suspended from it, hanging down to within about an inch of the floorboards. The floor consists of two layers, with slits that align exactly with the openings of each pipe. A person stands in the southeast corner operating **bellows** to generate air. This air is captured between the layers of the floor and is forced through the slits directly into the pipes. 

Each pipe is connected to the instrument's strings by a copper wire. When the bearded man plucks the strings, the wires pull on the **internal triggers** <term>guanlie</term> <note>A term for mechanical linkages or valves</note> within the pipes, causing them to sound. # Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms, Volume 28

## Southwest Oceans: The Northern and Southern Islamic Countries of Rum <note>original: 魯機 (Lǔjī). A Chinese transliteration of "Rum," a term the Islamic world used for the Ottoman Empire, derived from "Rome."</note>

Birds and beasts are found here in great abundance. The local products include silk, wine, oil, honey, spices, medicinal herbs, and various other goods. The climate varies greatly across the region: the mountain peaks are covered in permanent ice and snow, while the low-lying plains experience sweltering heat. The people possess excellent technical skills and craftsmanship, and maritime trade is flourishing. The religion followed by the inhabitants is **Islam** <note>original: 回教 (Huíjiào).</note>.

The territory is divided into five primary regions:

*   The first is **Asia Minor** <note>original: 小亞細亞 (Xiǎo Yàxìyà)</note>, also known as **Anatolia** <note>original: 亞那多里 (Yànāduōlǐ)</note>. It is located in the west and contains six prefectures.
*   The second is **Armenia** <note>original: 亞爾美尼 (Yà'ěrměiní)</note>. It is located in the east and contains five prefectures.
*   The third is **Kurdistan** <note>original: 古爾的 (Gǔ'ěrdī)</note>. It is located in the east and contains two prefectures.
*   The fourth is **Mesopotamia** <note>original: 美索布達迷 (Měisuòbùdámí)</note>. It is located in the southeast and contains three prefectures.
*   The fifth is **Syria** <note>original: 西利亞 (Xīlìyà)</note>. It is located in the south and contains four prefectures.

The most strategic and prosperous areas are primarily located within the Anatolia region. Half of the region of **Al-Jazira** <note>original: 亞爾日西拉 (Yà'ěrjìxīlā). An Arabic name meaning "The Island," referring to Upper Mesopotamia.</note> and the area known as **Bethlehem** <note>original: 白梭 (Báisū). Refers here to the Holy Land/Palestine region.</note>—which is the birthplace of **Jesus**—are both situated within the borders of the Syria region.
