->## FIRST.<-

1. **P**<note>The original text begins with a decorative woodcut initial 'P'.</note>recisely what it means to "plead" <note>original: *postulare*; in Roman law, this refers to the formal act of making a request or conducting a case before a judge</note> is taught by the Jurist in *law 1 of this title, "On Pleading."* Those who expressly perform this duty are called **Advocates**: *law 1, title 1 of this book*; and they can be compelled by a Judge to provide defense to a party even against their will. *Eberhard Speckhan, century 2, final question.* However, a person who is prohibited from pleading in court is not, as a rule, prohibited from giving legal advice at home: unless the underlying reason for both duties is the same. Therefore, **Bartolus** <note>Bartolus de Saxoferrato (1313–1357), one of the most influential legal commentators of the Middle Ages.</note> is of the mistaken opinion that a Doctor of Law who has bound himself not to plead against a certain person may still consult privately against that same person. *The most celebrated Paulus Busius, in his commentary on the Digests at law 1 of this title.*

2. A person who has been removed from legal advocacy for a set time is readmitted after that time has passed. *law 8 of this title, where Busius comments; law 5, "On City Councilors"; Duarenus on this title, second to last chapter; see Cujas, book 7, observation 19; Pacius, 2. Contradictions 20.* <note>The citations refer to Jacques Cujas and Julius Pacius, two of the greatest legal humanists of the 16th century.</note>

3. It is more correct to say that an Advocate acting in his own case may demand a Salary, provided that his adversary has been ordered to pay his legal expenses. *See Gail, book 1, observation 151, number 16; and Mynsinger, century 1, observation 2; Alessandro Trentacinque, Various Resolutions, book 2, title "On Pleading," final resolution.*

4. An agreement between an Advocate and a client regarding a "portion of the lawsuit" <term>quota litis: a contingency fee where the lawyer takes a percentage of the winnings</term>, or regarding a specific thing under litigation to be given in the event of victory, is considered invalid by law. *law "if against," 20, Code: "On Mandates"; law 6, final section, and the following law in the Digests of the same title; law 53, "On Agreements."* Furthermore, those who make such agreements are removed from their office with a mark of disgrace. *law "if any," Code on this title; Bronchorst, 1. Miscellany 21; Speckhan, century 2, class 4, question 45; Trentacinque, said title, resolution 1.*

5. A Judgment of **Stellionate** <term>stellionatus: a Roman law term for "swindling" or fraudulent dealings that do not fit into specific categories like theft or embezzlement</term> does not result in "infamy" <note>A formal loss of legal standing or reputation.</note>; rather, it carries an extraordinary punishment. *law 2, Digests "On the Crime of Stellionate"; law 7, "On Public Judgments."*

<margin>Chapter 3.</margin>
8. A <term>Procurator</term> <note>A legal proxy or agent who manages a case for another.</note> is one who undertakes the management of the business itself, not one who provides legal defense or counsel. He is contrasted with a <term>Defender</term>, who acts in the cause of an absent person without a formal mandate. *Law 51 and 61 of the Digests. Busius 1. Subtleties, chapter 15.* Therefore, a person over 17 years of age may properly fulfill the duty of a Procurator, even in Court; but he may not act as a Defender. *Law 51 of this title; compare Law 23 regarding minors, the paragraph "justa"; Institutes, on those cases where manumission is not allowed; Pacius, 2. <term>Contradictions</term> 17; Bronchorst 1. <term>Contradictions</term> 49; Busius, Subtleties 6. chapter 6; Lycklama 2. Selection 12.*

9. Bronchorst 1. Miscellaneous, assertion 26.* Similarly, family members can act and litigate on behalf of blood relatives and in-laws without a formal mandate, *Law 35 of the Digests,* provided they give a guarantee of ratification. *Law 39, the paragraph regarding dowry. Dissenting: through law 223, the paragraph on friends, regarding the meaning of words; and Law 4 of the Code regarding camp property; Jason and others on Law 12 of the Digests; Sichardus on Law 12 of the Code.*

10. A husband is admitted as the legitimate Procurator of his wife, even if he does not provide security, in matters concerning <term>Dowry property</term> and <term>Paraphernalia</term> <note>Personal property of a married woman that was not part of her dowry.</note>. In the case of <term>Receptitium property</term> <note>Property that the husband specifically agreed to return to the wife.</note>, he is compelled to provide security. *Final law of the Code on agreed pacts; Law 2 and Law "the husband" 21 of the Code on procurators; Bronchorst 1. miscellaneous, assertion 27; The Presiding Lord on appeals, said chapter, section 10.*

29. Formerly, the decision and power of an **Arbitrator** <term>Arbiter: a private individual chosen by parties to resolve a dispute outside of the formal court system</term> carried no weight, nor did it grant any legal action or defense based on a judged matter. Today, it has been decreed by the Constitution of Justinian (in the law *Cum antea*, Code, on arbitrations) that if a compromise is entered into by a simple agreement without a penalty, and the arbitrator's decision is either expressly ratified by both parties or tacitly strengthened by the passage of ten days, then an **action based on the facts** <note>original: "in factum actio"; a legal remedy granted by a praetor based on the specific circumstances of a case rather than strict civil law</note> is available to the plaintiff, and a defense is available to the defendant, and the sentence may be ordered for execution. *Bronchorst, 1. ## HISTORICAL SUPPLEMENT

**V**ery influential authors have believed that the Books of Moses and of the other Prophets were burned by the **Chaldeans** <note>The Babylonians, who destroyed the First Temple in 586 BCE</note> and were restored from memory by **Ezra**. <note>original: "Esræ"</note> *Irenaeus, Book 3, chapter 23; Tertullian, on the Apparel of Women; Clement of Alexandria, Book 1 of the Miscellanies; Eusebius in his Chronicle; Jerome to Heliodorus.* However, no trace of such unspeakable flames exists in the Sacred Scriptures; nor should it be believed that God permitted such a bright light of the Church to be obscured by any smoke. Furthermore, that copies of Moses' books remained is confirmed both by the testimony of Daniel, chapter 9, and by the letter of Jonathan to the **Spartans** <note>original: "Lacones"</note> in *1 Maccabees 12*. Likewise, Josephus maintains in *Antiquities of the Jews, Book 11, chapter 1* <note>The original cites "5. Antiquit. 11," likely a printing error for Book 11</note> that the prophecy of Isaiah was shown to **Cyrus**.
