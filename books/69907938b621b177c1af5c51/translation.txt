Nahnidatta (or Lahnidatta) was a great scholar of astrology from <note>Mithila is a historical and cultural region located in the northern part of the Indian subcontinent, famous for its long lineage of scholars in logic and law.</note>. He flourished in the early 14th century. His only work, *Nahnidatta-panchvimshatika*, became very famous across Northern India, as its many manuscripts have been found in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, and other places. This book, containing 25 verses, deals with the auspicious dates and <term>Muhurtas (specific moments in time deemed astrologically favorable for starting an activity)</term> for various Hindu ceremonies like <term>Upanayan (the sacred thread initiation ritual)</term>, <term>Vivaha (marriage ceremony)</term>, and so on. Its commentary by <term>M.M. (Mahamahopadhyaya, an honorific title meaning "Greatest Teacher")</term> Ruchipati (15th century) of the Khauala family from Mithila is being published here for the first time from its manuscript by Bhavanath Jha. This ebook was generated by the present editor on May 9, 2014.

# A Critical Edition of Ruchipati’s Commentary
# on the Twenty-Five Tales of Nahidatta

Determining the prescribed time for any auspicious act has been the primary and most ancient subject of <term>Jyotish Shastra</term><note>The traditional Indian science of astronomy and astrology.</note>. Even within the <term>Brahmana</term> texts<note>Ancient Indian commentaries on the Vedas concerning rituals.</note>, calculations are provided for the specific <term>Nakshatras</term><note>Lunar mansions or star constellations.</note>, the <term>Ayana</term><note>The six-month solar path, either northward or southward.</note>, and particular times suited for sacrifice. ## The Period of the Author

Solid evidence regarding the date of Nahidatta, the author of the *Balabodhini* <note>Literally, "Enlightenment for Children/Beginners," a common subtitle for introductory texts.</note> or *Nahidatta-Panchavimshatika*, is not readily available. A tradition in Mithila has long credited the authorship of this *Panchavimshatika* to "Nahi Prasiddha" Damodar of the Parua lineage. This Nahi Prasiddha Damodar was the grandson of the <term>Panjikar</term> <note>A traditional registrar of genealogical records in the Mithila region of Bihar.</note> Raghudeva, the son of Panjik Harideva, and the son of Panjik Horeshwar. ## **Distinguishing Features of the Commentary**

The *Nahnidatta-Panchavimshatika* is a text concerning <term>muhurta | the Vedic science of selecting auspicious times for various life events</term>; therefore, clarity is essential. The primary objective of this work is the determination of auspicious moments through the declaration of prescriptions and prohibitions. Consequently, the present commentator has made the text extremely simple by providing tables for <term>nakshatras | stellar constellations or lunar mansions</term>, <term>tithis | lunar days</term>, <term>yogas | specific planetary alignments</term>, <term>lagnas | ascendant signs</term>, months, fortnights, and the <term>ayana | the six-month northern or southern path of the sun</term>. Because of this, readers avoid becoming entangled in complexities of language, syntax, and sentence structure, and instead gain a proper understanding of what is permitted and what is forbidden. 

This represents a scientific method of commentary for understanding such technical texts. At the beginning, the commentator has increased the utility of the work by providing details on lunar days, constellations, the presiding deities of those constellations, planetary alignments, days of the week, and the auspiciousness of stars and the moon. For later astrologers—specifically those who might suggest additional ascendant signs for marriage beyond the traditional three, or those who might explain technical terms like <term>shubha-tithi | auspicious lunar day</term>, <term>parva | sacred festivals or junctures</term>, and <term>anadhyaya | days on which sacred study is prohibited</term> in alternative ways—this detailed account establishes a regulation of <note>original: "idam-ittham"</note> "this is exactly how it is."
