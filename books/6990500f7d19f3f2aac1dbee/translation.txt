# Eternal Wisdom
<note>original: "Letterna sapientia"</note>
God ordained that the divine mysteries, at least in the beginnings of religion, should be handled only by those who were true lovers of true wisdom. For this reason, it happened among the ancients that the same men researched the causes of things and also administered the sacrifices of Him who is the supreme cause of all causes. Therefore, in all generations of men, the same individuals were both philosophers and priests; and this was not without reason. For since the soul—as our **Plato** <note>referring to the Greek philosopher whose works the author sought to reconcile with Christianity</note> suggests—can fly to the celestial Father and homeland with two wings, which are the intellect and the will; and since the philosopher proceeds chiefly with the intellect and the priest with the will; and furthermore, since the intellect illuminates the will and this will inflames the intellect: it is reasonable that those who first found divine things for themselves through intelligence, or attained them from God, should also be the first to rightly venerate those divine things through the will, and teach the correct veneration of these things to others. Thus, the prophets of the **Hebrews** and the **Essenes** <note>a Jewish sect known for communal life and mystic piety</note> devoted themselves to both wisdom and the priesthood together. The philosophers of the **Persians**, because they were appointed to sacred matters, were called **Magi** <term>Magi: Persian priest-philosophers</term>—that is, they were priests. The **Indians** turned to their wise men called **Brahmins** <term>Brahmins: the priestly and scholarly class of India</term> for information on natural things and for purification.

# THE CREATOR OF ALL IS REFERRED TO
# BY THE CHRISTIAN AND SINCERE SOUL.

Nothing displeases God more than being utterly despised; nothing pleases Him more than being adored. Those men who, in some part, transgress His precepts, He punishes more lightly. But He truly strikes down with lightning those who, through ingratitude, malignancy, and pride, rebel against His dominion. For this reason, divine providence <note>The protective care of God or nature as a spiritual power.</note> does not permit any region of the world at any time to be entirely stripped of all religion, although He allows various modes of adoration to be observed in diverse places and times. 

Perhaps this variety of the universe, through divine order, brings forth beauty <note>original: "hornamento" - literally ornament or decoration, implying that diversity enhances the world's perfection.</note>. A great King cares more about being truly honored than about being honored with these or those specific gestures. King Alexander <note>Alexander the Great, used here as a secular parallel to divine sovereignty.</note>, over as many nations as he ruled, was honored in as many ways, whether he was traveling in person or sending his ministers. And everything that was done for his glory, in whatever manner, he accepted—even though one thing might have been more acceptable to him than another. 

It is fitting to think almost this same way regarding the King of the World <note>God</note>. He—in whatever way it may be, provided it is a human way, even if somewhat inept—would rather be honored than, through malignancy, not be honored at all. Men who are subject to Him with some reverence, He corrects immediately like a father, or at least punishes more lightly. But He exterminates and severely torments those who are ungrateful and willfully impious.
