SENECA, Lucius Annaeus, born at Corduba <note>Modern-day Córdoba, Spain</note> around 5 or 4 B.C., of a noble and wealthy family. After an ailing childhood and youth at Rome in an aunt’s care, he was a victim of life-long neurosis <note>The author likely refers to Seneca's documented struggles with asthma and chronic illness</note> but became famous in rhetoric, philosophy, money-making, and imperial service. After some disgrace during the reign of Claudius, he became tutor and then, in A.D. 54, advising minister to Nero, some of whose worst misdeeds he did not prevent. Involved (perhaps innocently?) in a conspiracy, he killed himself by order in A.D. 65. Wealthy, he preached indifference to wealth; an evader of pain and death, he preached scorn of both; and there were other contrasts between his practice and his principles. Wicked himself he was not. Of his works we have 10 mis-called ‘Dialogues’ <note>original: "Dialogi"</note>, seven being philosophical—on providence, steadfastness, the happy life, anger, leisure, calmness of mind, and the shortness of life; 3 other treatises (on clemency <note>The original text likely refers to "De Clementia" here</note>, benefits, and natural phenomena); 124 ‘Moral Epistles’ <note>original: "Epistulae morales"</note> all addressed to one person; a skit on the official deification of Claudius; and 9 rhetorical tragedies (not for acting) on ancient Greek themes. Many ‘Epistles’ and all his speeches are lost. Much of his thought is clever rather than deep, and his style is pointed rather than ample.
