Since it happened several years ago (after your mission to Spain and many other provinces was completed) that your Most Reverend Lordship returned to the City <note>Rome</note> by way of Venice—where you were greatly and deservedly honored by that Most Illustrious Senate—I sought you out, moved by a desire to see you. Having shown you such reverence as I was able, you soon asked me to bring you certain short works by Abbot Joachim, which you had heard were printed through my care. When these were brought and you had read for a little while, you were not a little delighted. You encouraged me, saying that I would do something wonderfully pleasing to you if I would see to the printing of other works by the same author, and especially his work on the Apocalypse. Because I realized this—since I strive day and night to do what is pleasing to your Most Reverend Lordship—by my faith, I would have done it as soon as the printers <term>Calchographi; literally "copper-writers," a term for early printers or engravers</term> could have managed it. 

But now (since a favorable opportunity has arisen), I have wished for this work to come into the light under your distinguished name, protected by your coat of arms on the front and dedicated to your Most Reverend Lordship. I did this partly because your Most Reverend Lordship requested this work from me; partly to further declare my devotion to you; and partly so that the present work might go out into public more honorably, possessing both the grace of novelty and authority among readers. Furthermore, it seemed to me that no one else was more worthy than you to have this famous work addressed to them. For (to omit the innumerable virtues of your wise mind), you far excel all others in the study of the best and most noble disciplines. You have emerged as a most shining light, not only in human affairs but truly in divine ones as well. 

What shall I say? For it is said that you shine not only in Latin literature (the height of which you have remarkably attained) but no less in foreign languages, such as Hebrew, Greek, Chaldean <note>original: "Caldayeis"; usually referring to Aramaic or Syriac, which were essential for biblical studies</note>, and Arabic; so much so that it is not easy to find your equal in this age of ours. You also preserve the holy religion of our true faith with a pure heart. You always display the greatest piety toward all, proven character, the highest equity, justice, and a certain divine clemency of soul. Finally, you are one who, most observant of the Platonic precept, loves all scholars and guides them with a singular and ample patronage. This is especially true since I have no doubt that our entire family of Hermits <note>The Augustinian Order (Order of Saint Augustine), to which both the editor and the Cardinal belonged</note> is being reformed for the better every day under your protection and leadership. Add to this that the same Abbot Joachim celebrates this order with pious honors and increases it with praise beyond others, so that in the final age of the world, the order... <note>The sentence is interrupted by the page break; "ordi" likely continues as "ordinis" or "ordinem".</note>

...that the Order of Hermits <note>original: "Eremitagen"; referring to the Order of Hermits of St. Augustine, to which the author and the Cardinal belonged</note> itself, supported by the power of divine grace, would bring about the faith of Christ (glowing like a burning fire in zeal and the love of God) and that the collapsed Church would be reformed and all things restored. For so he <note>Joachim of Fiore</note> says in chapter 14 in the explanation of this work: "There shall arise an order that seems new, but is not. Clothed in **black garments** and girded above with a **leather belt**. These will grow and their fame will be spread abroad; they will preach the faith, which they will also defend until the end of the world, in the spirit and power of Elijah <note>Elijah was considered the prototype of the monk and hermit</note>. This will be the order of Hermits, imitating the life of angels. Their life will be like a burning fire in the love and zeal of God to burn up thorns and thistles—that is, to consume and extinguish the ruinous life of the wicked—lest the evil abuse the patience of God any longer."

So says Joachim; and the Lord Archbishop of Florence <note>Saint Antoninus of Florence (1389–1459), a famous Dominican theologian and historian</note> recites this same passage literally in the last title of the third part of his historical work, where he treats our aforementioned order. Lord Ulrich of Weimar <note>likely Henry of Friemar the Elder (c. 1245–1340), an influential Augustinian author whose name was often variants of Ulrich or Henry</note>, an eminent doctor of our religion, also mentions this in his treatise on the origin of this order, adding: "And just as by the strength of the root, the branches are naturally brought forth high and wide, so by the virtue of the Institutor and Founder, and of the other succeeding fathers (who were of distinguished holiness and the primary founders of this order), it is most likely to be presumed that a copious blessing will descend upon their sons and descendants through divine mercy."

For the order itself was founded by our father **Blessed Augustine**, from whom it received its rule and form of living; nor can this be turned into doubt. Indeed, Joachim himself testifies to this in the introductory part, saying: "The Order of Hermits was founded by Blessed Augustine in Africa. But the Order of Canons Regular was founded in the western parts by Blessed Rufus <note>Saint Rufus of Avignon, traditionally associated with the Canons Regular</note>, although they also hold the rule of Augustine himself."

Likewise, on Jeremiah chapter 1, he says: "Although a new order of preachers <note>original: "ordo praedicantium"; while this eventually became the name for the Dominicans, Joachim uses it here for a prophesied spiritual group</note> then—that is, in the future tribulation itself—may seem to arise for the Church, nevertheless it was initiated first by the prophets, then by the apostles, and finally by the doctors of the third ecclesiastical period: beloved, known, and chosen by God, signified in Isaac and John the Baptist. From which order indeed came the Order of Monks, and from these two, the other orders afterwards emerged. These two orders are to be revealed again through their new and astounding reformation, so that they seem to arise anew. For just as Elijah lived in the fourth age of the world, leading a hermit's life (at which time likewise Elisha and the sons of the prophets inhabited the deserts) and is to be revealed again in the sixth age, so also the Hermits (who correspond to him by agreement and type) existed and flourished in the fourth ecclesiastical time, and likewise are old and yet to be revealed (in the manner aforesaid) and propagated until the end of the age. They shall enter the times of justice and abundance of peace, and shall see the breaking of the statue <note>A reference to the statue in the Dream of Nebuchadnezzar from the Book of Daniel, representing the fall of worldly kingdoms</note>, and shall overcome the nations and kingdoms with the sword of the divine word, once the truth of the faith has been recognized."

Wherefore Joachim himself continues in the same 14th chapter, saying: "I think that in that time after the ruin of **Babylon** (that is, the carnal church), the life of monks will be like rain to water the surface of the earth in all perfection and justice of fraternal charity. But the life of Hermits will be like a burning fire in the love and zeal of God to burn up the thistles..."

¶ The three states of the world <note> Joachim’s famous "Three Status": the Age of the Father (Old Testament), the Age of the Son (New Testament), and the approaching Age of the Holy Spirit</note>, beginning from Adam and proceeding until the end of the world. The first state of the world <note>original: "status seculi"; Joachim’s famous "Three Ages" theory—Age of the Father (Law), Son (Grace), and Spirit (Monasticism)</note> shone forth from Abraham: its beginning was from Adam, and its end was in Christ. The second state shone from Zechariah, the father of John the Baptist, in whom the priesthood of the Law fell silent; its initiation was from Uzziah, King of Judah, and it is predefined to last until the year 1260. <margin>3rd part 14, 26</margin> The third state begins to shine in our own days: its initiation was from the blessed Benedict <note>St. Benedict of Nursia, founder of Western monasticism</note>, and it shall reach its completion at the end of the age. <margin>1 Maccabees 1; Ibid.</margin> Truly, the first state was led to its end by Antiochus, the "root of sin," who came out of the land of Kittim (that is, Greece). The second state will be given its end by that "planting of the roots" of Frederick <note>A reference to Emperor Frederick II, whom Joachimites often identified as an Antichrist figure</note>: his offspring or the "arms" proceeding from him shall occupy the heights of authority, as is read in the book of Isaiah. <margin>Isaiah 9; Revelation 12</margin> "Each of them," it says, "after their leader, divided the Macedonian land freely, and placed crowns upon their heads, and evils were multiplied on the earth." <margin>Daniel 8</margin> To the third state, however, an end shall be given by the prophet teaching lies, the Antichrist, as if in the tail of the dragon; although he is to reign for only a short space of time, he will nevertheless devastate the entire world beyond what can be believed. <margin>Joel 2; Job 28; Matthew 2</margin> Hence, Joel says: "A devouring fire is before his face, and behind him a desert solitude." And Job: "The land from which bread arose has been overturned by fire in its place." For although Christ, the true bread, arose from the Synagogue, and <margin>Isaiah 6; Luke 4; Genesis 46</margin> always flows from the Roman Church through teaching, that Church is nevertheless burning with the great fevers of avarice <note>Greed or corruption within the Church hierarchy</note>; therefore, let her beware, lest her Lord overthrow her even now in this first tribulation.

Those who entered Egypt designate the apostles.

<margin>Joshua 3; Acts 19</margin> Those who were chosen in the desert signify those upon whom Paul laid his hands.

<margin>Acts 13</margin> Those who carried the cluster of grapes from the Promised Land on a pole prefigure the two future orders <note>referring to the Dominicans and Franciscans, or more generally to new monastic reformers</note> who will be witnesses; there is no doubt they represent the truth of the Gospel. <margin>Revelation 11; Acts 1</margin> "You shall be," He says, "witnesses for me in Judea, and Jerusalem, and Samaria, and even to the end of the earth." "Judea" signifies the Latins; "Jerusalem" signifies the clergy; "Samaria" the Greeks; and the "end of the earth" the peoples who are to be converted to the Lord. <margin>2 Timothy 3; Acts 8; Acts 13</margin> Just as Jannes and Mambres <note>The legendary names of Pharaoh's magicians</note> rose against Moses and Aaron, and as Simon Magus and Elymas rose against Paul and Peter, so shall the Patarenes <note>term for medieval heretical groups like the Cathars</note> and other heretics rise up against the two future witnesses; these heretics are prefigured by the two horns of the beast, as is read in the Apocalypse. <margin>Revelation 11; Revelation 13</margin>
