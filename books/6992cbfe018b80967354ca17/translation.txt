### Alternative Methods

**One method:** Multiply the total number of heads by <gloss>four</gloss>, then subtract the total number of feet. Halve the remainder to find the number of chickens. Alternatively, multiply the heads by <gloss>two</gloss> or divide the total feet by <gloss>four</gloss> <note>original: 四歸 (si gui). In abacus calculation, this refers to a specific division technique.</note> and subtract the total heads; the remainder gives the rabbits.

**Doubling the heads** and subtracting the result from the total feet, then halving the remainder, yields the number of rabbits. Without distinguishing between the two animals, assume all are chickens and multiply the total number of heads by two feet. Subtract this from the actual total feet. The remainder exists because each rabbit has two "extra" feet compared to a chicken; therefore, halving that remainder identifies the number of rabbits.

**Quadrupling the heads** and subtracting the total feet from that result, then halving the remainder, yields the number of chickens. Without distinguishing between the two animals, assume all are rabbits and multiply the total number of heads by four feet. Subtract the actual total feet from this calculated amount; the remainder represents the "missing" feet because chickens only have two feet each. Therefore, halving that remainder identifies the number of chickens.

This method is named **Division by Two Rates**, which is a form of **Differential Apportionment of High and Low Values** <term>guijian chafen</term> <note>original: 貴賤差分. This is a category of Chinese mathematics used for solving problems involving two different categories, such as "expensive" and "cheap" goods, or animals with different numbers of feet.</note>.

### ○ **Chapter 7: Surplus and Deficit**

<term>Surplus and Deficit</term> <note>original: 盈朒 (yíng nǜ). *Ying* refers to an excess or "too much," while *nu* refers to a deficiency or "too little." This chapter teaches the "Rule of Double False Position," a method for solving linear equations by making two guesses and calculating the result based on the errors produced by those guesses.</note>

**Surplus** means "too much." **Deficit** means "too little." This method involves making hypothetical assumptions of excess and shortage to solve for "hidden and complex" numbers. "Hidden and complex" numbers are those not immediately visible; "manifest" numbers are those that can be seen. Therefore, we use the manifest to deduce the hidden. For example, if several people are jointly buying an item: if they contribute too much money, there is a **surplus**; if they contribute too little, there is a **deficit**. When the exact number of people or the price is not known, we can calculate them using the amounts of surplus and deficit.

> **The Song says:**
> <note>These rhyming mnemonics were designed to be memorized by students and merchants to perform calculations quickly on the abacus.</note>
> 
> *The calculator wishing to know of surplus and deficit,*
> *Cross-multiplies the two groups and sums them to find the "Object Dividend."*
> *Combine the surplus and deficit to find the "People Dividend."*
> *Subtract the contribution rates to find the "Divisor."*
> <note>This mnemonic describes the formula for finding the price and the number of people. The "People Dividend" divided by the "Divisor" gives the number of people. The "Object Dividend" divided by the same "Divisor" gives the total price of the item.</note>

# **The Divisor divides the Object Dividend to find the Price; The Divisor divides the Person Dividend to find the Number of People**

The rule states: Set down the proposed rates alongside the surplus and deficiency amounts.  
<gloss>Proposed Rate</gloss> ╳ <gloss>Surplus Rate and Deficiency Rate</gloss>  
By cross-multiplying

the surplus and deficiency amounts with the proposed rates and summing them, the total <gloss>is some amount</gloss> which serves as the <term>Object Dividend</term> <note>original: 物實 (wù shí). This represents the total value or price of the items being purchased.</note>. Separately, sum the surplus and deficiency amounts; this total <gloss>is some amount</gloss> which serves as the <term>Person Dividend</term> <note>original: 人實 (rén shí). This is an intermediate value used to find the number of participants.</note>.

Take the difference between the two proposed rates; the remainder <gloss>is some amount</gloss> which serves as the <term>Divisor</term> <note>original: 法 (fǎ). In Chinese mathematics, this is the number by which you divide the 'Dividend' (Shi).</note>. Divide the Person Dividend [by the Divisor] to obtain the number of people. Divide the Object Dividend [by the Divisor] to obtain the price of the object. 

○ **Another Method:** Sum the surplus and the deficiency to create the Person Dividend. Subtract the proposed rates from one another, and use the remainder as the Divisor. Divide the [Person] Dividend [by the Divisor] to find the number of people. Then, multiply the number of people by a proposed rate; <gloss>whatever the result</gloss>, subtract the surplus or add the deficiency to obtain the price of the object.

If the problem involves people dividing objects <note>rather than people contributing money to buy an object</note>, then you must instead add the surplus or subtract the deficiency to obtain the number of objects.

Whether you cross-multiply the surplus and deficiency with the rates and sum them to find the Object Dividend, and sum the surplus and deficiency to find the Person Dividend; or simply sum the surplus and deficiency for the Person Dividend and use the difference between the rates as the Divisor—the underlying logic is identical. These are the intended applications of the mathematical process.

<note>The character for deficiency, 朒 (nǜ), is pronounced as "nǚ" and means "to shrink" or "to be short of."</note>

<note>
**Technical Terms:**
- **Surplus and Deficiency** (盈不足): The method of solving problems by comparing two incorrect guesses.
- **Object Dividend** (物實): The total value or price.
- **Person Dividend** (人實): The intermediate value used to solve for the number of people.
- **Divisor** (法): The number used to divide the Dividends.
- **Proposed Rate** (出率): The hypothetical amount each person is suggested to contribute.

## Chapter 8: Rectangular Arrays <note>The term *Fangcheng* (方程) is the classical Chinese name for simultaneous linear equations. It literally means "calculating by laying out a rectangular array" of counting rods.</note>

*Fang* means "regular" or "square." *Cheng* means "measure" or "procedure." These problems involve combining various items into a single inquiry. To simplify the complex, one must prioritize the core values. When items are numerous and prices are entangled, they must be arranged in columns and rows. Through the processes of increasing or decreasing (addition and subtraction), identifying "same" and "different" (positive and negative signs), and mutual multiplication, one seeks to find equivalent values. By subtracting the smaller from the larger, the remaining amount of the item becomes the **divisor** <term>Fa (法): The number used to divide, here representing the coefficient of the remaining variable.</term> and the remaining price becomes the **dividend** <term>Shi (實): The total value to be divided.</term>. Dividing the dividend by the divisor yields the price of one item, which is then used to deduce the rest. If the complexity is extreme, solve for each item in succession.
