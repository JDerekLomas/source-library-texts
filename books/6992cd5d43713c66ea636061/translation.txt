### Six Secret Teachings, Part 2
<note>original: "六韜" (Liu Tao). A famous military treatise traditionally attributed to Jiang Ziya (the Grand Duke) during the transition from the Shang to the Zhou Dynasty (c. 11th century BCE).</note>

**The Dragon Teachings: The King's Wings, Chapter 18** <gloss>When a sovereign leads an army, he must employ talented individuals to serve as his "wings and feathers" <note>A metaphor for the essential advisors and specialists who support a leader.</note>.</gloss>

King Wu asked the Grand Duke: "When a king leads an army, he must have his 'limbs and torso' <note>original: "股肱" (gugong), literally 'thighs and forearms,' meaning his most trusted and hardworking ministers.</note> and his 'feathers and wings' to establish a divine and majestic authority. How is this achieved?"

The Grand Duke replied: "In every mobilization of the military, the life of the army rests with the General. The quality of that command depends on his being well-informed and versatile, rather than clinging to a single method. He must assign offices based on individual talent, taking what is best from each person's strengths. He must adapt to changes as they happen to establish the necessary discipline and order. Therefore, a General should have seventy-two 'limbs, feathers, and wings'—specialized assistants—to correspond with the Way of Heaven <note>The number seventy-two is often used in ancient Chinese numerology to represent a complete or cosmic cycle, such as the five-day periods (pentads) of the lunar year.</note> and ensure the staff is complete."
