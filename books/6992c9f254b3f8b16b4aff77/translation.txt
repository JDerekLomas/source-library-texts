## Wind-Heat Sores

Wind-heat sores mostly develop on the four limbs, the chest, and the flanks. At the onset, they appear as small bumps or nodules. They are so intensely itchy as to be unbearable; scratching provides a brief moment of relief. However, if they are scratched frequently over a long period, there are none that do not eventually turn into open sores. In severe cases, fresh blood may even drip from the skin. While the condition resembles scabies, it is not actually scabies.

When these opposing forces of cold and heat agitate one another, the skin and hair follicles bear the brunt of the conflict, thus forming this condition. ## Yellow Water Sores

Yellow Water Sores are also known as "Dripping Pus Sores." This name refers to the fact that a new sore will form wherever the pus from a previous sore flows. 

These sores occur on the surface of the skin and hair, rather than deep within the muscles. Although the condition involves <term>Damp-Heat</term> in the **Spleen Meridian**, it also originates from dryness in the **Lung Meridian**. According to the <term>Five Elements theory</term>, the Spleen (Earth) is the "mother" of the Lungs (Metal). The Spleen arrives to care for its "mother," intending to use its inherent dampness to nourish and regulate the mother's dry state. Little did it know that this dampness contains... <note>In traditional Chinese medicine, organs have a "mother-child" relationship. When the "son" (Lungs) is dry/ailing, the "mother" (Spleen) attempts to compensate, but if the Spleen is burdened by "Damp-Heat," this compensatory moisture becomes pathological, leading to skin conditions like oozing sores.</note>
