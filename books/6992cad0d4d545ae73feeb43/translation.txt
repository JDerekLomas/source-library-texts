**Practicing the fast, offering sincerity, burning incense, chanting, performing the Way, reading the scriptures, making offerings, leaving the household life, repentance, making vows, bearing insults, and practicing patience**—all of these serve to harmonize and govern the mind. Through them, one enters the <term>Bodhimaṇḍa: the "place of practice" or sacred space where one attains enlightenment</term> and separates from all grievances and karmic knots.

The reasons for these practices are as follows:
*   **Copying scriptures** is done to broaden the gates of the Law and teach those who have not yet awakened.
*   **Creating sacred images** is to focus the thoughts and contemplate returning to the Supreme One.
*   **Establishing temples** is to gather the wise and virtuous to transform the foolish and deluded.
*   **Ordaining others** is to select those with a devoted heart to follow the words of holy conduct.
*   **Giving alms** is to abandon greed and stinginess to benefit the poor.
*   **Releasing captive lives** is to arouse great compassion and pity for the ignorant <note>referring here to animals or sentient beings trapped in the cycle of suffering</note>.
*   **Keeping the fast** is to wash the body and mind and restrain worldly desires.
*   **Offering sincerity** is to cut off all evils and stop the root of sin.
*   **Burning incense** is the wish to maintain a pure intention while making offerings to the **Celestial Worthy**.
*   **Chanting** is to sever all worldly attachments and maintain an unbiased mind.
*   **Performing the Way** <note>original: 行道 (xingdao); refers to ritual circumambulation or movement</note> is to circulate the breath and move the physical form to return to the Natural State.
*   **Reading scriptures** is to refine one's study of the subtle truths and understand what was previously unheard.
*   **Making offerings** is to reach both humans and gods and rouse a heart of respect.
*   **Leaving the household life** is to universally depart from the gates of worldly affliction.
*   **Repentance** is to change evil and restrain the emotions so as not to commit further wrongs.
*   **Making vows** is to settle one's original mind and reach the Source of the Way.
*   **Bearing insults** <note>original: 含垢 (hangou); literally "containing the dirt"</note> is to "harmonize one's light with the dust" <note>A classic Daoist phrase from the *Tao Te Ching* meaning to blend into the world without showing off one's brilliance</note> and not seek to be self-evident.
*   **Practicing patience** is to constantly reside in a state of soft yielding rather than being rigid and strong.

All these various "expedient means" and doors of conduct are established with the desire to harmonize the rigid and stubborn mind. If the mind is soft and weak <note>in the sense of being non-resistant and malleable</note>, then delusions do not arise, and these various gates of practice may also be set aside. Then, the fruits of suffering are extinguished, one ascends to the source of **Great Wisdom**, and dwells eternally in a state of clear, absolute emptiness and silence.

It is clearly empty and silent; they eternally sever the ties of habit. Without beginning or end, this realm exists forever and is indestructible.
3.  The third is where the **Transformation of the Way** gradually thins. The pure energy continues its flow downward..."
