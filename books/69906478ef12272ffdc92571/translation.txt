The intention is as follows: First, establish the Shaka year of birth. The current year should be noted. Furthermore, the <term>lunar day</term><note>Sanskrit: tithi</note> should be calculated from the moon; the month is determined by the sun's entry into a zodiac sign based on the estimation of that lunar day.

Count from the birth constellation <note>Nakshatra</note> to the sign occupied by the Sun, multiplied by thirty (30). Or: the Sun's position from the beginning of the zodiac, multiplied by thirty and increased by three in each quarter, gives the days of the Sun's Year-Dasha.

In the groups of three or four constellations, for the remaining portion of the hundred-year life expectancy <note>Shatayushi</note>: the Dasha of the planet is obtained by the sequence starting from Ardra <note>the 6th Nakshatra</note>. The wise should calculate this proportionally for each constellation. The elapsed time <note>nadis/ghatis</note> of that constellation is multiplied by the Dasha period and divided by the total duration of the constellation. The result is the "elapsed Dasha." Subtracting this from the total proportional Dasha gives the "remaining Dasha" to be experienced.

For example: In the group of four starting from Uttarabhadrapada to Bharani, the Dasha of Rahu <note>the North Node</note> is 12 years. If the total duration of Uttarabhadrapada is 57 units, and the proportional Dasha is 3 years, and 16 units have elapsed: 16 multiplied by 3 and divided by 57 equals 1 year elapsed. Subtracting this from the 3-year proportional Dasha leaves 2 years of Uttarabhadrapada Dasha remaining. This logic should be applied everywhere.

This is aligned with the Shaka era year, month, lunar day, and the smaller units of time (ghatis and palas). The rule states: Take the mean Sun at birth and the Shaka year, then add the months and other units elapsed until the start of the period. In that calculation, the mean Sun at the start of the period is derived from the Shaka era and the elapsed time. The period, converted into total days, is placed separately and multiplied by three, then divided by eight hundred and ninety-six (896). The resulting value is added to the total days to produce the civil (Savana) count, adjusted by the <unclear>fractional</unclear> parts from the moment of birth.

The time units (months, days, etc.) existing at that moment are to be understood as the mean Sun for that period.

### On the Commencement of the Planetary Period (Dasha)

At the commencement of a <term>Dasha</term><note>A period of time in which a specific planet exerts its influence over a person's life.</note>, one must first establish the <term>Shaka</term><note>The Shaka era, a historical calendar era beginning in 78 CE.</note> year of birth, along with the position of the **Mean Sun** <note>The theoretical position of the sun assuming a uniform circular orbit.</note> in signs, degrees, and so on. From these, following the sequence of the Shaka year, month, lunar day (<term>Tithi</term>), ghatis, and palas <note>Traditional units of time: 1 ghati is 24 minutes; 1 pala is 24 seconds.</note>, one calculates the <term>Ahargana</term><note>The total count of elapsed days from a fixed epoch.</note> for the civil (<term>Savana</term>) calendar at the time the solar period begins. This is the method for correcting the seeds of the calculation.

The Mean Sun obtained from the Shaka era, combined with the months and other factors, determines the start of the period. The period converted into days—multiplied by 1, 3, 8, and 88.6—results in the civil day-count, which is then added to the birth data.

The intention is as follows: First, record the Shaka year of birth. Then, the current year in the cycle starting from the "Prabhava" year <note>The first year in the 60-year Jovian cycle.</note> should be noted. From the birth position of the Mean Sun, the values for the month, lunar day, ghati, and pala are established. To these, one must add the specific duration of the first <term>Dasha</term> (planetary period) assigned to whichever planet is active, expressed in years, months, and days.

When these are combined, the resulting time—consisting of the Shaka year, month, day, ghati, and pala <gloss>attained</gloss>—indicates the Mean Sun at that specific moment. To verify the accuracy of this, the solar period that has been reached <unclear>of that</unclear> must be converted into a civil day-count with its fractional parts.

The resulting day-count should be added to the day-count at the time of birth. From the specific total day-count thus obtained, the positions of the Mean Planets and the True Planets <note>The actual observed positions of planets versus their theoretical mean positions.</note> are to be calculated. If the calculated Mean Sun matches the month and other factors previously determined, then the accuracy of all calculations is confirmed. This conversion of the solar period into civil time is performed specifically for the purpose of this verification.
