### Miscellaneous Notes, Part II

In Ningguo County of Xuan Prefecture <note>modern-day Anhui Province</note>, there are many two-headed snakes <note>original: "zhishou she" (枳首蛇), literally "trifoliate-orange headed snake," referring to snakes with a head at each end.</note>. They grow to be about a foot long, with black scales and white markings. The color and patterns of both heads are identical, but the scales on one head grow in the reverse direction. There are often dozens of them living together in a single hole under the doorsteps and thresholds of people's homes, appearing much like a cluster of earthworms.

Guan Qi, a Palace Deputy in Service to the Crown Prince <note>original: "Taizi Zhongyun" (太子中允)</note>, once served as the Supervisor of the Ever-Normal Granaries <note>original: "Changping Cang" (常平倉), a government system designed to stabilize food prices</note> for the Guangnan West Circuit. While he was conducting an inspection of the Yongguan region <note>centered around modern Nanning, Guangxi</note>, a minor official was poisoned by a parasite <note>original: "chong" (蟲). This likely refers to tropical diseases or "Gu" poison associated with the humid southern frontier in historical texts.</note>. The man's entire body was covered in festering sores. A doctor claimed he could treat the condition, so Guan Qi summoned him to examine the official.
