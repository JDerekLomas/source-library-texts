THE impossibility of completing my edition of *Meister Eckhart* <note>Eckhart von Hochheim (c. 1260–1328), a German theologian, philosopher, and mystic.</note> as quickly as it has been at various times desired and as I should myself have liked to do, as well as the wish no longer to withhold from scholars the writings of one of the deepest thinkers of all time, has decided me to publish in the meantime the portion contained in the text. The second part, with literary-historical introduction, notes, <term>glossary<note>A list of specialized or archaic terms with definitions.</note></term>, and various additions and appendices, will follow as soon as I can spare the time from my new duties.

This first instalment contains, with the exception of a few short pieces which I obtained only after it was printed, everything of Eckhart’s which, during eighteen years of tireless research, I have been able to collect out of manuscripts and printed books. It is little enough compared with the works of Eckhart still known to Trithemius <note>Johannes Trithemius (1462–1516), a German Benedictine abbot and polymath known for his chronicling of ecclesiastical writers.</note> and of which he gives quite a long catalogue in his book *On Ecclesiastical Writers* <note>original: "De Scriptoribus Eccles."</note> *(see Fabricius's Library of the Church, Hamburg, 1718, folio page 130)* <note>original: "s. Fabricii bibliotheca eccles. Hamburgh, 1718, fol. pag. 130"</note>; but on the other hand what I offer here is at least three times the amount of what has hitherto been known, and the teaching, the philosophic system of this remarkable man can now be presented in a more definite and complete form than it was possible to do from the scanty and untrustworthy data possessed before.
