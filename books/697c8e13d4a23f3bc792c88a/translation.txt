# To the Kindhearted
# Reader.

It is a common custom to place a preface or dedication before all treatises and books that are published, partly to bring the work and the author into public renown, partly to obtain a patron, friend, protection, grace, and favor, and partly to give thanks for benefits either already shown or hoped for.

For these and similar conditions <note>original: "Conditionen"</note>, no preface <note>original: "Præfation"</note> is necessary in this case: for the subject matter itself—namely, the Secret Chancery and Council Chambers of Anhalt—in which the wretched Bohemian disaster <note>original: "Böhaimbische Vnfall." The authors of such secret matters—namely Prince Christian of Anhalt <note>Christian I, Prince of Anhalt-Bernburg (1568–1630), was the chief advisor to the Elector Palatine and a lead diplomat for the Protestant Union.</note>, the Elector Palatine <note>original: "Churpfaltz"</note>, Margrave Joachim Ernst of Brandenburg, Albrecht Count of Solms, Volrath von Plessen, Dr. Ludwig Camerarius, etc., Achatz and Christoph von Dohna, etc.—are already well known in their own right. Some, despite their lower social status, have become famous because of these Bohemian and related functions <term>functionen: the diplomatic and administrative roles performed during the revolt</term> they performed. Furthermore, as can be seen in the context of the documents, these were written and signed with their own hands. Therefore, this work <term>opus</term> or treatise <term>tractat</term> is, in and of itself and in reality <note>original Latin: "reipla," likely a contraction of "re ipsa"</note>, the aforementioned authors' own work.
