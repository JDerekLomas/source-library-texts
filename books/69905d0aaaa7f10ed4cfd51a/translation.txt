“There are a million energies in man. What may we not become when we learn to use them all.” This is the declaration of the poet. Although poetry is usually inspired by transcendental <term>transcendental: relating to a spiritual or non-physical realm that goes beyond ordinary experience</term> visions—and is therefore more or less marked by apparent exaggerations—there is, nevertheless, far more actual, practical truth in this poetic expression than we may at first believe.

No one knows exactly how many energies exist within a human being, but there are so many that even the keenest observers of human activity have found it impossible to count them all. Because most of these energies are remarkable—to say the least—and some are so extraordinary that they appear both limitless in power and infinite in their possibilities, we may well wonder what humanity will become when we learn to use them all.

It has been only a few years—no more than twenty-five—since modern psychology began to announce the new science of human thought and action. Consequently, we have had only a short time to demonstrate what a more intelligent use of our energies and forces can achieve. Yet the evidence is already arriving from all sources, revealing results that frequently seem extraordinary. Humanity <note>original: "Man"</note> can do far more with life and self than in the past; we can activate and successfully apply far more ability, energy, and value than our ancestors ever imagined. This much has been proven during this brief introduction to a new age. What greater things might we reasonably expect, then, after fifty or a hundred more years of developing and applying these vast possibilities that we now know exist within us all?

The purpose of the following pages is not only to discuss these greater powers and possibilities within humanity, but also to present practical methods for applying them. For centuries, we have been aware that there is more to a human being than what appears on the surface, but only in recent years has a systematic effort been made to understand the nature and practical use of this "more."

Those who may believe that the human race is destined to remain as weak and imperfect as it has always been should consider the remarkable progress that has recently been taken in nearly all fields of human activity. They should then remember that the greater powers within humanity, as well as the scientific study of how to use our lesser powers, have been almost entirely neglected. The question that naturally arises is: what might we make of ourselves if we applied the same painstaking science to our own development and advancement as we now apply to other fields? If we did, would we not, within another generation or two, see unmistakable evidence of the coming of a new and superior race? And would strong men and women not become far more numerous than ever before in the history of the world? <note>In the context of this era's "New Thought" movement, the term "superior race" usually referred to an evolution of human consciousness and mental ability rather than biological or ethnic superiority.</note>

It is the purpose of the following pages to encourage as many people as possible to study and apply these greater powers within them. In this way, they may not only become greater, richer, and more worthy as individuals, but may also become the forerunners of that higher and more wonderful race of which we all have so fondly dreamed.

# PROMISE YOURSELF

To be so strong that nothing can disturb your peace of mind.

To talk about health, happiness, and prosperity to every person you meet.

To make all your friends feel that there is something valuable within them.

To look at the sunny side of everything and make your optimism come true.

To think only of the best, to work only for the best, and to expect only the best.

To be just as enthusiastic about the success of others as you are about your own.

To forget the mistakes of the past and press on toward the greater achievements of the future.

To wear a cheerful expression <term>countenance: a person's face or facial expression</term> at all times and give every living creature you meet a smile.

To give so much time to your own improvement that you have no time to criticize others.

To be too big for worry, too noble for anger, too strong for fear, and too happy to permit the presence of trouble.

To think well of yourself and to proclaim this fact to the world—not in loud words, but in great deeds.

To live in the faith that the whole world is on your side, so long as you are true to the best that is within you.

### THE RULING PRINCIPLE IN HUMANITY <note>original: "MAN"</note>

To make every part of this work as useful as possible to the greatest number of people, I will not make a single statement that everyone cannot understand, nor will I present an idea that anyone cannot apply to everyday life.
