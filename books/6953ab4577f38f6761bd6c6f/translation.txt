# THE FIRST BOOK OF THE STEGANOGRAPHY
## OF JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS TO THE MOST SERENE
### PRINCE, THE LORD PHILIP,
### COUNT PALATINE OF THE RHINE,
### DUKE OF BAVARIA,
->and Elector of the Holy Empire, etc.<-

It is the opinion of the most profound men that the most ancient sages, whom they are accustomed to call "philosophers" in the Greek tongue, hid whatever secrets of nature or Art they discovered through various methods and figures, lest they fall into the knowledge of wicked men. The doctors of the Jews also confirm that Moses, the most famous leader of the Israelite nation, did not reveal the ineffable secrets of mysteries in simple words when describing the creation of Heaven and Earth. Furthermore, among our own most learned men, Jerome <note>St. Jerome, the 4th-century scholar who translated the Bible into Latin</note> affirms that almost innumerable mysteries lie hidden in the Apocalypse of John <note>The Book of Revelation</note>. The sages of the Greeks, who were held in no small esteem among their own people, did the same. Moreover, our own most learned philosophers and poets, who devoted their efforts to composing fables, delivered one thing to the unskilled and another to the erudite through the same sequence of narrative by a sagacious invention. As a most devoted lover of Wisdom, even if I cannot imitate these men due to the slowness of my own wit, I nevertheless admire them, and because I am able, I do not neglect to read them with the greatest diligence. For when I consider that they devised great things by their own study for the welfare of men, I constrain myself with a certain force and grow fervent, pushing myself toward some imitation, however recent, of those who came before. Nor, as I believe, has this opinion entirely deceived me. Indeed, I have learned many things through continuous study of reading which I did not know before, and I have unlocked an entrance for the reader to investigate more secret and more inclined mysteries through my thoughts. For even if not...

And I did not create the invention of this most secret art so much at the instance of the most Serene Prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, and Prince Elector of the Holy Roman Empire—always the most invincible lover of philosophers, most wise, and most peaceful—because he is the same as my own wishes. To him, I have deservedly commended that this great secret be revealed, and not without the greatest labors have I compiled it into this, his volume. 

I have considered it my reasoning that the most noble things should not be offered to the common crowd of the unskilled, or especially to perverse men. Thus, I have directed this work (which teaches mysteries) toward the understanding of mysteries in such a way that no one from the number of the unskilled—no one, I say, unless he be a most diligent student who has studied this science with the utmost accuracy—shall be able to penetrate fully to our intention by his own virtue, unless he receives it from a teacher. The aforementioned words will allow the evil and immodest mind and intention of a lover or fornicator to be understood most broadly, even while someone else is present and praising the letters as excellent. In the same way, one can send back a single desire—as far and wide as one wishes—most secretly using those same letters or others, arranged in a public and sufficiently ornate sequence. 

Indeed, although this invention is excellent in itself and quite useful in practice, nevertheless, if it should come to the knowledge of the wicked—which may God forbid—the order of the entire Commonwealth <note>original: "Reipub." or Respublica; referring to the state or organized society</note> could be greatly disturbed over the course of time. Public trust would be endangered, and all letters, legal instruments, writings, and even the very speech of men would fall under the gravest suspicion. No one would then believe in letters, however holy and honest, without fear, and would rarely place faith in epistles. For no matter how honest and virtuous the words might be, it would be thought that trickery, fraud, and deception always lay beneath them. Men would become so fearful—no less toward friends than toward adversaries—as to be entirely full of suspicion. 

Nor can anyone, even if he lived for a thousand years, become so learned and expert in this secret doctrine of writing that there would not remain for others infinite, most hidden, and most secret ways within this very art of writing. These methods, working according to the will of any expert student, suggest themselves to all—methods which the student and his teacher have not yet even grasped. 

For since the spirits of the air <note>The "aerei spiritus" are central to Trithemius's system, acting as messengers who convey information across distances.</note>, both good and evil, were created by the Most High God for our service and progress—and through whose intelligence all these holy arts are revealed—they are to us infinite in number and incomprehensible in their virtues. # Prologue

It will be proven otherwise. There is in this science an infinite and most useful Chaos <note>The author uses "Chaos" here to describe a vast, unformed potentiality or a primordial source of knowledge</note>, which no one can perfectly comprehend; for however learned and experienced they may be in this art, you have grasped less than a seventh part, because that is what remains. This profound and most secret art has this property: it easily renders the student incomparably more learned than the teacher, provided he is disposed by nature to <gloss>perceive</gloss> these <gloss>spirits</gloss>. And in these matters, which shall come forth through Cabalistic tradition <note>original: "traditione cabalystica"; referring to the reception of mystical or esoteric knowledge</note>. 

But lest any reader of this art should think—when in the following book I show the names, offices, orders, differences, properties, prayers, and any other operations of the spirits, through whose intelligences all these secrets are perfected—and should establish or assert that I have practiced Necromancy <note>The conjuration of the dead or demonic magic</note> and magic, or have contracted a pact with Demons, or believe me to use or have used any other superstition, I have deemed it necessary and opportune to clear my reputation and name from such a hostile stain, fault, and blot, by claiming the truth for myself through a solemn protestation in this prologue. 

Therefore, before God and the entirely Almighty God, from whom nothing at all can be hidden, and as if in the presence of JESUS CHRIST his only-begotten son, who is to judge the living and the dead, I say in truth—nay, I even protest—that all and singular things which I have said or am about to say in this work, and all properties of the science or art, the methods, figures, or operations, traditions, receptions, formations, inventions, institutions, alterations, and everything which pertains to its speculation, invention, construction, operation, and practice, either in part or in whole, and all things contained in this our volume, rest upon true Catholic and natural principles. They all and individually stand with God, with a good conscience, without hostility to the Christian faith, with the integrity of ecclesiastical tradition; without any superstition whatsoever, without Idolatry, and without any pact with evil spirits.
