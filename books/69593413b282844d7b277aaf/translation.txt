# Of Both Worlds
## Namely, the METAPHYSICAL, PHYSICAL, AND TECHNICAL HISTORY of the GREATER as well as the LESSER WORLD

->Divided into two Volumes according to the difference of the WORLD.<-

->BY THE AUTHOR ROBERT FLUDD, also known as de Fluctibus, Esquire and Doctor of Medicine of Oxford.<-

### Volume One
->Concerning the History of the Macrocosm <note>The "Macrocosm" refers to the great world or the entire universe.</note>, divided into two treatises.<-

Of which

*The first concerns*
> The Metaphysical origin of the Macrocosm and its Creatures.
> The Physical progress of the Macrocosm in its generation and corruption.

*The second concerns the Art that Mimics Nature <note>original: "Arte Naturæ Simia" — literally "the ape of nature," a common Renaissance concept where human art and technology imitate the natural creative processes of God.</note>, produced within the Macrocosm and therein nourished and multiplied; whose principal daughters we have here reviewed by a living anatomy, namely:*
> Arithmetic.
> Music.
> Geometry.
> Perspective.
> The Art of Painting.
> Military Art.
> The Science of Motion.
> The Science of Time.
> Cosmography.
> Astrology.
> Geomancy <note>A method of divination that interprets markings on the ground or the patterns formed by tossed handfuls of soil, rocks, or sand.</note>.

->AT OPPENHEIM.<-
->At the expense of JOHANN THEODOR DE BRY.<-
->From the press of HIERONYMUS GALLER.<-
->IN THE YEAR 1617<-

It is yours, O <term>Creative Nature</term> <note>original: "Natura naturans," a philosophical term referring to God as the active, creative force behind the universe rather than the created world itself</note>, infinite and glorious, to regenerate my soul, which is wasting away in ignorance, by the power of your splendor. It is yours, by the admirable clarity of the Holy Spirit of discipline, to awaken, bring to life, and elevate to the true peak or summit of knowledge those sparks of reason that survived after the <term>Fall of Adam</term> <note>original: "Adami lapsum," the biblical loss of perfection in Eden</note> and have been most miserably submerged for a long time in the abyss of bodily darkness. TO WHOMSOEVER it is denied to understand the secrets and sacred mysteries of *Nature, stripped bare and uncovered, as offered to us by God, the best and greatest; O most mighty King, fortified by knowledge, for such a man it will be impossible to know himself and to aspire to the true enjoyment of the Holy Spirit of understanding. Therefore, let the storehouse of nature be diligently uncovered, with contemplation leading the way, so that we may finally arrive at the sanctuary of GOD and His wisdom. I speak now of great things—let it be said with Your Majesty’s kind permission—things which cannot be acquired or obtained by the royal power of the whole world, nor by violence, majesty, or any human art, but are drawn only from the spring of the good and sincere will of* GOD, *poured down from the holy heavens, and granted to the worthy according to the divine pleasure. May you be, O* MOST SERENE KING, *illuminated by a ray of divine light, purified from human mists and filth, adorned in mind with heavenly gifts, a despiser of earthly things, a contemplator of heavenly and divine things, free from Devilish arrogance, endowed with Christian humility, and full of Holy justice. ->To Your Majesty<-
->most devoted and most humble subject<-

## A MOST LUCID EXPLANATION
## OF THE EMBLEM AT THE BEGINNING
## OF THIS VOLUME.

BEFORE the creation of things, the incomprehensible and almighty Creator of the Macrocosm <note>The "Great World" or the entire universe.</note> divided the universal mass into three parts differing in purity by His immense power. From the purest part, He ordained that the Cherubim, Seraphim, Archangels, and all other orders of Angels be created; from the less pure, the heavens and the ethereal regions; and from the most impure, the elements and all sublunary <note>Things below the moon; the earthly realm subject to change and decay.</note> things, by saying: "Let them be made," and they were made.

*The first division of matter, however, He disposed beyond the comprehension of natural things because of the simple purity of its substance; for it is metaphysical and supernatural, which modern writers of Philosophy confirm is circumscribed by the Empyrean Heaven—that is, the burning and fiery realm. But the remaining parts of the less pure mass, namely the ethereal and the elementary, are rightly called "natural" because they are subject to Nature (the governess of the Microcosm under GOD). Furthermore, two efficient causes of these parts contained within the fabric of the Macrocosm are enumerated: namely, Nature, and her Ape, which we call Art. The Father and Lord of Nature is GOD, the Best and Greatest; she is closest to Him, and from Him we recognize that all her virtues and effects are derived. She is, as it were, the knot and bond of the elements, and possesses the power of rightly tempering the mixture of elements in their due proportions, mixing them aptly in every compound, and impressing upon each species its fitting form. Nature is also a certain infinite force procreating like from like, since she is the fertile mother of all qualities and things, which she increases and nourishes. And to describe her in a single word according to the opinions of Zoroaster and Heraclitus, she is an invisible fire, which they call the Soul of the World <note>original: "anima mundi"</note>. We, however, have depicted her visibly in almost the same manner as some of the more recent Philosophers; we have done this with the intention that her power might be better understood by the reader's mind. Thus, we have fashioned a naked virgin of tender and blooming age, with star-bearing and beautifully gilded hair, praiseworthy for the clearest keenness of her eyes, and lovely for the blushing color of her cheeks; and the virgin was so remarkable in the whiteness of her whole body, and so sweetly adorned with all the gifts of the greatest Creator, that the Poets could prefer neither Pallas, nor Venus, nor Juno, nor any other goddess, however charming, to her beauty. The mobile <note>referring to the *Primum Mobile* or First Mover, the outermost sphere of the heavens</note> is governed by her, and the eighth sphere, adorned with stars, is handled and turned by her hands. By the influence of that same sphere, the generations of elementary things are daily prepared and arranged by her fingers. The bodies of the Planets also serve as instruments for the works of Nature; they are, as it were, little hammers by which metals are produced in the terrestrial mines. The region of the heart and breast of this virgin <note>Nature is personified here as a celestial virgin</note> is the true seat and matrix of the heavenly Sun, and her womb is filled with the lunar body. From her breasts, she perpetually instills and suckles all the creatures of the elements with innate heat and radical moisture, from which their life and vegetation arise. 

By a certain golden splendor of her heart, the stars—both fixed and wandering <note>planets</note>—are illuminated, and they borrow, as it were, their souls, lives, and beauties from it. Their imperceptible influences are infused into her matrix by the Mercurial Spirit (which the Philosophers call the Spirit of the Moon) and are carried downward to the earth, penetrating even to its center; by their impressions, diverse generations, differing in both place and species, are continually made. By the actions of this Nature, the Planets come together, and by this joining, they produce various things differing in species in the animal, vegetable, and mineral kingdoms. 

Starting from the lower parts, beginning at the loins, the four Elements are encompassed; for the right foot occupies the earth, while the left occupies the water, signifying the conjunction of her sulfurous and mercurial nature. By these, it is argued that nothing can be generated or grow without the union of both. This Nature, I say—not a GODDESS, but the closest minister of GOD—has beneath her a handmaid or follower who, by imitating her Mistress and impressing the likenesses of things produced by her into herself through consensus, follows and imitates her footsteps and outlines in a wonderful manner. 

And hence it is that we have depicted her in the form and habit of an Ape under the feet of Nature; which, by a more worthy name, we call **Art**. This beast (I mean Art), born from human ingenuity, learns many secret, beautiful, and notable things from the diligent observation of her Mistress, with which she is accustomed to enrich men in every kind of learning; so also, conversely, she brings no small utility and profit to that same teacher and Mistress. For unless Art, produced by human ingenuity, sometimes gives aid in certain matters, Nature herself would soon fail in the perfection of her operation. 

For Art sometimes corrects Nature, supplies what is missing, and indeed, in the discourse of mineral things, seems to surpass her—if only we give some faith to those Chemical Philosophers <note>Alchemists</note>. This is especially true since it is certain and proven by experience that in the animal circle, eggs hatch more quickly, silkworms are multiplied and transformed by their growth into diverse forms in the manner of Proteus, and bees are procreated from the head of an ox; in the vegetable circle, crops grow and multiply by the artifice of the plow (by which fields are tilled and harrowed), trees by grafting, and herbs and other vegetables by planting; and in the mineral circle, metallic bodies and lesser minerals are prepared for medicine as well as for other uses, cleansed of their dross, and the **Physic Stone** <note>The Philosopher's Stone</note>, whose praises the Philosophers have preached so much, is forged. It is therefore our purpose in this volume to discuss these two effective causes of the Macrocosm both clearly and copiously.
