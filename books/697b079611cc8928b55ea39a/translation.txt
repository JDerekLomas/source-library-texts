### Chapter I. What is understood by the word Salvation in Holy Scripture:

§. 1. By Salvation <note>original: *Seeligkeit*. This term carries a dual meaning of both "blessedness" and "salvation," referring to the state of the soul's eternal happiness.</note>, the Holy Spirit in Holy Scripture refers not only to the redemption from every evil that came upon us through Adam’s fall <note>The biblical account of the first sin in the Garden of Eden, which the author views as the origin of human suffering.</note>, but also the bestowal of everything good. This was earned for us through Christ’s obedience, incarnation <note>original: *Menschwerdung*; literally "becoming human."</note>, suffering, death, and resurrection; it is granted to us in Baptism, revealed through the Word, and sealed through the holy Sacraments.

For just as Adam, through his fall into sin, did not only lose all the good things that were given to us by God in the Creation, but conversely brought every evil upon us—such as sin, curse, wrath, death, judgment, darkness, hell, damnation, and all manner of plagues and punishments, both temporal and eternal. In this way—if you continue comforted in faith, patience, and hope toward God, and remain steadfast with Christ until the end, and overcome with Him and Paul through the word of your testimony and the setting aside of your own life—you shall expect the same crown and reward as they, <note>Breckling refers to the crown of life promised to martyrs and faithful witnesses.</note> Revelation 2, 3, 12 and following; 2 Timothy 4; 1 Peter 5.

This is enough regarding the Confession; <note>original: "Bekentnis." In this context, it refers to the public profession of one’s faith, especially when such a profession leads to persecution by authorities.</note> if you know these things, blessed are you if you do them, John 13. And if you must then leave the world, temporal things, your parents, wife, children, houses, goods, and honorable name—yes, often even body and life—for Christ’s sake, you shall find all such things again in abundance, and receive them back a hundredfold here and there. For Christ has already provided richly for you in this eventuality, and has prepared a place for you here and there, where you shall be nourished and protected, both temporally and eternally, from the persecutions of the world and of the dragon, its god, within this Babylon. <note>original: "Babel." Radical Pietists often used "Babylon" to describe the established, institutionalized churches which they viewed as corrupt and oppressive.</note> Indeed, He has obligated Himself with an eternal handwriting to richly reward everything, even down to every drink of cold water given to you, Matthew 10, chapter 19; Mark 10; Revelation 12; John 14, 15, 16. Whoever desires further information should read the heartfelt book by the dear confessor Tilemann Heshusius regarding the Confession. <note>Tilemann Heshusius (1527–1588) was a prominent Gnesio-Lutheran theologian known for his uncompromising stance on doctrine and his frequent exiles due to his "confessions" of faith.</note>
