# Publisher’s Note

We experience immense joy in presenting this revised, modified, and enlarged edition of *Uttara Kalamrita* <note>A classic 17th-century Sanskrit treatise on Hindu astrology, traditionally attributed to Kalidasa</note> to our learned readers, featuring a new format and delightful presentation.

The enthusiasm with which readers embraced the previous edition, and the open-hearted praise our efforts received from astrologers <term>Jyotirvid: a scholar or knower of the stars/astrology</term> across India—particularly from those in South India—encouraged us to venture into making this work even more useful and accessible. The late astrologer Mr. Jagannath Bhasin was the first to present this work to Hindi-speaking readers in his simple and easy-to-understand linguistic style. Following the maxim "even the most careful traveler may stumble" <note>original: "Gacchatah skhalanam kvapi" — a Sanskrit proverb suggesting that errors are natural in any great undertaking</note>, our generous readers brought certain printing errors in the previous edition to our attention. We express our deep gratitude for their kindness and, while fully honoring their suggestions, we dedicate this work once again to them.

We have full hope and confidence that learned readers will certainly feel a sense of satisfaction with this new edition and will continue to encourage us as they have in the past.

—The Publisher

## **A Few Words**

“**Uttara Kalamrita**” <note>Literally "The Ultimate Nectar of Time," a celebrated 17th-century Sanskrit treatise on Vedic astrology</note> is a famous and universally accepted text in the world of astrology. Until now, the circulation of this original work has been mostly confined to South India. It is a matter of great joy that the enthusiastic publisher, who is continuously serving astrology lovers through high-quality publications, has made a commendable effort to promote it in North India through this translation.

As a result of this effort, we are fully confident that all countrymen will now be able to benefit from those astrological secrets which are the specialties of this book—such as the state of wealth, considerations regarding children, the nature of one's wife, the application of mantras <note>sacred chants or formulas</note>, methods for the yearly horoscope, whether a woman will be wealthy or ordinary, answering queries when a birth chart is unavailable, obtaining the correct ascendant for a question even without a clock, the strength of Saturn, and other such subjects.

For students of astrology, this book is of no less value than a treasure, because the list of significations provided in this book regarding the "Houses" <term>Bhavas: the twelve divisions of the zodiac in a chart representing different life areas</term> and "Planets" <term>Grahas: the celestial influencers in Vedic astrology</term> is so extensive that you will not find such a list in any other text, whether from North or South India.

Not only this, but you will also find new and useful material on the fundamental principles of astrology in this book. "Inverted Royal Yoga" <term>Vipareeta Raja Yoga: a specific planetary combination where the lords of "bad" houses create success through adversity</term>, the expansion of Parashara's royal yogas, lost horoscopy <note>the art of reconstructing a birth chart when birth details are unknown</note>, the placement of Venus in the twelfth and sixth positions, and many other such subjects are included, upon which you will likely find enlightenment for the first time.

This is undoubtedly an excellent addition to astrological literature. We hope that lovers of astrology will adopt this work with respect.

**Jagannath Bhasin** <note>A renowned 20th-century Indian astrologer and author known for translating and commenting on classical texts</note>

### Determining the Desired Time and Related Factors

->The exact time of birth in *nadis* and other units should be determined by the shadow of a gnomon or by instruments.<-
->One must calculate the day and night duration, and from the tropical sun and the desired time, find the ascendant.<-
->With a joyful heart and by the Grace of the Teacher, the wise scholar should calculate the planets through observational mathematics to find their true positions in signs and degrees.<-
->Likewise, one should determine the houses along with their junctions.<-

The exact time of birth for humans and other beings must be accurately determined using the shadow of a *Shanku* <term>Gnomon: a vertical pillar used to tell time by its shadow</term> or other instruments like clocks. One must also ascertain the length of the day and night at the time of birth. Similarly, using the *Sayana Ravi* <term>Tropical Sun: the sun's position adjusted for the precession of the equinoxes</term> and the *Ishta Kala* <term>Desired Time: the specific moment of the event being analyzed</term>, the *Lagna* <term>Ascendant: the rising sign on the eastern horizon</term> should be clarified. Thereafter, by the grace of the Guru, one should calculate the planets using mathematical methods so that their positions in the zodiac signs correspond exactly to their observed positions in the sky. ### Determining Strength from the Seven Divisions—

> Having first written down the auspicious strength arising from the signs <note>original: "Rupa-adyam bhavanoditam"</note> as previously described, one should then record half of that strength for the other six divisions—the Hora and so on—based on the lords of those divisions. || 9 ||
> By summing the strength generated from all these divisions, the total strength arising from the seven divisions <term>Saptavargaja-bala: the combined strength a planet gains from its position in seven specific zodiacal sub-divisions</term> is determined for all seven planets. From the point of debilitation to the point of exaltation, and from the exaltation point back to the debilitation point, the degrees traveled by the planets are divided by one hundred and eighty <note>original: "khaganendubhih," a numerical code where 'kha'=0, 'gagana'=8, and 'indu'=1</note> to find the strength in units. || 10 ||

Multiply the quotients obtained in both places by three and two respectively, and add 1 to each product. Divide these sums by seven. The remainder at the first position will be the <term>Varshapati</term> <note>Lord of the Year</note> and the remainder at the second position will be the <term>Masapati</term> <note>Lord of the Month</note>.

The planet ruling the day on which the birth occurs is the <term>Varadhipa</term> <note>Lord of the Day</note>, also known as the <term>Varesh</term>. After determining these three lords, only one remains to be found: the <term>Horapati</term> <note>Lord of the Hour</note>. The word <term>Hora</term> means one hour. There are two ways to indicate the time of birth—the <term>Ishtakala</term> <note>desired time of birth</note> calculated from sunrise, and the <term>Varapravritti</term> <note>commencement of the weekday</note>. Just as the time of sunrise varies everywhere due to differences in latitude and longitude, the time of the commencement of the weekday also varies. Roughly speaking, the start of the weekday can be considered from the local sunrise. To find the precise time, one must add or subtract according to the difference from the prime meridian using the <term>Chara Ghati</term> <note>ascensional difference</note> and other factors. The author has not made this explicit. Nevertheless, using the rough value does not result in any significant difference for practical purposes. A simple method for this is to subtract the local sunrise time from the birth time (in hours and minutes) to see which hour, or <term>Hora</term>, was active at the time of birth. For this, multiply the birth time or <term>Ishtakala</term> by 2 and divide by 5. The remainder will be the elapsed hours. Now, to find the <term>Horadhipa</term> <note>Lord of the Hour</note> of the current hour, count forward from the weekday lord in this specific sequence to find the <term>Horesh</term>.

The sequence is as follows: Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars.

In this manner, identify the <term>Varshesh</term> (Year Lord), <term>Masesh</term> (Month Lord), <term>Dinesh</term> (Day Lord), and <term>Horesh</term> (Hour Lord). Assign them strength in increasing quarters: 15 <term>Kalas</term> <note>minutes of arc used as a unit of strength</note>, 30 <term>Kalas</term>, 45 <term>Kalas</term>, and 60 <term>Kalas</term> respectively. This is the strength of the Year Lord and others. Thus, the method for calculating <term>Kala Bala</term> <note>Temporal Strength</note> has been explained up to this point.

### The Classification of Houses as Centers and Others

->The Ascendant, the seventh, the fourth, and the tenth houses are called the four Centers or Thorns; the houses following the Centers are the Succedents, and those following them are the Cadents. ## The Special Role of the Degrees of Yoga-Forming Planets—

> Even in the manifestation of a favorable yoga or an unfavorable one, if the two yoga-forming planets—
> with the slower one ahead and the faster-moving planet behind—are within twelve degrees of each other;
> the full effect occurs at zero degrees of difference, and zero effect at twelve degrees, with proportional results in between.
> If the faster planet is ahead of the slower one by these degrees, it destroys the desired outcome. || 32 ||

When two planets form a good or bad <term>Yoga</term><note>A specific planetary combination or configuration in astrology.</note>—this yoga will only manifest when the difference in the positions of these two planets (calculated by degrees within the sign or house) is not more than 12 degrees, and the slower-moving planet is situated ahead of the faster-moving planet in terms of degrees. This yoga is considered "complete" when the degrees of these two planets are identical. When the difference is 12 degrees, it is considered "broken" or ineffective. For a difference of less than 12 degrees, the result is calculated by proportion. If the faster-moving planet is 12 or more degrees ahead of the slower-moving planet, it results in a yoga that destroys one's desires.

## Detailed Consideration of Results from the Eight Padas—

> ->As far as the Lord of a House is situated from that House, the 'Pada' lies that many houses further from said Lord.<-
> ->One should predict the increase or decrease of fortunes based on the auspicious or inauspicious nature of that Pada.<-
> ->In this manner, the Padas for the Ascendant, Wealth, Home, Children, Spouse, Fortune, Kingdom, and Loss should be calculated.<-
> ->The Pada of the twelfth house is called Upapada, and the Pada of the seventh is Darapada. || 34 ||<-

The 'Pada' <note>also known as Arudha Pada; a calculated point in a horoscope representing the perceived or manifest reality of a house</note> of any house is located as many houses away from the House Lord as the Lord is from the house itself. One should judge the gains or losses of that house based on the condition of its Pada. In this system, the Padas for the following eight houses should be calculated: the 1st (Ascendant), 2nd (Wealth), 4th (Home/Happiness), 5th (Intellect/Children), 7th (Spouse), 9th (Fortune), 10th (Career/Kingdom), and 12th (Loss/Expenditure). The Pada of any house is generally referred to as a 'Pada,' but the Pada of the 12th house is specifically called the **Upapada** <note>literally 'the secondary foot,' often used to judge marriage and spouse</note>, and the Pada of the 7th house is called the **Darapada** <note>from 'Dara' meaning wife; relates to business partners and physical union</note>.

The determination of the Pada here is based on the *Jaimini Sutras* <note>an ancient, foundational text of Indian astrology attributed to the sage Jaimini</note>. Every house has a Pada. What is a Pada?
(i) To find the Pada of a specific house, look at where its Lord is situated.
(ii) Count the number of houses from the house in question to its Lord.
(iii) Count that same number of houses forward from the Lord’s position. The house where the count ends is the Pada of the original house.

For example, if we want to find the Pada of the Ascendant (Lagna): Suppose the Ascendant is Sagittarius. Jupiter (the Lord of Sagittarius) is situated in the 5th house in Aries. Since the Lord is five houses away from the Ascendant, we count five houses forward from the Lord (the 5th house). Thus, the 9th house becomes the Pada of the Ascendant.

If the House Lord is situated in the 7th house from its own house, then that house itself becomes its own Pada. For instance, if the Lord of the Ascendant is in the 7th house, then seven houses from the 7th is the 1st house again; therefore, the Ascendant itself is the Ascendant-Pada. (See *Jaimini Sutras*, Chapter 1, Quarter 1, Sutras 26-31).

The author of the *Uttara Kalamrita* <note>a famous 17th-century astrological treatise by Kalidasa</note> has stated that one should only calculate the Padas for eight specific houses (the Angles, Trines, and the 2nd/12th positions), excluding the 3rd, 6th, 8th, and 11th houses.
