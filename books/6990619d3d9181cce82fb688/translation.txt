On Tuesday, the 20th day of August, 1566, John Walsh was examined upon certain interrogatories <term>interrogatories</term> <note>a formal set of questions used in a legal investigation.</note> concerning witchcraft. First, being asked about his place of residence, he said that he lived in a parish called Netherbury <note>original: "Netherberry"</note> in Dorsetshire.

Secondly, being asked whether he was ever the servant <note>original: "man"</note> of Sir Robert Drayton, he said that he was retained in his service for a period of seven years.

any time any such toad <note>original: "Tode"; in 16th-century English witchcraft, toads were frequently cited as "familiars" or spirit companions used to carry out a witch's commands.</note>, or that he ever made any such images <term>Images</term> <note>Wax or clay effigies used in sympathetic magic; harming the image was believed to harm the person it represented.</note> to hurt man, woman, or child: He affirms by the oath which he has taken, that he never had such toads, or ever made such images.

And fifteenthly <note>original: ".xv."</note>, being asked whether he ever did any hurt to man, woman, or child, or to their goods or cattle <note>original: "Cattels"; in this period, "cattle" often referred generally to all livestock and movable property (chattels).</note>: he says by the oath that he has taken, that he never did any such hurt either in body or goods.
