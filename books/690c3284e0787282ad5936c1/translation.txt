# Preface of the Peasant himself/
### translated from the Low German.

Several signs, by which a person can examine themselves, whether they are rightly reborn, or not? He who is reborn, and reads this, will also rightly grasp and understand it, and feel it in his heart. But he who does not yet have the rebirth, to him this will be a completely incomprehensible and foreign work and writing, which he will not only not understand nor love, but rather hate, blaspheme, and despise. However, when pious hearts read this, they will value these few letters, and will know how to say even more about this blessed matter of rebirth from their own experience than they read here. By this, the kind reader (when he has read through this and also well understood it) can test and examine himself: whether he is a right and true converted Christian-person and child of God, or not? Read then happily, and be commended to God.

->This wish I direct toward you,<-
->whether you are pious, or not.<-

### I.

The first sign of a reborn person is that he, through the enlightenment of the Holy Spirit, learns to recognize his great damnable sins, and the eternal righteousness of God against and above them; which will bring him such anxiety, anguish, fear, and terror, that almost the whole world becomes too narrow for him. He then prays and sighs day and night, and has no rest in his heart, until God hears him, forgives him his sins, and accepts him again as His child in Christ; which anguish and anxiety he also does not forget, as long as his eyes remain open.

**2.** When he then feels in his heart that God the Father has forgiven all his sins through the bloody death of Christ, he receives rest, peace, and joy; then nothing in

the world is so painful to him as sin, as that with which he has so long and often angered and grieved GOD, his so dear Father. Then he begins to marvel, indeed to rejoice, and to weep from the inner movement of his heart, cries out, and sheds true tears of joy and love: then true childlike fear <note>original: "kindliche furcht" — a theological term for fear rooted in love and respect for a parent, rather than "slavish fear" (knechtlicher furcht) which is fear of punishment.</note> and shame come into his heart, and because of humility and low regard for himself, and high regard for the grace of God, he hardly dares to open his mouth before the holy, highly-praised Majesty of his God; then he begins to hate and leave sin, to kill and root it out, with which he had previously so often angered his God; and this he does then not out of slavish fear, but out of love for his Father.

**3.** Thereupon he also does away with, for that very purpose, his former sinful company, and just as he previously loved them, so he now hates them, indeed with a greater holy hatred, than he previously loved them. He hates them as children of destruction, as if a young devil sat within them, who would mislead him again into the company of cursed scoundrels. When he meets them, he turns red with shame, because the evil ways and works, against his will, then occur to him again. Instead of previously attending their company with a joyful heart, he now stands with them with such a trembling heart, like a sheep among wolves; he gladly looks at their backs, and says secretly within himself: O my soul, never come again into their counsel.
