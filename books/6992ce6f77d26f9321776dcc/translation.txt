For the entire army [of 12,500 men]:

### Methods for Selecting Ore

The highest grade of ore is purple or black in color. When chewed, it has a consistency like wax and a taste as sweet as honey <note>These sensory descriptions—texture and taste—were common mnemonic devices for pre-modern miners to identify mineral purity and the presence of lead or sulfur</note>. This is considered superb. Any ore that yields five mace or more is also of high quality.

The next grade is known as **Chicken-Dropping Ore** <note>original: *jifen kuang* (雞糞礦); likely named for its speckled, variegated appearance</note>. Its shape resembles malted grain, consisting of small, shattered pieces where black and white colors are intermingled. For every tael of this ore, one gets approximately eight mace of silver upon smelting.

The grade following that is **Yellow Sand Ore**. It is yellow in color and as fine as grains of rice. Every tael of this ore yields six or seven mace of silver.

Additionally, there is a **Common Grade**, which yields two or three mace of silver per tael. 

For the aforementioned four types of ore, the supplemental materials <note>This likely refers to lead or fluxing agents required for the cupellation process</note> should be used without restriction on quantity—the more, the better. Generally, twenty **catties** <term>jin: a unit of weight, approximately 600 grams</term> should be the standard rate; it is better to have too much than too little. All recovered ore should be blended and mixed evenly using earth from the original mine and local water.
