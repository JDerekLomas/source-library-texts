The sacred authors and interpreters have noted a very broad and clear distinction between the words for **creating**, **forming**, and **making**. Thus, **creation** <note>Latin: *creatio*</note> is the first arrangement of matter or of any individual thing, from which the potential of the form is later drawn out. **Formation** <note>Latin: *formatio*</note>, however, is the constitution of the form itself, whether it is raised up from the outside or from nothingness and enters into the things themselves, or is drawn out from the potential of matter by divine command. Therefore, the omnipotence or unity of God first compels things themselves into their own matter or the unity of the individual; this is the action of the Father, which is more eminent in Him than in the Son or in Wisdom. Consequently, the arrangement of the form—according to which the power of nature is most widely spread out—is known to relate chiefly to the divine Wisdom, whom we call the Son and the offspring of the Father.

It properly "makes" all things, so that just as it proceeds from eternity from the Father and the Son (or from power and wisdom), so also it joins the union of matter, which is from the Father, with the expansion of form, which is from the Son.
