The more excellent an art is, the fewer practitioners it has, and the more critics. Because of its loftiness, it is not for everyone to climb. **Plato** seems to have judged no art among all to be more noble than **Astrology** <term>Astrology: in this period, the term "astrologia" encompassed both the scientific mapping of the stars (astronomy) and the study of their influence on human affairs</term>, since he said that eyes were given to humans primarily for the sake of this art. —[had complained], even regarding those things that were easier to access and understand; thinking almost all natural things—from the lowest to the highest—to be inscrutable. And concerning those matters which they seemed to have investigated with some certainty, they were nevertheless judged by their successors—who were taught otherwise by experience and more skillful observation—to have strayed far from the target of truth and the nature of things, as if they were looking at objects only through a **lattice** <term>lattice: original "transenna," a screen or window-grating that obscures a full view; used here metaphorically to mean seeing things only partially or unclearly</term>. 

If an **Art**—by the merciful gift of **GOD**, the author of all good—were to bring out into the open, revealed and visible, the inner secrets of nature that are wrapped in the thickest veils (and therefore hidden from most **Philosophers** <note>Philosophers: specifically natural philosophers, the precursors to modern scientists, who studied the physical world</note> who see less clearly), then not only would a great increase in perfection be added to the remaining arts—which are currently incomplete due to the hidden nature of things—but the knowledge of **GOD** would also be more refined. For if, as **Paul testifies** <note>original: "Paulo teste." This refers to the Apostle Paul's argument in the Epistle to the Romans (1:20) that the Creator's invisible attributes are clearly seen and understood through the things He has made in the physical world.</note>, **GOD** is granted to be known through the contemplation of natural things, then a consequent love and piety toward **GOD**, more ardent and fervent, would be stirred up in the hearts of the living.

->### Dedication.<-

*I will say nothing of the sweetest nourishment of the mind that arises from this art, nor of its uses and benefits poured out most abundantly upon the human race, for these are perceptible in themselves as being most delightful and most fruitful. But **Alchemy** <term>Alchemy: The art of chemical transformation and the investigation of the inner properties of matter</term> is an art of such a kind that it not only searches out and opens up both the **essences** and the **powers** of the most hidden things in nature, and brings them into the light and sight of men—removing those obscurities that have until now produced ignorance and folly in the **haters of chemistry** <note>original: "misochymicis," a polemical term used by alchemists to describe those who despise or reject the chemical arts</note>—but it also ennobles what is base. Furthermore, by bringing forth from hiding into the open air powers that are of the greatest value for **human health** <note>salutem: in this context, it refers to both physical healing and the general well-being of humanity</note>, it skillfully adorns them once again.*

*This art shines forth as the most eminent of all, second to none: because to no other is it given to unearth the inner secrets and the most deeply hidden parts of nature so elegantly, and to bring them forth so skillfully for human uses and various other purposes in many ways. If it is permitted to reason in agreement with **Paul** <note>Referring to the Apostle Paul’s argument in Romans 1:20, where he suggests that God's invisible attributes are understood through the things He has made.</note>, a more evident **knowledge of God** <note>original: "Dei notitia"</note> can be drawn from the practice of this art of **Alchemy** than even from the records of the **Sacred Scriptures** themselves. For if, from the mere rough contemplation of this most beautiful work <note>referring to the Universe or the "Great Work" of creation</note>, which promotes us more excellently toward the knowledge of God when its hidden essences...*
