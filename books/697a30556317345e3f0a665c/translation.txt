### Medical Opinion.

If we wish to properly understand this case, we must above all take into mature consideration all the circumstances that occurred before the mother's delivery, while she was pregnant with this child. Now, it is clear from the <term>medical history</term> <note>original Latin: "Historia morbi."</note> that in the 13th week of her pregnancy, she suffered a heavy loss of blood and the discharge of a coagulated piece of matter, which was taken to be a <term>molar pregnancy</term> <note>original Latin: "molam"; a mass of fleshy tissue in the uterus that mimics pregnancy.</note>. For this reason, it could not have been otherwise than that she brought a miserable, weak child into the world. This is because nourishment was withheld from the fetus both by the spongy growth in the womb—which, being unattached, was easily discharged—and by the heavy loss of blood. 

It is necessary to know here that the <term>menses</term> <note>Menstruation.</note> do not occur because a <term>plethora</term> <note>original Latin: "plethora"; an archaic term for an excess of blood in the body.</note> is being evacuated, but rather because the blood which should serve for the nutrition of the fetus is being led away. Therefore, Nature halts the menses during pregnancy and the uterus is firmly closed. If the menses appear during such a state, it is an <term>unnatural condition</term> <note>original Latin: "status præternaturalis."</note>. While this sometimes manifests in extremely "full-blooded" pregnant women up to the midpoint of their pregnancy without harming the fetus, a heavy loss of blood occurring alongside a <term>mola</term> is much more significant. This undoubtedly deprives the fetus of nourishment and proper growth, so that the mother here necessarily had to bring such a miserable, weak child into the world. 

When this child was barely a quarter-year old, he was already afflicted very often with <term>convulsions</term> <note>original Latin: "convulsionibus."</note>—more internal than external—for nearly three-quarters of a year. This condition always passed quickly, though it lasted for over half a year.
