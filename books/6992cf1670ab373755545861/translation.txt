### Technical Vocabulary

*   **Guangning Guard** (<term>廣寧衞</term>): The primary administrative and military hub for Liaodong defense.
*   **Panshan Post** (<term>盤山驛</term>): Part of the imperial relay system used for rapid communication and logistics.
*   **Kuling River** (<term>枯凌河</term>): A significant waterway used for transport and as a natural defensive barrier.
*   **Fort** (<term>堡</term>): A smaller garrison or fortified village, part of the tiered defense system under a "Guard."
*   **Altar** (<term>壇</term>): A ritual site used for state-mandated religious ceremonies.
*   **Monastery** (<term>院</term>): Specifically a Buddhist temple complex, often used as landmarks or temporary housing for officials.
*   **Guard** (<term>衞</term>): A large-scale Ming military unit consisting of approximately 5,600 soldiers.
*   **River Mouth** (<term>水口</term>): A strategic point where a river meets the sea or another body of water, often requiring heavy fortification.

### River Defense, Part 1

Master Mao <note>Mao Yuanyi, referring to himself in the third person as a scholar-author</note> says: The defense of our frontiers consists of three primary domains: the land borders, the seas, and the rivers. Both the borders and the seas are direct neighbors to our enemies, so the rivers might seem slightly less critical by comparison.

However, the River <note>referring specifically to the Yangtze River, the "Long Jiang"</note> presses against the sea and stretches across the central regions of our land. If the outer defenses collapse, the River becomes a gateway for invaders; if internal rebellion occurs, it becomes the very heart of the conflict. Therefore, the strategic importance of the River is equal to that of the land borders and the sea.

Lake Tai <note>A massive freshwater lake in the Yangtze Delta, famous for its labyrinthine waterways</note> is close to the River and has long served as a **thicket for brigands** <note>original: "藪盜" (soudao), meaning a place where outlaws congregate like fish in a marsh</note>. Therefore, I have already provided detailed maps of its terrain and collected various strategic discussions regarding it.

<summary>Mao Yuanyi argues that the Yangtze River is a critical strategic frontier equal in importance to the northern borders and the coast, serving as both a gateway for foreign invasion and a potential heart of internal rebellion.</summary>

<keywords>Yangtze River defense, Mao Yuanyi, Lake Tai, military geography, internal security, Wubei Zhi</keywords>
