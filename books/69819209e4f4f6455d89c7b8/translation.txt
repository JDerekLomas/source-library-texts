# Preface of Doctor Johann von Staupitz

<note>The following section begins with a large decorative woodcut initial 'D', featuring foliate patterns typical of early 16th-century German printing.</note>

**T**O the illustrious, high-born princess, Lady Kunigunde <note>Kunigunde of Austria (1465–1520) was the daughter of Holy Roman Emperor Frederick III and the sister of Emperor Maximilian I.</note>, born Archduchess of Austria, Countess Palatine of the Rhine, Duchess in Upper and Lower Bavaria, etc. To my most gracious lady, I, Brother Johannes von Staupitz of the Augustinian Order, wish a joyful and blessed New Year, according to my poor prayers and that which God works in me for good.

¶ I take to heart, high-born princess, that this miserable life is filled with such countless tribulations, upheavals, persecutions, and also misery and woe, in which neither the Pope, nor the Emperor, nor the priest, nor the common people, nor the noble, nor the lowly, nor the old, nor the young, nor the rich, nor the poor, nor the beautiful, nor the deformed, nor the wise, nor the foolish, nor the pious, nor the wicked is spared; in the end, no one is exempted, and everyone must be burdened with suffering. Furthermore, I see that God from heaven has shown a way—through words and deeds—to mitigate all suffering or to lay it aside entirely; indeed, He has sealed this path with His death. This way is His love, which is above all things, and we are obligated and bound to possess it, though unfortunately, there are few to be found who walk that path.

Therefore, I have set out and resolved to write about God's lovely love and to dedicate this work to Your Princely Grace <note>original: "E. F. G." (*Euer Fürstlichen Gnaden*), a formal honorific</note>—to whom I wish all things to serve for divine joy—with the most humble intentions. I humbly and diligently pray that Your Princely Grace will accept it with gracious favor and not hold my folly against me. For I have been sufficiently prompted by the high-born Prince, the late Duke Albert <note>Albert IV, Duke of Bavaria (1447–1508)</note>, Your Princely Grace’s husband and my most gracious lord of highly praiseworthy memory, to remain a faithful servant to all the lords and ladies of Bavaria until my end. With this, I ask to be commended to you. In the year 1518.
