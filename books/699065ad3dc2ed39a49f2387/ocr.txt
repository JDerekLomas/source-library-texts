An elaborate engraved bookplate, likely representing the "Munificentia Regia" (Royal Munificence) gift of Bishop Moore's library to the University of Cambridge by King George I in 1715. 

The central feature is an oval coat of arms (four lions surrounding a central book), topped with a crenellated tower and a laurel wreath. The shield is flanked by two classical figures: on the left, Apollo stands holding a lyre, his arm resting on a stack of books; on the right, Minerva sits in armor with a helmet, shield, and spear. 

The composition rests upon a stone pedestal. In the center of the pedestal is a circular medallion containing a profile portrait of King George I. A ribbon draped around the medallion carries the inscription "MVNIFICENTIA" on the left and "REGIA 1715" on the right. Around the portrait, the Latin legend reads: "GEORGIVS D.G. MAG. BR. FR. ET HIB. REX F.D." (George, by the Grace of God, King of Great Britain, France, and Ireland, Defender of the Faith).
