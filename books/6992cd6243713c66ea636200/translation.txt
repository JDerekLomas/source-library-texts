Weakness has its uses, and strength has its additions. By combining these four and applying them with appropriate timing, one masters the situation. When the beginning and end of a strategy are not yet visible, no one can discern its intent. 

Like the movements of Heaven, Earth, and the Divine Spirits, the wise commander shifts and moves alongside things. Regarding those who act as shepherds of the people: those known as "scholars" <note>original: "士" (shi); referring to the educated elite or warrior-gentlemen</note> are heroes. Therefore it is said: if you ensnare these heroes in your net, then the enemy state will be impoverished. 

Heroes are the mainstays <note>literally the "trunk" (gan)</note> of the state; the common people are the foundation <note>literally the "root" (ben)</note> of the state. If you secure the mainstays and gather the foundation, then your governance will be implemented without resentment. 

The essence of employing the military lies in exalting courtesy <term>li: the traditional codes of conduct, respect, and ritual</term> and providing substantial rewards. When courtesy is exalted, wise scholars will arrive; when rewards are substantial, righteous scholars will treat death lightly. 

Therefore, if you reward the worthy without begrudging the wealth, and reward merit without letting the moment pass <note>original: "賞功不踰時"; meaning the reward should follow the deed immediately for maximum effect</note>, then those below will unite their strength, and the enemy state will be weakened. 

The way to employ men is to honor them with noble rank and provide for them with wealth; then the scholars will come of their own accord. Receive them with courtesy and inspire them with righteousness; then the scholars will die for you. 

A commander <note>original: "將帥" (jiangshuai)</note> must share the same tastes <note>literally "the same flavors"</note> and share the same dangers as his soldiers. Only then can the enemy be overcome. Thus, the army achieves total victory, and the enemy’s defeat is absolute.

The source of a general’s authority lies in his commands. 
The means by which a battle is won completely is military administration <term>junzheng: the systems of discipline, logistics, and internal governance of an army</term>.
