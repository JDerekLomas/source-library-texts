# The Science of Body Marks

**Om! Salutations to the venerable Ganesha, the remover of obstacles and the granter of success.**

**Om! Having bowed to the Primordial God, the All-Knowing and All-Seeing, I shall now describe the auspicious Science of Body Marks <note>original: "सामुद्रिकं" (Sāmudrikam). A tradition of physiognomy and palmistry where the body is read like a map of the soul.</note> for both men and women. 1**

**One should first examine the person as a whole, and only afterwards the specific marks. Of what use are physical signs for men or women who lack vitality? <note>original: "अर्क" (Arka). Literally "the sun," here referring to the essential radiance or life-force of a person.</note> 2**

**One should identify these marks on the left side for women and on the right side for men; this is the traditional instruction. 3**

**In this science, it is well said that there are five features that should be long, six that should be subtle, six that should be elevated, those that should be even and reddish, three that should be wide, and three that should be deep. 4**

**The arms, the ears, the knees, the nose, and the space between the breasts—these five being long is considered a praise-worthy sign. 5**

**For men, a short neck and short calves are also respected. The four features of a king are described as follows: 6 Those who possess subtle <note>original: "सूक्ष्म" (Sūkṣma). Meaning fine, delicate, or refined.</note> finger joints, hair, bones, and teeth, and whose speech is also subtle—those men are destined for a long life. 7**

**The armpits, the belly, the chest, the nose, the shoulders, and the forehead—one should point these out in all beings...**

### The Science of Hand Lines

One should designate as a king a man who possesses lines on his hand resembling a spear, a javelin, a sword, a bow, a discus, or a mace <note>original: "शक्ति तोमर देशासि धनुश्चक्र गदोपमाः" (śakti tomara deśāsi dhanuścakra gadopamāḥ) — these are the traditional weapons of the Hindu gods and symbolize sovereignty and power.</note>. 

With two barley-chains <note>original: "यवमाला" (yavamālā) — a specific chain-like pattern of small eye-shaped marks (yavas) on the lines of the palm.</note>, a man becomes a royal minister, wealthy and wise. With a single barley-line, he is distinguished and worthy of great riches. If there is a barley mark at the wrist, specifically three of them, he shall become a king. 

If these marks also appear on the back of the hand, the results vary according to the prominence of the flesh. Lines that are deep, auspicious, and firm indicate fortune, while those that are yellowish <note>original: "पिंगला" (piṅgalā)</note> or rough signify a lack of prosperity. Marks shaped like sprouts that appear in uneven places, or lines that are discolored, split, or dark blue, are considered inferior. A line that is broken into small sprouts signifies various troubles and doubts without a doubt. In times of hardship, a man named by these signs will struggle with unevenness in his life.

### On the Length of Fingers

If the line reaching the middle finger <note>original: "मध्यमां" (madhyamāṃ)</note> is consistently surpassed by the index finger <note>original: "प्रदेशिनी" (pradeśinī)</note>, he will be a lover of excellent jewels and will not find himself in calamity. If the little finger <note>original: "कनिष्ठा" (kaniṣṭhā)</note> is consistently longer than the line of the ring finger <note>original: "अनामिका" (anāmikā)</note>, it brings an increase of wealth for men. 

An upward line starting from the wrist <note>original: "मणिबंध" (maṇibandha)</note> that travels toward the fingers can be of five types. Such a line bestows happiness and the acquisition of a kingdom upon that man.
