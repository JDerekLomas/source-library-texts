# Summary

The accounts regarding the rise and fall of **Wang Zhi** <note>A notorious Chinese pirate lord who led the "Japanese" pirate (Wokou) raids</note> are largely consistent with the official biographies and records found in the *History of Ming*. Consequently, it can be said that **Hu Zongxian's** efforts in securing the defense of the southeastern coast were not entirely without merit.

Within the three volumes of the "Strategic Study" included in this work, every aspect of military administration is addressed with meticulous detail. These include:
*   Joint naval patrols and regional reinforcements
*   The pacification and recruitment of rebels
*   City defense and the organization of local **baojia** <term>baojia <note>A system of collective responsibility and neighborhood watch used for civil defense and tax purposes</note></term>
*   The issuing of imperial proclamations and the deployment of espionage
*   Tribute routes and border trade

Furthermore, it provides exhaustive technical documentation on every type of naval vessel, handheld weapon, military hardware, and firearm.

The book also preserves the various tactical proposals and memoranda submitted by notable figures of the era, such as **Tang Shunzhi**, **Zhang Shiche**, **Yu Dayou**, **Mao Kun**, and **Qi Jiguang** <note>Qi Jiguang (1528–1588) was a legendary military general and national hero famous for his innovative tactics against the pirates</note>. As a record of Ming Dynasty coastal defense, this work is exceptionally comprehensive and well-prepared.

It may be concluded that while **Hu Zongxian** himself was not a man of pure character, his talent was undoubtedly that of a preeminent hero of his age.

Respectfully reviewed and submitted in the fourth month of the 46th year of the **Qianlong** reign <note>May 1781</note>.
