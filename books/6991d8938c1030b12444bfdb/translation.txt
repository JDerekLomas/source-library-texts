-> Now, following the <term>Tantras</term>, the first sprout for the attainment of supreme bliss, <-
<note>Tantras are a category of esoteric scriptures focused on ritual, meditation, and yoga.</note>
-> which arises from the sequence of the six chakras and so forth, is described <gloss>ku</gloss>. <-
<note>The gloss "ku" likely refers to a specific manuscript variant or a commentator like Kalicharana.</note>

He who is familiar with the glory of the six lotuses <note>The "lotuses" are the chakras or energy centers.</note> through the knowledge of <term>Mahayoga</term>—he alone is capable of revealing the inner essence, and no other. May the foremost of the wise, the eldest, through the mercy of the Lord of Compassion, clearly manifest the supreme glory of the truth within the six lotuses. Now, the most compassionate <term>Purnananda Swami</term>, desiring to uplift the world submerged in the mire <gloss>ka</gloss> of suffering, and wishing to speak of <term>Kundalini Yoga</term> within the six chakras for the sake of attaining the knowledge of reality that grants liberation, makes this vow to secure the attention of the practitioners: "Now" <note>original: "Atha." In Sanskrit literature, starting a text with "Atha" is considered auspicious and indicates the beginning of a formal subject.</note>. 

"Now" signifies "thereafter"—meaning after the description of various rituals and duties previously explained in his own work, the <term>Shritattva-Chintamani</term>, and prior to this investigation of the six chakras. The grammatical connection is: "The first sprout <gloss>ku</gloss> for the attainment of supreme bliss is spoken of." "Supreme Bliss" is <term>Brahman</term> <note>The Ultimate Reality or Universal Consciousness in Hindu philosophy</note>, according to the scripture: "Brahman is eternal, supreme bliss" <note>original: "nityaṃ parāmānandaṃ brahma"</note>. Its "attainment" refers to the perfection consisting of the direct realization <note>insight or vision</note> of that state. For that, the "first sprout" is the nature of the first manifested practice which acts as the cause; this is spoken of by me, Purnananda. 

What is the nature of this "sprout"? It arises from the sequence of the six chakras and so forth, according to the Tantras. "According to the Tantras" means according to the Tantric system. The "six chakras and so forth" refers to those named <term>Muladhara</term>, <term>Svadhisthana</term>, <term>Manipura</term>, <term>Anahata</term>, <term>Vishuddha</term>, and <term>Ajna</term>. The word "and so forth" includes their associated channels <note>nadis</note>, symbols <note>lingas</note>, the five voids <note>the element of ether or space</note>, Shiva, Shakti, and others. The "sequence" refers to the order of meditation, the awakening of <term>Kundalini</term> <note>the latent spiritual energy at the base of the spine</note>, and its journey to the Lotus of Brahma...

> In the space outside the <term>Meru</term><note>The Meru or Merudanda refers to the spinal column, envisioned as the central axis of the human body, analogous to Mount Meru, the axis of the universe.</note>, on the left and right, are situated the two channels of the Moon and the Sun. In the middle is the <term>Sushumna</term> channel, comprised of the three <term>Gunas</term><note>The three fundamental qualities of nature: Sattva (purity), Rajas (activity), and Tamas (inertia).</note> and embodying the nature of the Moon, Sun, and Fire. Like a string of blooming Datura flowers, her body extends from the middle of the neck to the head. Inside her is the one called <term>Vajra</term>, which extends from the genital region to the head, shining brightly within the middle. ॥ 1 ॥

The "sequence" refers to the orderly procedure for the union of <term>Shiva and Shakti</term><note>The masculine and feminine principles of the divine, whose union in the crown chakra is the goal of Tantric yoga.</note> and similar practices. Through this sequence, it is "attained" or "reached"—meaning it is inseparable from the practice of yoga. Consequently, the "sprout," which is the nature of the spiritual action leading to the direct realization of <term>Brahman</term><note>The Ultimate Reality or Supreme Consciousness.</note> and inseparable from the yoga of the six chakras prescribed in the <term>Tantras</term>, is being described by me. This is the collective intended meaning.

Now, the yoga that is about to be described is not possible without <term>knowledge of the six chakras and the channels</term> (nadis); therefore, first, the necessary channels are investigated through three verses, beginning with "Of the Meru." "Of the Meru" refers to the <term>Merudanda</term> (spinal column). We will clarify later that this "staff" is the backbone that extends from the base (the <term>Muladhara</term>) to the neck. 

"In its outer space, the Moon and Sun channels": Shashi means the Moon, Mihira means the Sun, and Shira means the channel (nadi). Thus, the two channels embodying the Moon and Sun, known as <term>Ida</term> and <term>Pingala</term>, are situated on the left and right sides respectively.

This is stated in the *Bhuta-shuddhi Tantra*: "On the left of the Meru is Ida, and on the right is Pingala; both are situated there." 

Regarding their identity as Moon and Sun, the *Sammohana Tantra* says: "The channel known as Ida, which flows on the left, is white and of the nature of the Moon. She is the Goddess in the form of <term>Shakti</term>, the very embodiment of nectar. On the right is Pingala, a male-natured channel of the Sun's form. She is the Great Goddess of fierce nature, shining with the radiance of pomegranate blossoms." 

These two channels reach from the root in a straight line up to the <term>Ajna Chakra</term><note>The "third eye" center located between the eyebrows.</note> and then enter the nostrils. 

As stated in the *Yamala*: "Ida and Pingala are on the left and right. These two channels are straight, distinguished as left and right. Passing by all the lotuses <note>The "lotuses" refer to the chakras or energy centers aligned along the spine.</note>, the auspicious ones reach the nostrils." In the phrase "these two channels" <note>original: "shire te ca"</note>, the word "and" is used for emphasis.

# Investigation into the Six Chakras

By that [the <term>Sushumna</term><note>The central spiritual channel of the subtle body.</note>], encircling all the lotuses <note>The chakras are metaphorically described as lotuses (padmani) located along the central axis.</note>, through the distinction of left and right. This means that by the distinction of left and right, in the manner of a "braided binding" <note>original: "veni-bandha-kramena"</note>, they encircle all the lotuses and reach the nostrils. By this, a two-fold method of their positioning is shown. Elsewhere, their position is described as being "bow-shaped," as follows: "Know those two supreme channels, <term>Ida</term> and <term>Pingala</term>, to be shaped like a bow." 

He describes the manner of this bow-shape: That which is connected to the left testicle <note>original: "mushka"</note>, clinging to the <term>Sushumna</term>, relying on the right <term>jatru</term>, stays in the heart with a bow-like curve. Passing between the left shoulder and the <term>jatru</term>, it goes to the right nostril. <term>Jatru</term> is the junction of the shoulder and the arms <note>commonly understood as the collarbone area</note>. Similarly, that which rises from the right testicle goes to the left nostril. 

Rising from the left and right testicles, they reach the space between the eyebrows. There, the three channels—<term>Ida</term>, <term>Pingala</term>, and <term>Sushumna</term>—attain the state of a knot in the form of a "Triple Braided Union" <note>original: "triveni-rupa"</note> and meet at the nostrils. Thus it is said:

> In the <term>Ida</term> is the goddess Yamuna; in the <term>Pingala</term>, the Sarasvati.
> In the <term>Sushumna</term> dwells the Ganga. Their union is threefold.
> Joined at the base of the spine <note>literally "base of the banner" or "penis," referring here to the Muladhara region</note>, they are released at the separation of the eyebrows.
> This is called the Union of the Triple Braid <note>original: "Triveni-yoga"</note>; bathing there yields great fruit.

"In the middle" means in the middle-hollow of the <term>Merudanda</term> <note>the spinal column</note>. Thus it is said: "That which is situated in the middle of the <term>Meru</term>, going from the root to the <term>Brahmarandhra</term> <note>the 'Opening of Brahman' at the crown of the head</note>," and so on. Similarly, in the <term>Tripurasara-samuccaya</term> <note>A Tantric compendium: "The Collection of the Essence of the Three Cities."</note>: "That which goes into the internal cavity of the staff of the skull-base," and so on. 

Here, although a passage in the <term>Tantra-chudamani</term> <note>The "Crest-Jewel of the Tantras."</note> states: "The channel <term>Ida</term>, the nectar of the moon, is on the left of the <term>Meru</term>; on the right is the one named <term>Pingala</term>, associated with the sun. Outside of those two, in the middle, is the <term>Sushumna</term>, associated with fire," which suggests the <term>Sushumna</term> is outside the <term>Meru</term>, this statement is due to a difference in tradition. In the view of this author, however, the <term>Sushumna</term> is within the middle-hollow of the <term>Meru</term>, because the later verse stating that the six lotuses are "situated within the middle space of the <term>Meru</term>" implies that the <term>Sushumna</term>, which supports them, must also be within that hollow.

"Comprised of the three qualities" <note>original: "tritaya-gunamayi"</note>: This means she is of the nature of a triple-thread, consisting of three forms—<term>Sushumna</term>, <term>Vajra</term>, and <term>Chitrini</term>. Or, "triple-quality" refers to the three channels beginning with <term>Sushumna</term>; where there is a triple-turning, regarding them as "this is one, this is one, this is one."

## Investigation into the Six Chakras

"She consists of the three-fold qualities" <note>original: "tritayaguna tanmayi"</note> means she is composed of them. Or rather, she possesses the three qualities of <term>Sattva</term> (purity), <term>Rajas</term> (activity), and <term>Tamas</term> (inertia). Consequently, the <term>Chitrini</term> channel is composed of the Sattva quality, the <term>Vajra</term> channel is composed of the Rajas quality, and the <term>Sushumna</term> is composed of the Tamas quality. 

Regarding the phrase "having the forms of the Moon, Sun, and Fire": the <term>Chitrini</term> is of the nature of the Moon and is therefore white in color. The <term>Vajrini</term> is of the nature of the Sun and therefore has the luster of the filaments of a pomegranate flower. The <term>Sushumna</term> is of the nature of Fire and is therefore red in color. 

The proof of this three-fold nature is stated in the *Purification of the Elements* <note>original: "Bhuta-shuddhi," a foundational Tantric ritual text</note> after describing the characteristics of the Sushumna: 
"Within it, two finger-breadths <gloss>finger-breadths</gloss> above, are the <term>Chitrini</term> and the one called <term>Vajra</term>. Thus the Sushumna, containing the three qualities, dangles like a beautiful woman. She is the support of the three qualities—Sattva and the others—and embodies the nature of the Moon, Sun, and Fire."

Regarding the phrase "from the middle of the root to the head": the <term>Kanda</term> <note>literally "bulb" or "root"</note> is the origin of all the <term>Nadis</term> (subtle channels). Its characteristics are described as follows: 
"Two finger-breadths <gloss>finger-breadths</gloss> above the anus and two finger-breadths <gloss>finger-breadths</gloss> below the genitals, the <term>Kanda-mula</term> (root-bulb) is four finger-breadths <gloss>finger-breadths</gloss> wide, shaped like a bird's egg. From it, seventy-two thousand channels arise." 

Starting from that root-bulb, it "stays in the head," meaning it extends to the bottom of the twelve-petaled lotus located within the pericarp of the downward-facing thousand-petaled lotus <note>the Sahasrara chakra</note> in the head. 

Based on the sentiment of the *Hymn to the Guru’s Sandals*: "I worship the twelve-syllabled <note>the text likely intends "twelve-petaled"</note> lotus adorned by the hollow stalk of the <term>Kundalini</term>," the <term>Chitrini</term> channel, which takes the form of the <term>Kundalini's</term> hollow stalk, ends there. Because it is contained within the <term>Sushumna</term>, the <term>Sushumna</term> also ends at that point. Otherwise, if it were to extend above the thousand-petaled lotus, it would contradict the statement in the fortieth verse that "it flourishes in the Empty Space" <note>original: "shunya-desha"</note>. If the space were enclosed by the <term>Sushumna</term>, it could not be called an "Empty Space."

On this point, some cite a verse from the *Essence of the Revealed Truth* <note>original: "Nigama-tattva-sara"</note>: "Three channels are proclaimed to go through the middle of the <term>Meru</term> (the spine)," to argue that all three channels—<term>Ida</term>, <term>Pingala</term>, and <term>Sushumna</term>—are located inside the spine. 

This is incorrect. The fact that the <term>Ida</term> and <term>Pingala</term> are located *outside* the spine is established by the words of all the Tantras. Even the author of this work explicitly states they are outside by using the phrase "in the outer region of the <term>Meru</term>."

<summary>This page describes the internal structure of the central channel (Sushumna) as a nested system of three layers (Chitrini, Vajra, and Sushumna) corresponding to the three Gunas and celestial lights. It also defines the "Kanda" (root-bulb) as the origin of the 72,000 channels and clarifies that while the central channel is within the spine, the Ida and Pingala channels remain outside it.</summary>

Furthermore, if the **Ida** and **Pingala** <term>Ida and Pingala: the left and right lunar/solar channels</term> were located inside the **Meru** <term>Meru: the spinal column, envisioned as the central axis of the body</term>, it would be impossible for them to connect from the heart to the collarbone due to their bow-like curvature. According to the words of the *Essence of the Truth of the Tantric Scriptures* <note>original: "Nigamasara-tattva-vacane"</note>, there are three specific channels: the **Sushumna**, the **Vajra**, and the **Chitrini**. The **Ida** and **Pingala** are not included within that internal group. Here, the text describes the manner in which the **Sushumna** extends from the root to the head:

"The **Sushumna** is like a *Chavya* creeper <note>a type of climbing pepper plant</note> clinging to the **Meru** and moving forward. O Fair-Faced One! Upon reaching the end of the neck, it emerges, hanging down and turning sideways. Supported by the hollow stalk of the **Shankhini** <gloss>Shankhisvani</gloss> channel, it reaches the House of Brahman <note>original: "Brahmasadana," the spiritual center at the crown of the head</note>."

Similarly, in another place: "When the others are in the shape of a bow, the **Sushumna** takes the form of the *Pranava* <note>the sacred syllable 'Om'</note>. Separating from the backbone, it turns sideways and goes to the forehead, merging into the **Kundalini** <term>Kundalini: the primordial energy coiled at the base of the spine</term> between the eyebrows, and entering the Opening of Brahman <note>original: "Brahmarandhra"</note> through its mouth."

"Emerging" <note>original: "galita"</note> means it has come out from the spinal column. The phrase "separating from the backbone" has the same meaning. From this, it is understood that the spinal column exists only up to the neck. 

Regarding the phrase: "Supported by the hollow stalk of the **Shankhini** <gloss>Shankhisvani</gloss>": 
The characteristics of the **Shankhini** <gloss>Shankhisvani</gloss> are described as follows:

The Lord said:
"The **Saraswati** and **Kuhu** channels are located on the sides of the **Sushumna**. The **Gandhari** and **Hastijihva** are on the front and sides of the **Ida**. Between the **Gandhari** and the **Saraswati** is the one called the **Shankhini** <gloss>Shankhisvani</gloss>."

Additionally: "That channel named **Shankhini** <gloss>Shankhisvani</gloss> is said to extend to the end of the left ear." And elsewhere: "The **Shankhini** <gloss>Shankhisvani</gloss> turns sideways from the throat-cavity and goes to the forehead. O Goddess! It is joined and clung to the **Chitrini** <gloss>Chisvtrini</gloss> as far as the head."

Consequently, the **Shankhini**, starting from the root-bulb <note>original: "kanda-mula," the energy center below the navel</note> between the **Saraswati** and **Gandhari**, goes up to the throat in a zigzag motion; with one end it meets the opening of the left ear, and with the other end it reaches the top of the head.

On this subject, some say: "The spinal column, which is like a bone-pin extending from the feet to the Opening of Brahman, is said to be the axis of the fourteen worlds." By showing this quote from the *Essence of the Truth of the Tantras* as evidence, they claim the spinal column exists from the feet all the way to the Opening of Brahman. This point is questionable <note>original: "cintyam," indicating the author disagrees or finds the logic flawed</note>. That which is the backbone is indeed the only spinal column.

Here, the mention of the letter *Na* is not intended for meditating on the letters in reverse order; rather, *Na* is chosen specifically to exclude the letter *Ksha*.

The reason for this exclusion <note>original: "tyāga-bījam"</note> lies in how the letters are positioned on the thousand petals through repetition. If one uses the fifty-one letters of the alphabet (including *Ksha*) and repeats them twenty times, the total count reaches one thousand and twenty. However, if they are repeated only nineteen times, the count is only nine hundred and sixty-nine. Therefore, by omitting the letter *Ksha* and using only fifty letters, the math is reconciled: the fifty letters from *A* to *La*, repeated exactly twenty times in their natural order <note>original: "anulomena"</note>, fit perfectly upon the thousand petals.

This natural order of meditating on the letters is explicitly taught in the **Kaṅkālamālinī Tantra**:

> "The **Sahasrāra** is the great white lotus, facing downwards, adorned with the vibrating letters from *A* to *Ksha*."

In this context, because the word "ending with" <note>original: "anta"</note> can signify a conclusion or boundary, this technical compound <term>Bahuvrīhi: A complex Sanskrit compound where the words together refer to an external object—in this case, the set of letters</term> suggests that *Ksha* is the limit, thereby implying the use of the fifty letters ending with *La*. 

Alternatively, some suggest that the fifty letters (ending in *La* and *Ksha*) are arranged in a counter-clockwise direction <note>original: "vāmāvartena"</note>, similar to the mystical *A-Ka-Tha* triangle. However, since the statement from the **Kaṅkālamālinī** prescribes a clockwise arrangement <note>original: "dakṣiṇāvartena"</note>, there is no actual contradiction between these two scriptural authorities.

Regarding the appearance of these letters: since their specific forms are not described here, one should meditate on them as being white, following the tradition that the **Mātṛkā** <term>Mātṛkā: The "Little Mothers," referring to the divine powers embodied in the letters of the alphabet</term> is composed of white light.
