->With the help of Jesus!<-
<note>original: "J. J. J." (likely *Jesu Juvante Juamen*). It was common for authors of this period to begin a work with a pious invocation seeking divine assistance.</note>
->Benevolent reader!<-

Here I lay before your eyes several experiments on gold, intended both for your amusement and, depending on the person, for practical use. It concerns the **destruction of gold** <note>original: "destructionem auri." In alchemy, "destruction" does not mean wasting the metal, but rather breaking its metallic form to release its inner power or "tincture."</note> so long and so eagerly sought after by the world today.

If one asks me what I think of it, I can neither condemn nor praise it. It is certainly true that it is impossible to bring a substance <note>The author uses "Corpus" (body) to refer to the physical, metallic state of gold.</note> to the desired utility without destruction (which must come from the firing). For my part, I hold some belief in it, and I believe what the ancients taught us through their Phoenix—which, when it is burned to ashes, is **revived** <note>original: "reuiuiciret"</note> again, and from whose ashes many thousands of young ones emerge. In the same way, it is my opinion that if we can burn the beautiful Phoenix (that is, the gold), we will finally attain the desired goal of the **Philosopher’s Stone** <note>original: "Lapidis Philosophici"</note> which we so eagerly seek. Now, in my view, it matters not how the Phoenix is reduced to ashes, whether it happens in whatever way it may—**either by the wet way or the dry way** <note>original: "vel via humida vel sicca." The "wet way" refers to liquid chemistry/acids, while the "dry way" refers to high-heat furnace work.</note>, cold or warm—I would still hope to find what is sought. It is sufficient if I have the right <term>radical solution</term> <note>A "radical" solution (from *radix*, meaning root) is a process that dissolves a metal back to its primary essential matter, rather than just breaking it into smaller pieces.</note> of gold, for I then possess the true **Mercury of the Philosophers**. <note>original: "Mercurium Philosophorum." This is not common quicksilver, but a theoretical "prime matter" essential for the Great Work of alchemy.</note>

Now, however, I will remain silent regarding what I have seen during the days of my life (for I have been raised in **chemical labors** <note>original: "laboribus Chymicis"</note> from my youth, and have undertaken strange dealings therein without seeking fame), and how much <term>sophistry</term> <note>In the 18th century, "sophistry" in chemistry referred to fraudulent or deceptive methods used by "gold-cookers" to mimic alchemical results.</note> has been committed in the "destruction of gold." One person had a **solvent** <note>original: "Menstruum"</note>, another a powder; this one had this, the other that. Yet, when viewed in the light, it was nothing more than the kind of solution that could have been achieved with **Royal Water** <note>original: "Aqua regis." A powerful mixture of nitric and hydrochloric acids capable of dissolving gold.</note>. I generally tend to call this **division** <note>original: "diuisio"</note>, for it is nothing other than breaking the gold into **the smallest possible particles** <note>original: "minutissimas particulas"</note>. Much more is required for the task than one might imagine. For my part, I consider the many solvents used to destroy gold to be in vain, and I point you, Reader, back to the Phoenix. You will find that the Phoenix, according to the poems of the ancient heathens (who were, without doubt, philosophers), after collecting a quantity of all sorts of spices, sits upon them, is ignited by the sun, and thus burns itself. # Preface.

...at certain places, I believed that the **Philosophers** <note>original: "Philosophi." In this context, the author refers to alchemists and natural philosophers of the past.</note> had told us enough here: that we should abstain from all foreign things. They impressed this upon us well enough in an old *rhyme* with these words:

> Seek it not in herbs, animals, or fats,
> Nor in metals or in salts.

In what then? In itself; borrow it from it or its like, and seek it in that which is closest to it. It is in vain to worry about all kinds of **solvents** <note>original: "Menstrua." A technical alchemical term for liquids used to dissolve or "open" a substance.</note>; I believe it is invisible, already hidden everywhere, such that we simply cannot see it. We therefore seek it where it is not to be found, which is why we gain the [only] benefit as we work.

However, I do not wish to have shown or given instruction to anyone that he should seek it in gold—no—but rather that we should strive for how we can burn the Phoenix from itself, of which more shall be said in the future.

But, so that it does not run on too far, I will also mention which *destruction of gold* <note>original: "destructionem auri"</note> I think nothing of. Specifically, it is that of which it is said that gold can never again be reduced into a **metallic body** <note>original: "Corpus." This refers to the solid, recognizable state of the metal.</note>. As happened recently, an Italian was staying in Nuremberg; he also undertook (as was written to me) to destroy gold so that it could not be brought back into gold, as mentioned. A distinguished, sensible man gave him the answer: "What use is it then?" Another said: "It is best to work on gold when one keeps the ducats in one's purse." He held this great **secret** <note>original: "arcanum"</note> for the price of 1,000 ducats, and when no one would bite, he would have gladly given it for a pair of kettledrums. I therefore think nothing of it when one destroys it so that it cannot be brought back into a **metallic body** again. I also do not believe it to be possible; let someone give me such a destroyed **body**, and see if I do not finally bring it back into gold. The reader may live assured that I have done no small amount in this matter as long as I have lived.

Contained herein is the red **precipitation of gold** <note>original: "Praecipitatio auri." A process where gold is pulled out of a solution as a solid powder.</note>. When I first saw it, I thought I had "caught the hare by the ear" <note>A German idiom meaning to have finally grasped the secret or achieved success.</note>, yet after a proper **examination** <note>original: "examine"</note>, I found that I barely had it by the tip of its tail.

But without a more extensive preface, I will now briefly state the reason why I have prepared this present little work.
