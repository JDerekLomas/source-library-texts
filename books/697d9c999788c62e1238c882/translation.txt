# PREFACE.

\* 2

The great decay <note>original: "verval"</note> that one sees nowadays in Christendom must make us think seriously about its restoration. God will not always let things go in the manner in which they go almost everywhere today, or according to the flourish <note>In this context, "fleur" refers to the outward success or worldly fashion of the established churches.</note> that the spirit of this world has caused them to take, and still maintains. But He will eventually bring back once more the face or the form of the first Christianity; and the further one is now removed from it, so much closer may the time of renewal be. The **antichristendom** <note>For the Labadists, "Antichristendom" did not just refer to a single figure, but to the entire corrupt institutional church and worldly society that they believed had replaced true spiritual worship.</note> shall not always remain standing; and when shall it be nearest to its fall? Shall it not be then, when it shall fear it the least, and when it shall have climbed and been exalted as if to its fullness and highest step? And how clearly does one see that this is now fulfilled?

How much is the pure service of God, which consists in *spirit and in truth*, not degenerated, and how much is it not denied? In the **Papacy** <note>original: "Pausdom." A common 17th-century Protestant term for the Roman Catholic Church.</note> such happens through manifold superstitious and idolatrous services that have been introduced there. And it happens also, whether within or without the Papacy, because everywhere one finds so few true worshippers who offer to God that service of the heart and spirit which He demands, and in which He alone has His divine pleasure.

Where does one see that living truth reigning that inspired the first Christians, and that made them Children of the highest Truth? How much is the same darkened by the errors of some, and by the irregularities of othe— <note>The text breaks off here at the end of the page; it likely continues as "others" (d’anderen) on the following page.</note>
