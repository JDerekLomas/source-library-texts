# Introduction

The spiritual duties and <term>litanies (awrad)<note>singular: wird; specific rhythmic prayers or devotions performed at set times</note></term> are cut off by death; therefore, a person should seize the opportunity of their lifespan before time runs out. It has been mentioned in the traditions: "No hour passes a servant of God in which they do not remember Him, except that it becomes a source of regret for them on the Day of Resurrection." Imam Ahmad recorded in his collection of traditions on the authority of Abu Hurayrah (may God be pleased with him) that the Messenger of God ﷺ said: "No people sit in a gathering and then depart without mentioning God, except that they depart from something like the carcass of a donkey, and that gathering will be a source of regret for them on the Day of Resurrection" [Tradition No. 10837]. Imam Hasan al-Basri (may God the Exalted have mercy on him) said: "I have known people who were more protective of their hours than you are of your gold and silver coins."

Furthermore, these litanies vary according to the diverse aspirations and readiness of people, falling into three general categories based on capacity and energy, confirming the words of the Exalted: *“God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity”* [The Cow: Verse 286].

**First:** The litany of the **Strivers** in <term>spiritual exercises<note>original: riyadat; disciplines intended to train the soul</note></term> among the worshippers and ascetics. This consists of immersing their time in various types of obedience; their worship ranges between remembrance, supplication, prayer, fasting, recitation, and the study of knowledge.

**Second:** The litany of the **Wayfarers** toward God the Exalted. This consists of withdrawing from distractions, abandoning worldly attachments and obstacles, purifying hearts from evils and defects, and adorning them with virtues after emptying them of vices.

**Third:** The litany of the **Arrived** <note>those who have attained spiritual union or direct experiential knowledge of God</note>. As Sheikh Ahmad ibn Ajiba says in his book *Awakening Aspirations in Explaining the Aphorisms*: it consists of casting off personal whims and acting out of love for the Master. Their worship is a matter of contemplation or a spiritual gaze while remaining devoted in the Divine Presence. Whomever the Master has established in a specific litany should adhere to it, not overstep their bounds, and not look down upon others. # Introduction

By the mercy of God, you shall share your dwellings according to your deeds. [1/ 198].

No one belittles the spiritual gift <note>original: "al-ward," referring to a regular spiritual practice or litany</note> and seeks only the Giver <note>original: "al-warid," referring to the divine inspiration or the One who sends it</note> except for one who is a slave to the inspiration itself. As for the one who is a true servant of God, he does not turn his attention to anything else; rather, he adheres to the duties of servitude required of him, standing in recognition of the majesty of Lordship. For that is what endures, and through it, one reaches the pleasure of the Living, the Self-Sustaining.

Therefore, my Muslim brother, you must seize your times of leisure and health by occupying them with what benefits you in the Hereafter, confirming the saying of the Prophet (peace be upon him):
> "There are two blessings in which many people are cheated: health and free time." [Narrated by al-Bukhari and others].

# Biography of the Book's Compiler

## Sheikh Ahmad Ziyauddin al-Kumushkhanawi

He is the Sheikh, the Knower of God Almighty, Ziyauddin Ahmad bin Mustafa bin Abdul Rahman al-Kumushkhanawi al-Hanafi <note>A follower of the Hanafi school of Islamic jurisprudence</note>, a scholar of Prophetic traditions, a preacher, a Sufi, and a master of the Naqshbandi Order. He was born in Gümüşhane in the province of Trabzon, Turkey, in the year 1227 AH, corresponding to 1812 AD, and later traveled to Istanbul.

He resided in Egypt for three years, where he owned a printing press that published books of the Noble Sunnah <note>The body of traditional social and legal customs and practices of the Islamic community</note>, distributing them to impoverished scholars free of charge. He also established three libraries for public reading in his home country.

He returned to Istanbul and remained there teaching traditions, preaching, and writing until his death on the 7th of Dhu al-Qi'dah in the year 1311 AH, corresponding to 1812 AD <note>original: "1812 م" — This appears in the original text, but it is likely a typographical error; the year 1311 AH actually corresponds to 1894 AD</note>. He produced nearly fifty works, including:

*The Compendium of Texts on Expressions of Disbelief and the Correction of Creed and Deeds*; *The Compendium of Principles regarding the Saints, Their Categories, and the Foundations of Every Sufi Path*; *The Spirit of the Gnostics and the Guidance of Seekers* regarding Sufism; *The Medicine of Muslims* regarding preaching; and *The Harbor of Prophetic Traditions* <term>Ramuz al-Ahadith: literally 'The Harbor' or 'The Code' of Traditions</term>, arranged alphabetically. He authored this last work in 1858, and it has been printed many times in a single volume. This book contains 7,101 concise Prophetic traditions and consists of two sections: a section containing the sayings of the Prophet (peace be upon him), totaling 6,400 traditions, and another section containing traditions regarding the blessed characteristics and physical descriptions of the Prophet, totaling 701 traditions.

Regarding the characteristics of the book, Imam al-Kumushkhanawi says in his preface: "To proceed: the poor and weak servant Ahmad Ziyauddin says: I desired to compile books of traditions for my beloved friends, following their request for brevity and selection. I sought guidance from God Almighty and commenced the gathering of traditions from authoritative books, omitting the chains of transmission <note>The 'isnad' or list of narrators, removed here to make the text more accessible to general readers</note>, while ensuring the rulings and meanings remain clear, and encompassing the fundamental rules..."

# Biography of the Compiler of the Book, Sheikh Ahmed Ziyauddin Gümüşhanevi

...great principles of the religion, so that they might be easily memorized, their benefit made universal, and their blessings encompass all. By the grace of God Almighty, it has become a beautiful collection. At the end of each tradition <note>Hadith: a record of the words, actions, or silent approval of the Prophet Muhammad</note>, I have mentioned its source among the leading masters of Hadith and its narrators among the Companions—ranging from one to nine individuals. I have also indicated its classification, whether it be authentic, fair, strong, or weak, and I have arranged it alphabetically according to the first word of the tradition.

As for the commentary on the *Ramuz al-Ahadith* <note>The Code of Traditions</note>, it is titled *Lawami‘ al-‘Uqul* <note>The Radiance of Intellects</note> in five massive volumes. Imam Gümüşhanevi began its composition in the year 1864, and the printing of the book was realized between the years 1875 and 1877.

Regarding the statements of scholars concerning the *Ramuz al-Ahadith*, our venerable leader, the great polymath Muhammad Zahid al-Kawthari <note>A famous 20th-century Ottoman scholar and theologian</note> (may God Almighty be pleased with him), said:

He is one devoted to the science of Prophetic traditions and adorned with the jewels of the true monotheistic faith. I found that he gathered the secrets of the Sacred Law <term>Sharia: the religious law forming part of the Islamic tradition</term> and plucked the flowers from its garden orchards. May God thank the efforts of its author, reward him, and make Paradise his final resting place and abode."

With careful attention, he included the sources from the leading Imams and major narrators, indicating the various categories of Hadith <note>Prophetic traditions</note>—including the "Fair" <term>Hasan: a tradition of reliable but not perfect transmission</term>, the "Authentic" <term>Sahih: the highest grade of reliability</term>, the "Weak" <term>Da'if: a tradition with a flaw in its chain of transmission</term>, and the "Strong" <term>Qawi</term>—following the established path and methodology of the Hadith scholars.
