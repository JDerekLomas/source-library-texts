In Pythagorean numerology, the number six is often called the "number of marriage" or "conjugal number" because it is the product of the first male number (3) and the first female number (2).</note>.

These joined ternaries <note>original: "ternairs." Groups of three.</note> produce the metaphysical unity which is the septenary <note>original: "septenaire." The number seven.</note> or Sabaoth <term>original: "Sabaoth." Referring here to the Sabbath or the mystical "rest" of the seventh day, representing the completion of the alchemical work.</term>. In this [state], finally with the assistance of God, this work must rest once again. Each of these six parts or portions is the binary <note>original: "binair." The number two, or duality.</note>, and these duplicities are different and contrary to one another.

From this you have here the dodecahedron <note>original: "dodecædre." A three-dimensional geometric shape with twelve faces.</note>, which makes twelve; namely, six against six. From the division or difference of these, peace and union result. Through this union, infinity and the trinity are revealed. The Kabbalists <note>original: "Cabalistes." Students of the Kabbalah, a school of Jewish mysticism that influenced Western esoteric and alchemical thought.</note> say that within the seven parts, two ternaries meet, and there is one [part] in the middle. 

Twelve are at war: three are friends, and three are enemies; three give life, and three give death. God is the faithful King, who from the altar of His holiness rules over all: one over three, and three over seven, and seven over twelve. All of these are armed against one another, after which unity results.
