### OF THE CIRCLE AND THE SQUARE

The area of a circle is equal to a triangle whose base is the circumference and whose height is the radius. <insert>This was proven by Archimedes.</insert> Therefore, if you can straighten the curve of the circle, you can find its true square.

**Rule for the Measurement of Curvatures:**
Every curved line can be imagined as a portion of an infinite number of circles, some larger and some smaller.

# On the force found in triangular sails

To draw with a single rope equally that force which is found in a triangle of a sail which is struck equally by the wind.

---

If you wish to know, for any triangular sail, where the center of its force is located when it is struck by the wind in such a way that it forms a **perfect bulge** <note>original: "pancia" (belly/bulge); Leonardo uses this to describe the aerodynamic curve of a sail filled with wind</note> as if it were a portion of a sphere, do as follows: and keep in mind that this perfect bulge can never be formed except in a triangle that has curved sides and not straight ones, as you see above. Let **A** be a perfect triangle which is pulled from its three angles with equal force by three ropes; these ropes are extended in such a way that they join together at a single point of a master rope, which is the one that pulls those ropes—or rather, is pulled by those ropes—and you shall do the same for every triangular sail.

---

If you want to find, for every part of a triangular sail, where the center of the force is that the wind exerts upon it, the method is this: let three lines be drawn from the three angles to the middle of the opposite sides, and the point of their <unclear>common</unclear> intersection <note>Leonardo uses the term "intersegamento" for the geometric centroid</note> shall be the sought-after center. But if the sail were of varying density, or of varying resistance from other things, or if the sides were of varying lengths, then the center would not stay in such a place, but where it shall be shown by the weight or by the rope <unclear>...</unclear>

The center of the bulge of the sail will not be in such a place, but will be perpendicularly below the common point of such traction.
