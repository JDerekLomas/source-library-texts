# Furisode

<image-desc>A small decorative woodcut ornament featuring stylized leaves and floral motifs.</image-desc>

Recently, while passing through a small street occupied mainly by dealers of second-hand goods, I noticed a *furisode* <term>A formal, long-sleeved kimono traditionally worn by young, unmarried women.</term>, or long-sleeved robe, of the rich purple tint called *murasaki* <note>original: *murasaki*; the Japanese word for purple or violet.</note>, hanging in front of one of the shops. It was the kind of robe that might have been worn by a lady of rank during the time of the Tokugawa <note>The Tokugawa shogunate (1603–1867) was the final era of traditional Japanese military government.</note>. I stopped to look at the five family crests upon it; in that same moment, I remembered this legend of a similar robe that was said to have once caused the destruction of Yedo <note>The historic name for Tokyo.</note>.

Nearly two hundred and fifty years ago, the daughter of a wealthy merchant in the city of the Shōguns <term>The hereditary military dictators of Japan.</term>, while attending a temple festival, noticed a young samurai of remarkable beauty in the crowd and immediately fell in love with him. Unfortunately for her, he disappeared into the press of people before she could learn through her attendants who he was or where he had come from.
