## Curative Methods

Facial inflammations are those that break out on the face at the <term>Sibai: "Four Whites," an acupuncture point located in the depression below the eye</term> and <term>Juliao: "Great Crevice," an acupuncture point located level with the bottom of the nostril</term> locations. There are also those that occur on the bridge of the nose; although these pertain to the Lung <note>In Traditional Chinese Medicine, the nose is considered the "opening" of the Lung organ system</note>, they are also caused by "Wind-Heat" <note>An external pathogen associated with rapid onset, redness, and swelling</note>. 

According to the classics, the *Sibai* and *Juliao* points are located below the tear ducts, on both sides of the nose. Although these two points fall within the domain of the <term>Foot Yangming: the Stomach Meridian, which is rich in both Qi and Blood and runs through the face</term>, this meridian is extremely prone to the "stirring of Fire" <note>Fire represents intense, localized inflammation</note>. If there is no <term>Yin: the cooling, moistening, and nourishing fluids of the body</term> to balance and aid it, then once this Fire erupts, there are many times when it cannot be contained or stopped, frequently leading to unpredictable and dangerous changes. 

Therefore, the development of carbuncles at these two points is greatly to be feared. If, at the onset, the color resembles a radish and the shape gradually enlarges, or if "mother-and-child" sores <note>A larger primary ulcer surrounded by smaller satellite sores</note> develop, the patient may perish within eight or nine days. It is evident that these sores must be treated with great urgency. Supplementing the Yin to balance the <term>Yang: the active, heating energy of the body</term>, while using "internal support" <note>original: "Nei Tuo" (內托); a treatment strategy using tonics to help the body push toxins outward</note> combined with agents to neutralize the poison—this is truly the superior method of treatment.
