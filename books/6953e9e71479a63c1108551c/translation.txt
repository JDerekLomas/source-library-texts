# ADVERTISEMENT
### TO THE
## THIRD EDITION.

Having attached my name to the present edition of Euler’s Algebra, it may be proper to give some account of the translation. I shall do this with the greater pleasure because it provides a favorable opportunity to associate my own labors with those of my distinguished pupil and most excellent friend, the late Francis Horner, Member of Parliament.

When he was first placed under my instruction at the critical and interesting age of seventeen, he soon discovered uncommon powers of intellect and the most ardent thirst for knowledge. These were united with a docility of temper and a sweetness of disposition which rendered teaching him, indeed, a "delightful task." His diligence and attention were such that they required the frequent introduction of some rational amusement in order to prevent the intensity of his application from injuring his health; though his constitution was not delicate, it had never been robust.

Such speculations often require the admission of principles that are either unintelligible or incapable of proof. Therefore, I recommended to his notice *Euler’s Algebra* <note>Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) was a pioneering Swiss mathematician; his "Elements of Algebra" is considered one of the best introductory math texts ever written.</note> as providing an admirable exercise for his reasoning powers. I believed it was the best means of cultivating that talent for analysis, close investigation, and logical inference which he possessed at an early age, and which he afterwards displayed to such an eminent degree.

At the same time, I was of the opinion that translating a part of that excellent work from French into English—whenever he wished to vary his studies—would improve his knowledge of both languages and serve as the best introduction for him to the field of mathematics.

He was soon delighted with this occasional employment, which seemed to supply his mind with food that was both solid and nutritious. He generally produced, two or three times a week, as much as I could find time to revise and correct. In the course of the first twelvemonth <note>original: "twelvemonth"; a year.</note>, he had translated so large a portion of the two volumes that we determined to complete the whole work and publish it for the benefit of English students. However, he returned to Scotland before the manuscript was ready for the press; therefore, the labor of editing it necessarily devolved on me.
