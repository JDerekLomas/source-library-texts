This page consists of a decorative **marbled paper**, a traditional craft used in historical bookbinding. The pattern displayed is known as a **combed** or **nonpareil** style.

<note>Marbled paper was created by floating liquid pigments on a bath of thickened water (called "size"). A craftsman would use a stylus or a "comb" to drag the colors into intricate, repeating patterns before carefully laying a sheet of paper on the surface to "print" the design. This technique was widely used between the 17th and 19th centuries.</note>

The design on this specific sheet features repeating wavy bands of red, yellow, blue, and green pigments. These endpapers were not only used for their beauty but also served a practical purpose: they reinforced the hinge of the book and helped to conceal the construction and glue of the binding's interior.
