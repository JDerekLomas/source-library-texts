## REGARDING THE DEDICATION.

From the beginning of November 1907 to the end of March 1908, the writer devoted his time to investigating the teachings of Dr. Steiner, who was absent from Berlin nearly all that time. In the last of about six personal interviews with Dr. Steiner, the writer mentioned that he had commenced a book along occult <note>original: "occult lines"; referring to hidden or esoteric spiritual knowledge</note> lines; a compendium of the teachings of the East and West.

Dr. Steiner then urged that if any of the teachings promulgated by him were used, he ought to be mentioned as the authority and source of information. Consequently, the writer agreed to dedicate the work to Dr. Steiner.

During January, February, and March 1908, the Elder Brother—whom the writer now knows and reveres as Teacher—came at times, clothed in his vital body <note>In Rosicrucian terminology, the "vital body" or etheric body is the first invisible vehicle of the human spirit.</note> and enlightened the writer on various points. In April and May, after unwittingly passing a test, the writer was invited to journey to the estate on which the Temple of the Rosy Cross is located.

There he met the Elder Brother in his dense body <note>original: "dense body"; the physical, material body.</note>; there he was given the far-reaching, synthetic philosophy embodied in the present work—*which, in the opinion of many long-term students in England, on the Continent, and in America, embodies everything that has been taught publicly or esoterically <note>original: "esoterically"; intended for or likely to be understood by only a small number of people with specialized knowledge.</note> in the past, besides much more that has never before been printed.*

Therefore, the unfinished manuscript for the book mentioned to Dr. Steiner was destroyed. However, as the later and more complete teaching given by the Elder Brother corroborated the teachings of Dr. Steiner along main lines, it was thought better to dedicate the book to Dr. Steiner than to seem like a plagiarist. Of that there would have been small danger, however, for the plagiarist invariably gives less than the authority from whom he steals; it will be found that in any case where previous works are compared with the present one, this book will in all cases provide more information.

The dedication has therefore been a mistake; it has led many people who merely glanced at the book to infer that it embodies the teachings of Dr. Steiner and that he is responsible for the statements made herein. Such an inference is obviously unfair to Dr. Steiner, and a careful reading of pages 8 and 9 will show that it was never intended to convey such an idea. The writer does not see how to convey the true idea in a dedicatory sentence, and has therefore decided to withdraw the dedication, with an apology to Dr. Steiner for any annoyance he may be caused by the hasty conclusions concerning his responsibility for the *Rosicrucian Cosmo-Conception*.

# A WORD TO THE WISE.

The founder of the Christian Religion stated an occult <note>original: "occult" — here meaning hidden, esoteric, or relating to the inner mysteries of spiritual laws</note> maxim when He said: “Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child shall not enter therein” (Mark 10:15). All occultists recognize the far-reaching importance of this teaching of Christ, and endeavor to “live” it day by day.

When a new philosophy is presented to the world, it is met in different ways by different people.

One person will grasp with greediness any new philosophical effort in an endeavor to ascertain how far it *supports his own ideas*. To such a person, the philosophy itself is of minor importance. Its prime value will be its vindication of **HIS** ideas. If the work comes up to expectation in that respect, he will enthusiastically adopt it and cling to it with a most unreasoning partisanship; if not, he will probably lay the book down in disgust and disappointment, feeling as if the author had done him an injury.

Another adopts an attitude of skepticism as soon as he discovers that it contains something which **HE** has not previously read, heard, or originated in his own thought. He would probably resent as extremely unjustified the accusation that his mental attitude is the height <note>original: "acme"</note> of self-satisfaction and intolerance; such is nevertheless the case; and thus he shuts his mind to any truth which may possibly be hidden in that which he off-hand rejects.

Buddha, great, grand and sublime, may be the "Light of Asia," but Christ will yet be acknowledged the "Light of the World." As the sun outshines the brightest star in the heavens, dispels every vestige of darkness and gives life and light to all beings, so, in a not too distant future, will the true religion of Christ supersede and obliterate all other religions, to the eternal benefit of mankind.

In our civilization the chasm that stretches between mind and heart yawns deep and wide and, as the mind flies on from discovery to discovery in the realms of science, the gulf becomes ever deeper and wider and the heart is left further and further behind. At every birth, what appears to be a new life comes among us. We see the little form as it lives and grows, becoming a factor in our lives for days, months, or years. At last, there comes a day when the form dies and goes to decay. The life that came—from where, we do not know—has passed to the invisible beyond, and in sorrow we ask ourselves: Where did it come from? Why was it here? And where has it gone?

Across every threshold, the skeleton form of Death throws his fearsome shadow. Old or young, healthy or ill, rich or poor—all, all alike must pass into that shadow. Throughout the ages, the piteous cry has sounded for a solution to the riddle of life and the riddle of death.

So far as the vast majority of people are concerned, the three great questions—*Where have we come from? Why are we here? Where are we going?*—remain unanswered to this day. It has unfortunately become the popularly accepted opinion that nothing can be definitely known about these matters of deepest interest to humanity. Nothing could be more erroneous than such an idea. 

Each and every person, without exception, may become capable of obtaining first-hand, definite information upon this subject. Anyone may personally investigate the state of the human spirit, both before birth and after death. There is no favoritism, nor are special gifts required. Each of us has inherently the faculty <note>original: "faculty"; here meaning an inherent mental or spiritual power</note> for knowing all of these matters. It requires persistent effort to awaken them, and that seems to be a powerful deterrent. If these faculties, "awake and aware," could be purchased for a price—even if the price were high—many people would pay it to gain such an immense advantage over their fellow men; but few indeed...

There is also a superconscious memory. This is the storehouse of all faculties acquired and knowledge gained in previous lives, though they may remain latent <note>hidden or undeveloped</note> in the present incarnation. This record is indelibly engraved on the life spirit. It ordinarily manifests—though not to its full extent—as conscience and character, which gives life to all thought-forms. Sometimes it acts as a counselor, and sometimes it compels action with a resistless force, even when that action goes against reason and desire.

In many women, in whom the vital body is positive, and in advanced people of either sex where the vital body has been sensitized by a pure and holy life, by prayer, and by concentration, this superconscious memory inherent in the life spirit is occasionally, to some extent, beyond the necessity of clothing itself in "mind-stuff" and "desire matter" in order to compel action. It does not always need to risk being subjected to, and perhaps overruled by, a process of reasoning. Sometimes, in the form of intuition or teaching from within, it impresses itself directly upon the reflecting ether <note>the part of the vital body responsible for memory and thought transmission</note> of the vital body. The more readily we learn to recognize it and follow its dictates, the more often it will speak, to our eternal welfare.

The tissues are rebuilt and the rhythm of the body restored. The more thoroughly this work is done, the greater the benefit resulting from sleep.

It is this activity of the different vehicles <note>original: "vehicles"; refers to the various subtle bodies, such as the vital and desire bodies, that the spirit inhabits.</note> during sleep which forms the basis for the activity of the following day. If the work of removing that fatigue were not done, the bodies would remain asleep, as sometimes happens in a natural trance. It is precisely because of this harmonizing, recuperative activity that sleep is better than a doctor or medicine in preserving health. Mere rest is nothing in comparison with sleep. It is only while the higher vehicles are in the Desire World <note>The "Desire World" is the realm of feelings, emotions, and desires where the spirit travels during sleep.</note> that there is a total suspension of waste and an influx of restoring force. It is true that during rest the vital body is not hampered in its work by tissue being broken down by active motion and tense muscles, but it must still contend with the wasting energy of thought; furthermore, it does not then receive the outside recuperative force from the desire body as it does during sleep.

It happens, however, that at times the desire body does not fully withdraw, so that part of it remains connected with the vital body—the vehicle for sense-perception and memory. The result is that restoration is only partly accomplished, and the scenes and actions of the Desire World are brought into the physical consciousness as dreams. Of course, most dreams are confused because the axis of perception is askew due to the improper relation of one body to another. The memory is also confused by this incongruous relation of the vehicles; as a result of the interrupted restoring force, dream-filled sleep is restless and the body feels tired upon awakening.
