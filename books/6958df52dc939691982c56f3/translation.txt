The knowledge of plants, most illustrious Giovanni Mocenigo <note>original: "Ioannes Maurocene"; the Mocenigo were one of the most prominent ducal families of Venice</note>, was first discovered by the great Chiron the Centaur, son of Saturn, or by Apollo, or finally by Aesculapius <note>The Greek god of medicine</note> (whom blind mortality once worshipped as Gods), as Pythagoras, renowned for his wisdom, and others have recorded. Afterwards, having perceived the admirable powers of these plants for the health of bodies among all people, many not only of the wisest Philosophers—among whom was the man of famous name, Democritus, who traveled through many regions out of love for this science—but even the greatest Kings (whose names, signifying this, are preserved in certain plants to this day <note>A reference to plants like Gentiana, named after King Gentius, or Lysimachia, after King Lysimachus</note>) have labored intensely in their study. It is, therefore, no reason for anyone to find it strange if I myself, concerning the plants which I formerly inspected and observed in the regions of Egypt, have now brought to light a book written by me

...about to publish, I wished to dedicate this to you, a man illustrious for the nobility of your lineage and the brilliance of your singular virtues; especially since I know that you take the greatest delight in the study of all disciplines. For I have hoped that you would receive no small amount of pleasure from this new history of plants <note>original: "stirpium historia"</note>. There is also the fact that this Book of Plants <note>original: "Plantarum liber"</note>—which I had recently joined together with other books called *On the Medicine of the Egyptians* <note>original: "de medicina Ægyptiorum"</note> and dedicated to your most illustrious father—must now be published separately for the convenience of students. Therefore, it ought to be offered to you as the closest representative of your father, to whom, among others, it had first been destined. I have done this all the more gladly so that I might make my deep respect clearly seen toward you and your remaining brothers, to whom I have always been most devoted. Receive, therefore, most distinguished sir, this small gift from me with a cheerful countenance (for if I had anything greater or more excellent now, of which I know you are worthy, I would have offered it to you even more gladly); and I ask you again and again that you defend it from the tongues of detractors. Farewell, may you be long happy and mindful of me. At Venice, in the year of human salvation 1592.

### TO THE EXCELLENT READER.

<image-desc>Large decorative woodcut drop-cap 'P' featuring intricate scrollwork and floral motifs.</image-desc>

AFTER the books on the medicine of the Egyptians <note>original: "medicina Aegyptiorum"</note>, behold, excellent reader, we present to you this small volume, which is *On the Plants of Egypt* <note>original: "De plantis Aegypti"</note>. We had originally joined this work with those previous books, but now, looking out for your convenience, we have seen to it that it be printed separately. Receive, therefore, with a cheerful spirit, this small gift of mine. In this work, we seem at least to have achieved this much: that on account of the variety of subjects, the history of foreign plants <note>original: "stirpium historiam"</note>, and the likenesses <note>original: "effigies"</note> of the same—which are, in my opinion, excellently drawn—we believe we have written something not entirely unworthy of more refined ears. Farewell.
