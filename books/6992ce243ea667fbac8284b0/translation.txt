### The Role of the Spirit Flag and Percussion
In this formation, the <term>Spirit Flag</term> (original: *Lingqi* 靈旗) serves as a focal point for the armored units. These banners were not merely decorative; they were believed to summon divine protection and ward off malevolent influences during the procession. The presence of <term>Crossbowmen in Blue Armor</term> (original: *Qingjia Nu* 青甲弩) surrounding these flags suggests a specialized guard unit. The "Blue" (or cyan) color of the armor often corresponded to specific elemental or directional assignments within the five-element system (*Wuxing*) used in Ming military theory.

# **Banners of Colored Waterfowl and Fowl Feathers**

**Banners of Colored Waterfowl Feathers** and **Banners of Fowl Feathers** <note>original: 色鶩毛氅, 戈雞毛氅 (Sè wù máo chǎng, Gē jī máo chǎng). During the Jianlong era of the Song Dynasty [960–963 AD], for the **Suburban Sacrifices** <note>original: 郊祀 (jiāosì); the most solemn imperial rites performed at the outskirts of the capital to honor Heaven and Earth</note>, and in the established rituals of the Kaibao era [968–976 AD], all followed this design. * The **green banner** was embroidered with peacocks and used a five-pointed canopy. 
* The **crimson banner** was embroidered with phoenixes and used a six-pointed canopy.
