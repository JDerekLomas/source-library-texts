It is said to be among the seven great nations and serves as a powerful rival to **England** <note>original: *Ying-ji-li*</note>.

The nation's administrative affairs began in the year 1789 <gloss>the 54th year of the Qianlong era</gloss>. It was then decided to establish the **United States** <note>original: *Yu-nai-shi-die*</note>, with **Washington** <note>original: *Wa-shen-dun*</note> in the District of **Columbia** <note>original: *Ge-jiao-mi-a*</note> as the capital. Because there is no King, they established one **President** <term>Bo-lie-xi-ling: a phonetic transliteration of "President"</term>. 

The President oversees the entire nation’s military, criminal justice, taxes, and the promotion or demotion of officials. However, regarding major matters of state or the military, particularly those involving peace or war with foreign nations, the President must first consult with **Congress** <term>Xi-ye: likely a transliteration of "Senate" or a related term for the legislative body</term> before taking action. If their views differ, then a **majority rule** <note>literally "three divinations, follow two," an idiom for majority rule</note> is followed. 

For the promotion or transfer of high-ranking civil and military officials, or the changing of laws, the President must seek a consensus through consultation. By established rule, the **President** serves a four-year term. When the term expires, he is replaced. If his administration has been harmonious and the entire nation is pleased and supportive, he may remain for one additional term. However, there is absolutely no such thing as hereditary succession or lifelong tenure. 

As for the rules of **public election** <note>original: *gongju*</note>: first, the people of the various **states** <term>buluo: literally "tribes," used here by the author to describe the individual states of the Union</term> publicly elect individuals called **Electors** <note>original: *Yi-li-duo*</note>, who are then formally confirmed by the government offices of each state.

Later, when they <note>Referring to the Spanish.</note> further acquired the great nations of **Mexico** <note>original: *Moxige* (墨西哥).</note> and **Peru** <note>original: *Bilu* (祕魯).</note>, which yielded millions in gold and silver every year, they came to regard these islands as nothing more than worn-out sandals <note>A Chinese idiom (敝屣, bìxǐ) meaning something of no value that is easily cast aside.</note> and did not deign to manage them.

For a long time afterward, the islands were occupied by pirates, who appeared and disappeared to plunder, causing great distress to merchants and travelers. Soon, various other nations of **Europe** followed one after another to the west, competing to occupy the islands, so that they each came under different jurisdictions. Besides **Spain**, these included **Britain**, **France**, the **Netherlands**, **Denmark** <note>original: *Lianguo* (槤國). Xu Jiyu uses this historical transcription for the Danish kingdom.</note>, and **Sweden**. The climate of these islands is extremely warm, quite similar to that of the various islands of the **South Seas** <note>Referring to Southeast Asia, particularly the Malay Archipelago.</note> in **Asia**.
