### ON THE MOTIONS OF THE HUMAN BODY
->The proportions of the limbs and the coordination of the muscles<-

The human body in its movements must be considered as a machine of marvelous complexity. <note>Leonardo frequently compared the body to "natural' mechanics," viewing bones as levers and muscles as cords.</note> When the arm is raised, observe how the pectoral muscle <term>pectoralis major</term> contracts and thickens, while the muscles of the back are extended to allow for the rotation of the shoulder.

<margin>Note the tension in the neck during the lifting of a heavy weight.</margin>

#### OF THE WEIGHT OF MAN IN MOTION
When a man walks, his center of gravity shifts with every step. <insert>This is also true of the horse and other four-legged beasts.</insert> If the weight is not balanced over the supporting leg, the body must fall. Therefore, nature has provided that the opposite arm shall swing forward to maintain the equilibrium of the torso.

<column-break/>

| PART OF THE BODY | ACTION DURING FLIGHT | OBSERVATION |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The Head | Fixed upon the object | To maintain direction |
| The Torso | Twisted toward the lead leg | To generate force |
| The Feet | Pushing from the ball | <unclear>illegible</unclear> |

As I have stated in my earlier writings, "The soul cannot be understood without the functions of the body." <note>original: "L'anima non si può comprendere senza le funzioni del corpo."</note> For if the soul is that which moves the spirit, it must use the nerves and muscles as its instruments. <gloss>The nerves are the messengers of the will.</gloss>

->**The geometry of the stride**<-

Consider the angle of the knee when the foot first strikes the ground. It is not a straight line, but a curve that absorbs the shock of the earth. <note>Leonardo was one of the first to describe the shock-absorbing function of the joints.</note>

# ON THE MOTIONS OF THE HUMAN BODY

The man who carries a weight placed outside of his <term>central line</term> <note>The vertical axis of the body's gravity.</note> must throw as much natural weight of his own to the opposite side as is necessary to create an equilibrium around the central line.

### On the weight of a man standing upon one foot

When a man stands upon one foot, the foot that supports him is placed directly under the <term>pit of the throat</term> <note>The suprasternal notch; Leonardo used this as a primary landmark for the body's vertical balance.</note>, or under the middle of the neck.

<margin>The center of the weight of a man who stands still is always positioned above the center of the foot that supports him.</margin>

### On the movement of the arms

If you wish to represent a man who is throwing a stone with great force, you must show him at the moment he is twisting his body toward the side opposite to where he intends to throw. At that moment, the weight of his body is supported by the foot that is not on the side of the throwing arm.

<column-break/>

### On the balance of the body in motion

Observe how the center of gravity shifts when a man climbs a ladder. If he does not lean his chest forward, the weight of his back will pull him backward, and he will fall. Therefore, the weight of the part that leans forward must always be equal to the weight of the part that remains behind the point of support.

<insert>Nature has so arranged it that the weight of the limbs is distributed to assist the desired motion.</insert>

<gloss>The center of gravity is the soul of balance.</gloss>

->**The Mechanics of the Step**<-
