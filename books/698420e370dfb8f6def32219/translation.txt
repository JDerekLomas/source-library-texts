# A Guiding Rule.

## By which the kind-hearted Protestant reader should form his judgment.

**W**hen one diligently considers all the histories that have occurred involving the Protestants <note>original: *Evangelischen*</note> in Germany, France, England, Scotland, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and other neighboring places—even as they are described by the Catholics <note>original: *Papisten*</note> themselves—it is obvious and undeniably true: that the Catholics have never once kept faith with the Protestants in religious or political matters (insofar as these might affect them even through the consequences <note>original: *consequentz*</note>) for any longer, any more, or in any other way than they were compelled and forced by necessity and opposing power. 

Conversely, the Protestants have given the Catholics no greater cause, incentive, or advantage toward their own division, conquest, and subjugation than through the hope they have always harbored: that through Imperial, Royal, or other lords' decrees <note>original: *Decreta*</note>, permissions, and promises, they might in time secure the free practice of the Protestant religion. 

Finally, however, the practice of religion (I do not say the religion itself, which stems from the spirit of revelation) was successfully won through the sword once such hope had been entirely lost or cast aside. Whoever does not believe this rejects the primary foundation of worldly wisdom <term>political prudence or "Weltliche Klugheit," referring to the practical understanding of how power operates</term>, either out of a lack of understanding,
