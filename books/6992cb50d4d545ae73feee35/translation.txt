# The Great Bodhisattva <term>Guanyin</term>
### Master Sheng Yen <note>original: 釋聖嚴 (Shì Shèngyán); a renowned 20th-century Buddhist monk and scholar (1930–2009) known for founded the Dharma Drum Mountain organization and bringing Zen practice to a global audience.</note>

**Guanyin Bodhisattva** <note>original: 觀音菩薩 (Guānyīn Púsà); the Bodhisattva of Compassion, also known in Sanskrit as Avalokiteshvara.</note> has the deepest affinity with the common people of our nation. Consequently, among those who follow the Buddhist faith, there is none who does not have faith in Guanyin; some even go so far as to let their devotion to Guanyin represent the entirety of their Buddhist belief.

Indeed, the primary spirit of Buddhism is <term>Great Kindness and Great Compassion</term> <note>original: 大慈大悲 (dà cí dà bēi); "Kindness" refers to the wish to bring happiness to others, while "Compassion" is the desire to remove their suffering.</note>, and it just so happens that Guanyin Bodhisattva is the representative of this very spirit. Therefore, while some among the Chinese public may know that the founder of the Buddhist religion is **Shakyamuni Buddha** <note>original: 釋迦牟尼佛 (Shìjiāmóunífó); the historical Buddha who lived in India approximately 2,500 years ago.</note>, there is virtually no one who does not know of Guanyin Bodhisattva.

However, those who truly understand Guanyin Bodhisattva are few. Common devotion to Guanyin usually views her simply as a Bodhisattva who **responds to every plea** <note>original: 有求必應 (yǒu qiú bì yìng); a popular idiom suggesting that the deity will grant any sincere request or prayer.</note>. Because accounts of her miraculous spiritual responses are especially numerous, three Guanyin **Dharma Assemblies** <note>original: 法會 (fǎhuì); ritual gatherings for prayer, chanting, and teaching.</note> are held each year on the nineteenth day of the second, sixth, and ninth months of the <term>lunar calendar</term>... <note>The original Chinese text ends here mid-sentence. These dates traditionally mark Guanyin's birthday, her enlightenment, and the day she took her vows.</note>

Why does this Great Bodhisattva possess such a title?

According to the explanation in the <term>Shurangama Sutra</term> <note>original: 楞嚴經 (Léngyán Jīng); a classic Buddhist text emphasizing the realization of the enlightened nature of the mind through the "perfect penetration" of the senses.</note>, when this Bodhisattva first began their spiritual cultivation, their ears did not listen outward toward external sounds. They entered a realm of unshakeable, great sovereign freedom; for this reason, they are named **Guan Zi Zai**.

According to the *Universal Gate Chapter* of the <term>Lotus Sutra</term> <note>original: 法華經 (Fǎhuá Jīng); one of the most popular Buddhist scriptures, which teaches that all beings can become Buddhas.</note>:

# A General Introduction to the Great Compassion Mantra

1. The full name of this mantra is as stated in the title; "**Great Compassion Mantra**" is its abbreviated name. It is also known as the "**Dharani of the Unobstructed Great Compassion Heart of the Thousand-Handed and Thousand-Eyed One**," the "**Vast, Great, Complete, and Full Dharani**," the "**Unobstructed Great Compassion Dharani**," the "**Dharani that Relieves Suffering**," the "**Dharani for Prolonging Life**," the "**Dharani that Extinguishes Evil Realms**," the "**Dharani that Destroys Evil Karmic Obstacles**," the "**Dharani for Fulfilling Wishes**," the "**Dharani of Self-Mastery According to One's Will**," and the "**Dharani for Speedily Transcending the Ten Stages**" <note>the "Ten Stages" refer to the progressive levels of a Bodhisattva's development toward full Buddhahood</note>, among others.

2. This mantra consists of eighty-four lines in total. Aside from the line "**Sara Sara**" <note>original: "娑囉娑囉" - a Sanskrit phrase within the mantra</note>, which represents the **Five Turbidities of the Evil World** <note>the five types of degeneration—views, afflictions, sentient beings, lifespan, and the age itself—that characterize our current era</note>, the remaining eighty-three lines represent eighty-three <term>Bodhisattvas</term> <note>enlightened beings who choose to remain in the world to help others reach salvation</note>. Although many lines within the mantra are identical, the Bodhisattva represented by each line is different; for detailed illustrations, one should refer to the *Great Compassion Heart Dharani Sutra*.

### III. Mantras are "True Words," also called <term>Dharanis</term> <note>Sanskrit for "that which supports or holds"; a type of ritual speech or incantation</note>, or the "Methods of Total Retention." They are the crystallized essence of the mental methods through which all Buddhas and <term>Bodhisattvas</term> <note>enlightened beings who remain in the world to help others</note> cultivate their practice and attain the fruits of enlightenment. As there are countless Buddhas in the world, there are also countless gates of Dharani; conversely, a single gate of Dharani has been used by countless Buddhas to successfully achieve enlightenment. For those who study Buddhism, the value lies not in reciting many different types, but in the principle of "one-pointed focus without distraction" and diligent progress in a single method. One must understand that "all teachings are equal in their true meaning," and thus, one single method is equivalent to all methods. However, because mantras are in <term>Sanskrit</term> <note>the ancient liturgical language of India</note>, they are only occasionally translated by meaning. Those who recite the mantra do not necessarily need to know the literal meaning; this is because if the mind tries to interpret the meaning while reciting, it may instead give rise to a "discriminating mind" <note>the analytical mind that judges and categorizes, which is considered a barrier to deep meditation</note>, preventing one from reaching the state of "perfected maturity" and "one-pointed focus without distraction."

### IV. For example, the **Six-Syllable Great Bright Mantra**, which is "**Om Mani Padme Hum**," <note>literally "the jewel is in the lotus"</note> is the subtle "heart-seal" <note>a symbol of the Buddha's enlightened mind</note> of <term>Avalokiteshvara</term> <note>the Bodhisattva of Compassion, Guanyin</note>. Although it is a simple phrase, it can similarly achieve inconceivable merit and lead one to witness "Perfect Penetration" <note>a state of enlightenment where one is no longer hindered by the senses</note>. Other examples include the **Nine-Syllable True Word**, which is the end of the <term>Cundi Mantra</term> <note>a prayer to the "Mother of Buddhas"</note>: "**Om, cale cule cundhe svaha**." Seven million Buddhas have previously used this mantra to achieve the fruits of enlightenment. This **Great Compassion Mantra** was spoken by 9.9 billion Buddhas, as numerous as the "sands of the Ganges River," which further demonstrates its inconceivable and supreme power.

### V. The Great Compassion Mantra is an essential component found in almost all common liturgical ceremonies. Recently, because the number of lay Buddhists has grown, many often feel regret that they are unfamiliar with the proper way to read it. Therefore, we have specifically consulted various authoritative editions and added Chinese and English phonetic notations to both sides of the mantra text, with the aim of allowing practitioners to master it through self-study without the need for a teacher.

# The Method of Reciting over and Consuming Great Compassion Water

Any disciple of the <term>Triple Gem</term> <note>the Buddha, the Dharma (teachings), and the Sangha (community)</note> who has purified their mouth and practices with devotion may recite the Great Compassion Mantra to create "Great Compassion Water" to treat illnesses and to distribute to others to form <term>karmic connections</term> <note>spiritual bonds created through acts of kindness or sharing the Buddhist path</note>. When reciting, one must first burn incense and prostrate, then offer a cup of boiled water (the amount does not matter). One should then recite:

(1) The **Incense Praise**;
(2) The **Mantra for Purifying the Karma of the Mouth**;
(3) **"Homage to the Greatly Compassionate and Greatly Merciful, Savior from Suffering and Distress, Vastly Efficacious <term>Avalokiteshvara</term> Bodhisattva"** <note>the Bodhisattva of Compassion, known in Chinese as Guanyin</note>;
(4) The **Great Compassionate Heart <term>Dharani</term> of the One with a Thousand Hands and Thousand Eyes** (three times), followed by the Great Compassion Mantra forty-nine times;
(5) The **Mantra for Filling the Gaps** <note>recited to correct any accidental errors or omissions made during the ritual</note>;
(6) **"Homage to the Great Compassion Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva"** (ten times).

This Great Compassion Water relies on the Buddha's majestic power and can treat all manner of illnesses, whether large or small, chronic or mysterious. Those who consume this water must maintain a "pure mouth" <note>abstaining from meat, alcohol, and the five pungent spices like garlic and onions</note> and first recite "Homage to the Great Compassion Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva" ten times. (If a small child or a patient is unable to recite, any person related to them may recite it aloud on their behalf.) Then, consume the water, and results will be seen immediately. If the person consuming the water or their family members can make a vow to perform **Life Release** <note>the practice of saving animals from slaughter and setting them free</note> or observe a vegetarian fast for several days each month, the efficacy will be even greater. As for virtuous practitioners who cultivate in seclusion, when they recite over Great Compassion Water, the water can be limitless and the distribution of it also limitless; this is determined by the <term>merit</term> <note>spiritual "credit" earned through good deeds and practice</note> and intention of the one generating the aspiration.

## Brief Account of Miraculous Responses to the Great Compassion Mantra

During the Qing Dynasty, the wife of Peng Xizeng, a woman of the Yao family, suffered from a chronic abdominal mass. She made a vow to uphold the Great Compassion Mantra. One night, she dreamed of an old woman who gave her a single flower; she immediately felt her body become as light as a leaf, and her illness was soon cured. <gloss>(From the Collection of the One-Line Dwelling)</gloss>

Wan Wenyu of Jingshan related: "I uphold the Great Compassion Mantra and the Heart Sutra, and the miraculous occurrences are too numerous to recount in detail. I remember when my mother was critically ill and near death, I wept tears of blood and knelt before the <term>Great Being</term> <note>referring to the Bodhisattva Guanyin</note> to recite the Great Compassion Mantra once. My mother felt someone leading her back to the world of the living; her breath gradually warmed, she was able to speak and move, and within a few days, she was at peace." <gloss>(From the Collected Records of Sacred Scriptures)</gloss>
