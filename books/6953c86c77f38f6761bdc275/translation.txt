## OCCULTISM, by Robert Amadou (Julliard).

Mr. Robert Amadou has been set ablaze through his contact with Hermes Trismegistus, Pythagoras, Avicenna, Albertus Magnus, Paracelsus, Jacob Boehme, and Eliphas Levi... We already owe him an essay on Saint-Martin and Martinism <note>Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin (1743–1803) was a French philosopher known as "The Unknown Philosopher"; Martinism is the mystical tradition following his teachings.</note>, and he is currently preparing a *Literary Anthology of Occultism* with Mr. Robert Kanters. He is, therefore, a specialist—if one can call a "specialty" a science, an art, or to put it plainly: a philosophy, which (analogically) contains everything.

The author begins with this definition: Occultism is the collection of doctrines and practices founded on the theory that every object belongs to a single whole and possesses, with every other element of that whole, relationships that are necessary, intentional, non-temporal, and non-spatial.

Then we travel through, or rather fly over with him, the immense landscape that his patient clearing of the land has allowed him to synthesize: Doctrines, Practices, and Religions. The bibliographic notes are abundant and very well executed.

This is an excellent introductory work <note>original: "ouvrage initiatique"</note> that will whet the appetite of those minds tormented by a currently fashionable curiosity. <insert>P. L.</insert>
