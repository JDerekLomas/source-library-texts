# Preface
## By a Lover of Chemistry.
### Friendly Reader.

After the writings of the noble Englishman, the so-called Anonymous Philaletha <note>original: "Anonymi Philalethæ." This refers to Eirenaeus Philalethes, a famous 17th-century alchemist whose identity was a closely guarded secret; modern scholarship often identifies him as George Starkey.</note>—specifically his *Open Entrance to the King's Closed Palace* <note>original: "Eröffneter Eingang zu des Königs verschloßnen Pallast," referring to the famous Latin work *Introitus Apertus ad Occlusum Regis Palatium*.</note> along with his other works—were made public through the printing press, many people have emerged who wished to explain and interpret this author’s obscure language. In this labor, one has always been more unsuccessful than the other, according to how each person’s mind was disposed. Among them all, however, none have come to my attention who have correctly grasped the author’s true meaning. ...wishing was in vain for a long time, until I finally got this present treatise into my hands and read it. In this work, I have found that it not only agrees in all respects with the aforementioned Anonymous Author, but also fully explains that which was set down either imperfectly or unclearly in the *Open Entrance* <note>original: "Introitu aperto." This refers to Eirenaeus Philalethes' most famous work, *Introitus Apertus ad Occlusum Regis Palatium* (*Open Entrance to the King's Closed Palace*).</note>. This is especially true since the author, having received the art <term>the art: Alchemy</term> from the very mouth of Philaletha, was best able to understand his meaning. 

I immediately applied myself so that it might be translated from English into German, and Mr. Johann Lange of Hamburg <note>Johann Lange (1629–1715) was a well-known physician and translator who brought many English alchemical and medical works to the German public.</note> willingly took this task upon himself. However, because of his other duties, he could not devote much time to it; therefore, a well-meaning reader will not judge too harshly if perhaps a word here or there was not translated with perfect precision. Indeed, I can assure you that, as far as the sense and meaning of the author are concerned, nothing has been changed in the slightest, much less over-refined; but due to great haste, the English style of speaking could not always be perfectly adapted to the German, especially since the English original was written in verse.
