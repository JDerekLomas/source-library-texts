## RECENTLY CONDUCTED.

I have decided to add the calculations so that from them, anyone can understand that the progress of those same **planets** <note>Galileo calls the moons "planets" throughout the text, as the term "satellite" was not yet standard.</note>, both in longitude and also in latitude, agrees perfectly <note>original: "ad unguem," literally "to the fingernail"—a Latin idiom meaning with extreme precision</note> with the motions derived from the tables.

These are the observations of the four **Medicean Planets** <term>Medicean Planets; the name Galileo gave to the four largest moons of Jupiter to honor his patrons, the Medici family</term> recently and first discovered by me. Although it is not yet possible to gather their orbital periods in exact numbers from these data, it is at least permitted to declare certain things worthy of notice. And first, since they sometimes follow and sometimes precede **Jupiter** <term>Iouem</term> at similar intervals, and are separated from him by only the narrowest deviations toward the east and toward the west, and accompany him in both **retrograde** <term>retrogradum; the apparent backward motion of a planet in the sky</term> and **direct** motion, no one can doubt that they complete their revolutions around him. Meanwhile, they all complete a twelve-year period together <note>Jupiter takes approximately 12 Earth years to orbit the Sun.</note> around the center of the world. 

Furthermore, they revolve in unequal circles, which is clearly gathered from the fact that at greater distances from Jupiter, it was never possible to see two planets joined together; whereas near Jupiter, two, three, and sometimes all of them were found crowded together at once. It is also perceived that the revolutions of the planets describing narrower circles around Jupiter are faster; for the stars closer to Jupiter are more often seen in the east when on the previous day they appeared in the west, and vice versa. But the planet traversing the largest orbit seems to have half-monthly periods <note>Galileo is estimating the orbital period of Callisto, the outermost moon, which is actually about 16.7 days.</note> to someone accurately weighing the previously noted returns. 

Furthermore, we have an excellent and illustrious argument for removing the doubt from those who, while accepting with an even mind the revolution of the planets around the **Sun** in the **Copernican system** <term>Siſtemate Copernicano; the theory that the Earth and planets revolve around the Sun</term>, are so disturbed by the motion of a single **Moon** around the **Earth** <term>Tellurem</term>—while both complete an annual orbit around the Sun—that they think this constitution of the universe should be rejected as impossible. For now we have not just one planet revolving around another while both traverse a great orbit around the Sun; but our senses show us four stars **wandering** <meta>Catchword: gantes (the end of "vagantes")</meta>

<header>OBSERVATIONS OF THE STARS</header>

...they present wandering stars <note>original: "Stellas," here referring to the satellites</note>, while all together with **Jupiter** they traverse a great orbit around the **Sun** in a 12-year period. Finally, one should not pass over the reason why the **Medicean Stars**, even while they complete very narrow rotations around **Jupiter**, sometimes appear more than twice as large as their usual size. We cannot seek the cause in Earthly vapors <note>Galileo is referring to atmospheric distortion or "twinkling" caused by Earth's air</note>; for they appear enlarged or diminished while the masses of **Jupiter** and the nearby fixed stars are seen unchanged. That they approach [the Earth] and thus are distanced from Earth around their **perigee** <term>perigee; the point in an orbit closest to the observer</term> or **apogee** <term>apogee; the point in an orbit farthest from the observer</term> to such an extent that it explains such a change seems entirely improbable. For a narrow circular motion cannot provide this; whereas an oval motion (which in this case would be almost a straight line) is both improbable and does not agree with the appearances in any way.

What occurs to me in this matter I freely offer and present to the judgment and censorship of those who philosophize rightly. It is well known that by the intervention of Earthly vapors, the **Sun** and **Moon** appear larger, but the fixed stars and **planets** appear smaller. Hence the "Luminaries" <note>A traditional term for the Sun and Moon</note> appear larger near the horizon, but the stars appear smaller and often invisible; they are diminished even more if those same vapors are filled with light. Therefore, stars appear very small during the day and in twilight, but the **Moon** does not, as we noted above. It is established, moreover, that not only **Earth** but also the **Moon** has its own surrounding vaporous sphere, both from what we said above and especially from what will be said more fully in our *System* <note>Referring to his intended (and later famous) work on the Copernican system</note>. We can appropriately make the same judgment about the remaining **planets**; so that it seems not at all improbable to place a denser sphere [of vapor] around **Jupiter** than the rest of the ether. Around this sphere, just like the **Moon** around the sphere of the elements <note>The "sphere of elements" refers to the Earth and its atmosphere in the Aristotelian model</note>, the **MEDICEAN Planets** are carried; and by the intervention of this sphere, while they are at apogee they appear smaller, but while at perigee, through the al<unclear>ter</unclear>ation or thinning of that same sphere, they appear larger. The shortage of time prevents me from proceeding further; let the kind reader expect more on these matters soon.
