# General Narrative of the Continent of America in the Outer Atlantic

Alas! The nation of America <note>original: *Mílìjiān*</note> did not possess a king of heroic talent or fierce brilliance. It was once merely twenty-seven scattered tribes <note>Wei Yuan uses the term *bùluò* (tribes) to describe what were actually the early American states/colonies</note>, with several hundred thousand common people <term>common people: the original text uses *qiánshǒu*, literally "black-headed ones," an ancient Chinese term for the peasantry</term> dispersed like ponds across the land. 

Yet, indignant at the unprincipled and wolf-like English <note>original: *Yīngjílì*</note>, they were moved by a shared hatred; once the call for resistance was made, they joined together as one without even a formal agreement. They fortified their cities and cleared the countryside <term>cleared the countryside: *bìqīng-yěfù*, a scorched-earth defensive strategy intended to leave no resources for an invader</term>, cutting off the enemy's supply lines. In the end, they drove away their powerful foe and fully recovered their former territory. Can this not be called martial prowess?

Those who first opened North America <note>original: *Mòlìjiā*</note> were the French <note>original: *Fólánxī*</note>, but the English barbarians arrogantly seized it from them. When the Americans rose in indignation to expel the English, the French assisted them. Therefore, America and France have been allies for generations, sharing a common enmity toward the English. Thus, the English barbarians have not dared to seek revenge. To befriend those far away while attacking the enemy nearby—can this not be called wisdom? <note>Wei Yuan is referencing the classical Chinese diplomatic strategy of *yuǎnjiāo-jìngōng* (befriend the distant, attack the near)</note>

The chiefs of the twenty-seven tribes are divided into eastern and western routes, and they publicly elect one Great Chief <note>Wei Yuan's term for the President of the United States</note> to oversee the whole administration. Not only is the position not hereditary <gloss>it does not pass from father to son</gloss>, but the leader is replaced after only four years. This completely overturns the pattern of "government by the ruler's family" <term>government by the ruler's family: *guānjiā*, a traditional term for the imperial dynastic system</term> that has existed from ancient times to the present. Yet, despite this change, the hearts of the people remain in total harmony.

The largest among these is the **Kingdom of Peru** <note>original: *Bólù*, the Qing-era phonetic transcription for Peru</note>, which spans from three degrees south of the equator to forty-one degrees south. It is comprised of several dozen provinces, large and small, stretching across a vast territory of over ten thousand *li*. 

Within this country, gold, silver, and copper are found in great abundance. The palaces of the King are entirely decorated with plates of pure gold; conversely, iron is the rarest and most expensive of all metals. Their road system extends from the national capital to every corner of the realm. To create these thoroughfares, they have tunneled through mountains and leveled valleys, paving the way with stone to create smooth, level paths. 

Postal couriers, tasked with delivering royal commands, are stationed at intervals of every few *li*. By relaying the message from one runner to the next, a dispatch can travel two thousand *li* <note>approximately 600 miles or 1,000 kilometers</note> in just three days and nights. While the local dialects vary across the regions, there is one "standard language" that can be understood across the entire ten-thousand-*li* territory. This is equivalent to the **Official Language** <term>Official Language: *Guānyǔ*, the "Mandarin" or lingua franca used by the Chinese imperial bureaucracy</term> of China. This unified language is quite unlike the situation in the Western countries <note>referring to Europe</note>, where every nation speaks a completely different tongue. 

Thus, Peru and **America** <note>original: *Mílìjiān*. Here Verbiest likely refers to the northern regions of the Americas</note> are the two great and powerful nations of the American continent <note>original: *Mòlìjiā*</note>. 

Alas! If we were to set aside and ignore everything that exists beyond the "eight reaches" <note>a classical Chinese term for the most distant corners of the world</note> of our known world, how could we ever truly understand the sheer vastness of the universe?

### General Narrative of the History and Development of the American Continent <gloss>Added now; not present in the original sources</gloss>

This continent only became known in China during the Ming Dynasty <note>1368–1644 AD</note>; therefore, no records of it can be found in our ancient historical chronicles. This account of its history and development begins with the books written by Westerners during the Ming period.

The **Record of Lands Beyond Imperial Jurisdiction** <term>Record of Lands Beyond Imperial Jurisdiction: *Zhífāng Wàijì*, a world geography written in 1623 by the Italian Jesuit Giulio Aleni</term> states: **America** <note>original: *Yàmòlìjiā*</note> is the general name for the fourth great continent. The land is divided into North and South, connected in the middle by an isthmus. 

To the south of the isthmus lies **South America**. It begins in the south at the **Strait of Magellan** <note>original: *Mòwǎlàní*</note>, at a latitude of fifty-two degrees south of the equator. To the north, it reaches **Canada** <note>original: *Jiānádá*; though usually referring to modern Canada, in this context of South American boundaries, it refers to the northern regions of the southern continent or the approaches to the Isthmus of Panama</note> at a latitude of ten and a half degrees north. Its longitudinal span runs from two hundred eighty-six degrees in the west to three hundred fifty-five degrees in the east. 

To the north of the isthmus lies **North America**...

Until about one hundred years ago <note>Calculated from the time of the original Jesuit source in the early 17th century, referring back to the late 15th century</note>, there was a high official in the Western nations named **Columbus** <note>original: *Kuòlóng*</note>. Furthermore, he worried that if there were nations beyond the seas, they remained beyond the reach of civilized teachings and were perhaps sunken in "evil customs" <note>a reference to paganism or lack of Christian revelation</note>. They sailed swiftly with billowing sails and fired great cannons <term>dàpào: large-caliber firearms or artillery</term> that sounded like thunder. The local people were all shocked and spread strange rumors; some suspected the visitors were heavenly gods, while others called them sea monsters. The next day, her parents came with a crowd to see the newcomers. The sailors again gave them precious goods, and the locals were greatly pleased. They subsequently invited the Western guests to stay and provided them with land to build houses, making it easier for them to interact and trade. **Columbus** <term>Kailong: Christopher Columbus</term> ordered half of his crew to remain there, while the other half returned to report to the King and present the local products they had found. 

The following year, the King ordered ships to be loaded with seeds for a hundred varieties of grains and fruits. He also sent agricultural masters and skilled craftsmen to teach the people of that land, and the local sentiment grew even more joyful. After living there for several years, the settlers gained a deep understanding of the intricacies of the region, yet they remained confined to one corner of the land. 

Later, there was a man named **Amerigo** <term>Molige: Amerigo Vespucci</term> who traveled to the seas southwest of **Europe** <term>Ouluoba</term>. He discovered the vast land south of the **Equator** <term>Chidao: literally "The Red Path"</term> and named it after himself. Therefore, it is called **South America** <term>Nan Molijia</term>.

It is a firm rule that those who serve as **trial judges** cannot also participate in the **legislative assembly** to create laws; conversely, those who deliberate and establish laws cannot concurrently hold authority over trials. 

The legal system of this new nation consists of five levels of laws:

1.  **National Law**: This is the law common to all twenty-six states <note>The United States had 26 states between 1837 and 1845, providing a date for this record</note>.

3.  **Prefecture Law**: Each prefecture <note>referring to county-level governance</note> also has different regulations, which are observed only by those born or residing there.
4.  **County Law**: Each county establishes its own specific rules, and the local citizens follow their own system.
5.  **Administrative Law**: These are established by specific departments or offices, and are observed only by those under their direct jurisdiction.
