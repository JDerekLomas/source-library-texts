<image-desc>A full-page sheet of marbled paper, characteristic of historical book endpapers. The design features a traditional combed pattern, often called a "nonpareil" or "peacock" pattern, consisting of repeating rows of downward-pointing scalloped or fan-like shapes. The color palette is dominated by a deep terracotta red, interspersed with bands of light blue, yellow, and green. The patterns were created by floating pigments on a size and dragging a comb-like tool through them before transferring the design to paper. There are a few small, light-colored circular patches where the pigment has not fully adhered or has worn away.</image-desc>
