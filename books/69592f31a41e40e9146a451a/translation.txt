# Dedication

☛ Andreas Corvus <note>Latin: *Corvus* means "Raven." This is the pseudonym of Bartolomeo della Rocca (1467–1504), a famous Italian chiromancer and physiognomist.</note> to Giovanni Francesco Gonzaga <note>Gianfrancesco II Gonzaga (1466–1519), the Marquess of Mantua and a celebrated military commander.</note>, the most deserving Marquess of Mantua, with the highest reverence, sends his best greetings. <note>The Latin abbreviation S.P.D. stands for *Salutem Plurimam Dicit*.</note>

Most distinguished Prince, adorned in every respect: I have decided to publish for the general public this book on palmistry <term>chiromantia: the art of reading character and fortune from the lines of the palm</term> which was dedicated to your Highness in previous years. I do this at the request and urging of many honest men and friends. I certainly would not have yielded to this most honorable petition had your most auspicious and martial name, inscribed at the beginning, not protected both the work and its author from the bites of detractors by a certain patronage of its own.

Therefore, just as it previously entered your court—which is most full of every kind of virtue—reverently and without fear (as is fitting), so now let it go forth securely into the densest crowd of all sorts of people, supported by that shield of yours. By this protection of the twin Pallas <note>A reference to Pallas Athena, goddess of wisdom and war. The author suggests Gonzaga embodies both the military and intellectual aspects of the goddess.</note>—that is, by your most beautifully conducted deeds of Mars <note>The god of war.</note> and by your favor and magnificence toward Apollo <note>The god of music, poetry, and light.</note>—you have prepared a double trophy.

->Farewell.<-

**A**lthough the art of palmistry <note>original: "chiromantica," the study of the lines and features of the hand to predict the future or character</note> was gathered from the most excellent school of philosophers, we shall distinguish it through written theories and diagrams of hands with their lines into various chapters, along with their "mounts" <note>the fleshy cushions at the base of the fingers</note> and small furrows which can occur in the hands. From these, we recognize various meanings indicating various effects. First, therefore, we ought to know that the human hand is divided into five fingers, of which we call the first—consisting of two nodes or joints—the thumb <note>original: "pollicem"</note>.

We call the first the index or pointing finger; the third, the middle; the fourth, the ring finger; and the fifth and last, the pinky <note>original: "auricularem," literally the ear-finger, as it was used to scratch the ear</note>. Note, however, that beneath each of these fingers lies a "mount," each of which has been assigned its own name according to its various meanings in human life.

The mount of the thumb is called the **Mount of Venus**, whose symbol is this: ♀. The mount of the index finger holds the name of **Jupiter** with its own mark: ♃. To the middle finger, we ascribe the name of **Saturn** and this figure: ♄. We say the **Mount of the Sun** is situated beneath the ring finger <note>The text says "indici" (index) here, but the context of palmistry and the previous list suggests the author likely meant the ring finger, or "annularem"</note> with its written figure: ☉. The pinky finger possesses the **Mount of Mercury** with its figure: ☿.

It is also permitted to know that there are many lines in the hand itself. The first of these <margin>the restricted line</margin> we call the **Restricted Line** <note>term: "restricta" (the rascette or wrist lines). These are the horizontal creases separating the palm from the forearm</note>; we shall speak of it first. This is not because it is the most important in a person's life, but because it is the first at the connection of the hand and the arm. In this place, there are sometimes two or three lines, all of which we call the restricted line or lines.

Indeed, these lines signify the strength or weakness of human life, its length or brevity, and at what age a person will act more happily or unhappily. And what I say regarding good health and the prosperity of the body, you should understand the same regarding riches and the success of fortune—that is, concerning the goods of fortune, such as the abundance or lack of wealth. The name of this line (as we have said) is the **Restricted Line** or the **Joint of the Hand**, because the hand is formed at that very joint, extending from the roundness of the arm into a flat surface. Therefore, we have begun with it as the first principle, and we shall follow thereafter by speaking of the stronger and more principal lines.
