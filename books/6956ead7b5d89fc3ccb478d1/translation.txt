# OCCULT, Book III.

## CHAPTER II.

### Concerning silence, and the concealment of those things which constitute mysteries and secrets in Religion.

WHOSOEVER you may be who wish to devote yourself to this science, keep under a religious silence in the depths of your heart, like a secret of religion, a doctrine so sacred, and conceal it with an unshakable constancy, which never permits you to speak of it; for, as Mercury <note>referring to Hermes Trismegistus, the legendary founder of Hermeticism</note> says, it is an offense against religion to confide to many a discourse so filled with the majesty of God; and the divine Plato forbade publishing among the common people the sacrament <note>original: "sacrement," here meaning a holy rite or mystery</note> and the secrets contained within the mysteries. Pythagoras likewise and Porphyry obliged their disciples to a religious secrecy; in the same way, Orpheus required from those he received into the ceremonies of sacred things an oath of silence, through a kind of trembling reverence to prevent the mysteries of religion from reaching the ears of the unworthy and profane populace: This is why, in his hymn of the sacred word <note>original: "verbe sacré," likely referring to the Orphic 'Hieros Logos'</note> to the partisans of virtue, he speaks in these terms: *You friends of virtue, it is you whom I exhort to listen only to my words, and to strain your minds; on the contrary, you who despise sacred things, the laws, and ceremonies of religion, withdraw from here promptly; withdraw far from here, you wretches; go far away from our places.*

By keeping silence, then, and hiding those things which are secrets in religion, we shall thus make disciples worthy of this science: For as **Tertullian** <note>Quintus Septimius Florens Tertullianus (c. 155 – c. 220 AD), an early Christian author from Carthage.</note> says, one is obliged to keep the faith of silence in religions; those who do otherwise are on the edge of a precipice. From this comes the precaution of **Apuleius** <note>Lucius Apuleius (c. 124 – c. 170 AD), a Roman philosopher and author known for "The Golden Ass," which deals with the mysteries of Isis.</note> regarding the mysteries of sacred things: *I would reveal the mysteries to you if it were permitted for me to tell them, and I would give you the knowledge of them if it were permitted for you to listen; but both I who spoke, and you who listened, would be equally punished for our rash curiosity.* For a similar fault, we find in history that **Theodorus**, a tragic poet, was struck with blindness for wanting to apply certain things from the mysteries of the Jewish scriptures to some fable. Likewise, **Theopompus**, who had begun to translate some verses of the Divine Law into Greek, was confounded and lost his mind in a moment; it was for this reason that, following his misfortune, addressing God with great prayers to know the cause of this accident, he was answered in a dream that it was because he was making a criminal trade of divine things by exposing them to public profanation.
