-># INTRODUCTION.<-

established Laws, invented Arts, gave birth to Sciences, brought forth all virtues & all vices, produced in Society all revolutions & all changes, created this labyrinth of truths & errors, of opinions & systems, of politics, of morality, of legislation, of philosophy & of religion, in which, except for the Jewish people, the human race wandered until the birth of Christianity.

At the birth of Christianity, Christians turned this effort toward the dogmas <note>The term "dogma" refers to the core, authoritative doctrines of the Church.</note> & toward the morality of the Christian Religion.

The dogmas that it teaches are evidently revealed; but many of these dogmas are mysteries <note>In a theological sense, "mysteries" are truths of faith that exceed the grasp of human reason.</note>: it prescribes the laws best suited to making man happy, even on earth; but these laws combat the passions, or mortify the senses: it promises an eternal & infinite happiness, but one in which there will be degrees proportioned to merit: finally, it threatens with eternal misfortune those who do not believe its dogmas, or who do not obey its laws, & it provides all the necessary means for believing the truths it announces, & for practicing the duties it imposes; but it destroys neither the activity of the soul, nor the restlessness of the spirit, nor the source of the passions, nor the empire of the senses, & does not prevent in all men the deviations of reason, or the wanderings of the heart.

Thus the human spirit brought into the study of the dogmas of the Christian Religion, and into the practice of its duties, principles of illusion, disorder, and error.
