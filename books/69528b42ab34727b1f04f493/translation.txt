# Concluding Note

**O**f the woman who recorded the diary of her nerves, her desires, and her transfigurations <note>original: *Verklärungen*; a term often used in a religious context to describe a state of being filled with divine light or glory</note> through these visions, research has so far been unable to discover anything certain. She lived around 1300, but whether as a nun in a Flemish convent or in the world of Brussels as a woman of high standing, remains a matter of dispute. No likeness has survived, so that no one knows if she was fair, slender, and joyful, or if her body was weary and bloated and her face puffy. Her form, her expression, her unique self have vanished! Only her inner essence <note>original: *Überwesen*; literally "over-being," referring to the transcendent or spiritual self that exists beyond the physical persona</note>, the secret-inward one, is preserved for our gaze and endures in history.

How deeply this soul was attuned to the second, higher state of her "I"! Only in this state does she become certain that she truly exists, that she breathes and lives. During the remaining and abundant time when she—drifting humanly among men—lacks grace, she feels as if she were in exile; and all her high pride sinks away into self-accusation, acts of penance <note>original: *Bußübung*; formal religious practices performed to show repentance for sins</note>, and a dark sense of guilt. This lasts until suddenly the signs re-occur in her body, signaling that the emptiness seeks to be filled and that the pressure of the personality, surging against the blind walls, frees itself in that numbing devotion. This devotion is not a losing of oneself, but rather that over-abundant "possessing of oneself," to which the words of Christ in John 10:34 point:

> "Is it not written in the law: 'I have said, ye are gods?'"
