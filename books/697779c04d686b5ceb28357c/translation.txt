My highly beloved patron, Blessed Henry Suso, you who are flooded with heavenly delights in Heaven, yet—even in the perfect state you now occupy—burn with a much stronger and greater love for God and your neighbor. For this reason, you have willed that these your posthumous writings—among so many most precious writings which you, out of great humility on earth, did not wish to come to light but rather to remain hidden—these alone should come forth and now come to light in the German language. To this end, you have deigned to make use of my lowliness <note>literally "my nothingness," a common humble rhetorical device of the period</note>, which I then heartily and gladly, through the help of Divine Grace, have put into effect as much as was possible for me. I have done so as gladly as I have long and fervently desired to make my beloved brothers and sisters of the German fatherland partakers of this noble treasure, and to serve those inexperienced in the Latin language. 

Your love—as one who well knows how much the honor of God (who is honored through the honor of his saints) will be increased through this work, and the salvation of the neighbor promoted—has obtained this from the Divine Majesty for our latter times. These are times in which love will grow cold and decrease in many hearts, because living faith and the hope springing from it have already noticeably decreased, so that the majority of people look only to human means. Through this mistrust, the wells of the rivers of Divine Grace are blocked; and where they could have daily seen and experienced the wonders of God, just like the former holy friends and children of God, they make themselves unworthy of these wonders...

## Preface to the Following Dialogue.

<image-desc>A decorative row of repeating floral ornaments (fleurons) spans the top width of the text area.</image-desc>

Everyone knows that from the beginning, man was created by God primarily for this reason: so that he might enjoy Him as the true, highest, and eternal Good. Therefore, not only do humans desire this by their nature, but all creatures seek the highest Good, because nothing can be found among all created things that does not partake of this same Good to some extent. For from Him, as the foremost and inexhaustible fountain, a portion of this goodness is directed toward all things; and as the Psalmist <note>Reference to Psalm 145:16: "You open your hand and satisfy the desires of every living thing."</note> speaks: **When He opens His hand, all things are filled with goodness.** No one can truly be called or be "blessed" except according to how much they cling to or are united with the highest and true Good, in which alone the soul—adorned with the **image of God**—enjoys perfect peace and rest. The further the soul deviates from this Good, the more wretched and miserable it becomes. Therefore, by the impulse of nature, we all wish to be well-off, and all our labors, toil, and diligence aim toward one thing: namely, that we may be blessed <term>glückselig<gloss>A term denoting both earthly happiness and the state of eternal divine beatitude.</gloss></term>. Thus, some strive to accumulate possessions, others to achieve honor, and others to enjoy pleasures, for no other reason than that they might be blessed through the acquisition of these things.

But so that you may understand how God-loving souls can be fulfilled by nothing other than the highest Good, observe that wretched people, devoted to the aforementioned vanities, are not only failed by these things in finding happiness or satisfaction, but the more they have, the more they are tormented by an unceasing thirst and desire. They wander miserably, seeking more and more, envying others when they see them possessing an abundance, and spending their whole lives burdened with vain worries and anxieties. In this very fact they provide enough testimony that true blessedness does not consist in these things; and yet, when they chase these things, their only intention is to enjoy blessedness. This error has taken hold of almost all people, causing them to neglect the fountain of true blessedness and—as the Prophet Jeremiah <note>Reference to Jeremiah 2:13: "They have forsaken me, the spring of living water, and have dug their own cisterns, broken cisterns that cannot hold water."</note> says—to dig for themselves **cisterns** that are broken and hold no water, seeking blessedness in places where it cannot be found.
