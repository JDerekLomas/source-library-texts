#### General Account of France, Part II

**Illustrated Description of the Earth** states that France is bordered on the east by Italy, Switzerland, and Germany <note>original: 'Yaliman,' likely from the French 'Allemande' or Spanish 'Alemania'</note>. To the south, it is bordered by the Mediterranean Sea and Spain <note>original: 'Great Luzon.' In 19th-century China, Spain was often referred to by the name of its colony, Luzon (the Philippines), which was the primary point of contact between the two cultures</note>. To the west is the Atlantic Ocean, and to the north are the two nations of England and Holland.

The population is approximately 33.5 million. The capital city is named Paris <note>original: 'Bale City'</note>, and it contains 900,000 residents. The majority of the population follows Catholicism <term>Tianzhujiao: literally 'The Teaching of the Lord of Heaven,' the standard Chinese term for the Roman Catholic Church</term>, while a minority follow Protestantism <term>Yesujiao: literally 'The Teaching of Jesus,' often used specifically for Protestant denominations</term>. The people are practiced in etiquette and are extremely hospitable and polite in their social interactions. Their language serves as the "official tongue" of Europe <note>Referring to French as the international lingua franca of diplomacy and the elite during the 18th and 19th centuries</note>; for this reason, the officials of all nations are expected to understand it.

In the 27th year of the Daoguang reign <note>roughly 1847–1848</note>, the people rebelled, and the King fled to seek refuge in England.

In the 39th year of the Qianlong era <note>1774 CE</note>, the throne passed to Louis XVI <note>original: 'Lu-yi-si'</note>, and the nation fell into great turmoil. By the 57th year of the Qianlong era <note>1792 CE</note>, the monarchy was abolished, and assembly officials were established to manage the affairs of state. In the 9th year of the Jiaqing era <note>1804 CE</note>, the people of the nation collectively established him as their monarch. In the 20th year of the Jiaqing era <note>1815 CE</note>, he was defeated in battle by England and other nations. The people then restored the former king, Louis <note>Louis XVIII</note>, to the throne. 

Upon his death, the people instead established his younger brother Charles <note>Charles X</note> as king. He reigned for nine years until he was deposed due to civil unrest in the 10th year of the Daoguang era <note>1830 CE</note>.
