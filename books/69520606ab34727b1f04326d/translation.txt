# MARSILIO FICINO’S PREFACE TO THE BOOK *ON THE SUN* TO THE MAGNANIMOUS PIERO DE’ MEDICI.

**N**OW, Magnanimous Piero, I daily pursue that new translation <note>original: "interpretationem"; Ficino refers to his lifelong project of translating and commenting on the complete works of Plato into Latin</note> of Plato which was begun long ago under your family's auspices. As you well know, I explain this work with frequent divisions into specific points and with longer arguments, as much as the subject matter itself demands. 

Thus, when I recently reached that Platonic mystery where he most skillfully compares the Sun to God Himself, I decided to explain such a great matter at somewhat greater length—especially because our Dionysius the Areopagite <note>Ficino refers to the author now known as Pseudo-Dionysius, whom he believed to be the primary bridge between Platonic philosophy and Christian theology</note>, the foremost of the Platonists, whose translation I currently have in my hands, gladly embraces a similar comparison of the Sun to God. 

Therefore, while I labored over this "Sun" as if by a lamp for many nights now, I thought to select this most choice matter from my "Great Work" <note>likely referring to his massive "Platonic Theology" or his collected Plato commentaries</note> and to commend it in its own short volume. I send this mystery concerning the Sun to you as a gift of Phoebus <note>Phoebus is a common name for Apollo, the god of the Sun and the Muses; Ficino is using a classical metaphor for intellectual light</note>, specifically to you, a student of Phoebus (the leader of the Muses) and a patron of the Muses themselves. To you also the entirety of this new translation of Plato is dedicated. 

My hope is that, in the meantime, by this light—which is like a moon—you might glimpse what the whole Platonic work will be like (just as the Sun relates to the moon). If you have ever loved my Plato—or rather, your Plato, who has been yours for a long time—then I hope that, set ablaze by this light, you may love him more ardently hereafter and embrace that beloved one with your whole mind. <unclear>explicare</unclear> <note>The OCR is clipped here; "explicare" (to explain/unfold) completes the sense of the sentence.</note>
