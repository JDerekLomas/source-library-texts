### Hermes.

*In the upper sphere, in the middle of the fountain, is a vein which is the first rule of the philosophers.* <note>original: "In superiori Sphæra est in medio fontis vena, quæ est Philosophorum Regula prima." Hermes Trismegistus is the mythical founder of alchemy; here, the "fountain" and its "vein" likely symbolize the source of the "Universal Spirit" or the "mercury of the wise" that powers the alchemical work.</note>

### Arros. <note>Arros (often identified as Arisleus) is a traditional character in alchemical dialogues like the "Turba Philosophorum," frequently used to voice established chemical truths.</note>

*All metals, regarding their purity, are of the same substance; they differ only by impurity, and by a greater or lesser degree of digestion.* <note>original: "Metalla omnia quoad puritatem sunt ejusdem substantiæ, differunt tantum impuritate, majori vel minori digestione." Alchemists believed that all metals were "growing" in the earth to become gold; "digestion" refers to the natural heat of the earth that matures base metals like lead into precious ones over centuries.</note>

<summary>This page presents two foundational alchemical axioms attributed to Hermes and Arros. They emphasize the existence of a primary "first rule" or source of wisdom and the theory that all metals share a single fundamental substance, differing only in their level of maturity or "digestion."</summary>
<keywords>Hermes Trismegistus, Arros, alchemical digestion, purity of metals, first rule, universal substance, 18th-century alchemy</keywords>
