...it belongs to God alone to work all things in all, whether He be the cause of health or of diseases. Since those heathens <note>original: "Ethnici"; refers to the non-Christian Greek and Roman authorities.</note> were ignorant of this Christian standard, they falsely attributed to creatures those things that belong to the Creator alone, according to their own corrupt manner. And hence it was that they—as if submerged in the deep filth of Idolatry or sticking fast in the mud of ignorance—wandered entirely from the path, and thus vanished into their own imaginations. From this it happened that they accepted the creature in almost every work—indeed, even in the very practice of religion—in place of God their Creator. This, therefore, was the origin of universal ignorance, the beginning of the distraction of unity into multiplicity: taking the instrument <note>original: "organum"</note> (I say) which is driven by the internal and eternal acting principle, for the centrally moving and driving principle itself; and consequently accepting the shadow for the subject, and the fiction (I say) for the truth, in an unfitting manner.

Gentile physicians, therefore, recorded in writing that the primary cause of diseases is either heat or cold, or moisture or dryness, considering with a far-from-serious mind that God himself (as Scripture holds) effects and produces all these in the heaven or the air by His breath. Sometimes they posited either fire, or air, or water, or earth, but more frequently bile <term>cholera</term>, phlegm <term>pituita</term>, corrupt blood, or melancholy as the effectors of infirmities. Occasionally they imagined some of the macrocosmic <note>The "macrocosm" refers to the great world or universe, as opposed to the "microcosm" or the human body.</note> winds, or the inclemency of the air, or the evil influences of unfortunate stars, or their most wretched aspects (or some of them, speculating even more deeply) to be a striking angel, or some evil spirit dealing a blow, as the primarily efficient cause of diseases. Meanwhile, they failed to perceive that there is none among all these creatures that can bring either health or disease to another by itself, but only God, the one Jehovah, the creator and operator of all things, whose part it is (by means of His instrument, whether it be angelic, stellar, elementary, or windy) to act from the center, when and where He wills.

For the angel does not move or work, but God through the angel; nor the angel upon the star, but God by means of the angel and through the angel upon the star: and so in the rest. Whence it is certain that, according to the blameless testimony of the Apostle, God works all things in all, as He who, by the witness of the Prophet, does His will as much in the powers of heaven—that is, the angels and stars—as in the inhabitants of the earth, and there is no one who can resist.
