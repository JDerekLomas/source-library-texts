## For Both Fortnights
### A Guide to the Activities of the Birds
->A verse in the *Aruseerkkazhinediladi-asiriya-viruttham* meter<-

Eating, ruling, sleeping, death, and wandering—all exist in a cycle.
After ruling over the vast land, the bird "dies," then walks with purpose, and then slumbers.
In the auspicious daytime of the waning fortnight, it eats, dies, sleeps, and then rules;
Wandering far, it longs, travels, dies, and eventually rules once more.

The waxing fortnight <term>Purvapaksha; the period of the increasing moon</term> and the waning fortnight <term>Amarapaksha; the period of the decreasing moon</term> both consist of fifteen lunar days <term>Tithis</term>. For each lunar day, there are ten watches <term>Saamam; a period of 3 hours</term>. Within each watch, there are specific divisions of time measured in <term>Nazhigai; a traditional unit of 24 minutes</term>.

<margin>(4)</margin>
One and a quarter, one and a half, two, three-quarters, and a half—
In this proper way, the measures of time are divided.
For the waxing moon and the waning moon, this is the constant, subtle method,
Expressed and classified by the ancestors.

->For one watch consisting of six Nazhigai (approx. 2.4 hours)<-

->The Section on Activities<-

<margin>(5)</margin>
To explain it another way, observe the six measures of time.
Understand the subtle secret of how they join together.
Eating, walking, ruling, pining <note>original: *aengiye*; suggesting a state of weakness or transition</note>, and sleeping—
O one with eyes like a honeybee, keep this in your mind.

->The Section on Action<-

For the one who eats, it is one and a quarter; for walking, it is one and a half;
Look closely for the one who rules, it is two measures;
For the one who sleeps, it is three-quarters, and for death, it is one-half.
Such are the rhythmic results for the birds.

(Of these:) To understand the benefits of the three preceding verses: The waxing fortnight consists of fifteen lunar days and the waning fortnight consists of fifteen lunar days. For each lunar day, there are five watches for the day and five watches for the night. Each watch consists of six *Nazhigai*. Within those, the rising bird follows a sequence: in the first watch it eats, in the second watch it walks, in the third watch it rules, in the fourth watch it is drowsy <note>original: *thoongi*</note>, and in the fifth watch it sleeps. Furthermore, when examining the internal sub-periods <term>Antharabukti</term>:
The eating bird has 1.25 *Nazhigai*.
The walking bird has 1.5 *Nazhigai*.
The ruling bird has 2 *Nazhigai*.
The sleeping bird has 0.75 *Nazhigai*.
The dying bird has 0.5 *Nazhigai*.
Thus, these five activities total six *Nazhigai*, which constitutes one watch. Observe this order carefully.
