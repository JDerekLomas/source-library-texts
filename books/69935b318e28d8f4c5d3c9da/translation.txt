->*(Clarifying the Obligation of Repentance and its Virtue)*<-

Know that the obligation of <term>repentance<note>original: "tawba" — literally "returning" to God</note></term> is evident through transmitted reports and scriptural verses, and it is clear by the light of insight for one whose inner vision has been opened and whose chest God has expanded with the light of faith. Such a person is able to strive by the light that is before him in the darkness of ignorance, without needing a leader to guide him at every step. For travelers are either blind, unable to do without a leader for every step, or they are sighted, guided to the start of the path and then finding the way themselves. Similarly, people on the path of religion are divided in this way: there is the limited person who cannot bypass <term>imitation<note>original: "taqlid" — following religious authority without personal verification of the evidence</note></term> in his steps, so he needs to hear a specific text from the Book of God or the Sunnah of His Messenger for every footfall. He may lack such a text and become confused in his journey; thus, even if his life is long and his body large, his progress remains stunted and his steps limited. Then there is the fortunate one whose chest God has expanded for Islam, so he follows a light from his Lord. He suffices with the slightest hint to traverse rugged paths and cross exhausting obstacles. The light of the Quran and the light of faith shine in his heart. Because of the intensity of his inner light, he is satisfied with the slightest explanation, as if "his oil would almost glow even if no fire touched it; and if fire touches it, it is light upon light. God guides to His light whom He wills" <note>A reference to the "Verse of Light" in the Quran, 24:35.</note>.

This need for a transmitted text in every situation is for the person in the former state. If one wishes to know the obligation of repentance, he first looks with the light of insight at what repentance is, then at what "obligation" means, and then he combines the two. He will then have no doubt of its necessity. This is done by knowing that "obligatory" means that which is necessary to reach eternal happiness and salvation from eternal destruction. If happiness and misery were not tied to doing or leaving a thing, there would be no meaning in describing it as obligatory. The claim that "it became obligatory because of a command" is mere talk; for if we have no purpose—sooner or later—in doing or leaving an act, there is no meaning in our preoccupation with it, whether another made it obligatory on us or not.

Once the meaning of obligation is known—that it is the means to eternal happiness—one knows that there is no happiness in the Abiding Abode except in the meeting with God Almighty. Every beloved thing from which one is separated by death will inevitably become a source of anguish, acting as a barrier between the person and what he desires, causing him to burn with the fire of separation and the fire of Hell. He knows that nothing distances one from the meeting with God except following desires, intimacy with this vanishing world, and obsessing over the love of that which must definitely be parted with. He knows that nothing brings one closer to meeting God except cutting the heart's attachment to the finery of this world and turning completely to God, seeking intimacy with Him through constant remembrance and love, by knowing His majesty and beauty to the extent of one's capacity. He knows that sins—which are a turning away from God and a following of the whims of the devils, the enemies of God who are distanced from His presence—are the cause of being veiled and distanced from God Almighty. Thus, he does not doubt that turning away from the path of the "distanced" is obligatory to reach proximity. 

Turning away is only completed through knowledge, regret, and resolve. For as long as one does not know that sins are the cause of distance from the Beloved, he will not regret or feel pain because of his walking in that path. And as long as he does not feel pain, he will not return. The meaning of "return" is the abandonment of the act and the resolve to stay away. Thus, he does not doubt that these three meanings are necessary to reach the Beloved. This is how faith is when it results from the light of insight.

As for those who are not prepared for such an elevated station—the peak of which is beyond the reach of most of creation—there is in imitation and following a wide path through which one can reach salvation from destruction. Let such a person observe the word of God, the word of His Messenger, and the word of the Righteous Predecessors. God Almighty said: "And turn to God in repentance, all of you, O believers, that you may succeed" <note>Quran 24:31</note>. This is a general command. And God Almighty said: "O you who have believed, turn to God with sincere repentance" <note>Quran 66:8</note>. The meaning of "sincere" <note>original: "nasuh"</note> is "pure for God Almighty," free from impurities, derived from the word for "sincere advice." The virtue of repentance is further indicated by His saying: "Truly, God loves those who turn to Him in repentance and loves those who purify themselves" <note>Quran 2:222</note>. And the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The repentant one is the beloved of God, and the one who repents from sin is like one who has no sin." 

The Messenger of God (peace be upon him) said: "God is more joyful with the repentance of a believing servant than a man who stopped in a perilous, deadly desert land with his mount, carrying his food and drink. He laid his head and slept, and when he woke, his mount was gone. He searched for it until heat and thirst overcame him—or whatever God willed—and he said: 'I will go back to the place I was and sleep until I die.' He placed his head on his forearm to die, then woke up to find his mount right there with him, carrying his provisions and drink. God Almighty is more joyful with the repentance of the believing servant than this man is with his mount." The reports and traditions regarding this are innumerable, and the consensus of the community is established regarding its obligation. This is because repentance means the knowledge that sins and disobediences are destructive things that distance one from God the Exalted. This falls under the obligation of faith, yet heedlessness may cause one to be startled by it. Thus, the meaning of this knowledge is the removal of that heedlessness. There is no disagreement regarding its obligation. Among its meanings are: the immediate abandonment of sins, the resolve to abandon them in the future, and the remedying of past shortcomings; the obligation of these is beyond doubt.

As for **regret** over what has passed and grieving for it, this is also obligatory. It is the very spirit of repentance and the completion of the remedy. How could it not be obligatory? Rather, it is a type of pain that inevitably occurs following the realization of the truth of what has been missed of one's life and what was wasted in God's displeasure. 

If you say: "The heart’s pain is an involuntary matter that does not fall under choice, so how can it be described as an obligation?" Know then, that its cause is the realization of knowledge regarding the loss of the Beloved. One has a way to achieve its cause. By this logic, "knowledge" falls under the category of obligation—not in the sense that the servant creates this knowledge or originates it within himself, for that is impossible. Rather, knowledge, regret, action, will, power, and the empowered being are all the creation and action of God. *“And God created you and what you do.”* <note>A quotation from the Quran, Surah As-Saffat 37:96.</note> This is the truth according to those with insight; anything else is error.

If you say: "Does the servant not have <term>choice<note>original: "ikhtiyar" — the capacity to choose between alternatives</note></term> in action and omission?" We say: Yes. But that does not contradict our statement that everything is the creation of God the Exalted. Rather, the "choice" itself is also from the creation of God. The servant is **compelled in the choice he has.** For when God creates a healthy hand, creates delicious food, and creates a craving for food in the stomach, He then creates knowledge in the heart that this food will satisfy the craving. He also creates conflicting thoughts: "Does this food contain any harm, even though it satisfies the craving?" and "Is there any obstacle preventing its consumption?" Then, He creates the knowledge that there is no obstacle. 

When these causes converge, the **will** that prompts the eating becomes determined. This determination of the will—after the hesitation of conflicting thoughts and the occurrence of the craving—is what is called "choice." It must occur when its causes are complete. Once the determination of the will occurs through God's creation of it, the healthy hand moves toward the food inevitably. Since once the will and the power are complete, the occurrence of the action becomes necessary. Thus, the movement occurs. 

The movement is by God's creation after the attainment of power and the determination of the will, which are also both from God’s creation. The determination of the will occurs after the sincerity of the craving and the knowledge that there are no obstacles—which are also from God the Exalted. However, some of these creations are ordered upon others in a sequence according to the Way of God in His creation, and you will find no change in the Way of God. 

Just as God creates the movement of the hand in writing a poem, He does not do so unless He creates in it a quality called "power," and unless He creates in it "life," and unless He creates a "determined will." And He does not create the determined will unless He creates a craving and an inclination in the soul. This inclination does not fully stir unless He creates knowledge that the action is suitable for the soul, either immediately or in the end. He does not create that knowledge except by other causes relating to movement, will, and knowledge. 

Thus, knowledge and natural inclination always result in a determined will and power, and the will always results in movement. This is the order in every action, and all of it is from the invention of God the Exalted. However, some of His creations are conditions for others. For this reason, some must come before and others after. For example, the will is not created except after knowledge; knowledge is not created except after life; and life is not created except after a body. Thus, the creation of the body is a condition for the occurrence of life, because life is generated <note>The text uses "generated" (tawallada) but the context implies a conditional sequence rather than physical birth.</note> in the body. The creation of life is a condition for the creation of knowledge, because knowledge is not generated from life, but the locus is not ready to receive knowledge unless it is alive. The creation of knowledge is a condition for the determination of the will, because the will cannot be accepted except by a living, knowing body. Nothing enters into existence unless it is possible, and possibility has an order that does not accept change, for changing it is impossible. Whenever the condition of a quality is found, the locus becomes ready to receive that quality. Thus, that quality occurs from Divine Bounty and Eternal Power at the moment the readiness is achieved. ### (Explanation that the Obligation of Repentance is Immediate)

As for its obligation being immediate, there is no doubt in it. Recognizing that sins are destructive is part of faith itself, and faith is obligatory immediately. What follows from this obligation is the recognition that restrains one from the act. For this knowledge is not from the "Sciences of Revelation" which do not relate to action; rather, it is from the "Sciences of Conduct" <note>original: "Ulum al-Mu'amala" — practical religious knowledge regarding deeds and ethics.</note>. Every piece of knowledge intended to be a motive for action does not fulfill its duty unless it actually becomes a motive for it. Thus, knowledge of the harm of sins was only intended to be a motive for abandoning them. Whoever does not abandon them lacks this part of faith. Indeed, adultery and acts of disobedience do not negate that [theoretical knowledge]. It is as if a doctor says: "This is poison, do not consume it." If a person consumes it, it is said that he consumed it while "not believing"—not in the sense that he does not believe in the doctor’s existence or his status as a doctor, but rather that he does not believe the doctor's statement that the poison is lethal. For one who truly knows it is poison would never consume it at all. Thus, the sinner is necessarily deficient in faith. 

Faith is not a single gate; rather, it has seventy-odd branches. The highest is the testimony that "There is no god but God," and the lowest is removing a harmful object from the road. An example of this is someone saying: "A human is not a single existence, but seventy-odd components." The highest of these are the heart and the spirit, and the lowest is removing harm from the skin, such as having the mustache trimmed, the nails clipped, and the skin cleaned of filth, so that he is distinguished from the roaming beasts that are soiled by their own dung and have loathsome appearances due to their long claws and hooves. 

This is an exact analogy: faith is like a human being. The loss of the testimony of <term>Monotheism<note>original: "Tawhid" — the core Islamic belief in the absolute oneness of God.</note></term> causes total nullification, just like the loss of the spirit. He who possesses nothing but the testimony of Monotheism and the Messenger is like a human whose limbs have been severed, whose eyes have been gouged out, and who has lost all his internal and external organs, retaining only the essence of the spirit. Just as a person in such a state is close to death—where the weak, solitary spirit departs because it lacks the limbs that support and strengthen it—likewise, the one who possesses only the "root" of faith while being negligent in his deeds is close to having the tree of his faith uprooted when the violent, terrifying winds of the approach of the Angel of Death strike. 

Every faith whose root is not firmly established in certainty, and whose branches have not spread out through good deeds, will not stand firm against the storms of terror when the forehead of the Angel of Death appears. A <term>bad ending<note>original: "su' al-khatima" — dying in a state of sin or without faith, a primary fear in Islamic spirituality.</note></term> is feared for such a person, unless his faith is maintained by acts of obedience through the succession of days and hours until it becomes deeply rooted and firm. 

The sinner saying to the obedient person, "I am a believer just as you are a believer," is like the gourd plant saying to the pine tree, "I am a tree and you are a tree." How beautiful was the pine tree’s reply when it said: "You will soon realize your delusion in sharing this name when the autumn winds blow. At that time, your roots will be severed, your leaves will scatter, and your deception in sharing the name 'tree' will be exposed, having neglected the means by which trees remain firm. You shall see, when the dust clears, whether there is a horse beneath you or a donkey." 

This is a matter that becomes clear at the "ending." The hearts of the <term>gnostics<note>original: "al-arifin" — those with deep, experiential knowledge of God.</note></term> were only cut with fear because of the heralds of death and its terrifying precursors, which only the few can withstand. If a sinner does not fear eternal Fire because of his disobedience, he is like a healthy person indulging in harmful desires because he does not fear death due to his current health. Yet, death often happens suddenly. It is said to him: the healthy man fears illness, and then when he falls ill, he fears death. Likewise, the sinner fears a bad ending, and if his life ends poorly—God forbid—eternity in the Fire becomes mandatory. 

Disobediences are to faith what harmful foods are to bodies. They continue to accumulate within, changing the balance of the humors without the person realizing it, until the constitution is corrupted, and he falls ill suddenly, then dies suddenly. Likewise are sins. If one who fears destruction in this passing world must avoid poisons and harmful foods in every state and immediately, then one who fears eternal destruction is even more obligated to do so. 

If a consumer of poison regrets it, he must vomit and turn back from its consumption by neutralizing it and removing it from the stomach immediately and with haste, seeking to save his body which is on the brink of a destruction that only costs him this fleeting world. Therefore, the consumer of the "poisons of religion"—which are sins—is even more obligated to turn back from them through every possible rectification as long as a delay for rectification remains, which is one's lifespan. ### (Explanation that the obligation of repentance is universal across all persons and states, and no one is ever free from it)

Know that the external text of the Book <note>The Quran</note> indicates this, as God Almighty said:

> And turn to God in repentance, all of you, O believers, that you may succeed.

The address is universal to all of them. The light of insight also points to this, as the meaning of <term>repentance<note>original: "tawba" — literally "returning." In a spiritual context, it is the act of turning away from sin and back toward God.</note></term> is to return from the path that leads away from God and toward Satan. This return is only conceivable for a person of intellect. However, the instinct of the <term>intellect<note>original: "al-aql" — in this context, it refers to the spiritual faculty of discernment rather than just logic.</note></term> does not reach maturity until after the maturity of the instinct of <term>desire<note>original: "al-shahwa" — the biological and ego-driven cravings of the self.</note></term>, anger, and other blameworthy traits, which are Satan's means of enticing humans. The perfection of the intellect only occurs around the age of forty, its foundation is completed at the onset of puberty, and its beginnings appear after seven years of age. 

Desires are the soldiers of Satan, while intellects are the soldiers of the angels. When they meet, a battle necessarily arises between them, as one cannot coexist with the other because they are opposites. The struggle between them is like the struggle between night and day, or light and darkness. Whichever one overcomes the other necessarily displaces it. 

Since desires reach maturity in childhood and youth before the intellect matures, the "soldiers of Satan" have already taken the lead and seized control of the territory. The heart, by necessity, finds intimacy and familiarity with the requirements of these desires through habit. This dominates the heart and makes it difficult to detach from them. Then, the intellect—which is the party of God and His army, the savior of His allies from the hands of their enemies—begins to appear bit by bit, gradually. If it does not grow strong and mature, the kingdom of the heart is surrendered to Satan, and his promise is fulfilled, as he said: 

> I will surely bring his descendants under my sway, all but a few.

But if the intellect matures and grows strong, its first task is to suppress the soldiers of Satan by breaking the desires, abandoning habits, and forcing the human nature back toward acts of worship. Repentance is nothing other than this: returning from the path of the "guide of desire" (which is the news of Satan) to the path of God Almighty. 

There is no human in existence whose desires do not precede their intellect. The instinct that serves as Satan's equipment comes before the instinct that serves as the angels' equipment. Therefore, "returning" from what was first established—the following of desires—is necessary for every human being, whether they be a prophet or a simpleton. Do not think that this necessity was exclusive to Adam (peace be upon him). It has been said:

->Do not think that Hind alone is treacherous / It is the nature of every beautiful woman to be Hind.<-

Rather, this is an eternal decree written upon the human race. One cannot imagine it otherwise unless the "Divine Norm" (Sunna) were to change, and there is no hope of changing that. Thus, anyone who reaches maturity as an ignorant disbeliever must repent from their ignorance and disbelief. Anyone who reaches maturity as a Muslim, following their parents but heedless of the reality of their Islam, must repent from that heedlessness by understanding the meaning of Islam. The Islam of one's parents will not suffice until one becomes a Muslim for oneself. Once they understand that, they must return from their habits and their tendency to indulge in desires without restraint, returning instead to the mold of God's limits in what is forbidden and what is permitted. This is one of the most difficult gates of repentance, and it is where most people perish because they are unable to master it. All of this is "returning" and "repentance." 

This proves that repentance is an individual obligation (*fard ayn*) for every single person. It is inconceivable that any human could do without it, just as Adam could not. The nature of the child cannot accommodate what the nature of the parent could not accommodate at all. As for the explanation that it is an ongoing obligation in every state: it is because no human is free from sin committed by their limbs. Even the prophets were not free from this, as mentioned in the Quran and the traditions regarding the mistakes of the prophets, their repentance, and their weeping over their errors. Even if a person is free at some times from sins of the limbs, they are never free from the *thought* of sins in the heart.

Even if a person is free at certain times from worldly anxiety, they are never free from the whisperings of Satan, who introduces scattered and distracting thoughts that divert one from the remembrance of God. If one is free from those whispers, one is still not free from negligence and shortcomings in the knowledge of God, His attributes, and His actions. All of this constitutes a deficiency. This deficiency has causes, and abandoning these causes by occupying oneself with their opposites is a "return" from one path to its opposite—and the meaning of <term>Repentance</term> (Tawba) is precisely this "return."

It is inconceivable for a human being to be entirely free of such deficiency; people only differ in the degree of it. As for the root of this deficiency, it is inevitable. For this reason, the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: “Verily, my heart becomes clouded <note>original: "layughanu 'ala qalbi" - referring to a light veil or shadow over the heart's clarity</note> until I seek forgiveness from God seventy times in a day and night.” For this reason, God honored him by saying: *“That God may forgive you what preceded of your sin and what will follow”* [Quran 48:2]. If this was his state, what then of the state of others?

If you were to say: “It is not hidden that the anxieties and thoughts that occur to the heart are a deficiency, and that perfection lies in being free of them; and that falling short in knowing the essence of God’s majesty is a deficiency, and that as knowledge increases, perfection increases; and that moving toward perfection from the causes of deficiency is a ‘return,’ and this return is ‘repentance’—however, these are virtues, not religious obligations (<term>fara'id</term>). Yet you have stated that repentance is obligatory in every state. Repentance from these [subtle] states is not obligatory because attaining absolute perfection is not a legal requirement in the Divine Law (<term>Shar'</term>). So what do you mean by saying repentance is obligatory in every state?”

Know then, as previously mentioned, that man in the beginning of his creation is never free from following desires. The meaning of repentance is not merely abandoning these desires in the future; rather, the completion of repentance lies in rectifying what has passed. Every desire a person follows produces a darkness that rises to the heart, just as the darkness of one’s breath rises to the surface of a polished mirror. If the darkness of desires accumulates, it becomes "rust" (<term>rayn</term>), just as the vapor of breath becomes tarnish on a mirror’s surface when it accumulates. As God the Exalted said: *“Nay! But their hearts are rusted by what they used to earn”* [Quran 83:14].

When this rust accumulates, it becomes a "seal" (<term>tab'</term>) stamped upon the heart, like grime on a mirror; if it accumulates and remains for a long time, it sinks into the substance of the iron and corrupts it, so that the mirror can no longer accept polishing. It becomes like something cast in dross. To rectify the following of desires, it is not enough to simply stop them in the future; one must erase those traces that have been imprinted on the heart. Just as images cannot appear in a mirror by merely stopping the breath and vapors that blacken its surface in the future—not unless one works to erase the rust already imprinted upon it—so too, while darkness rises to the heart from sins and desires, a light rises to it from acts of obedience and the abandonment of desires. Thus, the darkness of sin is erased by the light of obedience. This is what the Prophet (peace be upon him) alluded to when he said: “Follow a bad deed with a good deed, and it will erase it.”

Therefore, a servant is never, in any state, beyond the need to erase the traces of bad deeds from his heart by performing good deeds that oppose them in their effects. This applies to a heart that was originally pure and polished, then became darkened by accidental causes. As for the "initial polishing," it takes a long time. Polishing to remove existing rust from a mirror is not like the work required to manufacture the mirror's surface in the first place. These are long labors because they involve uprooting the source, and all of this falls under the category of repentance.

As for your claim that this is not called "obligatory" but is rather a "virtue" and a "seeking of perfection," know that the word "obligatory" (<term>wajib</term>) has two meanings:

First: That which falls under the legal ruling (<term>fatwa</term>) of the Sacred Law and is shared by all people. This is the amount which, if all people occupied themselves with it, the world would not fall into ruin. If all people were commanded to "fear God with the fear He is truly due," they would abandon their livelihoods and renounce the world entirely. This would lead to the total nullification of piety itself; for whenever livelihoods are ruined, no one has the leisure for piety. Rather, the labors of life—such as farming and weaving—consume the entire lifespan of every individual just to meet their needs. Therefore, all these higher degrees [of spiritual refinement] are not "obligatory" in this general sense.

Second: The "obligatory" is that which is indispensable for reaching the desired proximity to the Lord of the Worlds and the "Praiseworthy Station" (<term>al-Maqam al-Mahmud</term>) among the Truthful (<term>Siddiqin</term>). Repentance from everything we have mentioned is "obligatory" to reach that goal. Just as it is said: "Purity is obligatory for voluntary prayer"—meaning for the one who desires it, for it cannot be achieved without it. As for the person who is content with deficiency and deprivation of the merit of voluntary prayer, purity is not "obligatory" upon him for that specific purpose. Similarly, it is said that the eye, ear, hand, and foot are conditions for the "existence of a human"—meaning they are conditions for one who wishes to be a *complete* human who benefits from his humanity and reaches high ranks in the world. As for the one who is content with the mere basis of life, satisfied to be a lump of meat on a butcher's block—a moist mass with a soul—for such a life, eyes, hands, and feet are not conditions.

The fundamental obligations found in the general legal rulings only lead to the "basis of salvation." The "basis of salvation" is like the "basis of life." Whatever lies beyond salvation—the felicities that make life meaningful—are like the limbs and tools that make life complete. This is what the Prophets, the Saints (<term>Awliya</term>), and the scholars have strived for, one after the other. This was the object of their longing and the center around which they circled.
