## Celestial Anomalies of the Moon

When two moons appear, the state will fall into chaos, which will inevitably lead to the destruction of the nation. Alternatively, it signifies that high-ranking ministers will execute the sovereign, or there will be great flooding. <note>In historical Chinese astronomy, the appearance of "two moons" usually refers to a "paraselene" or "moondog"—an optical phenomenon caused by ice crystals in the atmosphere—which was interpreted as a sign of competing powers.</note>

When two moons merge together, it signifies a subject <term>assassinating the ruler</term> <note>The original term "shì" (弒) specifically refers to the murder of a superior or sovereign by a subordinate.</note>.

When two moons appear to fight with one another, the <term>Yin energy</term> <note>Yin (陰) represents the moon, the feminine, and the subordinate; its "stripping away" of the Yang (the sun/ruler) indicates a reversal of the natural social order.</note> is clashing and stripping away the status of the superior. This signifies the Empress deceiving the Sovereign, or generals and ministers plotting rebellion. Alternatively, it portends external threats or invasions.

# Book of Jin, Treatise Volume 12: Astronomy

The <term>fixed stars<note>original: 經星 (jingxing); these are the "constant stars" that maintain their positions relative to one another, distinguishing them from the "wandering stars" or planets.</note></term> range from the faint to the prominent. **Zhang Heng** <note>A renowned polymath of the Han Dynasty (78–139 AD), famous for his work in mathematics, astronomy, and his invention of the first seismoscope.</note> states in his work ***The Spiritual Constitution of the Universe*** <note>original: 靈憲 (Ling Xian)</note>:

"As for the stars: their physical substance is born from the Earth, while their essence is perfected in Heaven. They are arranged in rows or stand scattered; each is assigned to a specific path and category. Among the 'officials' of the inner and outer regions, those that are constantly bright number 124. Those that can be named number 320, totaling 2,500 stars. However, the divinations used by the 'people of the sea' <note>Refers to maritime navigators whose star lore was often separate from the official imperial tradition.</note> are not yet included in this count.
