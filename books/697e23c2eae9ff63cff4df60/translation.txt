# CHAPTER I. <note>original: "CAPUT I."</note>
## Jacob Böhme
### From where he obtained his
### knowledge. <note>The German "Wissenschafft" in the 17th century refers to a systematic body of knowledge or spiritual science, rather than the modern definition of laboratory science.</note>

Concerning this, he writes thus in his works: This (which I am writing) has remained hidden from the world and has never been so completely and fully revealed in any human heart; I myself am far more amazed by this high revelation <note>original: "Offenbahrung"</note> than the reader perhaps will be.

I do not write this for my own glory, for my glory lies in my hope for the future; I am just as much a poor sinner <note>original: "Sünder"</note> as all other men, and I also belong before this mirror <note>In Böhme’s theology, the "mirror" often represents the process of self-reflection and divine recognition where the soul sees its own nature in God.</note>: but rather, I marvel that God wishes to reveal Himself so entirely in such a simple man, and even drives him to write it down, when there would be many better writers <note>original: "Scribenten," implying professional scholars or university-educated authors.</note> who could write and present it much more loftily than I, who am but a mockery and a fool to the world.
