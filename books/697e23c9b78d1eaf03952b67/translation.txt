## The Author to the Reader.

When I decided to revisit subjects removed from common types of learning because of their depth, and to look again at their writers, both the nature of the undertaking itself and friends of the truth led me to the opinion that this little book should also be published in Latin. For some have thought this work was born for our homeland <note>The author likely refers to Germany, as Latin was the international language used to reach beyond local vernacular audiences.</note> alone, but I was able to gather that foreigners would also claim a share of it for themselves. It appears that among the French, Belgians, Italians, and Spaniards, <term>Mystical Doctrine</term> <note>original: "Doctrina Mystica"; this refers to the study of the soul's direct union with the Divine, often beyond formal ritual or dogma.</note> has flourished almost more than it has among our own people. Added to this is the singular spirit of this <term>Theosophy</term> <note>original: "Θεοσοφίας" (Theosophia); meaning "divine wisdom," a tradition seeking direct knowledge of God and the mysteries of nature.</note>, which is so universal and spreads so widely that it never allows itself to be confined within the borders of a single sect or religion among Christians. Rather, it is found to conform to the most wise saying of the Apostle Peter <note>A reference to the New Testament, Acts 10:34–35: "God is no respecter of persons: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him."</note>, that from every nation, those who fear God and sincerely cultivate justice are rendered acceptable to Him.

This reality moved me, since I barely had enough leisure time myself, to hand this writing over to a friend to be translated into Latin and shared with you, kind Reader. He took up this task all the more eagerly because he understood that many, not without cause, wish to know this kind of subject matter in these times. In translating, he employed suitable faithfulness and industry, using a style that is not clumsy or scholastic <note>The "scholastic" style was often viewed as overly technical, dry, or pedantic; the author here prides the translation on being accessible and clear.</note>, but plain and unaffected, and one which is not at odds with the subjects themselves. You, Reader, may you use this little book to your benefit; at the same time, live mindfully that this manner of writing and reading ought to be applied to your practice throughout your entire life.

## Second Preface
### *to Readers free from the spirit of factions.* <note>original: "à partium studio alienos"; this refers to readers who are impartial and not biased by the fierce religious sectarianism of the era.</note>

I had long ago collected these materials, together with those others pertaining to <term>Church History</term> <note>original: "Ecclesiasticam Historiam"; the study of the historical development of the Christian Church, its doctrines, and its figures.</note>, and since they were so ready and at hand, I more easily arranged them into order. For in these recent years, my <term>Purpose</term> <note>original: "Propositum"; a firm resolution or life's calling.</note> remains fixed and unmoving: to no longer entangle myself in purely historical matters and others of this sort, nor to waste time upon them. Nevertheless, it will be abundantly clear to wise readers from these very materials that this writing leads more toward a true recognition and love of the invisible <term>Deity</term> <note>original: "Deitatis"; the essential nature or being of God.</note>, rather than toward a purely historical, vain, or empty knowledge (which "puffs up") <note>A reference to 1 Corinthians 8:1 in the New Testament: "Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth."</note>.

Nor, however, is it my intention to weave a more long-winded excuse for the subject matters themselves, or for the manner of presentation and other circumstances. For to the wise and those who fear God, this would be superfluous; however, among the perverse and the <term>world-wise</term> <note>original: "cosmosophos"; from the Greek "kosmos" (world) and "sophos" (wise), referring to those who possess secular or worldly philosophy rather than divine wisdom.</note>, it would not be worth the effort—

...but, since of their own accord and frivolously they desire to know nothing but their own traditions and arguments or foundations, which <term>corrupted reason</term> <note>original: "ratio corrupta"; in this context, human intellect that has been tainted by sin or pride and is thus unable to perceive divine truth without help.</note> provides. 

It will suffice, however, to have cautioned this one thing: that I have indeed earnestly applied my efforts so that I might perform the duty of a <term>Historian</term> <note>original: "Historici"; a chronicler of events and lives.</note> free from the bias of all factions regarding <term>mystical matters</term> <note>original: "materias mysticas"; topics concerning the direct, internal, and spiritual experience of God.</note> and their writers. Therefore, I have also mentioned their defects, insofar as it was necessary. Nevertheless, my mind has always been guided toward Christian discretion and moderation, so that it was not permitted for me to become a participant in the detestable blasphemies hitherto uttered by many against <term>mystical Theology</term> <note>original: "mysticam Theologiam"; the study of God through direct experience or "hidden" spiritual wisdom.</note>; rather, I considered it my duty to look more upon their good intention than upon certain defects.

Hence, the enlightened will find in several places of this little book milder and, as it were, condescending judgments concerning certain Persons and matters than even the accurate discernment of a pure <term>Evangelical Spirit</term> <note>original: "Evangelici Spiritus"; a perspective rooted strictly in the teachings of the Christian Gospels.</note> might seem to allow. In general, however, and once for all, I affirm and protest that I have by no means wished to yield even the smallest bit from the <term>pure evangelical truth</term> <note>original: "pura veritate evangelicâ"</note>, and that all ill-will has been absent from my word. Rather, I desire with the greatest longing to believe, teach, and live according to the sole rule of the new <term>Creation in Christ</term> <note>original: "Christo Creationis"; the concept of a spiritual rebirth or transformation through faith.</note>, and to persuade others of this same thing and pray for them. ...to consider it a loss. Indeed, a true disciple of Christ, who is devoted to no faction, can and ought to love and revere any teacher in his own right; nevertheless, Christ alone, as <term>essential Truth</term> <note>original: "Veritas essentialis"; the fundamental, unchanging reality of God that exists beyond human opinion or description.</note>, remains a closer friend to him.

It will be my task, however, to entrust all things to the all-governing <term>Wisdom and Love of God</term> <note>original: "Sapientiae & Amori DEI"</note>, and to sincerely commend the reader to the same.
