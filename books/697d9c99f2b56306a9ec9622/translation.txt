### To the Christian Reader.

Attentive Reader / as I still find here and there among the well-practiced **Brothers of the Cross** <term>Cruys broeders: A term for fellow Protestants who suffered persecution or exile for their faith.</term> / whom I especially love / so I also found a few years ago at **West-Souburg** <note>A village in the province of Zeeland, Netherlands.</note> my old friend and Brother **Pieter Pauwels**, who also for many years Christianly acquitted himself in the service of Elder in the church at **Norwich** <note>original: "Norwitz." Many Dutch Protestants fled to Norwich, England, to escape Spanish persecution.</note> during my time there: With him, I found a year and a day ago the draft of these two Treatises / and that for no other purpose / than to warn his children against this blind heap of **Anabaptist** <term>Herdoopscher: Literally "Re-baptizer." A derogatory term used by the state church for the various radical Reformation groups.</term> sects / in which he himself had been for some years in previous times through similar blindness. In this, he has Christianly followed the teaching and the command of God / notably in **Psalm 78** <note>This Psalm emphasizes teaching the next generation about the deeds of God and the errors of their ancestors.</note>. And although he (having never studied) has always supported himself with **farming** <note>original: "lantneeringe."</note> / nonetheless I found / that the Spirit of the Lord has been alive in him in this / and therefore he desired / to hand the same over to me entirely / to improve / to add or subtract / to put into good order / etc. to come to light under my name / just as he has entirely turned it over to me.

Secondly / let it not seem strange to you / that I here and there point to the **original text** <note>Referring to the original Greek and Hebrew of the Bible.</note> / also expressing some words more closely in our mother tongue according to the power of the same / the matter has required such / to point out the bold impudence of the Anabaptists / in using (or much more misusing) a passage of Holy Scripture here and there / also to provide a closer and clearer opening from the **original sources** <note>original: "originale fonteyne."</note> regarding the subject matter contested by these manifold sects.

Lastly, I wish to have this well-advised and prayed for / since even the Holy Scripture itself has its detractors / that this work of ours / coming into the hands of these sects from both sides / and he who can be made neither learned nor softened by it but much more takes his pleasure / in smearing this or that with the pen / that the same person does not (improperly) keep himself **nameless** / as a **certain someone** <note>original: "quidam," a Latin term for an unnamed person, often used dismissively in polemical writing.</note> has done for some time (in this his doing I can call him nothing else) / against the Dedicator **Joannes Beu** <note>Johannes Beeuwens, a Reformed minister in Middelburg.</note> / Minister of the Word of God in the church of Christ at **Middelburg** / alleging the texts of Holy Scripture / with such foundation and understanding / as (so they say) **Bartelt read the Psalter** <note>A Dutch idiom referring to someone who reads words without understanding their meaning or context.</note> / but if he knows a closer foundation / let him attack the matter itself / and from the foundation of the Holy Scripture (since here and there groundless foundations are drawn out) / let him act / read, and judge rightly.

### To the Christian Reader.

Attentive Reader, as I still find here and there some of the well-practiced <term>Brothers of the Cross: A term used for fellow believers who suffered persecution or "bore the cross" for their faith.</term>, whom I especially love, so a few years ago at West-Souburg I also found my old friend and brother Pieter Pauwels. For many years, during my time there, he served faithfully as an Elder in the church at Norwich <note>Norwich, England, was a major center for Dutch Protestant refugees fleeing Spanish persecution in the 16th century.</note> in a Christian manner. With him, I found a year or so ago the drafts of these two treatises. They were written for no other purpose than to warn his children against this blind heap of Anabaptist <term>Herdoopscher: Literally "Re-baptizing," the term Moded uses for the various Anabaptist groups.</term> sects, in which he himself had remained for some years in previous times through similar blindness. In this, he has Christianly followed the teaching and command of God, notably in Psalm 78. And although he (having never formally studied) always supported himself through farming <note>original: "lantneeringe," referring to earning a living from the land or rural trade.</note>, I nonetheless found that the Spirit of the Lord was living within him in this work. Therefore, he desired to hand the whole matter over to me to improve, to add or subtract as needed, and to put into good order, etc., so that it might come to light under my name, just as he surrendered it entirely to me.

Secondly, do not let it seem strange to you that I here and there point to the original text <note>Moded is referring to the original Greek and Hebrew of the Bible.</note>, and also express some words more closely in our mother tongue based on the strength of the same. The matter required this in order to point out the bold shamelessness of the Anabaptists in their use (or much rather, their misuse) here and there of a passage of Holy Scripture. It was also done to provide a closer and clearer opening, from the original fountain <note>original: "fonteyne," a common metaphor for the original biblical languages.</note>, into the subject matter contested by these many various sects.

Lastly, I wish to have this well-warned and requested: since even the Holy Scripture itself has its detractors, this work of ours, coming into the hands of these two-sided sects, may result in them becoming neither more learned nor softened by it. Rather, their desire will be much more to stain this or that with their pens. Let such a person not (improperly) keep himself nameless, as a certain person <note>original: "quidam," a Latin term for "a certain someone" or "an anonymous individual."</note> did for a certain time (in this his doing I can call him nothing else) against the dedication of Joannes Hen. <note>Joannes Hendricx, a Reformed minister.</note>, Minister of the Word of God in the Church of Christ at Middelburg. He cited the texts of Holy Scripture with such a foundation and understanding as (as the saying goes) "Bartelt read the Psalter" <note>A Dutch proverb—"lezen als Bartelt de Psalter"—referring to someone who reads words without understanding their meaning or reads poorly.</note>. But if he seeks a closer foundation, let him attack the matter itself, and from the foundation of the Holy Scripture (rather than setting down here and there a proverb pulled up without foundation), read and judge rightly.
