# The Complete Library in Four Sections
<note>original: "Siku Quanshu" (四庫全書). Commissioned in 1772 by the Qianlong Emperor, this was the largest collection of books in Chinese history, intended to preserve and categorize the empire's vast literary and intellectual heritage.</note>

<note>original: "Zi Bu" (子部). This is the third of the four traditional branches of Chinese literature. It contains works by "Masters" or thinkers, covering a wide range of subjects including philosophy, military strategy, medicine, astronomy, and religion.</note>

## Section III of the Category of Insects and Crawling Creatures
<term>Insects and Crawling Creatures (Chongbu, 蟲部): In the 16th-century taxonomy used by Li Shizhen, this category was much broader than the modern definition of "insect." It encompassed a wide variety of small invertebrates, including spiders, worms, and even some mollusks.</term>

Species born by transformation
<note>original: "Huasheng lei" (化生類). These are "metamorphosis" creatures. In traditional biology, this referred to animals thought to be born through spontaneous generation or dramatic transformation rather than through eggs or live birth.</note>

## Section IV of the Category of Insects and Crawling Creatures

Species born of moisture
<note>original: "Shisheng lei" (濕生類). This subcategory includes creatures believed to arise from dampness or water-saturated environments, such as various aquatic larvae or earthworms.</note>

## Section I of the Category of Scaled Creatures
<term>Scaled Creatures (Linbu, 鱗部): This classification includes aquatic and terrestrial animals with scales, primarily encompassing fish, but also including snakes and legendary creatures like dragons.</term>

# Section II of the Category of Shelled Creatures
<term>Shelled Creatures (Jiebu, 介部): This taxonomic group, known as the "Armored" category, encompasses animals protected by a hard outer shell. In the traditional Chinese "Five Creatures" system, these were associated with the element of Water and the direction of the North.</term>

## Tortoise and Soft-shell Turtle Species
<note>original: "Gui bie lei" (龜鼈類). This subcategory distinguishes between the hard-shelled tortoise (*gui*) and the leathery soft-shell turtle (*bie*). These animals were revered for their longevity and were frequently used in medicine; the tortoise plastron (lower shell) was especially prized for its cooling properties and its ability to nourish the blood.</note>

## Mussel and Clam Species
<note>original: "Bang ge lei" (蚌蛤類). This refers generally to bivalves and various mollusks. In the context of the Compendium, these were studied not just as food, but for the medicinal properties of their shells and the pearls they produced, which were ground into fine powders to treat ailments of the eyes and skin.</note>
