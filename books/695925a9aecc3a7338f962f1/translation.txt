Though opinions may vary, with the ancient serpent <note>A traditional biblical reference to the Devil or Satan.</note> fanning the flames, the resulting calamity is not nearly so deplorable as that which follows from the persuasion—once imbibed through his instinct—concerning that kind of evil which certain demented old women, whom they call <margin>Witches. Sorceresses.</margin> **Lamiae** <note>original: "Lamias"; a term from classical mythology for child-devouring demons, used here for witches.</note> or **Sagas** <note>original: "sagas"; wise-women or female fortune-tellers, often used pejoratively.</note>, are believed to inflict upon men and beasts without the drinking or application of any actual poison. For while from the former disputes various disagreements arise here and there according to men's affections, or as each person seems to abound in his own judgment, or according to the weight and quality of the talent granted by God; nevertheless, these often will not have such great importance as to touch the salvation of the soul in any way, or even to entirely disturb or break off friendships. 

How truly execrable, however, is the alienation from God and the most intimate bond with the demon—how great the hatreds among neighbors, how quarrelsome the factions in vicinities, what rivalries among peasants, what dissensions in cities, and how frequent the slaughters of the innocent—which that most fertile calamity gives birth to, with the <margin><unclear>Dissension.</unclear></margin> devil acting as the father of the tragedy.
