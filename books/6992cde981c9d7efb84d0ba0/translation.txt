### Explanatory Terms

<term>Six Palaces (Liufu)</term>: In physiognomy, these are three pairs of facial regions (left and right) that act as "storehouses." When these areas are fleshy and well-structured, it suggests a life of abundance.

<term>Three Talents (Sancai)</term>: The traditional triad of Heaven, Man, and Earth. In the face, Heaven is the forehead, Man is the nose and cheeks, and Earth is the chin and jaw.

<term>Three Stops (Santing)</term>: A method of dividing the face horizontally to analyze the progression of a person's destiny through time.
*   The **Upper Stop** spans from the hairline to the eyebrows.
*   The **Middle Stop** spans from the eyebrows to the tip of the nose.
*   The **Lower Stop** spans from the base of the nose to the bottom of the chin.

<term>Heavenly Granary (Tiancang)</term>: Specifically refers to the area of the temples. In traditional reading, a "full" or "plump" Heavenly Granary indicates that one will not lack for food or resources.

<summary>This page illustrates the fundamental "map" of Chinese physiognomy, dividing the face into three horizontal life stages (the Three Stops) and three vertical cosmic zones (the Three Talents). It identifies the Six Palaces—bilateral points on the forehead, cheeks, and jaw—that serve as indicators of wealth and stability.</summary>

### Explanatory Terms

<term>The Nine Provinces (Jiuzhou)</term>: The legendary administrative divisions of ancient China established by the Great Yu. In physiognomy, mapping these provinces onto the face suggests that the "microcosm" of the human body contains the entire "macrocosm" of the state.

<term>The Eight Trigrams (Bagua)</term>: Eight symbols used in Taoist cosmology to represent the fundamental principles of reality. In this diagram, they are arranged according to the "Later Heaven" sequence, which maps them to specific compass directions and facial features (e.g., Fire at the top/South/forehead).

<term>Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches (Ganzhi)</term>: A system of cyclical characters used for recording time, direction, and astrological data. Here, they provide a precise coordinate system for the face.

<term>Correlative Cosmology</term>: The belief that different realms of existence—the human body, the empire, and the heavens—are interconnected and influence one another. By reading the "geography" of a face, a physiognomist believed they could understand a person's destiny as part of the larger cosmic order.

### Technical Vocabulary and Concepts

**Light Clear Eyebrows** <note>original: 輕清眉 (qīng qīng méi)</note>
These are considered highly auspicious for social mobility. The "Imperial Capital" refers to the goal of every scholar: passing the civil service exams to serve the Emperor.

**Short Elegant Eyebrows** <note>original: 短促秀眉 (duǎncù xiù méi)</note>
Though "short" often implies a lack of persistence in some systems, here "elegant" (秀) compensates. The "Promise of Chicken and Millet" <note>original: 雞黍約 (jīshǔ yuē)</note> is a famous historical allusion to Fan Shi and Zhang Shao, two friends who kept their promise to meet years later, symbolizing ultimate trustworthiness and integrity.

**Spiral Eyebrows** <note>original: 旋螺眉 (xuánluó méi)</note>
This is a "hard" feature. In traditional Chinese physiognomy <term>Physiognomy: 相術 (xiàngshù), the art of reading a person's character or future from their facial features.</term>, such intense features are considered dangerous for commoners as they imply a volatile or overly aggressive energy, but they are ideal for military officers <note>original: 武職 (wǔzhí)</note> who must command authority.

**One-Character Eyebrows** <note>original: 一字眉 (yīzì méi)</note>
Named for the Chinese character for the number one (一), which is a single horizontal stroke. This shape suggests a focused, direct nature. "Husband and wife shall live in harmony" <note>original: 夫婦齊眉 (fūfù qíméi), literally "husband and wife leveling their eyebrows"</note> is a classic idiom for mutual respect and a long, happy marriage.
