# FIRST DIALOGUE ON LOVE,
## BY LEONE EBREO.

### PHILO AND SOPHIA
### INTERLOCUTORS.

<margin>Ph.</margin>
Knowing you, O Sophia, causes in me both love and desire.

S O. These effects that the knowledge of me produces in you seem discordant to me, O Philo; but perhaps passion makes you speak thus. 

P H I. They discord from yours, which are devoid of any correspondence <note>original: "correspondenza." Philo is suggesting that his feelings of love and desire are not being reciprocated or matched by Sophia’s own feelings.</note>. 

S O. On the contrary, they are themselves contrary affections of the will: to love and to desire. 

P H I. And why contrary? 

S O. Because we love those things we judge to be good which we already have and possess; and those that we lack, we desire. Thus, that which is loved is first desired; and after the desired thing has been obtained, love comes, and desire fails. 

P H I. What moves you to hold this opinion? 

S O. The example of things that are loved and desired. Do you not see that when we do not have health, we desire it? But we would not say we truly "love" it then. And once we have it, we love it, and do not desire it. Riches, inheritances, jewels—before they are possessed, they are desired and not loved; after they have been obtained, they are no longer desired, but are loved. 

P H I. Although health and riches cannot be loved when they are lacking because we do not possess them, nevertheless they are loved "to be had" <note>original: "s'amano d'hauerle." Philo is arguing that we can love the idea or the potential possession of something even if we don't have it yet.</note>. 

S O. This is an improper way of speaking: to say "to love," meaning to want to have a thing. That should be called "desiring" it. For love is of the thing itself loved... 

<summary>This page marks the beginning of the first dialogue, where Philo (representing the Lover) and Sophia (representing Wisdom) debate the philosophical definitions of love and desire. Sophia argues that desire is for things we lack, while love is for things we already possess.</summary>
