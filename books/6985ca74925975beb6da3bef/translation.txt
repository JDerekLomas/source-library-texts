# Preface to the Christian and Favorable Reader.

Christian and most favorable reader, whoever wishes to walk upon this footpath toward the Royal, <term>Chemical Wedding Hall</term> <note>The "Chemical Wedding Hall" (original: *Chymischen Hochzeitsaal*) is a famous allegorical concept in alchemy, representing the mystical union of opposites—often symbolized as a King and Queen—to produce the Philosopher's Stone.</note> and proceed correctly, must above all things surrender themselves entirely to God, call upon Him diligently for guidance, and live according to Nature in all things.

So that the Christian and favorable reader may better understand my intention for this entire work, I shall divide this, my preface, into two distinct points and treat them accordingly.

First, I will explain from whom one should request, seek, and obtain such <term>Art</term> <note>In early modern texts, "Art" (*Kunst*) specifically refers to the "Great Art" of alchemy—the practical and spiritual skill required to transform substances.</note>.

Second, I will explain how a person seeking this Art (alongside their prayer to God) must and should live according to Nature alone in all things, if they wish to accomplish and obtain anything fruitful.

<summary>This introductory page outlines the spiritual and natural requirements for pursuing alchemy, framing the pursuit as a journey toward a "Chemical Wedding" that requires both divine favor and a life lived in harmony with nature.</summary>

# Preface.

If the favorable reader, who seeks the Great Art, will now examine himself in these two points as if looking into a mirror, he will soon realize whether he is able to travel and walk this path toward the <term>Chemical Wedding</term> <note>original: *Chymische Hochzeit*; a foundational concept in alchemy representing the union of opposites to create the Philosopher's Stone.</note> or not.

First, from whom one should ask for, seek, and obtain such Art is shown and presented to us in the following testimonies and examples.

In the New Testament, in Matthew chapter 6, our Savior and Redeemer Jesus Christ himself says: "Seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you."

Likewise, the Holy Apostle James says in the first chapter: "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights."

And Sirach in the first chapter: "The fear of the Lord is honor, and glory, and gladness, and a crown of rejoicing. The fear of the Lord maketh a merry heart, and giveth joy, and gladness forever." <note>These biblical citations serve to legitimize alchemy as a divine gift rather than a secular or forbidden craft, emphasizing that spiritual preparation is a prerequisite for physical success.</note>

# Preface.

The **Philosophers** <note>In this context, "Philosophers" refers specifically to alchemists, who were often called "natural philosophers."</note> also exhort us (to seek this Art from God) in their writings and books. Among others, **Alanus** <note>Likely referring to Alain de Lille (c. 1128–1202), a French theologian and poet whose works were frequently cited in alchemical literature.</note> speaks with these words:
> Son, set your heart more toward God than toward the Art, for it is a gift from God, and He shares it with whom He will; therefore, have peace and joy in **God**, and so you will have the Art.

Likewise, the excellent King and **Philosopher Geber** <note>Geber is the Latinized name of Jabir ibn Hayyan, an 8th-century polymath considered the father of chemistry; however, early modern texts often cite the "Pseudo-Geber," a 13th-century European author writing under his name.</note> says: 
> Science is a gift of God, given to us through revelation, and breathed into man. Yet it is also explained through instruction and sharp-witted understanding.

**Brother Christian RosenCreutz** <note>The legendary founder of the Rosicrucian Order, a secret spiritual and scientific society.</note> of blessed memory, also exhorts us to the fear of God on the first day of his **Chemical Wedding**, where he says: 
> I had never expected that it would involve such difficult and dangerous **conditions** <note>original: *conditionen*; meaning requirements or stipulations for entry.</note>. For whereas I thought I needed only to appear at the wedding to be a welcome and dear guest, now, however, [divine] providence points me to things of which I was never [certain]—

# Preface.

[...providence pointed the way.] Indeed, so I also found nothing in myself that could help me; therefore, I finally reached for my usual and most certain way and laid myself down in my bed, after completed and zealous **prayer**, to see if my **good angel**, by **divine permission** <note>original: *göttlicher verhengnüß*; refers to the belief that spiritual knowledge is only granted if God allows it.</note>, might appear to me in this doubtful matter—as had happened several times before—and report [on it]. This then happened to the praise of **God**, for my own benefit, and as a faithful and heartfelt warning and improvement for my **neighbor** <note>original: *Nehesten*; referring to one's fellow man or community.</note>.

**In the same place** <note>original: *Ibidem*</note> ) But before I departed from my **little hut**, I first fell upon my knees in my **apparatus** <note>original: *apparat*; here referring not just to tools, but to the seeker's spiritual readiness and ritual equipment.</note> and **wedding garment** <note>original: *Hochzeitkleide*; a symbolic reference to the "Chemical Wedding," representing the purity of soul required to witness the alchemical transformation.</note> and prayed to **God** that, if such a thing were to be, He would let it reach a good end for me. I also made a **vow** before the face of **God**: That, should something be revealed to me through His grace, I would use it neither for the honor nor the reputation of the world, but for the promotion of His name and in service to my fellow men. In such a **vow** and good hope, I departed from my **cell** <note>original: *Cällen*; a monk-like room used for prayer and study.</note> with joy.
