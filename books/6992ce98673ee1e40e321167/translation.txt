## Sudden Fire Sets Forth in Procession
<note>“Sudden Fire” (Xuhuo) refers to a powerful Daoist thunder deity, often depicted as a fiery-tempered spirit who governs rapid atmospheric changes and storms.</note>

When the sun first rises, if there are black clouds shaped like a rooster’s head, or like banners and flags, mountains, a flock of birds, a dragon’s head, a snake, a fish, a <term>lingzhi mushroom</term><note>A symbol of longevity and divinity in Chinese culture</note>, a ceremonial parasol, or a peony flower, it indicates that there will be rain during the **Wei or Shen hours** <note>Between 1:00 PM and 5:00 PM</note> of that same day. If the clouds resemble **fish scales**, it indicates that there will be wind.

## Sudden Fire Meets the Golden Tiger on an Excursion
<note>The "Golden Tiger" likely refers to a celestial omen or a specific symbolic alignment in the metal element, which corresponds to the West and autumn in traditional cosmology.</note>

If the phenomenon appears during the **Si or Wu hours** <note>9:00 AM to 1:00 PM</note>, the effect will manifest at the **Wei hour** <note>1:00 PM to 3:00 PM</note>. If it appears during the **Wei or Shen hours**, it will manifest at the **You hour** <note>5:00 PM to 7:00 PM</note>; this indicates wild winds and sudden torrential rain. 

If it emerges from the <term>Qian</term><note>The Northwest direction in the Eight Trigrams</note> position, there will be executions or military punishments. 

If it enters the <term>Kun</term><note>Southwest</note>, <term>Xun</term><note>Southeast</note>, or <term>Gen</term><note>Northeast</note> positions, there will be no result. 

If it emerges from the <term>Zhen</term><note>East</note> or <term>Kan</term><note>North</note> positions, there will be crashing thunder and lightning. 

If it emerges from or enters the <term>Li</term><note>South</note> or <term>Dui</term><note>West</note> positions, it is a sign of great fortune.

# Calling Fire Unfurling the Banner

When the **Calling Fire** <note>original: "欸火" (*Aihuo*); an atmospheric phenomenon or celestial omen interpreted as a signal from the heavens</note> descends into the **Water Realm** <note>original: "水府" (*Shuifu*); in traditional Chinese cosmology, the divine administrative division governing the oceans and weather</note>, it is said to be "unfurling its banners to issue commands." 

If this omen is observed during the **Shen or You hours** <note>Between 3:00 PM and 7:00 PM</note>, it indicates an immediate response of thunder, rain, heavy mist, and hail. The intensity will be such that dry land will be **submerged beneath waves** <note>original: "陸地沉波" (*ludi chenbo*); a vivid description of flash flooding</note>. 

If the phenomenon is instead sighted at midnight, during the **Zi hour** <note>Between 11:00 PM and 1:00 AM</note>, it foretells earth-shaking thunder and torrential rain on the following day.

## The Appearance of the **Auspicious Bird**

This phenomenon, the **Appearance of the Auspicious Bird** <note>original: "徽鳥" (*Huiniao*); a specific solar or atmospheric phenomenon depicted as a bird-like shape within a solar halo</note>, is also known as the **Red Rat** <note>original: "赤鼠" (*Chishu*)</note>. 

When this omen is seen in the southern sky during the fifth or sixth lunar months, and the **Milky Way** <note>original: "天河" (*Tianhe*); literally "The Heavenly River"</note> is entirely free of clouds at midnight, it is a sign of an **enduring drought** <note>original: "久旱" (*Jiuhan*)</note>. This is a matter of **Natural Law** <note>original: "天數" (*Tianshu*); the predetermined cycles or "numbers" of heaven</note> and cannot be averted by offering prayers.

## The **Golden Tiger** Goes to Battle

When the **Golden Tiger Great Baleful Star** <note>original: "金虎大煞" (*Jinhu Dasha*); "Golden Tiger" corresponds to the element of Metal and the West, while "Great Baleful" refers to a highly inauspicious planetary alignment</note> enters the fray, it signifies that during the **Wei hour** <note>Between 1:00 PM and 3:00 PM</note>, heavy thunder and rain will erupt. 

The **thunderclaps** <note>original: "霹靂" (*Pili*); specifically sharp, sudden cracks of thunder associated with lightning strikes</note> will shake the heavens and earth, striking down evil, snapping trees, and sending sand and stones flying. If this omen occurs in the third lunar month, there will be heavy snow; if there is no snow, there will instead be thunder and **hail** <note>original: "雷雹" (*Leibao*)</note>.
