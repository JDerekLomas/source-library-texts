### THAT SWEDENBORG
<note>Newspaper clipping from the San Francisco Chronicle, 1875. The left margin of the original clipping was damaged, but the text has been restored for readability.</note>

In connection with the city of Stockholm, I must mention a spot of great interest to travelers—the house of Emanuel Swedenborg <note>Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772) was a Swedish scientist, philosopher, and Christian mystic who claimed to have conversations with angels and spirits.</note>. Whether one is a follower or believer in all his teachings or not, he was a man not without honor in his own country. He was looked upon, to my great surprise, as a half-insane man, and hence it is not to be wondered at that this is a neglected, dirty place, which even its nearest neighbors know little or nothing about. 

The house in which he lived is occupied by the poorer classes of people, while his "study" is a small wooden house in the garden, and this is unoccupied. It is empty, though kept tolerably clean. One is fortunate if, after many inquiries in the immediate neighborhood, he finds anyone to show or direct him to the spot. It is in a kind of back-yard partitioned off from a common court, surrounded by buildings, and it is only accessible through one of these houses. 

The front of the little garden-house presents a few steps from the ground, with a window with outside wooden shutters on each side of the floor. A low <gloss>attic room</gloss> under the slanting roof is shown as the place where, in his inspired moods, he often passed the night. The doorway is shaded by trees, one of which is said to have been planted by his own hand. Every visitor is allowed to break off or cut a memento from a beam inside the house, and to carry away as many leaves from the trees as he chooses; nature sends a fresh supply of leaves every year. It seems to me that some of Swedenborg's wealthy followers in our own country might well do something toward the preservation and cleanly maintenance of this spot, which should be almost sacred to them. — *Stockholm Correspondent for the San Francisco Chronicle.* **1875.**
