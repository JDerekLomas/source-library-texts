Everything passes away; everything here below has an end; God alone is eternal and unchanging. Principalities, kingdoms, and great fortunes—all are subject to change. Yet, commonly, these changes occur only imperceptibly. One sees them in advance from the incidents that announce them and which precede them. Never has one occurred so swiftly and so suddenly as the fall of the Order of the Knights Templar <term>Tempelherren-Orden: A wealthy and powerful medieval Catholic military order suppressed in the early 14th century</term>. In a single day, in the same hour, they were destroyed; and this was a tragic proof of the terrible consequences that the hatred and wrath of kings can bring about. They—these likenesses of the Most High Lord <note>A reference to the "Divine Right of Kings," the belief that monarchs derive their authority directly from God</note>—are sometimes, like Him, creators and destroyers on earth.

Such was the fate of the Order of the Knights Templar, the history of whose dissolution we now undertake to write.
