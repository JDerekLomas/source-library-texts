# CLASSIFICATION AND EXPLANATION OF THE FIGURES
## WHICH ARE FOUND IN THE 1st VOLUME (DOGMA).

**FIRST FIGURE.** The Great Symbol of Solomon. . . . **Frontispiece**

The double triangle of Solomon, represented by the two old men of the Kabbalah <note>original: "cabale"</note>; the Macroprosopus <note>The "Great Countenance" or the vast face of God in Kabbalistic thought</note> and the Microprosopus <note>The "Lesser Countenance," representing the manifest aspects of the divine</note>; the God of light and the God of reflections; the merciful and the avenger; the white Jehovah and the black Jehovah.
The small figures on both sides are analogous to the main subject.

**2nd FIG.** Priestly Esotericism formulating reprobation. . . **103**

A priestly hand making the sign of esotericism and projecting the figure of the demon in its shadow. Above, one sees the Ace of Pentacles <note>original: "deniers"</note> from the Chinese Tarot <note>original: "Tharot"</note> and two superimposed triangles, one white and one black. This is a new allegory explaining the same mysteries; it is the origin of good and evil; it is the creation of the demon through mystery.

**7th FIG.** The Pentacles of Ezekiel and Pythagoras. . . . . . . **333**

The four-headed cherub from the prophecy of Ezekiel, explained by the double triangle of Solomon.
Below, the wheel of Ezekiel, the key to all pentacles <note>original: "pantacles"</note>, and the pentacle of Pythagoras.
The cherub of Ezekiel is represented here as the prophet describes it. Its four heads are the quaternary of the Merkabah <note>original: "Mercavah"; the "Chariot" or throne of God in Jewish mysticism</note>; its six wings are the senary <note>a group of six</note> of Bereshit <note>original: "Bereschit"; the first word of Genesis, meaning "In the beginning"</note>. The human figure in the middle represents reason; the eagle's head is belief; the ox is resignation and labor; the lion is struggle and conquest. This symbol is analogous to that of the Egyptian sphinx, but it is more appropriate to the Kabbalah of the Hebrews.

**8th FIG.** Ardhanarishvara <note>original: "Addha-Nari"</note>, the great Indian Pentacle. . . . . . . . . . **366 .**

This pantheistic image represents Religion or Truth, terrible for the uninitiated <note>original: "profanes"</note> and gentle for the initiates. This figure has more than one analogy with the cherub of Ezekiel. The human figure is placed between a bridled calf and a tiger, which forms the triangle of Kether <note>The Crown</note>, Geburah <note>Severity/Strength</note>, and Gedulah or Chesed <note>Greatness or Mercy</note>. In the Indian symbol, one finds the four magic signs of the Tarot in the four hands of Ardhanarishvara.

**1^st FIG. Goat of the Sabbath. — Baphomet and Mendes. . . Frontispiece.**
A pantheistic and magical figure of the absolute. The torch placed between the two horns represents the balancing intelligence of the triad; the head of the goat, a synthetic head which unites certain characteristics of the dog, the bull, and the donkey, represents the responsibility of matter alone and the expiation, within bodies, of bodily sins. The hands are human, to show the sanctity of labor; they make the sign of esotericism above and below, to recommend mystery to the initiates, and they point to two lunar crescents, one white which is above, the other black, which is below, to explain the relationship between good and evil, mercy and justice. The lower part of the body is veiled, an image of the mysteries of universal generation, expressed only by the symbol of the <term>caduceus</term> <note>the staff of Hermes, featuring two snakes winding around a winged rod</note>. The belly of the goat is scaled, and must be colored green; the semi-circle above it must be blue; the feathers, which rise to the chest, must be of various colors. This trident, a figure of the ternary <note>The number three or a threefold principle.</note>, is formed of three pyramidal teeth superimposed on a Greek or Latin Tau <note>A T-shaped cross.</note>. On one of the teeth one sees a *Yod* <note>original: "jod" — the tenth letter of the Hebrew alphabet, often symbolizing the creative spark.</note> crossing a crescent on one side, and on the other a transverse line, a figure which recalls hieroglyphically the zodiacal sign of Cancer <note>original: "l'écrevisse" (the crayfish).</note>. On the opposite tooth is a mixed sign recalling that of Gemini and that of Leo. Between the claws of the crayfish one sees the sun, and near the lion the astronomical cross. On the middle tooth is traced hieroglyphically the figure of the celestial serpent, having for its head the sign of Jupiter. On the side of the crayfish one reads the word **OBITO**, go away, or retreat; and on the side of the lion one reads **IMO**, even so, or persist. In the center and near the symbolic serpent one reads **AP DO SEL**, a word composed of an abbreviation, a word composed cabalistically and in Hebrew, and finally a common, whole word: **AP**, which must be read as **AR**, because these are the first two Greek letters of the word **ARCHAEUS** <note>The vital life force in Paracelsian alchemy.</note>; **DO**, which must be read as **OD** <note>A term for a hypothetical vital energy or "Odic force."</note> and **SEL** <note>Salt, one of the three alchemical primes.</note>. These are the three primary substances, and the occult names of Archaeus and Od express the same things as the sulfur and mercury of the philosophers. On the iron stem which serves to handle the trident one sees the letter **P. P. P.** three times, a phalloid and lingamic <note>Relating to the Lingam, a symbol of generative power.</note> hieroglyph; then the words **VLI DOX FATO**, which must be read by taking the first letter as the number of the pentagram in Roman numerals <note>V = 5</note>, and thus completing it as **PENTAGRAMMATICA LIBERTATE DOXA FATO** <note>Latin: "By Pentagrammatic Liberty, Glory to Fate."</note>, a character equivalent to the three letters of Cagliostro **L. P. D.**: liberty, power, duty. On one side, absolute liberty; on the other, necessity or invincible fatality; in the middle, **REASON**, the cabalistic absolute which creates universal equilibrium. This admirable magical summary by Paracelsus can serve as a key to the obscure works of the cabalist Wronski, a remarkable scholar who allowed himself to be led away more than once from his **ABSOLUTE REASON** by the mysticism of his nation and by pecuniary speculations unworthy of such a distinguished thinker. We nonetheless render him the honor and glory of having discovered before us the secret of the trident of Paracelsus. Thus Paracelsus represents the passive by the crayfish, the active by the lion, the intelligence or balancing reason by Jupiter or the king-man dominating the serpent; then he balances the forces by giving to the passive the fertilization of the active figured by the sun, and to the active the space and the night to be conquered and enlightened under the symbol of the cross. He says to the passive: Obey the impulse of the active, and walk with it by the very equilibrium of resistance. He says to the active: Resist the immobility of the obstacle; persist and advance. Then he explains these alternating forces by the great central ternary: **LIBERTY**, **NECESSITY**, **REASON**. **REASON** at the center; **LIBERTY** and **NECESSITY** as counterweights. # PRELIMINARY DISCOURSE.

## ON THE RELIGIOUS,
## PHILOSOPHICAL, AND MORAL TENDENCIES
## OF OUR BOOKS ON MAGIC.

---

Since the first edition of this book was published, great events have been accomplished in the world, and others, perhaps even greater, are on the eve of being fulfilled.

These events had been announced to us, as is customary, by wonders <note>original: "prodiges"; refers to supernatural omens or signs believed to precede major historical shifts</note>: the tables had spoken, voices had issued from the walls, and bodiless hands had written mysterious words, as at the feast of Belshazzar <note>A reference to the biblical story in the Book of Daniel, where a supernatural hand writes on a wall to prophesy the fall of Babylon</note>.

Fanaticism, in the final convulsions of its death throes, has given the signal for that final persecution of Christians foretold by all the prophets. More than ever, science and religion, despotism and liberty, seem to engage in a fierce war and swear an irreconcilable hatred. Do not, however, believe these bloody appearances: they are on the eve of uniting and embracing forever.

The discovery of the great secrets of religion and of the primitive science of the Magi <term>Magi: the ancient Zoroastrian priest-sages, from whose name the word "magic" is derived</term>, by revealing to the world the unity of universal dogma, annihilates fanaticism by providing the reason behind wonders.
