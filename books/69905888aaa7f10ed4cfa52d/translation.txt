...really mean by the livingness which we attribute to spirit and the deadness which we attribute to matter.

At first, we may be disposed to say that livingness consists in the power of motion and deadness in its absence; but a little inquiry into the most recent researches of science will soon show us that this distinction does not go deep enough. It is now one of the fully established facts of physical science that no atom of what we call "dead matter" is without motion. On the table before me lies a solid lump of steel, but in the light of up-to-date science, I know that the atoms of that seemingly inert mass are vibrating with the most intense energy. They are continually dashing hither and thither, impinging upon and rebounding from one another, or circling round like miniature solar systems, with a ceaseless rapidity whose complex activity is enough to bewilder the imagination. 

The mass, as a mass, may lie inert upon the table; but so far from being destitute of the element of motion, it is the abode of the never-tiring energy moving the particles with a swiftness to which the speed of an express train is as nothing. It is, therefore, not the mere fact of motion that is at the root of the distinction which we draw instinctively between spirit and matter; we must go deeper than that. # *Spirit and Matter*

There can be no conception of anything as being at a distance from us. When the elements of time and space are eliminated, all our ideas of things must necessarily be understood as existing in a universal "here" and an everlasting "now." This is, no doubt, a highly abstract conception, but I would ask the student to endeavor to grasp it thoroughly, since it is of vital importance in the practical application of Mental Science, as will appear further on.

The opposite conception is that of things expressing themselves through conditions of time and space, and thus establishing a variety of relations to other things—such as size, distance, and direction, or the sequence of events in time. These two conceptions are respectively the conception of the abstract and the concrete, of the unconditioned and the conditioned, of the absolute and the relative. 

They are not opposed to each other in the sense of being incompatible; rather, each is the complement of the other, and the only reality is found in the combination of the two. The error of the extreme idealist <note>One who believes that reality is purely mental or spiritual.</note> is in endeavoring to realize the absolute without the relative, and the error of the extreme materialist <note>One who believes that nothing exists except matter and its movements.</note> is in endeavoring to realize the relative without the absolute. On the one side, the mistake is in trying to realize an "inside" without an "outside," and on the other, in trying to realize an "outside" without an "inside." Both are necessary to the formation of a **substantial entity.**

## II.

## THE HIGHER MODE OF INTELLIGENCE CONTROLS THE LOWER.

We have seen that the descent from personality—as we know it in ourselves—to matter—as we know it in what we call inanimate forms—is a gradual descent in the scale of intelligence. It moves from that mode of being which is able to realize its own will-power as a capacity for originating new chains of cause and effect <note>original: "trains of causation"</note>, to that mode of being which is incapable of recognizing itself at all. The higher the grade of life, the higher the intelligence; from which it follows that the supreme principle of Life must also be the ultimate principle of intelligence. 

This is clearly demonstrated by the grand natural order of the universe. In the light of modern science, the principle of evolution is familiar to us all, and the accurate adjustment existing between all parts of the cosmic scheme is too self-evident to need emphasizing. Every advance in science consists of discovering new, subtle connections in this magnificent universal order—an order which already exists and only requires our recognition to bring it into practical use. # The Higher Mode of Intelligence.

We cannot avoid the conclusion that a paramount intelligence must be inherent in the Life-Principle, which manifests itself *as* this order; and thus we see that there must be a great cosmic intelligence underlying the totality of things.

The physical history of our planet shows us first an incandescent nebula <note>A cloud of gas and dust in outer space.</note> dispersed over vast infinitudes of space. Later, this condenses into a central sun surrounded by a family of glowing planets, which at that stage were hardly yet solidified from the plastic primordial matter <note>The fundamental, original substance from which all things are created.</note>. Then follow untold millenniums of slow geological formation: an earth peopled by the lowest forms of life, whether vegetable or animal. From these crude beginnings, a majestic, unceasing, and unhurried forward movement brings things stage by stage to the condition in which we know them now. 

Looking at this steady progression, it is clear that, however we may conceive the nature of the evolutionary principle, it unerringly provides for the continual advance of the race. But it does this by creating such numbers of each species that, after allowing a wide margin for all possible accidents to individuals, the race shall still continue:

> "So careful of the type it seems,

But the progress toward higher intelligence is always in the direction of narrowing down this margin of accident. It involves taking the individual more and more out of the "law of averages" and substituting the law of individual selection. In ordinary scientific language, this is the "survival of the fittest." 

The reproduction of fish occurs on a scale that would choke the sea with them if every individual survived; however, the margin of destruction is correspondingly enormous. Thus, the law of averages simply maintains the normal population of the species. But at the other end of the scale <note>referring to the evolutionary scale, specifically human beings</note>, reproduction is by no means so enormously in excess of survival. 

It is true that there is an ample margin of accident and disease cutting off numbers of human beings before they have lived the average duration of life; nevertheless, it is on a very different scale from the premature destruction of hundreds of thousands compared to the survival of just one. It may, therefore, be taken as an established fact that as intelligence advances, the individual ceases to be subject to a mere law of averages. Instead, the individual gains a continually increasing power of controlling the conditions of his own survival.

We see, therefore, that there is a marked distinction between the **cosmic intelligence** <note>the universal or collective mind</note> and the **individual intelligence**. The factor that differentiates the latter from the former is the presence of *individual volition* <note>the faculty of using one's will to make choices</note>. Now, the business of Mental Science—

# *The Higher Mode of Intelligence*

The goal is to determine the relationship between this individual power of volition <note>original: "volition"; the faculty or power of using one's will</note> and the great cosmic law that provides for the maintenance and advancement of the human race. The point to be carefully noted is that the power of individual will is itself the result of the cosmic evolutionary principle at the point where it reaches its highest level. 

The effort of Nature has always been upward from the time when only the lowest forms of life inhabited the globe, and it has now reached its peak in the production of a being with a mind capable of abstract reasoning and a brain fitted to be the physical instrument of such a mind. At this stage, the all-creating Life-principle reproduces itself in a form capable of recognizing the working of the evolutionary law. 

The unity and continuity of purpose running through the entire progression until now indicates, beyond a doubt, that the place of such a being in the universal scheme must be to introduce the operation of that factor which, up to this point, has been noticeably absent—namely, the factor of intelligent individual will. 

The evolution which has brought us to this standpoint has worked by a cosmic law of averages; it has been a process in which the individual himself has not taken a conscious part. But because man is what he is, and leads the front of the evolutionary procession, if he is to evolve further, it can now only be by his own conscious cooperation with the law which has brought him to this point.

# XII.

## THE WILL.

The Will is of such primary importance that the student should be on his guard against any mistake regarding the position it holds in the mental economy <note>original: "mental economy"; the functional organization of the mind</note>. Many writers and teachers insist on will-power as though it were the creative faculty. No doubt intense will-power can produce certain external results, but like all other methods of compulsion, it lacks the permanency of natural growth. 

The appearances, forms, and conditions produced by mere intensity of will-power will only stay together as long as the compelling force continues. However, if that force is exhausted or withdrawn, the elements that were forced into an unnatural combination will at once fly back to their proper affinities <note>original: "affinities"; natural attractions or inherent relationships between elements</note>. The form created by compulsion never had the germ of vitality within itself and is therefore dissipated as soon as the external energy which supported it is withdrawn.

What we call our creations are actually new combinations of already existing material, whether mental or physical <note>original: "corporeal"</note>. This is amply demonstrated in the physical sciences. No one speaks of creating energy, but only of transforming one form of energy into another. If we realize this as a universal principle, we shall see that on the mental plane, as well as on the physical, we never create energy. We only provide the conditions by which the energy already existing in one mode can express itself in another. 

Therefore, what we call man's "creative power" is actually a receptive attitude of expectancy. This attitude, so to speak, creates a mold into which the plastic and as-yet-undifferentiated substance can flow and take the desired form. 

The will occupies much the same place in our mental machinery as the tool-holder does in a power-lathe <note>A power-lathe is a machine tool used for shaping metal or wood; the tool-holder keeps the cutting bit steady against the rotating material.</note>. If we say, using the word in its widest sense, that the imagination is the creative function, we may call the will the centralizing principle. Its function is to keep the imagination centered in the right direction.
