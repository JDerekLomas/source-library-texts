# THE FIRST OF THE FIFTEEN BOOKS OF THE **PREPARATION FOR THE GOSPEL** BY EUSEBIUS OF PAMPHILUS.
### What the promise of the Gospel's subject matter is.

<image-desc>A large ornamental drop cap 'T' within a square frame decorated with dense floral and vine patterns.</image-desc>

THINKING it necessary to present what Christianity really is to those who are unaware of it, I promise that the present treatise will encompass the **Evangelical Demonstration** <note>original: "εὐαγγελικὴν ἀπόδειξιν" – This refers to Eusebius's companion work, the *Demonstratio Evangelica*, which followed this "Preparation."</note>. This work I address to you, Theodotus <note>Theodotus was the Bishop of Laodicea and a friend of Eusebius to whom the work is dedicated.</note>, a man beloved of God and a sacred leader among bishops, with my prayers. I do so in the hope that I might obtain assistance from your god-loving service, which will greatly contribute to the subject I have set forth concerning the teaching of the Gospel. 

However, it is first good to clarify what this "radiant Gospel" <note>original: "φαιδρὸν εὐαγγέλιον"</note> intends to reveal. To all people, it brings good news of the highest and greatest blessings—blessings that were proclaimed long ago but have recently shone forth upon all. It does not concern itself with "blind" wealth <note>original: "οὐ τὸν οὐ βλέποντα πλοῦτον" – A reference to the Greek god of wealth, Plutus, who was traditionally depicted as blind.</note>, nor with this brief and suffering-filled life, nor with things belonging to the body and its decay. Instead, it offers what is dear and fitting to souls that possess a rational nature <note>original: "νοεράν οὐσίαν" – literally "intellectual essence."</note>, things to which the affairs of this world are attached like a following shadow. 

The essence of these blessings is Piety <term>eusebeia: a Greek term for religious devotion, godliness, or the proper way to honor the divine</term>—not the kind that is falsely named and full of error, but the kind that inscribes true contemplation into the soul. This is the turning of the mind toward the one and only truly acknowledged God, and a life lived according to His will. From this arises a friendship with Him, followed by a truly god-beloved and thrice-blessed end, which hangs down from the highest power above, is guided toward it, and finally comes to rest within it. 

What could be more blessed than this virtuous and prosperous friendship with God? Is He not Himself the steward and provider of life, light, truth, and all good things to all? Does He not encompass the cause of the existence and life of all things? What could still be lacking for the person who has sought friendship with Him? What could the person lack who is made at home with the Creator of all good things? What could such a person not achieve, who claims the Great Protector and King of All as both a father and a guardian? 

Indeed, one cannot say otherwise: the person who draws near to the King of All through the Divine Word, and who has been deemed worthy of this all-blessed friendship through a knowledgeable piety, will find that all things—soul, body, and external circumstances—are well. This good and saving friendship between God and men was sent down from the all-good God by the **Divine Word** <term>Logos: "The Word" or "Divine Reason," referring here to the second person of the Trinity through whom Christians believe God communicates with humanity</term>, like a boundless...

What the promise of the evangelical <term>subject</term> <note>original: "ὑποθέσεως"; referring to the overall thesis or purpose of the Gospel message.</note> is.
What our custom is to say to those who attempt to slander our affairs.
That we have not chosen to hold the opinions of the <term>saving word</term> <note>original: "σωτηρίου λόγου"; a reference to the Christian message or Logos.</note> without examination.
That our faith concerning the most beneficial things is not taken up at an opportune time without critical judgment.
That it was not with an irrational state of mind that we withdrew from our <term>ancestral, superstitious error</term> <note>original: "πατρίου δεισιδαίμονος πλάνης"; Eusebius is referring to the Greek and Roman paganism of his ancestors.</note>.
How much they previously believed, and what sort of things they record concerning the first <term>creation of the world</term> <note>original: "κοσμοποιΐας"</note>, and how reasonably we withdrew from them.
How much the natural philosophers have disagreed concerning first principles, and how we have departed from these after careful judgment.
That the ancients did nothing more than <term>theologize</term> <note>original: "ἐθεολόγουν"; here meaning "to treat as gods" or "worship as divine."</note> about the stars appearing in the sky.

That the opinions and legends of the ancients regarding gods were introduced later into life.
