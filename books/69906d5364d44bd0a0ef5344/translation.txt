## **Moonlight of the Planets: The Auxiliary Tables** <note>original: "Graha-kaumudyam-upakaranani." This is a manual for calculating planetary positions.</note>

**Salutations to the Auspicious One.** <note>original: "Shrih." A standard invocation at the beginning of a Sanskrit text.</note>

Now begin the auxiliary tables in the **Moonlight of the Planets**. From the current **Shaka year** <note>The Shaka Era is a traditional Indian calendar system starting in 78 CE.</note>, subtract the base year of the era <note>The text likely refers to subtracting an epochal year to find the "elapsed years" for calculation.</note>. Use the specific **multipliers** <term>gunakaih: numerical factors used in astronomical equations</term> in their proper order, and add the **epochal constants** <term>kshepakaih: fixed values added to a calculation to account for the planet's position at the start of a specific era</term>. By doing so, the positions of the planets—beginning with the **Sun**—at the end of the day can be accurately determined. 1.

The multiplier for the **Sun** <note>original: "Bhanu."</note> is thirty <note>original: "kharaamah." Using the Bhuta-sankhya system where 'kha' (void) is 0 and 'rama' is 3, read right-to-left as 30.</note>. For **Mars** <note>original: "Kuja."</note>, the multipliers and constants are determined by the lunar days. 2. For **Rahu** <note>The ascending node of the Moon, treated as a "shadow planet" in Indian astronomy.</note>, the values are [omitted/unclear in transcription]. 3.

### **Planetary Parameters and Constants**

| Planet | Multiplier/Constant Values <note>These numbers are encoded using symbolic words (e.g., 'Veda' for 4, 'Agni' for 3).</note> |
|:---|:---|
| **Sun** | 30 |
| **Mars** | Calculated through the "Veda" (4) and "Agni" (3) factors |
| **Mercury** | Derived from the "Veda" (4) and "Anga" (6) parameters |
| **Venus** | Associated with the "Shashi" (1) and "Vidhu" (1) cycles |
| **Saturn** | Calculated using the "Shriti" (5) and "Ishvo" (5) constants |

The **epochal constants** <note>original: "kshepaka."</note> for the lords of the days—starting with the **Sun** <note>original: "Mihira."</note>—are to be set in their respective positions. For **Mars**, the value of four-thousand and fifty is used. For **Mercury**, the multipliers are derived from the "Rasa" (6) and "Guna" (3) values. 5.

The calculation continues with **Saturn** <note>original: "Suta."</note> and the remaining heavenly bodies, ensuring that the **planetary masses** <term>pinda: a cumulative total or "lump" sum used in recursive astronomical formulas</term> are correctly aligned with the observed heavens.

### **The Correction of Planetary Motion**

The movement of **Mars** <note>original: "Mahija," literally 'Born of the Earth.'</note> and **Mercury** <note>original: "Budha."</note> should be considered with their respective increments. 6. Within the span of a **Manu-day** <term>manu-dina: a period of 14 days used as a cycle in these calculations</term>, the results are determined by the **corrected daily motion** <term>sphuta-gati: the actual, non-average speed of a planet as seen from Earth</term>.

At the city of **Kanchipuram** <note>A famous historical center of learning in South India, often used as a prime meridian or reference point in regional astronomical texts.</note>, the positions of the **Sun**, **Jupiter**, **Venus**, and **Saturn** are established. 7.

### **Epochal Constants** <term>kshepakas: specific numerical values added to an equation to account for a planet's position at the start of a calculation era</term>

| Planet | Constant Values (Degrees, Minutes, Seconds) |
|:---|:---|
| **Sun** <note>original: "Arka."</note> | 5 | 10 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| **Mars** <note>original: "Ku."</note> | 1 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| **Mercury** <note>original: "Budha."</note> | 14 | 4 | 3 |
| **Jupiter** <note>original: "Guru."</note> | 8 | 5 | 4 |
| **Venus** <note>original: "Shukra."</note> | 5 | 5 | 6 |
| **Saturn** <note>original: "Shani."</note> | 2 | 5 | 4 |
| **Rahu** | 1 | 5 | 4 |
| **Moon** <note>original: "Shanka."</note> | 4 |

The difference in these values is calculated by multiplying the specific planetary factors and dividing by sixty. These should be refined in their proper sequence. 8. By using these individual differences, the **retrograde motion** <term>vakra: the apparent backward motion of a planet in the sky</term> and the primary speeds are determined. If the motion is increasing, it is added; otherwise, perform the inverse calculation. 9.

Through the interval of the **Manu-days**, the fixed and **true motion** is found. By multiplying by the era cycles and dividing by the appropriate factors, the sum is either increased or decreased. 10. The true speed and the path of the **retrograde** motion are determined through the calculation of these intervals, which provides the reasoning for the planetary results.

### **Calculations for the Five Planets**

11. From the conjunctions of the **Mars-sentences** <note>original: "Bhauma-vakya." These are mnemonic sentences used to memorize planetary positions over long cycles.</note>, the slow-moving constants are derived through regular rules. This applies to the son of the **Sun** <note>Saturn</note> and the son of the **Moon** <note>Mercury</note>. 12.

**Thus concludes the manual titled "The Wonder of the Planets."** <note>original: "Grahakautuka Karana." A 'Karana' is a concise astronomical handbook designed for practical calculation.</note>

For the latitude of the observer, calculate the interval. These values are added to the position starting from **Aries** <note>original: "Mesha," the first sign of the zodiac.</note> based on the days elapsed. 13.

Take the **Shaka year** <note>The Shaka era began in 78 CE.</note> and add twelve. Multiply and divide as previously instructed to find the remainder. This will produce the results corresponding to the names of the planets. The division results in "degrees" <term>bhaga: 1/360th of a circle</term>, and the multiplications are the "factors." 14.
