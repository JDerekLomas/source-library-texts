### The Official of Atmospheric Divination <note>original: *Shijin Shi* (眡祲氏); a classical title for an official who interprets solar and atmospheric phenomena</note> oversees the method of the **Ten Vapors** <note>original: *Shihui* (十煇); often translated as the "Ten Halos" or "Ten Radiations"</note> to observe calamitous and auspicious omens and to distinguish between good and bad fortune.

**The first is called Incursion** <note>original: *Jin* (祲)</note>, which refers to the vapors of *Yin* and *Yang* and the five colors encroaching upon one another. Some say it refers to types of "hugging" arcs, "ear" halos, and "back-turning" jade-ring arcs <note>These terms refer to complex parhelia (sun dogs) and atmospheric refraction patterns</note>, appearing like rainbows but shorter.

**The second is called Imagery** <note>original: *Xiang* (象)</note>, which refers to clouds or vapors taking on a distinct shape. An example is when red clouds appear like birds flanking the sun as if in flight. It is also said that when red clouds flank the sun as it moves, it is an "image of the sun" <note>meaning the sun’s appearance reflects the state of the ruler</note> signifying that treacherous ministers are flanking the sovereign and throwing the state into chaos.

**The third is called The Chisel** <note>original: *Juan* (鐫)</note>. This occurs when vapors at the side of the sun "pierce" or "stab" it. Its shape is like the small chisel ornament worn by young boys.

**The fourth is called Oversight** <note>original: *Jian* (監)</note>. This refers to cloud-vapors that descend upon or sit directly atop the sun.

**The fifth is called Darkness** <note>original: *An* (闇)</note>. This refers to eclipses of the sun or moon, or when the solar light becomes dim and shadowed.

**The sixth is called Obscurity** <note>original: *Meng* (瞢)</note>. This refers to a hazy or misty state where the sunlight is not bright or clear.

**The seventh is called Pervasiveness** <note>original: *Mi* (彌)</note>. This refers to a "white rainbow" <note>A rare atmospheric phenomenon often interpreted as a sign of impending assassination or military aggression</note> that stretches across the sky and pierces through the sun.

**The eighth is called Order** <note>original: *Xu* (序)</note>. This refers to vapors appearing like mountains above the sun. Others say it refers to the layering of "crowns," "ears," and "back-turning" jade-ring arcs appearing in an overlapping sequence at the side of the sun.

**The ninth is called Ascension** <note>original: *Ji* (躋)</note>, which refers to halo vapors. Some say it refers to a rainbow. It is what the *Classic of Poetry* <note>original: *Shijing* (詩經)</note> means by "the morning rainbow ascending in the west."

*   **Green** signifies famine.
*   **Red** signifies war.
*   **White** signifies mourning.
*   **Black** signifies worry.
*   **Yellow** signifies a ripe harvest.

It is also said that "Reflection" refers to the process of contemplation. When red vapors take the shape of humans or beasts, one can contemplate and reflect <note>original: *si* (思)</note> upon them to determine whether they represent good or bad fortune.

Furthermore, it is said: Vapors at the side of the sun are called **Back-turning** <note>original: *Bei* (背)</note>; their shape resembles the character for "back" <note>This refers to an arc that curves away from the sun, likely a circumzenithal or supralateral arc</note>. A vapor shaped like red **Man-jade** <note>original: *Man* (璊)</note> is called **The Red Gem**. It is also written as **The Chisel** <note>original: *Juan* (鑴); a variant of the term used on the previous page</note> and is also known as **Cave Vapor** <note>original: *Xueqi* (穴氣)</note>.

Vapor that curves toward the sun is called an **Embrace** <note>original: *Bao* (抱)</note>. A shape appearing as a black dot is called an **Ear Pendant** <note>original: *Er* (珥); while usually referring to sun dogs, here it is specified as a dark point or prominence</note>.

Vapor located above the sun is called a **Cap** <note>original: *Guan* (冠)</note> or a **Crown** <note>original: *Dai* (戴)</note>. When it is on the side and directly opposite, it is an **Ear Pendant**.

If the vapor is like an "ox ring" <note>a circular nose-ring for livestock</note>:
*   Curving toward the sun, it is an **Embrace**.
*   Curving outward, it is **Back-turning**.

If there is vapor that "stabs" or "pierces" the sun, it is **The Chisel**. The Chisel signifies "assisting in an injury" <note>original: *fushang* (扶傷)</note>.

Regarding the **Solar Crown** <note>original: *Ridai* (日戴)</note>: its shape is straight but slightly raised at the top. When it sits atop the sun, it is the Crown. The Crown represents **Virtue** <note>original: *De* (德)</note>.

<page-num>Sixteen</page-num>

***

If a **sun halo** <term>sun halo: "ri yun," an atmospheric ring around the sun, interpreted in Chinese astrology as a mirror of the imperial court's stability</term> contains **red vapor** <note>original: "chi qi," often associated with fire, blood, and martial energy</note> that resembles four leopards intersecting within the sun, it signifies a grave misfortune for the ruler of the Central Kingdom.

***

If there is an opening at the top of a **sun halo** and **red vapor** appears below it, it foretells a great victory in military operations. The generals and commanders involved will be rewarded with the rank of marquis.

***

If a **black snake** appears to pierce through the sun within a **sun halo**, the monarch will face great hardships. This omen warns that high-ranking ministers are plotting, soldiers will turn on one another in mutual slaughter, and bandits will rise up across the land.

***

If a **sun halo** is supported from below by **yellow vapor** <note>original: "huang qi," yellow being the color of the earth and the central imperial authority</note>, the state will successfully acquire new territory before the year is out.

***

If the sun is surrounded by a **purple vapor halo**, the commander of an army currently besieging a city will suffer significant losses. In such a case, the city cannot yet be taken; it is advisable to keep the fortifications closed and delay the assault until the following day.

***

If a **sun halo** contains **five-colored clouds** <note>original: "wu se yun," representing the harmonious or conflicting presence of the Five Elements</note> shaped like a **pestle** <term>pestle: "chu," a heavy, blunt tool used for pounding, here representing a striking force</term> piercing the sun:
* If the vapor enters from the outside, the "outsiders" <note>invading forces or rebels</note> will be victorious.
* If the vapor emerges from the inside, the "insiders" <note>the defending or imperial forces</note> will be victorious.
* If there is no active military campaign in the field, this omen signifies the movement of troops or the existence of a conspiracy among the people.

# Treatise on Armament Technology, Volume 151

## Records of Divination: Solar Omens, Part III

<page-num>Seventeen</page-num>

***

If a **sun halo** <term>sun halo: "ri yun," an atmospheric ring around the sun, interpreted as a sign of heavenly influence over earthly affairs</term> is accompanied by **blue-black clouds and vapor** that move to obscure the sun, it is an omen that enemy raiders will come to **storm the camp** <term>storm the camp: "zhuo ying," literally to hack or chop at the military encampment in a surprise raid</term>. If the army is prepared in advance, they will achieve victory.

***

If a **red and yellow secondary rainbow** <note>original: "ni," the dimmer, outer arc of a double rainbow, often viewed as a distinct and more malevolent omen than the primary arc</note> enters the center of the sun during a **halo** event, a great battle is imminent. The direction from which the rainbow enters indicates the point from which the most lethal military danger will emerge.

***

If the sun displays a **blue and red halo** but lacks its usual brilliance, and there are **arcs and rings** <term>arcs and rings: "bei qiong," specific optical phenomena where "bei" are arcs curving away from the sun and "qiong" are bright, jewel-like spots or rings</term> appearing within the **Mansion of the Heart** <note>original: "Xin du," the fifth lunar mansion, traditionally associated with the Emperor’s person and the internal state of the realm</note>, this is called the **Great Turmoil**. This portends certain massive warfare, a great state funeral, and disaster for the kingdom.

***

If a **sun halo** has gathered clouds at its sides, the colors of these clouds determine the omen:
*   **Yellow and white** clouds signify good fortune.
*   **Blue and white** clouds signify the movement of troops.
*   **Black and white** clouds signify **internal rebellion**.
*   **Blue and red** clouds signify the **disbanding of the army** <note>original: "bing jie," which can imply either the peaceful resolution of a conflict or the scattering of defeated forces</note>.
*   **Blue and black** clouds signify military combat and the shedding of blood.

***

If a **sun halo** contains red, black, and white clouds, the situation will remain stable and peaceful. If **purple and yellow** are consistently present, it foretells a great victory. However, a **blue halo** signifies that half the land will suffer calamity.

***

If a **halo** and a **rainbow** appear together at sunrise, the ruler of the land will be forced to partition the territory. If a **halo** and a **rainbow** appear at sunset, it is the feudal lords who will divide the land among themselves.

## Records of Divination: Divining the Sun, Part II

<page-num>Seventeen</page-num>

***

If a **sun halo** <term>sun halo: "ri yun," an optical phenomenon where a ring forms around the sun, interpreted by ancient strategists as a boundary or defensive wall</term> contains a rainbow that pierces the halo but does not reach the sun itself, then in battle, if you attack from the direction where the piercing occurred, you will be victorious. However, the gains will be small.

If a rainbow pierces through the halo and extends outside of it, you should attack from the point where the rainbow emerges. By following the direction of the rainbow to strike, you will achieve victory.

If a sun halo has a single rainbow piercing the interior of the halo, the side that attacks following the path of that rainbow will win and **slay the enemy general**.

If there is a single-layered sun halo and a **rainbow or secondary rainbow** <term>secondary rainbow: "ni," the dimmer, outer arc of a double rainbow, often associated with secondary or hidden influences</term> passes directly through the sun, emerging from both sides, it is a sign that the general will **abandon the camp**.

If a sun halo is accompanied by two secondary rainbows that seem to "embrace" the sun, the army should be on high alert and prepared for an engagement.

If within a sun halo there are four or five **"disorderly snakes"** <note>original: "luan she," likely referring to sinuous, twisted bands of vapor or light</note> that pierce and pass through the sun, it signifies that the ruler and his ministers are deceiving one another. Rebellious troops will arise, and the world will fall into great chaos.

If the halo contains a cloud that is perfectly round, resembling the shape of a mouth, it indicates there are conspirators planning a rebellion among the **inner officials or soldiers** <note>original: "nei chen bing," referring to those close to the seat of power or within the commander's inner circle</note>.

If a sun halo contains vapor shaped like a **person standing within the ring**, enemy bandits will enter the city within three days.

***

If a **sun halo** <term>sun halo: "ri yun," an atmospheric phenomenon where ice crystals create a ring around the sun, interpreted here as a celestial mirror of earthly politics</term> contains a cloud shaped like a person residing within the halo and facing toward the sun, it signifies that a **loyal minister** will receive an imperial mandate and an **imperial envoy** <note>original: "chishi," an official messenger carrying a decree from the Emperor</note> shall return. Upon the envoy's return, if the halo displays **auspicious colors** <term>auspicious colors: "wang se," literally "kingly colors," referring to the vibrant, healthy hues associated with the dominant element of the current season</term>, there will be cause for celebration. If these auspicious colors are absent, there will be cause for sorrow.

If a sun halo contains vapor shaped like a **person lying down**:
* If the figure is above the sun, the nation will face defeat.
* If the figure is inside the halo, the ruler will suffer from sorrow.
* If the figure is outside the halo, a high-ranking minister will suffer or die.

If a figure resembling a **horse** is seen within the sun halo, it foretells that the army will be defeated.

If there is vapor within a sun halo shaped like an **ox** residing in the center, then within three days, **invaders** will enter the city at a time when no defending soldiers are outside the walls. One must take strict precautions against **treacherous infiltrators** <note>original: "jian ke," literally "wicked guests," referring to spies or internal traitors acting in concert with the enemy</note>.

If within or outside a sun halo there is a cloud-like vapor shaped like a **dead snake** <note>original: "si she," likely referring to a limp, dark, or stagnant band of cloud that appears to hang from the halo</note> attached to the ring, the **Great General** will die. In this situation, it is disadvantageous to be the one to initiate a military strike.

***

If a **sun halo** contains a cloud shaped like a **bird pecking at the sun**, with its neck and beak positioned inside the halo and its wings spread outside:

If there are also clouds **piercing through the center**, it signifies that enemy soldiers are coming to attack; preparations for battle must be made.

If a sun halo is surrounded by numerous clouds resembling **plumage or feathers** <note>original: "mao yu"</note> that press toward the sun and do not disperse, the nation beneath it will suffer the anxieties of war.

When a sun halo has **four residual vapors** protruding from it like **sharp blades** <note>original: "feng," the cutting edge or point of a weapon</note> pointing in four directions, the land beneath it is an ominous place.

If the outer ring of a sun halo is lined with shapes resembling **halberds** <term>halberds: "ge ji," traditional Chinese polearms combining a spear point with a side-blade, used here to represent imminent armed conflict</term> protruding in four, five, or six directions, one must guard against an incoming assault and slaughter; there will surely be combat in two or three different locations.

If there is vapor above the sun halo shaped like a **canopy** <term>canopy: "gai," a ceremonial parasol used by high-ranking officials; its appearance here suggests a heavy, looming atmosphere of authority in crisis</term>, it foretells military chaos and the nation being in peril. The year will be dominated by **disastrous fires** <note>original: "xiong huo," referring to both literal conflagrations and metaphorical calamities associated with the fire element</note>.

If there is vapor beside the sun halo shaped like a **hanging bell**, a general will die. If the vapor is shaped like a **tally-staff** <note>original: "jie," a staff held by imperial envoys or high commanders as a symbol of authority</note>, a **ceremonial banner**, or a **collapsed house**, the army stationed in that direction will be scattered and defeated.
