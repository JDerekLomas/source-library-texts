<meta>This page serves as the ornate title engraving for David de Planis Campy’s 1628 medical treatise. It establishes the work's authority by flanking the title with the greatest names in ancient and modern medicine—Hippocrates and Paracelsus—under the patronage of King Louis XIII.</meta>

<image-desc>The page is an elaborate engraved title. At the top center, a medallion features a portrait of King Louis XIII in a ruff, surrounded by the words "LOUIS XIII. THE GALLIC HERCULES." <note>The "Gallic Hercules" was a traditional image of the French King representing strength through eloquence and wisdom as much as physical power.</note> To the left of the King is an orb with Hebrew text "Heavens" <note>original: "שמים" (Shamayim)</note> and the labels "CHAOS" <note>original Greek: "XAOZ"</note> and "Magnesia" <term>Magnesia: in alchemy, a term for the primary substance used in the "Great Work."</term>. To the right is an orb containing alchemical geometric symbols. 

Below the top medallion, the main title is written on a draped cloth. 

On the left side, an oval portrait of Hippocrates <note>The "Father of Medicine" from Ancient Greece.</note> is labeled "HIPPOCRATES" in the frame, with a Latin quote below. On the right, an oval portrait of Paracelsus <note>A Swiss physician and the father of toxicology/modern chemistry.</note> is labeled "TH. PARACELSUS THE GERMAN." He holds a vessel labeled "AZOTH" <term>Azoth: the "universal solvent" or "mercury of the philosophers," believed to be the essential agent of transformation.</term>, and a Latin quote appears below his portrait.

At the bottom left is a circular alchemical diagram with terms like "first matter" <note>original Latin: "materia prima"</note>, "fire," "air," "water," "earth," "Soul," and "Spirit." At the bottom right is an emblem of Mercury (symbol and name "MERCURY") on a mountain. The publisher's imprint is in a rectangular box at the bottom center.</image-desc>

## THE HYDRA
# OF DISEASE
## EXTERMINATED
### BY
## THE CHEMICAL HERCULES.
<note>The "Morbific Hydra" is a metaphor for stubborn, multi-headed diseases. The "Chemical Hercules" refers to Iatrochemistry—a school of medicine that used chemical and mineral preparations rather than just traditional herbal remedies.</note>

->OR<-

->The seven diseases held to be incurable until now, rendered curable by the Medical Chemical Art.<-

->*Wherein is briefly treated their definition, causes, differences, signs, prognosis & Cure.*<-

->The whole according to ancient & modern medicine, divided into seven books.<-

->*By David de Planis Campy, called the Edelph, Surgeon to the King.*<-
<note>"Edelph" was a pseudonym used by the author, derived from Greek roots meaning "noble brother."</note>

->Dedicated to the Most Christian King of France & of Navarre, **LOUIS THE JUST**, 13th of the name.<-
<note>King Louis XIII, who reigned from 1610 to 1643.</note>
