### DISCOURSE, AND
# STORIES OF
# SPECTERS, VISIONS AND
## APPARITIONS OF SPIRITS, ANGELS,
## DEMONS, AND SOULS, SHOWING
->themselves visible to men.<-

### DIVIDED INTO EIGHT BOOKS.

WHEREIN, THROUGH THE MARVELOUS VISIONS and prodigious Apparitions occurring in all ages, drawn and collected from the most celebrated authors both Sacred and Profane, the certainty of Specters and visions of Spirits is manifested: and the causes of the various kinds of their Apparitions are given, their effects, their differences, and the means to recognize the good and the bad, and to drive away Demons.

ALSO TREATED ARE ECSTASIES AND *raptures: Of the essence, nature, and origin of Souls, and of their state after the death of their bodies: Plus of Magicians and Sorcerers, of their communication with evil Spirits: Together with the remedies to protect oneself from Diabolical illusions and impostures.*

->By <term>PIERRE LE LOYER</term>, Councilor to the King at the Presidial seat of Angers. <note>Pierre Le Loyer (1550–1634) was a prominent magistrate and scholar whose work attempted to provide a scientific and theological framework for understanding the supernatural. The "Presidial seat" refers to a high court of justice in Renaissance France.</note><-

# ->TO GOD, THE BEST AND GREATEST, TO THE SAINTS, AND TO THE PIOUS READER.<-

Supreme and foremost Father, sower and origin of all things; Christ the Word, light and splendor shining forth from the bosom of the Father; Holy Spirit, the warmth of both, illuminating, quickening, and cherishing all things; one God, one divine power: I invoke you as a suppliant. May you be willing and propitious to me as I rightly begin this work; may you favor, preserve, and grant me a sound mind and a sincere and pure understanding. Be present as I unlock the sacred mysteries <note>original: "arcana sacra"</note>, and ward off from my eyes all ghosts <term>larvas: in this context, frightening spirits or masks of the dead</term>, furies, curses, storms, all encounters of the night, and all terrors of the graves. May you likewise forbid the poisons of an evil tongue, fierce and rapid slanders, the evil eye <term>fascinum: a curse or spell cast by the gaze</term>, binding-curses <term>defixiones: ancient ritual tablets used to "bind" or curse individuals</term>, and solemn devotions to destruction. Defend me from my enemies, especially those of whom I feel I must speak. Therefore, O God, be honored by these offerings: by this banquet to be set forth, and by this sacrificial cake. May you accept this—not as a spring-born fruit of my wit, but as one I might almost call late-ripening <note>The author uses the term "cordo," referring to fruit that ripens late in the season, perhaps suggesting his work is the product of his later years or long labor.</note>.

And you, nourishing Virgin, mother of Christ and of God, who has surpassed the heavenly intelligences in immortal glory as much as you once, when placed among us mortals, excelled all women in virtue and praise: you are the patroness and guardian of the good, and the enemy of the possessed, of darkness-dwellers, of witches, and of shape-shifters <term>versipellium: literally "skin-changers," often referring to werewolves or those who change their form through magic</term>. You are the vanquisher of the rabble, of ungodly beasts, and of monsters—for thus do I point out the heretics, those scouts of the Antichrist. And you, Michael the unconquered, whether you...

WHETHER YOU ARE CALLED THE LEADER OF THE ANGELIC ARMY <note>original: "Dvx angelici exercitus"</note>, OR GENIUS <term>Genius: in classical Roman belief, a guardian spirit; here the author attempts to reconcile pagan terminology with Christian theology</term>, OR AS MIGHT BE MORE PIOUSLY SAID IN A CHRISTIAN MANNER, THE GUARDIAN AND PRINCE OF THE CHURCH <note>original: "Princeps Ecclesiæ"</note>, AS WELL AS THE CONQUEROR OF TITANS AND FURIES <note>original: "Erynnium," referring to the Greek deities of vengeance or demons</note> IN WHICH TITLES YOU REJOICE: O HEAVENLY SPIRITS AND HUMAN SOULS ALREADY RECEIVED INTO HEAVEN, BE PRESENT AND AID ME WITH YOUR PRAYERS.

WHATEVER YOU UNDERSTAND IN MY WRITINGS, CONSIDER IT GOOD; WHATEVER YOU DO NOT UNDERSTAND, CONSIDER IT ALSO TO BE GOOD. IF SOME THINGS DO NOT PLEASE YOU OR SEEM TO BE WRITTEN IN ERROR, JUDGE THEM FAIRLY AND KINDLY <note>The Latin phrase "æqui bonique" is an appeal to the reader’s sense of equity and "good faith"</note>; ADVISE ME, OR LET THEM BE AS IF THEY WERE NEVER WRITTEN. ### GAIUS PLINIUS TO HIS FRIEND SURA. <note>original: "C. PLINIVS SVRÆ SVO"</note>

->I WOULD VERY MUCH LIKE TO KNOW WHETHER YOU THINK THAT GHOSTS<-
->EXIST AND HAVE THEIR OWN PROPER FORM AND SOME SPIRITUAL POWER,<-
->OR WHETHER THEY ARE EMPTY AND VAIN IMAGES PRODUCED BY OUR FEAR.<-
<note>This is the opening of Pliny the Younger’s famous letter (Book 7, Letter 27) regarding the existence of the supernatural.</note>

### NAZARIUS IN THE PANEGYRIC ADDRESSED TO
### CONSTANTINE AUGUSTUS.
<note>Nazarius was a 4th-century rhetorician; a "panegyric" is a public speech of high praise.</note>

*It is on the lips of everyone in the Gallic lands that armies were seen, who identified themselves as being sent by divine power. Although heavenly things do not usually come before the eyes of men—because our simple and coarse sight, clouded as it is, is eluded by the thin nature of such a refined substance.*
<note>original: "In ore est omnium Galliarum exercitus visos..." This refers to reports of celestial soldiers appearing to support Constantine the Great.</note>

### SEXTUS THE PHILOSOPHER.
<note>Likely Sextus Empiricus, a skeptic philosopher who here records the views of the earlier philosopher Democritus.</note>

<margin>Children of the gods who have been granted a good lot. <note>original Greek: "εὐλογχίων λόγχια τὰ τῶν θεῶν παιδία." Hesychius was a 5th-century lexicographer.</note></margin>
Democritus says that certain images <term>Eidola: in Epicurean and Democritean philosophy, these are thin films of atoms that shed off objects and can be perceived by the mind or senses</term> approach men, and that of these, some are doers of good and others are doers of evil. For this reason, he even prays to encounter "well-allotted" images. He says these are great and even oversized, and that they signal the future to men by emitting voices.
<note>original Greek: "Δημόκριτος εἴδωλα τινά φησὶν..."</note>

### HOMER, ILIAD BOOK 23. <note>original Greek: "Όμηρος Ἰλιάδ. ψ." The letter psi refers to the 23rd book.</note>

->O heavens! Truly there is even in the halls of Hades<-
->A soul and an image, yet no mind remains within at all.<-
->For all night long the soul of poor Patroclus<-
->Stood over me, wailing and mourning,<-
->And gave me each instruction; it looked wondrously like the man himself.<-
<note>original Greek: "Ὤ Πόποι ἦ ῥάτις ἐστὶ..." This passage describes Achilles’ realization after the ghost of his friend Patroclus appears to him in a dream.</note>
