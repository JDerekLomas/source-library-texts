# Regulation of Formation Training: Formation Seven

<page-num>27</page-num>

->Within the Central Army, how can the generals and lords establish a formation?<-

When the tactical situation is simple and easy, one beat of the drum and one wave of the flag are enough to form the lines. A second drum beat and a second flag signal, and the troops march out to battle. Within the span of a single breath, they open and close, change and transform. Command moves them **like the arm moves the finger**, or as easily as **driving a carriage and whipping a horse**. 

If one were to attempt to create a momentum based on "interlocking coils" <note>original: *panqu* (蟠曲). This refers to winding, labyrinthine movements that theorists often drew in diagrams but were nearly impossible to execute under the pressure of real combat.</note>, the result would be nothing but unbearable tediousness and chaos.

Furthermore, the practice of drawing "interlocking lines" is for the purpose of the diagram itself, not the reality of the field. The **Formation Diagrams of Kongming** <note>Zhuge Liang (181–234 AD), also known by his courtesy name Kongming, was the legendary strategist of the Three Kingdoms period credited with the "Eight Formations."</note> consist of eight-by-eight units multiplied together <note>resulting in a total of 64 sub-units</note>. In these drawings, the units appear blended and indistinguishable. An observer must use a brush to "hook" and connect them just to define the positions of the **Nine Armies**. 

The four teams at the center, when connected, constitute the **Grip of the Extraordinary** <term>Grip of the Extraordinary (*Woqi* 握奇): A term from the classical "Grip of the Extraordinary Classic" (Woqi Jing), referring to a small, elite reserve force held by the commanding general to be deployed as a "surprise" or "extraordinary" force at the decisive moment.</term>. Twelve teams connected together form the **Central Army**. Along the flanks, the remaining forces extend to meet the enemy...
