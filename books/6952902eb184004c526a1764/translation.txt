### **The Trojan Women**

**W**hen Troy had been captured and <note>original: "astruau" or "eversa"; the text likely suggests the city was laid low or overturned</note> destroyed, and after many had escaped the hands of the enemy and the burning of the city, a great number of them were tossed about by adverse storms. Driven by their lack of skill in navigation, they reached that part of Italy where the river Tiber enters the sea. From there, they entered the mouth of the river with their ships; they were suddenly permitted to disperse to seek out provisions, for they were suffering from a great scarcity of supplies.

Meanwhile, among the women who had been left behind at the ships, a thought first arose, and then a discussion: that they would be consulting the best interests of themselves and their kin if they finally put an end to these long wanderings and sea-voyages and at last established some homeland for themselves, seeing as they were no longer able to recover their own home once it had been lost.

After these matters had been discussed among them for a short time, they all formed a single conspiracy and set fire to all the ships. The initiative began with one of them whose name is said to have been Roma. <note>This refers to the legend of Roma, the Trojan woman who supposedly prompted the burning of the ships to force the refugees to settle, eventually leading to the naming of the city of Rome.</note>
