# THE GOLDEN AGE
RESTORED.
<note>original: "AVREVM SECVLVM REDIVIVVM"</note>

<margin>Gen 30, 14.</margin> As I thought upon the wonders of the Most High, upon the mystery of sealed Nature, and upon the fiery and fervent love of one’s neighbor: I remembered the Harvest of the Sages, wherein Reuben, the son of Leah, found the mandrakes <note>original: "Dudaim"; these plants were anciently associated with fertility and alchemy.</note> in the field, which were given by Leah to Rachel for the sake of sleeping with the Patriarch Jacob. But my thoughts were very deep and led me further to Moses, how he made the Solar Calf <note>The Golden Calf of Exodus 32.</note> cast by Aaron drinkable, in that he burnt it with fire, ground it to powder, strewed it upon the water, and gave it to the children of Israel to drink. I marveled greatly at the sudden and skillful destruction by this man of God; but when I truly composed my thoughts, I recognized the truth, and my eyes were opened no differently than the two disciples at Emmaus, who recognized the Lord in the breaking of bread. And my heart burned within me, but I laid myself down to speculate further and fell asleep over it. And behold, King Solomon appeared to me in a dream with all his power, wealth, and glory; he led with him his entire harem, there were 80 queens and concubines, but of the <margin>Sol. song VI. 8.</margin> **young women there was no number; but one was his dove, his pious one, the most beautiful and dearest in his heart.** And according to Catholic custom <note>The author uses "Catholic" here in the sense of "universal" or "grand tradition."</note> they held a stately procession, wherein the Center was highly honored and loved; its name was like a poured-out ointment, whose scent surpasses all spices, etc. And its fiery spirit is a key to open the Temple, to go into the Holy place, and to take hold of the horns of the Altar.

After the procession was finished, Solomon showed me the unique Center in the Triangle of the center <note>original: "Trigono centri"; a geometric symbol for the alchemical heart of matter.</note> and opened my understanding, and I perceived that behind me stood a naked female figure with <margin>VII. 1-5.</margin> a bloody wound in her breast, from which blood and water dripped. Her thighs, however, were joined together like two jewels made by the hand of a master; her navel was like a round goblet that never lacked drink; her belly like a heap of wheat set about with roses; her two breasts like two young twin roes; her neck like a tower of ivory; her eyes like the pools in Heshbon by the gate of Bath-rabbim; her nose like the tower of Lebanon which looks toward Damascus; her head stood upon her like Carmel, and the hair upon her head was like the King's purple bound in folds. But her clothes, which she had cast off, lay at her feet; they were entirely stained, stinking, and poisonous. And she began to speak: "I have put off my coat; how shall I put it on again? I have washed my feet; how shall I <margin>V. 3. 7.</margin> defile them again? The watchmen that go about the city found me, they smote me, they wounded me, and took away my veil." Then I was terrified out of fear and lack of recognition and fell to the earth. But Solomon bade me stand up again and said: "Do not be afraid, for you see uncovered Nature and the most secret thing that is under heaven and upon earth. She is beautiful as Tirzah, lovely as <margin>VI. 4</margin> Jerusalem, terrible as an army with banners; and yet she is the pure, chaste virgin from whom Adam was made and created. Truly, the entrance to her gardens is sealed and hidden, for she dwells in the gardens and sleeps in the double cave of Abraham on the field of Ephron <note>The Cave of Machpelah, a site of burial and hidden secrets.</note>, and her palace is in the depths of the Red Sea and in transparent clefts. The air has carried her, and the fire has nourished her <note>A reference to the *Emerald Tablet* of Hermes Trismegistus.</note>; therefore she is a Queen of the land. She has milk and honey in her breasts; yes, her lips are like dripping honeycomb, honey and milk are under her tongue, and the scent of her garments is to the Sages like the scent of Lebanon, but to the ignorant, an abomination." And Solomon spoke further: "Rouse yourself, look upon my entire harem, and seek her equal." And immediately I had to strip the entire harem; I searched, but I could not judge my thoughts, and my eyes were held so that I did not recognize her.

But when Solomon noticed my weakness, he separated his harem from this naked female figure and said: "Your thoughts are vain, and your understanding is burnt by the sun, and your memory is black as fog, so that you cannot judge rightly. However, if you do not throw away your cause and will take heed of the present opportunity, this naked virgin's bloody sweat and snow-white tears can refresh you again, purify your understanding and memory, and fully restore them, so that your eyes recognize the *magnalia* <note>The "great works" of God.</note> of the Most High. You shall truly explore the height of the uppermost, the depth of the lowermost, and the foundation of all Nature, and the power and operation of all elements. Your understanding will be of silver, and your memory of gold; the colors of all precious stones will appear before your face, and you will know their birth, and separate the good from the evil, the goats from the sheep. Your life will be rest, but the bells of Aaron shall wake you from sleep, and the harp of David my father from slumber." At this speech of Solomon's, I was even more terrified and was exceedingly dismayed, partly because of the powerful words, and partly because of the great magnificence and glory of the royal harem present. And King Solomon took me by the hand, led me through a wine cellar into a secret but very stately hall, wherein he refreshed me with flowers and comforted me with apples; its <margin>II. 4.</margin> windows, however, were of transparent crystals, and I looked through them. He spoke: "What do you see?" I answered: "I see from this into the previous chamber from which I came, and your royal harem stands to the left, and the naked virgin to the right side; and her eyes are redder than wine, her teeth whiter than milk, but her clothes at her feet are uglier, blacker, and more foul than the brook Kidron." "Awaken one from all of them," said Solomon, "as your beloved; I value her and my harem equally, and as much as the courtliness of my ladies delights me, so little do her loathsome clothes terrify me." And immediately the King turned and spoke quite friendly with one of his queens. There was an old, hundred-year-old Governess among them who had a gray coat on, a black cap on her head set with snow-white pearls, lined inside with red taffeta, and embroidered with blue and yellow silk and quite skillfully stitched through. Her cloak was heightened with all kinds of Turkish colors and Indian figures. This old woman gave me a secret wink and swore a solemn oath that she was the mother of this naked virgin; that the girl was born of her body, a chaste, pure, and hidden virgin who until now would not suffer the gaze of any man. And though she allowed herself to be used among the peoples everywhere in the streets, yet no man before this had ever seen her naked nor had a single one touched her, for she was the virgin of whom the Prophet says: "Behold, we have a secret born son, who is transformed among others; behold, the virgin has given birth, such a virgin whose name is Apdorossa <note>likely meaning "Hidden" or "Secret"; from the Greek *apokryphos*.</note>, that is, the secret one, who cannot suffer another." Because this her daughter was still unmarried, she had the dowry lying at her feet for the sake of the current danger of war, so that she might not be robbed by a devouring rabble and deprived of her stately wealth. I, however, should not let myself be deterred by the sight of the unsightly clothes, but choose her daughter before all others as my love and life's delight; then she would give me and reveal to me a lye to clean her clothes, so I would obtain a liquid salt and an unburnable oil for my household, and an unspeakable treasure, and her right hand would constantly embrace me, <margin>II. 6. VIII. 3.</margin> and her left would constantly lie under my head. And as I

<column-break/>

wanted to declare myself more clearly, Solomon turned back, looked at me, and said: "I am the wisest on earth; beautiful and delightful is my harem, and the glory of my queens surpasses the gold from Ophir; the ornament of my concubines outshines the rays of the sun, and the decoration of my virgins the moonlight. So heavenly are my ladies, unfathomable my wisdom, and unquenchable my understanding." Then I answered and bowed, half-terrified: "Behold, if I have found grace before you, since I am poor, give me this naked virgin whom I have chosen among all for the preservation of my life; her clothes are indeed soiled and torn, but I will clean them and love her from the heart, and she shall be my sister, my bride, because with one of her eyes and one chain of her neck she has taken my heart and made me so fervent that I lie sick for love." And immediately Solomon delivered her to me, and there was a tumult in the harem so that I woke up from it, and knew not what had happened to me. Nonetheless, I held it for a dream and had all sorts of subtle thoughts about it until morning. But when I stood up and had done my prayer, behold: there I saw the naked virgin's clothes lying before my bed, but not her. And I began to grow anxious and fearful, and all my hair stood on end, so that cold sweat also ran down over my whole body. Yet I took heart, repeated my dream, and thought upon it in the fear of the Lord; but my thoughts were not able to fathom it. For these reasons, I did not dare to inspect the clothes, much less to recognize anything in them, especially since my bedchamber and almost all the rooms were filled with a strong, contrary smell from the clothes lying there. I thought that if I were to touch or turn them, something notable might happen to me; for in my sleep I was heavily poisoned and inflamed by the stench of the clothes, so that my eyes could not see the time of grace, nor my heart recognize the great wisdom of Solomon.

But after the aforementioned clothes had lain for 5 years in my bedchamber and I did not know what use they were, I finally thought to burn them to clear the room. And when I had spent the whole day occupied with such thoughts, the following night the old hundred-year-old woman appeared to me in a dream and addressed me quite harshly in the following manner: "You ungrateful man! I have entrusted my daughter's clothes, under which her most noble jewels are kept, to you for 5 years now, and in all that time you have neither cleaned them nor aired them for the sake of the moths and worms; and above all that, you now finally want to burn the clothes with fire. Is it not enough that you are a cause of the death and destruction of my daughter?" Then I became somewhat hot in the forehead and gave her as answer: "How shall I understand that? Do you want to make a murderer out of me? I have not seen your daughter with my eyes in 5 years, nor heard the least thing from her; how then can I be a cause of her death?" And she would not let me finish speaking, but said: "It is all true; however, you have sinned against God, therefore my daughter could not be granted to you, nor could the philosophical lye I promised for washing and cleaning her clothes follow. For as you began, when Solomon willingly gave you my daughter and you had a loathing for her clothes, the planet Saturn, who is her grandfather, grew angry and out of pure wrath transformed her back into what she had been before her birth. And so, through your disdain, you have angered Saturn and caused her death, decay, and final destruction. For she is the very one of whom Senior <note>Referring to Senior Zadith, a famous 10th-century alchemist.</note> says: 'Alas, woe! Bring me a naked woman, for unsightly was my first body, and I had never yet become a mother until I was born a second time; then I brought forth the power of all herbs' roots; in my being I became victorious, etc.'" Such and similar cutting words seemed very strange to me, yet I restrained my temper as much as was humanly possible; nevertheless, I solemnly protested that I knew nothing of her daughter, much less of her death, decay, and destruction. True, I had kept her clothes in my bedchamber for 5 years, but because of great blindness had not recognized them nor been able to think of their use, and was therefore innocent before God and every man. This my rightful, because well-founded, excuse did not please the old mother poorly; she looked at me and said: "I perceive and notice from your upright conscience that you are innocent, and your innocence shall also be richly and well rewarded. Therefore, I will reveal to you out of a good heart and in secret: namely, that my daughter, out of special love and affection for you, has left a gray, standardized <note>original: "Normalertes"; perhaps meaning standardized or specific.</note> little box as an inheritance among her remaining clothes, which is covered with a coarse, black, foul lining." (And with that she gave me a glass with lye and spoke further:) "That same little box you shall clean well of the stench and filth it has gotten from the clothes; then you will need no key, but it will open itself, and you will find two things therein: a white silver booklet full of magnificent diamonds written upon lead and polished snow-white; and also a golden piece adorned with precious Solar rubies. And this is the treasure and entire estate of my late daughter, all of which she bequeathed and left to you as an inheritance before her transformation and passing. If you now skillfully manage this treasure together, purify it to the highest degree, and make it liquid, yet lock it with great patience in a warm, hidden, steamy, transparent, and moist cellar and protect it from frost, wind, hail, sudden lightning, hot thunderclaps, and other external destruction until the Harvest of the Sages, then you will for the first time feel and share in the great glory of the inheritance." And with that I awoke once more and sighed; I called out to God anxiously that he would open my understanding to seek the little box that was promised and pledged to me in the dream. After finishing my prayer, I searched with the greatest diligence and desire in the clothes and found it; but the lining was so hard closed around it and grown on by nature that I was not able to get it off. For it would neither be cleaned with the lye nor allow itself to be split with iron, steel, or other metal. So I left it standing once more and knew not what to do with it; I held it for a work of sorcery and thought of the saying of the Prophet: "For though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet thine iniquity is marked before me, saith the Lord God." <margin>Jer 2, 22</margin>

And another year passed that I still, with speculating and industrious reflection, did not know how to remove the lining, until finally, to drive away melancholic thoughts, I went for a walk in a garden. After long wandering, I sat down upon a kissing-stone <note>original: "Kissing Stein"; likely a large, smooth garden stone.</note> and fell fast asleep over it. "I slept, but my heart was awake." <margin>V, 2</margin> Then the old hundred-year-old Governess appeared to me again and said: "Have you received my daughter's estate?" I answered with a sad voice: "No, the little box I have indeed found, but to separate the lining from it is still impossible for me at this time; the lye you gave me will not attack the lining." At this my simple speech, the old woman laughed, smiling, and said: "Will you now eat mussels and crabs with the shells? Must they not first be ripened and prepared by the ancient planet-cook Vulcan? <note>Vulcan refers to alchemical fire.</note> I told you that you should purify the gray box with the gifted lye that sprang from the same to the highest degree, and not the external raw lining; specifically, that same lining must first be burnt with the Fire of the Sages, then it will be perfectly suitable." And hereupon she gave me several glowing coals bound in white silk, with further instruction and indication that I should make from this a philosophical and quite skillful fire and burn the lining, then I would soon find the poor little box. And hourly a North and South wind arose; both blew together through <margin>IV 16</margin> the garden, from which I awoke, cleaned my eyes from sleep, and perceived that the glowing coals wrapped in white silk lay at my feet. I seized them hastily and with joy, prayed diligently, called upon God, studied and labored day and night, thinking meanwhile of the magnificent and excellent saying of the philosophers, where they say: *Ignis & Azoth tibi sufficiunt.* <note>"Fire and Azoth are sufficient for you." Azoth is the universal solvent/mercury in alchemy.</note> Of which Esdras also says in the fourth book: "He gave me a cup full of fire, and its appearance <margin>Esdrae IV. 39</margin> was like fire, and I drank it; then wisdom grew in me." And God has given the fifth understanding, and my spirit was kept in memory, and my mouth was opened and not closed further; and when 40 nights were passed, 204 books were completed, 70 for the wisest alone, and they were worthy to be read and written upon box-wood. And I proceeded thus in silence and hope, as the old mother had revealed to me in the dream, until a long time after the promise of Solomon my understanding became silver and my memory golden. But after, upon the instruction and teaching of the old Governess, I had properly and quite skillfully set and locked up the treasure of her daughter—namely: the magnificent, shining Lunar diamonds and the Solar rubies, which both spring from one little box and are found in one landscape—I heard the voice of Solomon, which said: "My friend is white and <margin>Sol song V. 10-16</margin> red, chosen out of many thousands; his locks are curly, black as a raven; his eyes are like doves' eyes by the water-brooks, washed with milk, and fitly set; his cheeks are as the growing spice-beds of the apothecaries; his lips are like roses dripping with flowing myrrh; his hands are like golden rings full of turquoise; his body is like pure ivory adorned with sapphires; his legs are like marble pillars founded upon golden feet; his form is like Lebanon, choice as the cedars; his throat is sweet and altogether lovely; such is my friend, my friend is such a one, ye daughters of Jerusalem! Therefore you shall hold him <margin>III 4</margin> and not let him go, until you bring him into his mother's house, into his mother's chamber." And when Solomon had spoken these words, I knew not what to answer, but became silent; nonetheless, I had the intention to open the enclosed treasure again, so that I might find peace...

# EPILOGUE.
<note>original: "EPILOGVS"</note>

...remained unmolested, I heard another voice: "I charge you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles and the does of the field, that you do not stir up nor awaken my beloved until she pleases." For she is an enclosed garden, a spring shut up, a fountain sealed. She is the vineyard at Baal-hamon, the vineyard at En-Gedi, the garden of nuts and spices, the mountain of myrrh, the hill of frankincense, the bed, the litter, the crown, the palm and apple tree, the flower of Sharon, the sapphire, the turquoise, the wall, the tower and the breastwork, the pleasure garden, the throne of grace, the fountain of living waters, the prince’s daughter, and the love of Solomon in delights. She is her mother’s favorite, and the chosen one of her mother; her head is filled with dew, and her locks with the drops of the night.
<note>This entire passage is a dense weave of metaphors from the biblical Song of Solomon, used here to describe the "First Matter" or the perfected Nature in alchemy.</note>

Through this speech and revelation, I was so far informed that I recognized the goal of the Sages. I left the closed shoe standing untouched until, through the mercy of God, the working of noble Nature, and the labor of my own hands, everything was happily completed.

Shortly after this time, on the very day of the new moon, an eclipse of the sun occurred, which appeared quite terrifying. At first, it showed dark green and somewhat mixed colors until it finally became coal-black, blocking out heaven and earth. The people were afraid, but I rejoiced and thought of the great mercy of God and the new birth, just as the grain of wheat gives us a sign from Christ himself: unless it is cast into the earth to rot, it brings forth no fruit. And it happened that the darkness was covered with clouds, and the sun began to peek through; yet three parts were still deeply darkened. And behold: an arm broke through the clouds—and my body trembled before it—holding a letter with four hanging seals in its hand, upon which was written: "I am black but comely, O daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar, like the curtains of Solomon. Do not look upon me because I am black, for the sun has scorched me," etc. But as soon as the **fixed** <note>The stable, solid part of the alchemical matter.</note> acted into the **moist**, a rainbow stretched out, and I thought of the covenant of the Most High, and of the faithfulness of my Guide <note>original: "Ductoris"</note> and of him who had instructed me. And behold: through the help of the planets and the darkness, the sun overcame the gloom, and a most lovely, bright day followed over every mountain and valley. Then all fear and terror ended, and all who saw and lived through this day rejoiced in the Lord and said: "The winter is past, the rain is over and gone, the flowers appear on the earth, the time of singing has come, and the voice of the turtledove is heard in the land. The fig tree and the vines have gained eyes and buds and give off their fragrance. Therefore, let us hasten to catch the foxes, the little foxes that spoil the vineyard, so that we may gather ripe grapes, drenched with prepared wine, and be fed at the right time with milk and honey-comb, so that we may be drunken and satisfied." And after the day had declined and evening fell, the whole sky changed color, and the seven stars rose with yellow rays and allowed the night its natural course until, in the morning, it was overtaken and overshadowed by the redness of the sun. And behold, the Sages who dwelt in the land rose from sleep, looked toward heaven, and said: "Who is she that breaks forth like the morning, fair as the moon, clear as the sun, and there is no spot on her? For her glow is fiery, a flame of the Lord, so that even many waters cannot quench love, nor the floods drown it. Therefore we will not leave her; she is our sister, and though she has become small and has no breasts, we will bring her back into her mother’s house, into a transparent hall where she was before, to suck her mother’s breasts. Then she will go forth like the tower of David, armed with breastworks, whereon hang a thousand shields and all the armor of the mighty." and as she went out, the daughters praised her as blessed, and the queens and concubines praised her. But I fell down upon my face, thanked God, and praised his holy name.

<column-break/>

And now, you dear and true **Sons of Wisdom and Learning**, <note>original: "Sapientiæ & doctrinæ filij"</note> in all power and glory, the great mystery of the Sages and the revelation of the Spirit is completed. Of this, the Prince and Monarch Theophrastus <note>Theophrastus Paracelsus, the famous 16th-century physician and alchemist.</note> says in the *Apocalypse of Hermes*: that it is a single Divinity <note>original: "Numen"</note>, a divine, miraculous, and holy office, wherein he encloses the whole world; it is in him, truly becomes one another, and truly overcomes the elements and the 5th substance <note>The "Quintessence."</note>. ## PREFACE
->To the Reader seeking the Art.<-

Do not be surprised, dear reader and true seeker of the secrets of Nature, why in this last age of the world—when it already walks with one foot in the grave—I have undertaken to write this short treatise. This is because almost all libraries are found full of books written about this subject <note>Alchemy</note>, the majority of which, however, contain false and invented philosophy and recipes. I have written it not for my sake, but for yours: to show you the foundation of truth and to lead you away from the wandering paths, for which you are not a little concerned.

As for me, I already know what is necessary for me to know in this regard; I have no need of books, having read as many of them in twenty-two years as I could get into my hands, of which there were not a few, both written and printed. You will find herein the Matter <note>the raw material</note> and the Solution <note>the process of dissolving the material</note> described theoretically, as well as the entire practice perfectly contained in an allegory, as clearly and plainly as you might seek from any philosopher. I have also endeavored to speak with the philosophers, and therefore I have precisely indicated all the places where they testify to experience, so that you yourself may look them up and compare the concept and my citations <note>original: "allegata"</note> against one another, and thereby sharpen your understanding.

Indeed, I could have written this treatise with less effort without such citations and revealed myself to the Brothers of the Golden Cross <note>original: "Fratribus aureæ crucis"</note>. But for your sake, as already mentioned, it is intended for the best, so that you may draw all the more understanding from it. You should also not wonder why I have hidden my name and do not wish to reveal myself to you personally: I do not seek my own vain honor or to make a great name for myself in the world, but rather your benefit. Furthermore, my teachers—I mean the true philosophers—did not teach me to risk my life for the sake of great prestige or to offer it for sale to spiritual robbers, nor to load great sins upon myself by profaning this great secret.

The reader will have heard from Sendivogius <note>Michael Sendivogius (1566–1636), a famous Polish alchemist</note> that as often as he revealed himself to great lords, it always resulted in harm and danger for him. I also warn you that various philosophers, who did not sufficiently guard their treasures, were strangled and robbed of their Tincture <note>the legendary substance that transforms lead into gold</note> by greedy and proud fellows who risked their souls for it. Reason confirms that anyone who openly carries a great treasure would not gladly be robbed. Sendivogius hid his name in his anagrams. A short time ago, a new Brother of the Golden Cross likewise made himself known with an anagram and an enigma, whose example is well known to me. Why then should I place myself before the eyes of the whole world?

Let it suffice you, dear friend, that I make myself known to the wise and at the same time make my name known to you, which I have done without fail. I commend the rest to our Lord God, who will make me known to you if it pleases Him and if it will be useful to me and to you. Do not let yourself lust to search for my name: for even if you should ever find it out or know me, you would still have to be satisfied with this treatise. For I have sworn, according to what is right, with the philosophers Bernard of Trevisan and Raymond Lully, to reveal nothing further to anyone than what has been done here.

Do not trouble yourself either about whether I have this treasure in my hands: ask much more whether I have seen how the world was created? How the darkness was in Egypt? What is the cause of the rainbow? How the clarified bodies will look after the general resurrection? Which color is the most permanent?

But I ask you, who read through my little book: Have you seen the general, great, salty sea, without any corrosive, which is sufficient to lead the tinctures of all things to the highest mountains? Tell me, where does sulfur become sulfur, and where does mercury <note>original: "Mercurio"</note> become mercury? Also <note>original: "Jte" from Latin "Item"</note>, where does sulfur come from mercury, and mercury from sulfur? Have your eyes beheld the image of burning love, where man and woman embrace each other so that they can never be separated for all eternity, but instead, for the sake of magnificent love, they become one thing? If you understand what I am now saying, and have worked such a thing with your hands and seen it with your eyes, then I am your companion, and I notify you that I also know it, and it is my pleasure to have another acquaintance—for which intent I have also wished to write this little treatise.

Should there be anyone who complains about the difficulty of this art, let him know that in and of itself it is not difficult, and that for those who love God and are deemed worthy by Him for this, it comes quite easily.

But should someone wish to accuse me of having written the art too brightly and clearly, so that everyone could understand it: to him I answer that I have indeed described it understandably enough for the worthy, to whom God grants such things; the unworthy, however, will leave it well enough alone. I have previously told the entire art word for word to the over-clever; but they laughed at it in their hearts, not believing that there is a twofold resurrection of the dead in our work. Therefore, our art in theory and practice is a pure gift of God, which He gives when and to whom He wills, and it does not depend on anyone’s willing or running. I have known it with all its circumstances and manipulations for a good 17 or 18 years, and yet I had to wait until it pleased God to grant me grace.

No man should doubt the certainty and truth of this art, since it is as true, as certain in nature, and as undoubtedly ordained by God as the sun shines by day and the moon glows by night. With this, I will close this short preface and begin the treatise itself. But you, beloved Brothers of the Golden Cross <note>original: "Fratres crucis aureæ"</note>, who keep yourselves hidden here and there in secret and enjoy the high gifts of God in His fear: do not withhold yourselves from me, and if you do not know me, know that the cross proves the faithful and brings their faith to light, but security and pleasure stifle it. God be with us all. AMEN.

They did this through their writings to indicate to them both that the faithful God had also enlightened their understanding, blessed the work of their hands, and revealed to them the great secret of this earthly wisdom—for which they are rightly bound to give Him praise, honor, and glory in humility—and also so that they might leave behind guidance and instruction for their fellow Christians and art-seeking students. Thus, if it should so please the dear God, they too might reach the knowledge and science of such secrets.

And such people have been among all kinds of nations, such as the Egyptians (among whom Hermes Trismegistus was the most prominent), Chaldeans, Greeks, Arabs, Italians, French, English, Dutch, Spanish, Germans, Poles, Hungarians, Jews, and others more. However, it is no small wonder that although the aforementioned wise men wrote in different languages and at different times, such a harmony and unanimous consensus is found in their writings that every true philosopher can immediately recognize that God has gladdened them with this great blessing and that they have the work itself in their hands. Likewise, because truth emerges from harmony, dissonance must conversely bring every sophist and presumed philosopher to light.

For whoever has never rightly recognized the secret of the wise and follows his own path according to his own brain, every knower of the art sees his errors.

And **Scites** in the *Turba* <note>The *Turba Philosophorum*, or Assembly of the Philosophers, an ancient alchemical text</note> says: the foundation of this art is something singular, which is stronger and higher than all things, and is called the "sharp vinegar," which has caused the gold to become a pure spirit, without which neither the white nor the heavy color may come to be. Therefore the Philosopher says: If you had this one thing, it would be retained with him, and become one with him, and turn him into a spirit, and dye him with a spiritual and unchangeable color, and receive from the salted heavenly water the eternal Tincture, which cannot be extinguished; if you have this, you will see the error [of others], otherwise it will burn away.

One might, however, conclude from these words of Scites that not one, but two things are required—namely, the Earth and the Vinegar: as we say, the Stone is an Earth, and the Vinegar is a Spirit, and the Spirit is given to the Body so that the dryness is not consumed by the fire, but rather protected from such burning by the water. I must grant such an argument and conclusion, for you need add nothing more to it; otherwise, our established Philosophical sayings will be maintained in their dignity and truth. For it is certain that there is only one single material for our blessed Stone, which has many names among the Sages, which Nature prepares for the artist; Nature has ordained such a great treasure as one and alone, and otherwise no other thing in the world.

<margin>Material of the stone <note>original: "Materia lapidis"</note>.</margin>
This material is before everyone's eyes; the whole world possesses it, grasps it, loves it, and yet does not know it: it is a thing that is precious and yet disregarded and lowly, and is found in all places. **Theophrastus Paracelsus** <note>The famous Swiss physician and occultist</note> calls it in his book *Tincturam Physicorum* <note>The Tincture of the Physicians</note> the Red Lion, named by many, known by few. **Hermes** calls it in the first chapter of his book "Mercury," which grows from the Earth and has sprung from Nature. **Arnoldus** <note>Arnaldus de Villa Nova, a 13th-century physician and alchemist</note> again calls it *Es* or Ore; in the *Rosarium Philosophorum* <note>The Rose Garden of the Philosophers</note> it is called Salt. In summary, this Material has as many names as there are things in the world. Therefore, if you have this little stone, which is called our Stone, which all the Sages knew—I would say that without prior knowledge of Nature and its properties, one approaches the art as a donkey goes to the manger, not knowing why it opens its mouth, as Arnoldus says.

As one sees, everything that grows under the earth is called Minerals. These three families of creatures <note>Animal, Vegetable, and Mineral</note> encompass everything created under the Moon from the four elements, and no more or fewer are found. Each family is confirmed in its own kind and species, so that none can be transformed from its kind and species into another. Just as if one wanted to make an animal out of a stone, that would be against Nature: or to make a piece of wood out of an animal, or a plant out of lead. Such a thing, I say, is impossible by the decree of the Great King. For if such were permitted in Nature, the three families would be reduced, or even all turned into one. But because the entire constitution of the body would be disrupted, the hearts of all would not permit such a change of species. And what is more, He has not only fixed these three families each in its kind: but has also willed to leave each within a family firm and inviolate in its own type, so that each might remain increased in its form, and not be changed from its form into the form of another. Such as a man into a horse, or an apple tree into lettuce, or a badger into a bear or an eagle; it is likewise certain that Nature has not allowed such things. And as it was from the beginning, so it shall remain until the day when the Almighty, who said in the beginning "Let there be," shall say "Let it pass away": for each species has its own seed. But those which have a common Material, seed, and composition of elements, can perform a refinement and improvement of their state, provided their Material is pure and perfect.

<margin>Theophrastus <note>Paracelsus</note>.</margin>
He who does not know the beginnings of Nature within himself is still far removed from this Art. And **Rosarius** says: I advise you that you let no one in until you know the root of Nature. And so he wishes to recognize our order: When he has recognized it, he needs no more than a single thing, and it requires no great expense. For it is no more than one stone, one medicine, one seed, in which the water and the spirit, a fire and a fire, are all separated by the help of Nature and through skillful handiwork, so that, as **Theophrastus** says, it is transformed into the White Eagle <note>A term often referring to the sublimation of sal ammoniac or a purified mercurial spirit</note>, so that the brilliance of the Sun <note>Gold</note> does not outshine the Spagyrist <note>Alchemist</note>, or (as **Basilius Valentinus** calls it) it becomes a white mountain and snow, and yet a spirit red as blood, both of which spirits have the third hidden within them. Therefore King **Aros** speaks not poorly when he says: Our Stone is a material consisting of fire and water, and consists of the most stable, most spiritual and most bodily, cold and moist, warm and dry nature, and can be made from no other thing. And **Richardus Anglicus** says: It is a stone and a medicine, which is called by the naturalists the Stone, from the water, from the flesh, from the spirit, White or Red: in which many fools have erred in various ways, interpreting the verse: *Est redis in dictis rectissima norma salutis* <note>original Latin: "In the words spoken is the most direct rule of salvation/health."</note>. Everyone now easily understands that the Stone is nothing other than a water, which is added to the body and dissolves it into a spirit—that is, into a Mineral Water from which it was made in the beginning—and thus from the body and spirit comes a Mineral Water, which is called Elixir, that is, a Ferment <note>original: "Fermentum"</note>. For it is impossible that the Material of a thing from which the Tincture and medicine is made should not purify all bodies. Therefore our medicine must spring from one thing, which is the water and spirit of the metals: which we then call the Mercury of the Sages, of the Sulfur and Quicksilver above the earth, from which Gold and Silver were made under the earth. And **Bernard, Count of Trevisan** and the March speaks: our work is made from one root and two Mercurial substances, which are taken and brought forth from the mine <note>original: "Minero"</note>. And **Basilius Valentinus** says in the book of *Natural and Supernatural Things* in the 4th chapter: I will tell you in truth and through the love of God this: that there is no other thing upon which our Material can exist, than only a heavenly spirit, with the root of the spiritual or natural Mercury, which is both the beginning of the Spirit or Salt in one, and is found in one Material, and not in many. It is indeed called the Metallic Earth in many things: Mercury resembles all Philosophers on its own, and the Sulfur on its own, alongside the Salt specially given, so that the Mercury is found in one, the Sulfur in one, and the Salt in one, yet it is only one Material, and from this the Stone is made, from which most is found in each, and particularly in ours can be used by name and prepared for the medicine and transformation of metals. For above all the filthiness and malice of the earth, and above all three beginning things, is a single thing, and is found and drawn out in a single thing at once, which can make all metals into one, and is the true Spirit of Mercury and Soul of Sulfur <note>original: "Spiritus Mercurij vnnd Anima Sulphuris"</note> and the spiritual Salt, all together in one thing, which is everywhere, and dwelling in one body, and is the Dragon and the Lion, it is the King and the Lion, it is the Spirit and the Corpse, which makes the corpse of the Gold spiritual and gives the Tincture. Thus our prepared Material is called the Man and the Woman.

<margin>Roger Bacon <note>13th-century English philosopher and friar</note>.</margin>
Item, the active and the passive, as **Zimon** says in the ***Turba***: know that the secret of this work consists of the man and the woman; the man is the active, and the woman is the passive, that is the Sulfur and the Mercury. Roger Bacon in the 1st chapter calls the Sulfur the Orpiment <note>Arsenic trisulfide, used as a yellow pigment and alchemical material</note>, the Woman. The man rejoices in his accepted wife, and it shall happen to him before her: and the woman receives from the man a coloring seed, and is colored by him. And **Diomedes** speaks: Join the male son of the red servant to his sweet-smelling wife, and they shall be so joined together to give birth to the Art, into which you should pour no foreign thing, neither powder nor any other thing, and let the conception suffice you, so the true son will be born to you: O how exceedingly precious is the material of this red servant, without which no order can exist. Others call it *argentum vivum* <note>Quicksilver/Mercury</note> or Menstruum <note>Solvent</note> and Sulfur or Work. As **Roger Bacon** says in the *Mirror* <note>original: "Speculo"</note>, chapter 3: From Sulfur and Mercury all metals are born, and nothing clings to them, nor is anything added to them, nor does anything change them, except what comes from them. Therefore we must take related Mercury and Sulfur for the Material of the Stone. And **Menabadus**: He who adds the quicksilver to the body of the magnet, and the woman to the man, draws out the hidden nature with which the bodies are colored.

And **Lully** in his *Codicil*: The property of our Mercury is that it allows itself to be coagulated by its Sulfur. And in the *Practice* of his *Testament* he says: The quicksilver is an overflowing and flowing moisture, which is preserved from burning. Then take the body, spirit, and soul. Thus speaks **Arnoldus** in *flore florum* <note>Flower of Flowers</note>: The Philosophers have said our Stone is composed of the body, the soul, and the spirit, and they are purified far above all filthiness of the earth, and compared above all three bodies, because it is weak: The water has given us the spirit, and that with truth, because it is a spirit: but the ferment we have taken from the earth: Now when this spirit is with the body, it gives life, which it did not have before, and brings forth a better form. And a little before he says: The spirit is not added to its body except through the mediation of the soul: For the soul is the middle between the body and the spirit, and holds them together. And **Morienus** speaks: The soul enters nimbly into its body: but if you wanted to add it to another body, you would labor in vain. And **Lilium**: The soul, the spirit, and the body must be joined to each other, if the Material is not to be joined to anything foreign. But what is the use of listing and explaining all the names by which our Material is called here? We shall be satisfied with these: for we have sufficiently demonstrated from what substances and according to what we have specifically researched, from whence these three families come and must be obtained; we wish to consider the Solution, as the primary part of the whole Art, a little, and through that consideration demonstrate the Material.

Regarding the considerations and weighing of what our Material is, and when it must be obtained, it is to be known that the Almighty Creator, before he created man, ordained three kinds of the earth, as he himself was, created two kinds of things, namely the Heavenly, and those which are under the Heaven. The heavenly are the Heavens themselves, and the Heavenly inhabitants, of which we shall not philosophize further at this time. But the things which are under the heaven, and created from the four elements, are found in their number only as three kinds, namely: first, everything that has life and feeling, and are called *Animalia* [Animals]; after that, everything that grows out of the earth and does not feel, and are called *Vegetabilia* [Vegetables].

<column-break/>

<margin>Mercury <note>original: "Mercurius"</note>.</margin>
...if nothing foreign comes between them, and the unclean, stinking, and combustible Sulfur does not prevent it, as we then see that in many places pure and fine gold is found without the mixing of other metals. But because in the ore-houses a foreign sulfur commonly comes to the quicksilver and contaminates it, preventing its perfection, so also different metals are born according to the nature of such foreign sulfur. As **Aristotle** says in the 4th *Meteorologica*: If the essence of the quicksilver is good, and the combustible sulfur is impure, then he transforms it into Copper. But if the quicksilver is pure, and [other things are] impure and earthy, and the sulfur is also impure, and they eat from it <note>This likely refers to the chemical reaction or "consumption" of the materials during smelting</note>. It seems, however, as if Tin had good quicksilver, which is pure, but an evil and poorly combustible sulfur. Lead, however, has a bad quicksilver, and a bad, high-reaching quicksilver, and a bad, tasteless, and stinking sulfur, which is why it does not like to be coagulated.

<margin>Aristotle.</margin>
This hindering, impure, and stinking sulfur, however, is not the right fire which thoroughly cooks the metals; rather, it is the quicksilver's own sulfur which performs this, as **Bernard, Count of Trevisan** says: This will be demonstrated: a sulfurous material could come to it in the birth of the metals: but on the contrary, it is manifest that in the quicksilver, when Nature works, its own sulfur is enclosed: Which, however, does not rule in the same, except through the warm movement which is consumed by the metals, and all un-pure, unclean moistures, shadows, are changed: And in this way different metals are born in the earth. For in the metals, as **Arnoldus** states in the 1st chapter of his *Rosarium*, there is a superfluous, impure moisture, which is locked in the innermost essence of the quicksilver itself, which in the beginning of its essence mixed into it: The other, however, which came from outside its nature and corrupted it. And this indeed can be separated from it with great effort, but the former is removed by no artist's ingenuity.

<margin>Arnoldus.</margin>
Therefore, the great heat of the fire separates the combustible moisture from the metals, and makes the other moisture, which is more tangible in itself, and prepares that which is of its nature, but casts off the foreign and lets it burn away. The internal sulfur, however, which cooks the quicksilver and ripens it to perfection, is equally pure and impure, thinned and constrained, and is different according to the kind and form of the metals, so that it cannot become gold until such is finally separated from it, and the pure, incombustible and constant sulfur alone remains with it; then gold comes from it, which is the red quicksilver flowing over the earth. This internal sulfur, however, is nothing other than a ripened Mercury, or the ripest and most timely part of the quicksilver, therefore the quicksilver also accepts it so gladly, but lets other and foreign sulfur lie: Just as a magnet draws iron to itself so gladly because it is of its kind: it [the sulfur] attaches itself to the poor and pure quicksilver, and clings to the same, so that it is more strongly linked with the other, and thus more perfect metals are made from it.

<margin>Avicenna <note>11th-century Persian polymath and alchemist</note>.</margin>
Such sulfur, however, is not found above the earth, as **Avicenna** speaks, except only what is found in the two bodies, namely Gold and Silver, as the Goths also highly valued. Hail, as **Richardus** speaks in chapter 12: It is the red sulfur in Gold through greater ripening, but the white in Silver through lesser ripening.

<margin>Richardus Anglicus <note>A 13th-century alchemist</note>.</margin>
If then all this is so—namely, that there is a single and general Material from which the metals are made, and Nature understands this for us as soon as, or according to how, it has separated the foreign and impure sulfur of the other metals from itself through the length of time in constant cooking, becoming perfect Gold and Silver: it is demonstrated to us that it is Nature in its kind: so we must indeed confess and say that Nature also works its change and perfection in its kind in this family according to the purity and subtlety of the subject, just as in the Vegetable and Animal kingdoms, as said above.

<margin>Vegetables <note>original: "Vegetabilia"</note>.</margin>
This, dear seeker of natural things, I have wanted to indicate a little more circumstantially, so that you may understand and grasp all the more thoroughly that we must take the Material of our Stone alone from the Mineral kingdom. For if you should undertake to make our Stone from an Animal material: it stands in your way that these are divided into three kinds, as mentioned above: since the Stone is mineral, but the material is animal. And even if you had a perfect material, which could be drawn out from something that is not in it. Therefore, because every species remains in its species and every genus or family in its genus, it is impossible that one family should turn back or seek another family and bring forth fruit according to its natural property in no other nature that is contrary to it: Thus each thing is matched with its seed of corresponding nature, as **Arnoldus** speaks: Take heed and turn away, my friend, from wanting to transform pure Animal material into the Stone of nature: and blood, as granted and given to animals by the Creator, also belongs to the animals, from which God built them together so that an animal came from it.

<margin>Animals <note>original: "Animalia"</note>.</margin>
Therefore, it is to be wondered at those who consider themselves great artists and seek their Material of the Stone in menstrual blood, in sperm, in eggs, in hair, in blood, and all other sorts of filth, and have filled so many books with such recipes that they take in, fool, and mislead other reasonable people with such worthless things.

<margin>Arnoldus.</margin>
**Roger Bacon** contradicts them in the 3rd chapter of his *Mirror* regarding the folly of such people. And **Arnoldus** in the 1st chapter of the *Rosarium* calls them fools who ground their intention on animal and vegetable things, which are yet far separated, since one finds minerals which lie much closer.

<margin>Thomas Aquinas <note>13th-century theologian often attributed with alchemical works</note>.</margin>
It is also in no way to be believed that any Philosopher chose things so far removed in the observed art, unless by way of analogy. So also our Stone knows: as **Basilius Valentinus** says: nothing of the things which do not withstand the fire, and immediately the Material of the Stone is caught in the fire. Therefore you may well omit searching in animal things, because Nature is not allowed to boil in such. But if someone wanted to claim one could make flesh out of a man, or search from a stone, he will err no less for the above-stated reasons than he who wanted to make the first stone files out of an animal. For all herbs and animals, as is known to the one who comes from them, are combustible, and nothing remains behind but a bare salt with its earth, which it received from nature in its composition. And let no one be misled that some pretend to make the Philosophical Stone from wheat: much less listen to those who understand making this stone from bones and their pieces. For because they do not correctly understand the writings of Raymond Lully, they prove with their great understanding that they understand nothing, and mislead both themselves and others. Indeed, it is not without truth that very magnificent and excellent Menstrua [solvents], without which nothing prominent can be performed either in medicine or alchemy, can be prepared and directed from these. But that the Stone of the Sages could be made from them, or its seed drawn from them, is not permitted to Nature by the Creator of all things, but rather, as mentioned above, confirmed to remain in its kind.

Therefore, every sensible person must easily perceive and conclude that, because our Stone, as said, is inseparable: and the animal and vegetable things are all combustible, it must be sought and made from an incombustible Material, which is found in no other way than in the Mineral kingdom.

Since then our mentioned Philosophical Stone is a Mineral work, it is rightly asked—since there are many kinds, such as stones, among which all sorts of metals and earths are understood, salts, middle minerals, and the like—which kind among these is the right Material?

To this I answer, that it is not possible to make such from stones; the reason is because in them there is no liquid and meltable Mercury, or Sulfur, which thoroughly cooks the metals and brings them to their first Material, because of their too coarse sulfur and superfluity of earthy property. So also no clever seeker of natural secrets will seek such Material of the Philosophical Stone in salts, alums, and similar things, because the lesser minerals are not from the Mercury... and the transformed spirit, but find no Mercury and Sulfur at all, as the Philosophers require and wish to have.

Much less [can one make] a middle mineral, such as a Magnesia, Marcasite, Antimony, etc., from such things. Much less become a metal: how then could the Material of the Philosophical Stone be taken from them, which is yet the end and perfection of all metals and mineral things? And how can the common man take Mercury, who has no fellowship or friendship [with them], but rather they burn, break, and spoil the same—how could they then serve for their improvement? Hear now what Richardus Anglicus states in the 1st chapter of the *Rosarium*: that the metals among themselves are not improved; first, because they are not born from the first Material of the metals, which is Mercury.

In that same generation, with the generation of Mercury, the first is distinguished in form and shape. Therefore, no metals can come from them for our work and stone, since there is one single Material and Seed of a single thing from which it is born. But what is said about it being Mineral will be understood through this and through the *Rosarium Philosophorum*, as is also clear from Aristotle and Avicenna. Therefore, if metals are to be made from them, they must first go into the first Material.

But because this cannot happen by any art, they also cannot be metals, and finally no Material of the Stone. Furthermore, because the mineral ores cannot be the beginning of the art through art, which is why Arnoldus in the *Rosarium Philosophorum* teaches that one cannot [use] the metals and the Tincture: but because the lesser minerals are foreign to the metals in nature, though they may partake in some mineral power, they yet cannot serve for the Stone of the Sages. And even if the metallic nature is not theirs at all, it flows from them, and they keep only what is of their nature. Therefore, those are fools who bring so much and such varied work and deceptions to light to deceive people, namely useless things which have neither nature with them, nor can give it from themselves.

Nor let anyone be misled by the writings of the Philosophers when they say: "our Stone is mineral": for they do not think on that which is in the mines or works... He who works without salt will not awaken the dead corpses. And in the book *Soliloquij* it stands: He who works without salt shoots with a bow without a string. Since they have a very different meaning than that of mineral salts: just as the *Rosarium* is in the beginning of the 1st chapter when it speaks: "the salt of the metals is the Philosophical Stone." For our Stone is a coagulated water, in gold or silver, and resists the fire, but our mineral salts do not: in which it was made in its kind. But that the Philosophers' coagulated water is not mineral water, but their Mercury, Geber teaches in the *Book of Furnaces* in the 23rd chapter when he says: Dissolve for yourself Sun and Moon in a water which is called the Salt of the Sages. The Philosophers also sometimes call their speeches Salt, as seen in *Clangore Buccine* <note>The "Clang of the Trumpet," another alchemical text</note>, where it is said: let the Body become alum and Salt... which are made from our Earth and Slime, and that our Stone in its beginning is not that which stands in the Scale: that is of the other water, that it hardens the earth in the indestructible Salt, alone through its attractive power. And Arnoldus in the *Scala* [Ladder] speaks: our salt is... and that which has no equal in the preservation of youth is the salt from the Tincture: We see those who have it, by which it was prepared, compared to the natural warmth of a healthy youth, and because of this comparison they have also named the Stone with many names, and call many more names: "Mercury smokes," etc., a constant medicine happens, and *Aquam vitæ* [Water of Life]. The whole science of its preparation is that it be reduced into a pure and triune water, which is called Elixir, that is, a Ferment.

<margin>Mercury <note>original: "Mercurius"</note>.</margin>
From this it is now clearly seen that neither according to the teaching of the Philosophers, nor the properties of Nature itself, can the Material of the Stone be taken from the lesser minerals.

Item, let us look around a little to see whether the Material of our Great Stone can be made from the middle minerals, such as Marcasite, Antimony, Magnesia, and others, especially since the Philosophers mention them so often? And especially since Basilius Valentinus teaches how to coagulate Mercury for a long time, so that under mastery it would never come to an end. And Thomas Aquinas: Our Antimony or the "hooked black earth" etc., and Parmenides in the *Turba*: take quicksilver and burn it in the body of Magnesia, or in the Sulfur that does not burn.

It is yet to be known that the Philosophers did not make such speeches with the meaning that their Stone could be made from such materials, which are coarse stones, but rather they speak such only by way of analogy. For the Philosophers' Orpiment and Magnesia is a very different thing from the common ones, namely the Material itself, which they otherwise call the Agent <note>original: "Agens"</note>, the Lion, the Dragon, and by many more names: just as they also called Mercury "Orpiment" because it has the power [to turn] the gold into a superfluous color, and it is called Magnesia because of its great virtue and magnificence, which springs from it as said.

That Thomas Aquinas calls it Antimony, he does so because of the black shimmering color into which it comes after the dissolution. For when our Stone has become black, the Philosophers have compared it to all black things.

But someone might argue and say to me that some of these middle minerals are not only born from Mercury and Sulfur, but also become metals. As one sees that Magnesia or Bismuth goes mixed in flux with Lead and Tin.

Stone, **Antimony** <note>original: "Antimonium"; a brittle, silvery-gray metalloid often used in alchemy to "purify" gold</note> does not only mix with metals, but it naturally becomes lead; likewise, persons of both high and low status have often seen gold produced from it. Since these substances are born from **Sulfur** and **Mercury**—into which they can also be reduced again through art—and share an origin with the metals, could the Matter of the Stone be taken from them? To this I answer: first, a distinction should rightly be maintained among these intermediate minerals—namely, between those that contain Mercury and those that do not. The first group, which contains Mercury, should be held in high regard because their Mercury can be transformed into gold and silver through our Medicine; therefore, in my view, they are rightly called "half-metals," as they have the disposition to become metals. The others, however, which contain no Mercury, are of no account. But because of the thick and burning sulfur found within them, and because it is certain that Antimony is the enemy of all metals and burns them—except for Gold, which can endure the poison of Antimony due to its constancy and be purified by it—one cannot choose them for the Matter of our Stone, which must be a pure, perfect Mercury and a pure, delicate, and incombustible Sulfur. Anyone can easily see how this Matter is entirely infected by its own sulfur if they take it to school and examine what it has "learned" <note>An alchemical metaphor for testing the substance's properties through fire and chemical reaction</note>.

For one sees in **Zinc**, which one might wish to claim as pure Mercury due to its luster and weight: as soon as it enters the fire for roasting, it quickly vanishes in smoke like a pale-yellow sulfur. **Marcasites** <note>Iron pyrites or similar crystalline minerals often mistaken for precious ores</note> are entirely incapable of containing Mercury due to their great earthly impurity; they are merely an ore that can only be cleaned of its excessive blackness through clever manipulation and brought into a very white and beautiful **Regulus** <note>The metallic heart of a mineral left after smelting</note>. It certainly looks as if something special could be made from it, so much so that many who otherwise consider themselves wise believe this Matter must be driven far, even unto the transformation of metals. But regardless of all this, even if Antimony is cleansed of its blackness as much as possible, its "evil nature" remains, along with its blackness and toxicity. This can be recognized because it cannot be extended or forged under the hammer, which is the property of every metal by which it is recognized alongside other characteristics.

But it has a coarse, impure Mercury, not to mention that it still always carries a volatile sulfur with it. And those who consider themselves great Philosophers, and who wish to persuade others through their books and extensive writings that this is the very stamp of their **Universal** <note>The "Universal Medicine" or the Stone that works on all things</note>—we shall kindly tolerate them, though I cannot agree that their opinion is sound. For it is foolish to seek something in a thing that is not there, as **Arnoldus** <note>Arnold of Villanova, a 13th-century physician and alchemist</note> says; and because it is written in the practice of the *Turba*, "Alchemy consists solely in pure Mercury." So also says **Lullius** <note>Ramon Llull</note> in his *Last Testament*: "Our Tincture is nothing but a pure fire." And in the *Vade Mecum* he speaks: "It is only the subtle spirit that tinges <note>colors or transforms</note> and cleanses the bodies of their obstructions." But minerals, like other things, are so coarse and impure that they cannot be cleansed or internally purified by any means except through our Tincture. Therefore, the Matter of our Stone cannot be obtained from them; since the foundation, as **Richardus** <note>Richardus Anglicus</note> says in Chapter 1, is that something cannot be taken from where it does not exist.

But what should one say of **Vitriol** <note>sulfate minerals, often blue or green</note>, which through its wonderful properties leads many into error, especially since a large part of it becomes copper, and it can even transform iron into copper? Concerning this, it is truly to be known that Vitriol is nothing other than a beginning and the Matter of copper. In the ores of the earth, where the mountain vapors and misty Mercury encountered a place where a thick, impure sulfurous spirit and a rich Sulfur were found in great abundance, the Mercury immediately accepted it, coagulated therein, and sought to become a metal. But because the "evil nature" of the earth and sulfur—the thick clouds and vapors—would not yield, and the quantity of the aforementioned Sulfur far exceeded the necessary amount, the Mercury was drawn away in that separation and was transformed and turned into the nature of Vitriol.

This is clearly seen in that if one adds common sulfur (as the cause of this corruption) to copper and calcines it—for Art performs with strong fire in a short time what Nature must accomplish with slow fire—it burns the copper entirely and brings it into the form of Vitriol through common maneuvers. And according to whether there is much or little sulfur, the Vitriol becomes richer or poorer in color. Hence it is said that Vitriol is of no other property than the copper-like one, just as one finds copper where one wishes to dig for **Cupressum** <note>Cyprian copper</note>.

It is also well to note that the acidic spirit within Vitriol stems from the sulfur, since one also draws such acidity out of common sulfur. In blue Vitriol, one can also sense the sulfurous smell, and the **Spirit of Sulfur** <note>sulfuric acid</note> transforms the **Sulfur of Mars** <note>iron sulfur</note> into a Vitriol, just like the **Spirit of Vitriol**. Because there is also a coarse Sulfur in iron, the sharpness of the Vitriol discards it, seeks out this Mercury (which is not very unlike its own), and through the union of its sulfur—which overcomes the Mercury—it becomes good, malleable copper.

If there is such an abundant and harsh sulfur in Vitriol, and so very little Mercury (which has not yet even reached its purification), we shall obtain no more from it than from the others. We must heed the words of **Alphidius** in all this, who says: "My son, beware and separate yourself from the dead stones, for there is no path to walk in them, since their life is not increased but consumed; such as the salts, **Orpiment** <note>arsenic trisulfide, a yellow mineral</note>, **Arsenic**, **Magnesia**, **Marcasite**, and the like."

And Arnoldus says in *Flos Florum*: "The cause of their error is because the four spirits—namely Orpiment, **Salmiac** <note>Sal ammoniac / ammonium chloride</note>, Mercury, and Sulfur—and the stones are outside the perfect and imperfect metals, except for the Mercury and Sulfur from which the Mercury proceeds."

Someone might conclude from these last words of Arnoldus that common Sulfur and Mercury are the Matter of our Stone, because they are placed among the four spirits and because Sulfur coagulates Mercury. To this I must ask, with **Richardus** in Chapter 11, whether every sulfur coagulates Mercury? I answer: no. For every common sulfur, as the Philosopher says, is hostile to metals. It is to be known that sulfur is born from the fatness of the earth in the crevices of the mountains and is made hard by moderate heat; and then it is called sulfur.

However, sulfur is of two kinds: living and combustible. The living is the active part in metals, and when it is purified of all impurity by Nature, it becomes the Matter of our Stone, of which more later.

The combustible and common sulfur, however, is not the Matter of metals or of the Stone, but their enemy. For common and combustible sulfur does not enter into our mastery, say **Avicenna** and **Richardus Anglicus**, because it did not spring from there; it always corrodes, blackens, and destroys, no matter how it is prepared by Art, for it is a destroying fire.

Therefore, it hinders fluidity when it is made. We see an example of this in iron, which carries a constant, coarse, and impure sulfur with it. When that is burned, an earthly being comes from it, like a dead body. How could this give life to others? For it has a twofold excess: namely, that which allows itself to be ignited, and the Earthly.

From this one understands common sulfur, and not that of the Philosophers, which is a living, vital juice that makes our dead bodies alive and ripens them, etc. Therefore, common sulfur cannot be the Matter of our Stone. But what shall we say of common quicksilver? Since all Philosophers say that the Matter of our Stone is a Mercurial substance, and it has many properties attributed to impure quicksilver: one is that it is the beginning of all things that can be melted, as Arnoldus says in *Rosarius* Book 1, Chapter 2. For all meltable things, when they are melted, become of a watery nature and mix with it, because it is of their substance—even though the bodies in their composition are different from quicksilver, depending on whether it is pure or impure and left with pure or foreign sulfur. And in Chapter 4 he says: "**Mercurius vivus** <note>living mercury/quicksilver</note> is, in all its effects, artificial and the most perfect and constant thing. For it protects against burning and causes flow when it is made, and is the Tincture for redness, and a spirit that penetrates the bodies, and a water that cleanses them of the mixture as long as it lasts; and it is friendly and sociable, and the means to join the Tinctures together because it mixes through and through with them and is related to their nature, since it is of their nature. It alone is that which overcomes the fire, but is not overcome by the fire; rather, it rejoices in it and rests therein."

But there are some who, obsessed with this nature, have in their considerations no other Matter than the common, as the Mercurial form. In this Mercury lies the constant and incombustible sulfur which completes our work, without any other substance than the pure Mercurial substance. Since there are such glorious virtues in Mercury, must it not necessarily follow that it is the Matter of our Stone? To this we answer that just as there are two kinds of sulfur, so there are two kinds of Mercury: the common and that of the Philosophers. Common Mercury is still a raw, unripe, and volatile spirit which does not remain in the fire like the philosophical kind, but is turned into smoke by gentle heat and chased away. Therefore the Philosophers commonly say: "Our quicksilver is not common quicksilver." So also says Lullius in his *Clavicula*, Chapter 1: "We say that common quicksilver cannot be the quicksilver of the Philosophers, with whatever art it may be prepared; for the common kind cannot be kept in the fire unless it happens through another bodily quicksilver that is warm, dry, and more ripe."

Most Philosophers have written of the glory of pure Mercury in pure metals according to their abundance, from which many wonderful works have arisen, and the nature and property of this subject has been learned all the more. But the purpose for which they spoke—namely, the great treasure of earthly wisdom—has never yet been found therein, because the manner was not shown to them. Truly, it appears so wondrous in its work that it might seduce one who calls himself wise. For example: I have seen one who colored it with wool and hair so that he dealt entirely with it, bringing it through all colors until **Citrinitas** <note>the "yellowing" stage of the alchemical process</note>.

But when it would not yield itself from such a color and he gave it stronger fire, it smoked away, and he failed; and according to the Philosophers, since the way with fire cannot be mistaken, the Mercury is volatile. But the Mercury flew up the chimney, carrying the gold with it and consuming it. From which, nonetheless, much gold was later gathered and reduced again.

Thus it has been seen that common Mercury, being a body itself, can neither exclude nor act within another body—namely gold—except perhaps with colors, because the heat acts upon the moisture revealed therein. Had he wished to make it into the Philosopher’s Stone, as Arnoldus says in *Flos Florum*, he would not have proceeded in such a manner. For thus speaks Arnoldus when talking of such laborers: "They chose dry bodies and moistened them until the weight was the beginning and origin of the metals; with the sulfurous and choking heat, they sublimated the Mercury for themselves, thereafter they fixed it, they distilled and coagulated it, but when they came to the **profection** <note>the final stage or success</note>, they found nothing, etc."

Therefore, we cannot consider common quicksilver as the Matter of our Stone. We have hitherto sought the materials of our Stone in animals, in vegetables, in stones, and in the intermediate and coarser minerals. Yet we have not found them. We must therefore look further to see if we can find it in the metals; and if we find it, whether it is in all of them at once, or only in some, and in which it is to be met. For it is well known and attested by **Roger Bacon** in his *Speculum*, Chapter 3, that all metals are born of sulfur and quicksilver, and that there is nothing else in them, nor does anything foreign join them, except for the impurity that comes to them from without. Since every improvement of a thing increases the nature of that from which it is, as Richardus says in Chapter 1, and otherwise in all of nature, each thing is ordained by its Creator to bring forth and bear its own kind, etc. Thus nothing can become metal except from metal. And just as irrational animals cannot bring forth their like for increase except through the nature of their like, so you must not undertake to make metals from things other than metals, and much less the Stone, except from its own seed from which our Stone itself was made from the beginning.

But to find this seed, you must consider within yourself to what you wish to apply our Stone; then it will become known to you that it must flow from nowhere else but a metallic root, from which the metals themselves are ordained by the Creator to be drawn. So there is also a great similarity in the "conversion" of metals and of the great Stone, since in one and the other, the sulfur and quicksilver—which contain the **Calis** <note>likely *calx*, the refined powder</note> as the noble soul within them—are necessarily required. It is not possible to obtain the salvation of utility in metallic form until these three, taken from metallic being, are brought together into one body, because nothing must be added to metals that does not have its origin from them.

<column-break/>

Thus it is manifest, as **Bacon** says, that no other thing which did not take its origin from Mercury and Sulfur is sufficient to make these perfect and bring them to transformation. Therefore, for the birth of the great Stone, a metallic Materia must necessarily be taken. Whether this can come from the imperfect metals, we shall examine briefly.

Many are found who wish to have the *materia lapidis* <note>matter of the stone</note> for the "white" from Tin or Lead, and for the "red" from Copper or Iron, or both—doubtless misled by the Philosophers themselves. For thus speaks **Geber** in *Liber Fornacum*, Chapter 9: "The dough which one wishes to ferment, we make, as is our custom, from the imperfect bodies." And hence we give the common rule that the white dough is drawn from **Jupiter and Saturn** <note>Tin and Lead</note>, but the red from **Mars and Saturn or Mars and Venus** <note>Iron, Lead, or Copper</note>. So also **Basilius Valentinus** <note>Basil Valentine, a legendary 15th-century alchemical monk</note> teaches in his book *Of Natural and Supernatural Things* to make a Tincture from the conjunction of Mars and Venus.

And in his other book *Of the Tincture* he says: "The Tincture of Sol and Luna <note>Gold and Silver</note> etc., from white; further the Tincture of **Victor or Venus** <note>Copper</note>, likewise the Tincture of Mars, both of which also contain the Tincture of Sol within them, if they have previously been brought to strong constancy." These are followed by the Tincture of Jupiter and Saturn until the coagulation of Mercury, and thereafter the Tincture of Mercury itself.

But the researcher of natural secrets should know that this was not the meaning of the Philosophers regarding the imperfect metals in their coarse sense; otherwise, they would speak against themselves, which cannot be, since the Wise must never lie in their writings, even if they hide the truth in hidden words. For ultimately, the Matter of our Stone cannot be found in the imperfect metals alone, nor in all of them together, so that they might be improved in the slightest. From them alone such a thing cannot be, because in the Matter of our Stone the purest essence of Mercury and Sulfur is required, as Avicenna, Lullius, and all Philosophers generally say: "We must choose a pure Mercurial substance for our work." But this purest substance of Mercury cannot be found naturally in the imperfect metals, because they are infected by their foreign and impure sulfur and are too unclean. They are therefore like "leprous bodies" <note>a common alchemical term for impure metals</note> that cannot be brought to internal and perfect purification by any art; they do not remain constant in the fire, except for Gold and Silver, which is the only material that remains constant in the fire—but that does not belong here yet.

Let us hear what Geber writes in his *Summa*, Chapter 63, regarding the purification of these imperfect metals and the properties of the perfect Mercury: "Through this we have found our art, and discovered ourselves from the poor state of two secrets: one being that there are three causes for the destruction of every (imperfect) metal by fire. The first is that the burning impurity of the sulfur found in them, ignited by strong fire, consumes the entire essence of the bodies, chases it into smoke, and finally destroys it, however good their quicksilver may be."

"The second cause is that the metallic flame is increased by them, passes through them, and expels them in smoke, as also happens."

"The third cause is that their bodies can be opened by calcination; then the flame of the fire can pass through them and chase them into smoke. This also happens when the quicksilver and sulfur do not join correctly; the bodies must necessarily perish and come to nothing. But if they are not together, the swift movement of the fire endures for a moment. The second part of this secret is the goodness which teaches us how Mercury becomes so wonderful by nature. For quicksilver, because there are no causes of destruction or absorption within it, cannot be divided into parts of its joining, but remains constant with its fire; it is a wonderful thing, and one must necessarily measure the cause of its perfection. Therefore let us praise the highly exalted and highest God, who created it and gave it the essence that the Philosophers could not give—things that may be found in nature. For it is a wonderful thing to be brought into the works which we find therein in its **proximate power** <note>original: "potentia propinqua"; a philosophical term meaning it is ready to be transformed</note>. For this is that which overcomes the fire, but is not overcome by the fire; rather, it rests friendly therein and rejoices."

With these recounted words, Geber proves without fail that the Matter of our Stone cannot be in the imperfect metals, because they are not constant in themselves, and if one wished to purify them, they would flow away entirely. But our Mercury, due to its purity, is constant in the fire, and nothing can harm it.

But there is still a new confusion upon this, which is entirely contrary to our intention; as mentioned above, only a single Matter is required. **Haly** <note>likely Khalid ibn Yazid or another Arabic authority</note> attests to this clearly when he says in *Liber Secretorum*, Chapter 9: "It is one stone from which we must make our Matter; let the wise work with this, and from this flows what is commonly said: nothing else is mixed with it, neither in the whole nor in its parts."

And **Morienus** <note>Morienus Romanus, a legendary hermit-alchemist</note> says: "This mastery stems initially from one root, which thereafter divides into many parts, and finally is turned back into the one." If the imperfect metals cannot be the Matter of the Stone, why then did the Philosophers command us to work with them? To this I answer: when the Philosophers command taking the impure bodies, they did not mean copper or iron, but the Matter as they understood it from the Earth, as Arnoldus says in *Flos Florum*: "Mercury is attributed to the Earth, that is, to the imperfect body." For though their Earth is impure, they have the Art to make something from it; yet it is still impure with respect to the *lapis Physicus* <note>Physical Stone</note> and must be purified.

And herein Art surpasses Nature, because it performs that which Nature could not complete. But that this Earth, as said, is imperfect before perfect purification is attested by Arnoldus, when he says that it cannot yet "tinge" <note>transform other metals</note> and make perfect, and has no more than what Nature has bestowed upon it. But when it is "born again," it becomes a "new man" and is a spirit that can be led generally into the imperfect metals. In this state, it is entirely black and is then compared to Iron or Antimony; thereafter it becomes Gray and is called **Jupiter** or Tin or Bismuth; and thereafter it becomes White. After the white, it is called **Mars and Venus** before it is brought to complete Redness. That Basilius Valentinus is of the same opinion and seeks something far different than what he describes in the aforementioned book, he himself testifies in his treatise *Of the Great Stone*, where he investigates the *materia lapidis* and says: "In Sol <note>Gold</note> the gift of all three powers is together, and therefore it exists as the power of fire." And of Luna <note>Silver</note>, because of its free Mercury, it does not fly away so quickly in the fire and stands the test. He finally speaks: "The redeemer Venus <note>Copper</note> is clothed and overtaken with excessive color, and her white body is pure Tincture and exactly such a color as also dwells in the best metal, showing red for the sake of excess. And because her body is [impure/flimsy], the constant Tincture can have no permanent dwelling but must disappear along with her body. For where the body is consumed by death <note>destruction in fire</note>, the soul also cannot remain but must yield and flee. For the dwelling is destroyed and burned with fire, so that its place is not to be recognized, nor may any further dwell there. In a fatter body, however, she dwells gladly with pleasure." The constant Salt has assigned and left to the combative **Mars** <note>Iron</note> a hard, rigorous, and coarse body, from which the bravery of his spirit is proven; and this war-lord is not easily overcome, for his body is so hard that one cannot easily wound him. But if someone were to say that one has a constant Sulfur, that same sulfur must, according to Basil, be taken from Mars; then it must be asked if it can be made from it. Consider what has been said many times and drawn from Basil himself: that our Matter must not be taken from many things together, because the **Universal** is a single thing, and is found and drawn out of a single thing at once. Whether it be the Spirit of Mercury and the Soul of Sulfur along with the Spiritual Salt, all united together, enclosed under one heaven and dwelling in one body, one will recognize—because the Universal is a perfect thing—and turn one's thoughts without further pondering. Consider the saying of **Plato** <note>Plato in an alchemical context, likely Pseudo-Plato</note> in the second part: "Why do you calcine and dissolve the other bodies with great labor, when you can find in one (single thing) what you seek? But if you ever wish to use them, it is necessary that you first turn them into the nature of the perfect bodies."

Therefore, you faithful searcher of natural secrets, abandon all animal and vegetable things, all salts, alum, vitriol, arsenic, orpiment, magnesias, antimony, all imperfect and impure metals, and seek your Stone with **Arnold of Villanova** (*Rosarium* Part 1, Chapter 7) in the Mercury and Sulfur of Nature, which is in Gold and Silver, since the entire art is hidden herein. For just as fire is the beginning of making a fire, says **Ripley** <note>George Ripley, a 15th-century English alchemist</note> in the First Gate, so also Gold is the beginning of gold-making. When it is meant to make gold and silver through the Philosophical art, do not take just any gold and silver, but such a kind which, naturally and wisely—but not by hand—calcined, brings forth a new birth, which no creature increases like all other things.

It is to be known, as Richardus says in Chapter 10, that one should seek no other Gold, so that they may bring forth fruit with our work and the mediation of Nature; since they have it within them and are that which one seeks, and nothing else. And why should one not choose these two, since they have a pure and perfect Mercury and Sulfur with them, as Richardus testifies in Chapter 12? Avicenna says that in every Gold there is a red sulfur. Such sulfur is not found upon the earth, but only from the further **Elixir** <note>the prepared substance of the Stone</note>, like ours; no other matter is so subtle that one may have the Sulfur and Mercury from such matter from which gold and silver are made under the earth. For these are the Matter which Nature has made into metals, which tinge the other bodies into true redness and whiteness after they are purified. As Arnoldus says in *Rosarius* Book 1, Chapter 5: "The mastery helps the perfect bodies and makes the imperfect ones perfect without the mixture of any other thing." For there is no other Elixir than ours; no other Redness of redness, coloring and transforming every body. Silver is the Tincture of whiteness, which colors all bodies into true whiteness.

So the kindhearted reader learns from this that the metals Gold and Silver, in their metallic form, are the Matter of our Stone; they are the middle ground between our Matter and the perfect metals, just as our Matter is the middle ground between them and our Great Stone. But hear what Arnoldus further says in his *Rosarius* Book 1, Chapter 7, at the conclusion of his booklet: "Let those be silent who do not have our Tincture, but another which cannot truly or apparently be transformed into any metal; and let those be silent who say that there should be another sulfur than ours, which is hidden in the belly of the **Magnesia** <note>an alchemical term for a "raw" mineral substance</note>."

"Also, let those be silent who wish to draw out another *Argentum vivum* <note>living silver / quicksilver</note>, for only from the ferment of a metal can it be; and those who wish to draw out another water than our 'ever-living' water, which mixes with nothing else but that which is of its own nature, nor softens or dissolves any other thing but that which is of its own metallic nature. And let us hear what kind of Elixir it should be: no other Elixir than ours; no other colors than ours; no other sublimation than ours; no other dissolution than ours; no other putrefaction than ours; no other Matter than ours."

Therefore, abandon all salts, alum, vitriol and all other **atramenta** <note>vitriolic or blackening substances</note>, borax, strong waters <note>acids like nitric acid</note>, and all herbs, animals, beasts and what comes from them—hair, blood, urine, human seed, flesh, eggs, and mines—for the metallic art is not in them. For in every work, the entrance and our Matter are from them, and according to the testimony of all Philosophers, Gold and Silver should be joined together, and such is found in no other thing than in the metals, as appears from Geber, etc.

Yet they are not our Stone so long as they remain in their metallic form; for it can only be from the ferment of a metal, and have another water with it at the same time. How then can they be the Stone, which has a worthy and intermediate form between the metal and Mercury, if they are not first destroyed and the metallic form taken from them? Therefore speaks also **Raymund Lullius** in the 56th chapter of his *Testament*: "Therefore the good artist takes the metals for the 'middle' in the work of mastery, and indeed the perfect ones, namely Gold and Silver, because both are of a moderate equality and great purity of sulfur and mercurial essence." They are sought out, pure and well-ripened through the action of Nature, to which proportion the artist allows them to flow together, beginning to fulfill his purpose through his artificial beginnings without crude means.

And further he speaks in his *Codicillus*: "Without these two, namely Gold and Silver, the mastery cannot be completed, because herein is the purest substance of Sulfur, which Nature has entirely and perfectly purified. To reach such purification, Art is much weaker than Nature and cannot attain it, even if it exerts itself greatly."

From these two teachings, when they are prepared with their Sulfur or Arsenic, our Medicine can be made; but without them, by no means. And in the preface to his *Key* <note>original: "Clavicula"</note> he says: "I advise you, O my friends, that you work in nothing but **Sol and Luna**, bringing them back into their first Matter, namely into our Sulfur and Mercury. For from the bodies," says Arnoldus in *Rosarius* Book 1, Chapter 7, "the very wise and red Sulfur is drawn out, because therein is most especially the purest sulfurous substance, enchanted to the highest degree by Nature."

<meta>This page continues the investigation into the "Materia" or starting material of the Great Work. Having previously rejected common minerals like Antimony and Zinc, the author now turns to the testimony of the ancient alchemical masters to define the true nature of "Philosophical Gold" and "Philosophical Silver," which are distinct from the common metals used for currency.</meta>

Thus speaks Nicarius in the *Assembly of the Philosophers* <note>original: "Turba"; referring to the *Turba Philosophorum*, one of the oldest Latin alchemical texts</note>: I command those who follow to take the gold they wish to multiply and renew, and thereafter divide the water into two parts. One part shall be used to press the gold together; for the Ore, when it falls into the water, shall be called the Ferment of the Gold. But how does the Philosopher here call his water "gold" when he says that when the Ore falls into this water, it shall be the Ferment of Gold? My follower who seeks this Art should know that the gold of the Philosophers is not common gold, as Senior <note>Zadith ben Hamuel, a 10th-century alchemist</note> says, and as is stated in the first exercise of the *Assembly*: just as Mercury is the beginning of all metals, so the Sun <note>Gold</note> is the end and last of the metals. All metals, whether pure or impure, contain within them the Sun, the Moon, and Mercury <note>Gold, Silver, and Quicksilver</note>. But there is only one true Sun which is drawn out from them.

Thus you understand that the gold of the Philosophers is a very different Sun or Gold than common gold, for it has already been extracted from it. Alphidius also speaks a good word on this: Know for a truth that Philosophical Gold is not common gold, neither in color nor in substance. Therefore it is said that it gladdens the heart of man, and silver does the same. But that which is extracted from them is a white and red, true, fixed, and living Tincture. Moreover, it is the Philosophical Gold that one does not need to buy dearly, as Alphidius says; and Morienes <note>a legendary hermit-alchemist</note> says that every person has as much of it as they need. Geber says: When we buy what we want with a little of this thing and with a little gold. Morienes says our gold is living gold, and our silver is living silver, which can bring nothing other than life and increase. Common gold and silver are dead; they have performed no more than what was granted to them by Nature, until they are awakened again from the dead by the wise artist and regain their life; then they live and can increase their own kind. And Sendivogius <note>Michael Sendivogius, a famous Polish alchemist</note> says something of this: But the water of the common [metals], and the life of our metals, is described by the excellent, still-living philosopher Michael Sendivogius in the 11th Treatise of his booklet on the Philosophical Stone thus: You should be warned not to take dead gold or silver, but the living, which has a certain life. Thereafter, place them in our fire, and a dry moisture will come from them. First, dissolve the earth in the water—which is the Mercury of the Philosophers—and from this water or Spirit, the bodies of the Sun and Moon <note>original: "Corpora Solis & Lunæ"</note> are dissolved and consumed, so that no more than a tenth part remains with one part, and this is the deep-rooted moisture of the metals.

Now, to speak further of the Gold of the Philosophers, it must be known that the Philosophers call not only their water "gold," but also their earth. Regarding the first method <note>original: "Modo"</note>, the philosopher Nicarius spoke above, and the *Rosary of the Philosophers* <note>original: "Rosarius Philosophorum"</note> confirms it with the following words: What do you say to this, that the Philosophers speak thus: "Our gold is no common gold, and our silver is no common silver"? To this I say: they call the water their "gold" which rises up to the heights through the power of the fire; and this gold is truly not common gold. For the common man would not be able to believe that it could rise to the heights due to its subtlety.

But that the Philosophers also called their earth "gold" is likewise testified by this *Rosary*, where it says: Know that the Ore of the Philosophers is gold. And Albi says: The Stone is gold. It is called gold because we have called their water the white and foliated earth. Therefore the author who wrote the *Blast of the Trumpet* <note>original: "Clangorem Buccinæ"</note> says in the chapter on dissolution <note>original: "solution"</note> (as Hermes also speaks): Sow your gold in the white and foliated earth, which has been made subtle through calcination <note>the process of reducing a solid to powder via heat</note> and well-dissolved; which is to say: sow the gold—that is, the soul and tinging power—into the white earth, which has become white and pure through proper preparation, and in which there is no water.

From this it is evident that the gold of nature is not the material of the ferment, but the gold of the Philosophers is the tinging ferment. And in the *Ladder of the Philosophers* <note>original: "Scala Philosophorum"</note>, grade 7, it stands thus: Their earth, in which their gold is sown, is white; their soul is gold; the Body is the place of Wisdom where it is gathered; it is the dwelling of the Tincture.

And a little further on, this author says: Therefore Hercules says: Pour our gold—that is, dissolve the Magnet-Lead <note>original: "Leid Magnetis"</note>, which has become white and like the brilliance of heaven. And it is the third thing that flows into the best, and the gold that is extracted from it is called the gold of the Philosophers, and it is a Tincture, which is a soul. For with the water, the Spirit rises up into the higher air, and this white Body—when the gold has become white—they have called "our gold" according to our weakness. Therefore Senior says: Mix gold and gold—that is, water and ashes. And Hermes: Sow gold in foliated earth. This is why the Philosophers write that our gold is not common gold.

Someone might say and ask regarding the aforementioned opinion: why do the Philosophers understand their gold sometimes as water and sometimes as earth? It appears that they either contradict themselves and are not in agreement, or that they do this intentionally to mislead their followers. To this the answer is: all and every Philosopher, when they showed the truth most clearly, wrapped it again in hidden speech. They were not in disagreement, but agree with one another wonderfully, as if they all spoke with one mouth. They create no confusion and do not seek to mislead the worthy follower, but have placed all their secrets clearly—yet in figurative speech—before everyone's eyes. They darken and hide them from the unworthy and godless, as much as the highest God has granted them grace, so that such noble pearls are not thrown before swine who wish to serve nothing but their own desires, and so that Nature is not trampled underfoot. This is proven by the Work itself in the present question.

For the good-hearted follower of our Art has been sufficiently informed, and it has been repeated many times, not only from where our Material should be obtained, but also that it consists of one Material which, through the artist's wisdom, is dissolved into two things: namely, Water and Earth, or Mercury and Sulfur. Now, if the Philosophers call the water "gold" or the earth "gold," they do no wrong, and it is within their free will to call it what they wish. Indeed, they also call their Stone "gold," a "true perfect gold," a "reborn gold," and many other such names. That everyone cannot immediately understand their meaning is to be attributed to the protective jealousy <note>original: "Mißgunst," literally "envy" or "grudging," but here referring to the intentional secrecy of alchemists</note> regarding the secrets of the noble Philosophers.

And so the art-loving researcher of super-earthly secrets has been abundantly and completely informed about the Material of our Great Stone: that it must be drawn from no vegetable growth, from no animals or anything taken from them, from no mineral, from no imperfect metal, but from gold and silver—and that our gold and our silver are not common and dead gold and silver, but the gold and silver of the Wise, which is living gold and living silver.

Now it is also necessary that he be instructed regarding that Dissolution <note>original: "Solution"</note>, which is one of the greatest secrets of the entire Work.

<column-break/>

Now to speak of Dissolution: it happens when one makes a dry thing wet, a hard thing soft, and a hidden thing manifest. That is, when a hard thing is transformed into water—not indeed into common water, as the philosophers Parmenides and Agadmon teach in the *Assembly* when they say: "There are some who, when they hear of the dissolution of bodies, imagine it is the water of the clouds." If they had read and understood our books, they would know that our water is permanent. Rather, it is transformed into the water of the Philosophers, which is the First Matter <note>original: "erste Materiam"</note>, as Arnoldus says in the *Rosary*, book 1, chapter 9: The work of the philosophers is to dissolve their Stone into their Mercury; that is, to bring it back into its First Matter.

And Avicenna says: Because you wish to work, it is necessary for you to labor first in the dissolution and sublimation of the two spirits, which is the first step of the Work, so that quicksilver may come from it. Therefore Arnoldus also describes the Dissolution in book 2, chapters 1 and 2, as being a separation of the parts and a preparation of the first matter.

And Richardus Anglicus says: The beginning of our Work is to dissolve the Stone into its First Matter; it is the union of Body and Spirit, so that a Mercurial Water comes from it. Just as Dissolution is the first and most necessary part of our Work, it is also the most difficult part of it, as Eubaldus Vogelius correctly testifies when he says: "Those who have labored in it can testify what a difficult work Dissolution is."

And Bernard, Count of Trevisan <note>original: "Bernhardus Graue von Trefne"</note>, writes in his letter to Thomas of Bologna: He who knows the art and science of Dissolution has reached the secret of the Art, which is to mix the forms and to draw the natures out of the natures which lie powerfully hidden within. For such a Dissolution must not be done with sharp waters <note>acids like nitric acid</note>, for all sharp waters destroy and ruin the Body which they are meant to dissolve and prepare for increase. Instead, our Mercury is our dissolving water—not a water that wets the hands, but rather a "dry water," which is the First Matter, called both Mercury and Sulfur.

Zeumon speaks of this in the *Assembly* thus: If you do not grind, break, moisten, and diligently rule the bodies until you extract their oiliness and make an incomprehensible spirit, you labor in vain.

And Richardus Anglicus speaks of Dissolution, saying that the Philosophers have pondered diligently how they might dissolve the sulfur out of the perfect bodies and improve their properties through Art, so that something is created which Nature does not possess—things never before seen in them, even though they were perfectly hidden within them. They say this can never happen without the dissolution of the body and its restoration into the First Matter, which is nothing other than the quicksilver from which they were originally composed, and this without any mixture or addition of a foreign thing. For our bodies dissolve into no water except that which is of their own kind, and which can be made thick by the bodies, says Bernard in his epistle to Thomas of Bologna. And a little before in the same letter he says: Dissolution requires a mutual incorporation, namely of the dissolver and the dissolved, so that from both—the male and female seeds—a new form may spring. I tell you in all truth that no water dissolves the metallic form through Natural reduction except that which remains with them in matter and form, and which brings the dissolving bodies back into their First Matter.

And Morfoleus speaks in the *Assembly*: Every body is dissolved with the spirit with which it is mixed, and without doubt becomes spiritual with it; and the golden ash is harvested from the bodies and colored by that spirit with which a tinging color, stable against the fire, is mixed.

If all this is so, the art-seeking disciple must diligently observe what kind of water the Philosophers mean in their Dissolution. As Raymond Lully <note>original: "Raymundus"</note> says in the *Compendium of the Soul* <note>original: "Compendio animæ"</note>, it is one of those things without which nothing can be accomplished in the mastery of this Art. For there is an earth in the world in which Mercury flows, and in the epistle in his *Codicil* he calls it "our Menstruum" <note>a solvent</note>; for this is the water with which the metals are dissolved while their forms are preserved.

Because this is the great secret which the Philosophers have not only kept most highly hidden in all their writings but have also been forbidden to reveal, I will nevertheless lead you—as far as I am permitted—onto the right path with two philosophical sayings. The first is contained in the book of the *Rosary*: Dissolution is twofold. The first is the foundation of the Art: the dissolution or the bringing back of the Body into water, which is quicksilver. This they have called Dissolution when they said: "Let the gold hidden in the body of the leprosy be dissolved so that it becomes its First Matter." And this is so that sulfur and quicksilver may come from it, not that it is turned back into water; for our Dissolution is nothing other than the Body becoming moist again and restored to the nature of quicksilver, and the Spirit being reduced to the nature of Sulfur—which holy Sulfur is drawn and made from two sulfurs when the Spirit meets the Body.

The other saying is set down by Ripley in the preface to his *Twelve Gates*: I will teach you in truth that the metals are in their Mercury, which are the keys of science, and which Raymond called his "Menstrua," without which nothing is done correctly. Two of these are "repelling," which one praises for Dissolution, but the third is the essential Mercury of the Sun and Moon, whose properties I will explain to you. For the essential Mercury of the other metals is the most noble material of our Stone. In this "other," our menstrua are not seen with the eyes, but only through the effect.

This is our Stone, if someone understands our writings correctly. It is the soul and glittering essence of the Sun and Moon, and the very subtle airy nature by which the earth receives its sharpness. For what is gold and silver, asks Avicenna, other than a pure white and red earth? If that aforementioned shine is taken from them, the earth will be held in very low esteem. But when it is commonly esteemed, we call it "our Lead." The property of the shine stems from the Sun and the Moon; and these, in summary, are our Menstrua.

We calcine the perfect bodies naturally with the first [menstruum]; but no impure body is added to it except one, which is commonly called the "Green Lion" by the Philosophers, which is the medium for joining the tinctures between the Sun and Moon with perfection.

With the second [menstruum], which is a vegetable moisture that makes living what was previously dead, both material beginnings—as well as the formal one—shall be dissolved; otherwise they are of little value.

With the third, which is an altogether permanent moisture, incombustible and of its own nature, the Tree of Hermes is burned to ashes. This is our philosophical natural fire, our Mercury, our Sulfur, our

<column-break/>

Tincture: our soul, our Stone, which was lifted up by the wind and born into the earth. Guard this well in your heart.

I may tell you that this Stone is a potential vapor of sulfur; but you must be careful how you obtain it. For this Menstruum is in truth invisible, although it can appear to the sight in the form of a clear water through the other Philosophical water when the elements are separated.

From and with this Menstruum, the Sulfur of Nature can be made through excessive labor, when it is naturally sharpened and calcined to a pure spirit; then you can dissolve your basin or mass <note>original: "massam"</note> with it.

Now that the seeker of the Art has heard this, the entire secret of Dissolution is discovered. I will now hold together with the moderation of Nature and consider that you might mock all works that Nature performs—that is, how Nature must unwind again in her clear, pure, clear Stone—then you will be able to find all truth therein quite specifically. But if you still cannot notice where the gates are bolted, then look into yourself and do not merely contemplate the power of Nature; for no spectacles or eyeglasses can bring you there, but rather a fervent prayer followed by diligent study and the contemplation of Nature's moderation.

After the revelation of the high and great God, I learned this solely and alone from diligent study and frequent repetition of good books; not indeed that I learned the material from them—which I learned only from divine revelation and diligent contemplation of Nature, confirmed and reconfirmed therein—as well as the dissolution, which is only one single method among all Philosophers, and without which neither the ancient nor modern Philosophers can accomplish anything. Therefore they have called it the "Secret of the Art" <note>original: "Secretum artis"</note> and the "Arcanum of the Philosophers" <note>original: "Arcanum Philosophorum"</note>, which shall remain so for eternity. I have understood all the work, for which I give praise, honor, and glory with my mouth and heart to the Creator of all things, now and forever, Amen.

Although I previously promised not to burden myself further, I will tell you a little more through the love of God: some would want to know if there is only one single Dissolution, or if it is divided according to "before and after" <note>original: "secundum Prius & posterius"</note>, as they are accustomed to say in the schools.

To this I answer: The first Dissolution is the resolving of the matter into its First Matter, as mentioned above. The other, however, is the perfect dissolution of the Body and Spirit together, when that which dissolves and that which is dissolved remain together; and with this dissolution of the Body, the coagulation of the Spirit occurs. Here, my dear reader, you can see with your own eyes everything you desire to read and know. And yet it is but a single work of the Wise, and a "child's play" because of the little labor required for it. He who understands all this correctly—and since he who knows the beginning well will also, through God's blessing, attain the end—will see all this glory with his own lovely eyes. It is an image of the eternal glory that awaits us when we are translated to our heavenly fatherland, teaching us to despise all earthly lusts and seek only the infinite and inexpressible joy.

With this I will conclude this short treatise, and whatever else you need to know, I will place clearly before your eyes without any lack or error in the following Parable, in which you will simultaneously find the entire Practice. If you contemplate it diligently, you will reach the desired end and true wisdom. May God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit, praised for all eternity, help you and all of us and preserve us in this, Amen.

Once, as I was walking in a beautiful, green, and young forest, contemplating the miseries of this life and weeping over how we have fallen into such misery and sorrow through the grievous fall of our first parents, I strayed from the common path while continuing in such thoughts. It happened that I came upon a narrow, almost unbeaten, thorny path, which was so overgrown with bushes and shrubs that it was difficult to see if such a path had ever been used. Therefore, I initially wanted to turn back, but this was no longer in my power, as such a strong wind blew from behind me that I could sooner take ten steps forward than one step back.

Therefore, I had to wander on and pay no mind to the rough steps. Having traveled far on such a path, I finally came to a lovely meadow, which was surrounded by many beautiful green trees like a round circle and called by the inhabitants the Meadow of Happiness <note>original: "Pratum felicitatis"</note>. There I found a group of old men, all with ice-gray beards, except for one who was a fairly young man whose beard was still black, as well as one among them whose name was known to me, but whose face I could not quite see at the time. He was even younger. They were disputing about all kinds of things, especially about a high and great secret hidden in Nature, which God kept hidden from the great world and revealed only to the few who love Him.

I listened to them for a long time, and their discourse pleased me very well, though some wanted to stray from the point—not regarding the material or the work, but concerning the parables, similarities, and other accessories. In this they followed the fictions of Aristotle, Pliny, and others, which each had copied from the other. I could no longer contain myself, but mixed my "mustard" <note>idiom for "offered my opinion"</note> in with theirs and refuted such empty things from experience. For this reason, the majority of them tested me in their faculty, putting me through the fire <note>original: "durch die Brände jagten"; to cross-examine or test severely</note>, but my foundation was so good that I stood with all honor. They were all amazed by this and unanimously decided to accept me into their college, at which I rejoiced from the heart.

But, they said, I could not yet be a true colleague until I first came to know their Lion properly, and knew perfectly what he could do both inwardly and outwardly. Therefore, I should apply myself diligently to making him subject to me. I was confident in myself and wanted to try; I wanted to do my best, for their company pleased me so much that I would not have left them for anything.

They led me to the Lion and described him to me very diligently. But when I first looked at him, I was greatly terrified: for he was not only like Adam in meaning, so large and significant that thousands could not understand him, but they told me nothing further except that I must make him firm and know how to guard myself against his sharp claws and teeth. Now the Lion was very old, fierce, and large; his yellow mane hung over his neck, and he seemed entirely invincible, so that I soon regretted my rashness <note>original: "Temeritet"</note> and would have gladly turned back if my promise—and the fact that the old and young were all looking at me, waiting to see what I would do—had not held me back. I stepped toward the Lion with good confidence in his pit and began to flatter him. He, however, looked at me with his fiery eyes so fiercely that I could hardly keep my feet for fear. Nevertheless, I remembered having heard among my good friends that many people had ventured into the lion's pit, but few had subdued him because they did not know how to handle him correctly; even when they thought they had subdued him, it had not happened rightly or truly.

While I was in these thoughts and had learned through this athletic struggle—moreover, I was well-versed in Natural Magic—I forgot the coaxing and attacked the Lion so nimbly, bravely, and skillfully that before he was aware, I pulled the blood out of his body, even out of his heart, which was so red but very chloric <note>likely meaning greenish or bile-like</note>. I anatomized further and found in him, to my great wonder, that his inner bones were as white as snow, and there were many more of them than of his other parts.

When my dear elders, who were standing above around the pit watching me, noticed this, they disputed heatedly among themselves, as much as I could hear through their noise. Because I was deep down in the pit, I could not hear what they were saying to one another. I did not want to cause further delay and left the pit with them for an open space. I found myself—I know not how—on a very high wall, the height of which rose over 100 cubits toward the clouds; yet at the top it was not even a foot wide. From top to bottom it was fastened with an iron railing, or rather iron hand-holds, right in the middle of the wall with many enclosed stairs. On this wall I came, I say, and it seemed to me that someone was walking some distance ahead of me, carrying something in his hand, but I did not know what.

I also saw some who wanted to go that way fall down behind me. Therefore, with the help of my iron railing, holding on tight with my hands, I got through and thus went onto the other path. I had just reached the part of the wall where it was very dangerous and perilous to descend, and only then realized that I should not have stayed on the other side; yet I could no longer turn back on this narrow path. Although I tried to turn back and get onto the other path, I trusted my good feet, held on tight, and came down successfully without danger. When I had walked a good while, I saw and knew of no more danger, nor did I know where the wall or the hand-hold had gone.

After I had come down, there stood a beautiful rosebush on which grew beautiful red and white roses, though there were more red than white. I plucked some of them and stuck them in my hat.
