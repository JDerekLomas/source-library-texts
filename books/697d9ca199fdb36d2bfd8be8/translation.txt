...could tell and sufficiently illustrate; that you were born, I say, from a most noble family, were educated in the Court of the most powerful Kings, have attained the highest honors, and hold great authority and favor with Princes. I will say only this most briefly: of those steps of honors and duties that usually lead to the Supreme Magistracy <note>original: "Magistratum"; here referring to the office of Superior General or the highest administrative position within the religious order.</note>, You have fulfilled them all—and you have done so in such a way that no one has ever achieved them in a shorter time, with greater goodwill from others, or with such apparent divine favor. For indeed, in a Religious family <note>original: "Religiosæ familiæ"; the author uses the term "family" to describe the religious order, emphasizing its communal and spiritual bonds.</note>—especially one that is highly adorned both by the praise of virtue and the splendor of learning, and is furthermore spread far and wide across all regions of the earth—there are men innumerable in multitude, diverse in their variety of peoples and nations, and most distinguished in their lineage, duties, and authority.
