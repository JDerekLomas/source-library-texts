The world is carved up like a melon and split like a bean <note>A traditional idiom for the partitioning of territory.</note>. One can point to it in the palm of a hand or draw it in the sand. I have verified these matters with the foreign ships that trade in Fujian and Guangdong, and with the genuine envoys who have come from afar to offer tribute through multiple layers of translation <note>original: "chongyi," literally "multiple translations," referring to the process of translating through several intermediate languages.</note>. These matters are largely supported by evidence; these words are not baseless inventions. It seems possible to fully describe the geographical features of Mother Earth <note>original: "Fuao," a deity representing the earth and fertility.</note> and exhaust the locations of the four continents.

However, if we use these descriptions to verify the maps, there are actually only three continents <note>Referring to the contiguous landmass of Afro-Eurasia.</note>; there is no such thing as four or five. According to the *Shuowen Jiezi* <note>An influential 2nd-century Chinese dictionary.</note>, a place in the water where people can live is called a *zhou* <note>The character 州 (zhou) originally meant a river island or a landmass surrounded by water.</note>. This meaning likely originated before the Great Flood. After the floodwaters subsided and people settled on the high grounds, the name remained unchanged. Later generations added the "water" radical to the side of the character to create the word *zhou* <note>original: "洲" (continent/island). Wei Yuan is highlighting the evolution from 州 to 洲.</note>.

Thus, when the philosopher Zou Yan <note>An ancient Chinese philosopher (c. 305–240 BCE) known for his "Greater Nine Continents" theory.</note> spoke of the heavens, he said that the region surrounded by the Lesser Seas is called "Shenzhou" <note>The "Divine Continent," a classical name for China.</note>. When the Buddhist scriptures discuss geography, they say that the salty seas define four continents. Therefore, the term "continent" is a name specifically for land surrounded on all four sides by water. It should not be used as a rule for regions that are connected to one another by land.

In the kingdoms of these lands, the mountains of **Europe** and **Libya** <note>original: "Liweiya," the classical name used for Africa.</note> all originate from the Pamir Mountains <note>original: "Congling" (Onion Range), the hub of the great mountain systems of Asia.</note>. They wind and stretch continuously until they end at the Western Sea. Thus, even dividing the lands east and west of the Pamirs into just two continents would be difficult to justify; how much more so is it to forcibly divide the lands to the west of the Pamirs <note>Referring to the separation of Europe and Africa from Asia.</note>...

# Illustrated Treatise on the Maps

The discourse on maps is closely related to the meaning of the **Four Continents** <note>The *Caturdvīpa*, or four great island-continents that surround Mount Meru in Buddhist cosmology</note> described in the Buddhist scriptures. Some argue that the "Southern Continent" should be located below the **Equator** <term>chidao; literally "the Red Road"</term> in the temperate zone, and that **Jambudvipa** <note>The continent inhabited by ordinary humans</note> should not be considered the "Southern Continent." This view arises because people are stubbornly fixated on the North and South Poles as the definitive "North" and "South." 

They do not realize that the Buddhist texts use the **North and South Poles** to distinguish **Up and Down** <note>Treating the axis of the Earth as a vertical line in the cosmos</note>, while using "Front, Back, Left, and Right" to define the four directions. This is identical to the Confucian concept of the **Six Directions** <note>The *Liuhe*: North, South, East, West, Zenith (Up), and Nadir (Down)</note>. 

To claim that the zenith is "North" is merely the narrow, slanted perspective of people living in the Middle Kingdom. In reality, how could the **Celestial Pole** <note>The fixed point in the sky around which stars appear to rotate</note> be biased only toward the North? And how could one possibly define the South Pole as the "South" in an absolute sense?

Buddhist scriptures provide names for the four continents:
1. **Jambudvipa** in the South <note>original: Nan Yanfutī</note>
2. **Aparagoyaniya** in the West <note>original: Xi Qūyání</note>
3. **Purvavideha** in the East <note>original: Dōng Fúpótí</note>
4. **Uttarakuru** in the North <note>original: Běi Yùdānyuè</note>

Therefore, the *Sutra of the Arising of the World* <note>The *Abhiniṣkramaṇa Sūtra*, a text describing the origins of the universe</note> states that when it is high noon in **Jambudvipa**, the sun is just beginning to set in **Purvavideha**. <gloss>In Aparagoyaniya, the sun is just rising.</gloss> <gloss>In Uttarakuru, it is exactly midnight.</gloss> 

This remains true even if you swap the locations. Furthermore, what is considered "West" from the perspective of **Jambudvipa** is considered "East" by **Aparagoyaniya**. What is considered "West" by **Aparagoyaniya** is... <note>The text cuts off mid-sentence, likely continuing to Uttarakuru on the next page.</note>

**Uttarakuru** <note>original: Shanyue Zhou; the Northern continent in Buddhist cosmology</note> considers a certain direction to be the East. What is called "West" by the people of **Uttarakuru** is considered the "East" by those in **Purvavideha** <note>original: Fupoti Zhou; the Eastern continent</note>. What is called "West" by **Purvavideha** is considered the "East" by those in **Jambudvipa** <note>original: Yanfuti Zhou; the Southern continent, representing our known world</note>. This same logic applies to the South. 

The *Sutra of the Great Mound* <note>original: Loutan Jing; an early translation of the *Dirgha Agama* describing Buddhist cosmology</note> states: "The sun circles **Mount Meru** <note>The central world-mountain</note>. When the sun rises in the East, it is midnight in the South, sunset in the West, and midday in the North." Rotating clockwise in this manner, the sun creates the cycle of day and night. 

Such is the true meaning of the **Four Continents** in the Buddhist scriptures. Such is the true meaning of the **Four Directions** in the Buddhist scriptures. I ask that this be used to inform those Westerners who divide the world into two, four, or five continents, and to inform those Westerners who are stubbornly fixated on the North and South Poles as the only "North" and "South."
