### Description of the Binding

This image displays a highly ornate 17th-century red morocco <term>morocco: a high-quality goatskin leather known for its durability and ability to take gold-tooling</term> leather binding. The cover features extensive gold-tooling in the "fan" <note>original: "éventail"</note> style, a popular decorative technique of the Baroque period.

The decoration consists of multiple concentric rectangular borders filled with repeating lace-like and foliate <note>leaf-shaped</note> patterns. The central field is divided into compartments; the corners contain large quarter-circle fan designs composed of delicate <term>pointillé: a technique using small dots to create shimmering gold lines</term> gold work.

At the center of the design is an octagonal frame housing a coat of arms <note>heraldic symbols identifying the book's owner</note>. The shield features a sheaf of wheat <term>garb: a heraldic term for a bundle of grain</term> with two bands across it. The shield is surmounted by a crown held aloft by two winged <term>putti: chubby male infants, common in Renaissance and Baroque art</term>.
