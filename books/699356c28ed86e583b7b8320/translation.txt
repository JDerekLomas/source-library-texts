# Decorative Silk Samples

This page contains no written text but displays a series of vertical panels featuring patterned <term>silk</term> <note>A natural protein fiber produced by silkworms, used for millennia in China to create luxury textiles for clothing and bookbinding.</note> textiles. These samples likely represent the various materials used for the decorative <term>mounting</term> <note>The process of backing a paper manuscript with silk and additional paper layers to create a durable, rollable scroll.</note> of the Leifeng Pagoda manuscripts.

### Textile Varieties and Motifs

The collection includes several distinct types of fabric:

*   **Beige and Silver Damask**: A reversible, patterned fabric featuring intricate woven <term>medallion patterns</term> <note>Circular decorative motifs that were highly popular during the Tang and Song dynasties, often featuring floral or animal designs.</note>.
*   **Peach and Blue Brocade**: A rich fabric woven with multi-colored threads to create raised floral motifs.
*   **Greenish-Gold Silk**: A shimmering textile displaying a repeating pattern of botanical elements.

These fragments provide a rare visual record of 10th-century textile technology and the aesthetic preferences of the era in which the Leifeng Pagoda was constructed.

# The Heart of All Tathagatas: The Casket Seal Dharani

This is the **Scripture of the Dharani of the Precious Casket Seal of the Secret Heart of the Whole-Body Relics of All Tathagatas** <note>original: *Ciyi qie rulai xin mimi quanshen sheli baoqieyin tuoluoni jing*. A <term>Tathagata</term> is an honorific title for a Buddha, meaning "One who has thus come."</note>.

During the Song dynasty, **Qian Chu**, the King of the **Wuyue Kingdom** <note>A coastal kingdom (907–978 AD) in what is now Zhejiang province, known for its devout patronage of Buddhism.</note> and Grand Marshal of the Realm’s Armies, commissioned the creation of **eighty-four thousand copies** of this scripture. These were deposited within the **West Gate Brick Pagoda** to serve as an eternal offering.

This scroll is one of those very treasures once hidden within the **Leifeng Pagoda** <note>A famous five-story tower in Hangzhou. It was completed in 975 AD; the "West Gate Brick Pagoda" mentioned above refers to this same structure.</note>. Following the collapse of the tower, this scripture was revealed to the world. Having been granted the opportunity to read it through once, I feel my <term>karmic blessing</term> <note>original: *fuyuan*. In Buddhist thought, this is the spiritual merit or "destined connection" that allows a person to encounter sacred objects.</note> is indeed profound.

->Inscribed by **Tan Guohuan** for **Mr. Lan’er**<-

The fifteenth day of the twelfth month in the **Year of the Wood Ox**, the 2952nd year of the **Buddhist Era** <note>This date corresponds to January 28, 1926, in the Gregorian calendar. The Leifeng Pagoda had collapsed just over a year prior, in September 1924.</note>.
