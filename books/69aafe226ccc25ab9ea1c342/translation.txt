This text represents the conclusion of **Tablet VII** of the Babylonian Creation Epic, known by its incipit *Enūma Eliš* ("When on high"). It focuses on the recitation of the fifty names of the god Marduk, bestowed upon him by the other gods to honor his victory over Tiamat and his ordering of the cosmos. The tablet concludes with a didactic epilogue urging the wise to remember these names and a royal colophon from the Library of Ashurbanipal.

<keywords>Marduk, Enūma Eliš, Ashurbanipal, Nineveh, Fifty Names of Marduk, Tutu, Asari</keywords>

### Translation

**Language:** Akkadian (Neo-Assyrian dialect)
**Script:** Cuneiform (Neo-Assyrian)

**Obverse**
1. [Asari], the [crea]tor of [pastures] of [old],
   <note>Asari is the first of the fifty names of Marduk. The name is etymologized here as a provider of agriculture and grain.</note>
2. [They raised his kingship] in [their] heart[s].
3. [The god Tutu], [he is] the [creator of] their [renew]al.
   <term>Tutu</term>: A name of Marduk meaning "Creator of Renewal," associated with the purification of the gods.
4. [They glori]fied their [shrine]s and [broken].
5. [broken] ... [broken]
(lines 6-134 are missing from these fragments)

**Reverse**
(lines 1'-8' are too fragmentary for a continuous translation)
9'. [... the god] Marduk (Sumerian: {d}AMAR-UTU)
10'. [... in their mouths they] chanted.
11'. [They named his fifty names (MU-MEŠ)] and his [mi]ght.
    <term>MU-MEŠ</term>: (Akkadian: *šumū*) "names". This refers to the climactic naming ceremony where the gods grant Marduk fifty titles to reflect his total authority.
12'. [Let him be honored and among all the gods (DINGIR-MEŠ)] let him be pre-eminent.
    <term>DINGIR-MEŠ</term>: (Akkadian: *ilū*) "gods".
13'. [Let them be held (in memory) and let the] first (generations) [re]veal (them).
    <note>This refers to the "former" ones (the ancestors) teaching the epic to the "later" ones.</note>
14'. [Let the wise and the knowing] consult [together].
15'. [Let the father repeat (them) and teach (them to)] the [son].
    <note>Lines 13'-15' constitute the "Epilogue," emphasizing that the poem is a sacred text meant to be passed down through oral and written tradition.</note>

**Colophon**
16'. [Seventh tablet of "When on high" (Enūma Eliš), not] fi[nished].
    <term>NU AL-TIL</term>: (Akkadian: *ul qati*) "Not finished/complete". This indicates that the series continues or that this specific copy was part of a larger set where the text might carry over.
17'. [Ashurbanipal, king of the universe, king of the land of Ashu]r.
    <note>Ashurbanipal (reigned 668–631 BC) was the last great king of the Neo-Assyrian Empire; he assembled a massive library of literary and scientific texts in his capital, Nineveh.</note>

### Scholarly Commentary
This specific tablet is a composite of several fragments found in the ruins of the **Library of Ashurbanipal** at Nineveh. *Enūma Eliš* was the central myth of the Babylonian New Year Festival (*Akitu*), though this copy was used for archival and scholarly purposes in Assyria. 

The **Fifty Names of Marduk** (the focus of Tablet VII) are a sophisticated piece of ancient philology. Each "name" is a Sumerian epithet that is then "translated" or explained into Akkadian within the poetic lines. This demonstrates the high level of bilingual scholarship practiced by the royal scribes. The epilogue (lines 13'-15') serves as a liturgical "seal," confirming the text as authoritative wisdom to be studied by the elite (*enqu* "wise" and *mūdû* "knowing").
