# TRANSLATOR’S NOTE.

*The reader has seen in the first volume, at the end of the foreword, what should be understood by the words: inqualification, <note>original: "inqualification" — a term Böhme used to describe the inner-working or penetration of qualities within the divine nature.</note> body, or circumscription, begetting, etc.*

*Independantly of these various words, several others are found, either in this volume or in the preceding one, which are not standard French, such as to "originize" oneself, <note>original: "s’originiser" — a neologism created to express the act of something arising from its own source or origin.</note> enflaming, depreciator, etc.; but these are so clear that they will be easily understood.*

*The word "geniture" <note>original: "géniture" — an archaic term for offspring or the act of begetting.</note> has slipped by error into several places in this second volume. Although this word is French, it is not of a high enough register to hold its place in such serious matters. The reader is asked to substitute the word "begetting" or "generation" <note>original: "engendrement ou génération."</note> in its place.*
