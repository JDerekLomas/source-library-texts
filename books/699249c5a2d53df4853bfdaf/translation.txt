## In the name of our Lord, the Exalted, the Most High

### This chapter is mentioned in explanation of how the servants shall never reach the shore of the ocean of true knowledge

...except through utter detachment from all that is in the heavens and on the earth. Purify your souls, O people of the earth, that you may perhaps attain the station which God has ordained for you, and <unclear>be established</unclear> within the tabernacle which God has raised high in the heaven of the Bayan <note>The term 'Bayan' refers to 'the Utterance' or 'the Revelation'; it is a central concept in the Bábí and Bahá'í faiths regarding divine communication.</note>.

The essence of this chapter is that the wayfarers on the path of faith and the seekers of the cups of certitude must cleanse and sanctify their physical beings and their very selves from all worldly conditions. That is, they must cleanse the ear from listening to [idle] talk, the heart from doubts related to the "veils of glory" <term>subuhat-i-jalal: a mystical term referring to those religious or intellectual concepts that, while seemingly majestic, actually act as veils preventing the seeker from recognizing new divine truths</term>, the spirit from attachment to outward means, and the eye from observing transitory words. Relying upon God and seeking recourse in Him, they should travel the path so that they may become worthy of the radiance of the suns of divine knowledge and understanding, and become the site for the manifestation of the infinite outpourings of the Unseen.

Furthermore, consider the verse regarding the direction of prayer <term>Qibla: the direction Muslims face during prayer</term>. After the **Sun of Muhammadan Prophethood** migrated from the dawning-point of Batha <note>Batha is a name for the valley of Mecca</note>, He would turn toward Jerusalem for a time during the ritual prayer <term>salāt: the formal Islamic prayer performed five times daily</term>. This continued until the Jews began to utter certain unseemly words, the mention of which is not appropriate for this occasion and would lead to a lengthy digression. This greatly distressed His Holiness [Muhammad]. One night, He stood gazing at the heavens in a state of contemplation and wonder, until Gabriel descended and recited this verse:

->We have seen the turning of your face toward the heaven; now We shall surely turn you to a direction of prayer that shall please you.<- <note>original Arabic: *qad narā taqalluba wajhika fī s-samāʾi...*; Quran 2:144</note>

Eventually, one day, while His Holiness and a group of His companions were performing the obligatory midday prayer, and after they had already completed two units <gloss>rak'ahs</gloss> of the prayer, Gabriel descended and said:

->Turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque<- <note>original Arabic: *fawalli wajhaka shaṭra l-masjidi l-ḥarām*; referring to the Kaaba in Mecca</note>

In the very midst of the prayer, His Holiness turned away from Jerusalem and faced the Kaaba. At that moment, a tremor of agitation and distress fell upon the companions, to such an extent that a group of them broke off their prayer and turned away in rejection.
