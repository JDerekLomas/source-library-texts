# Preface.

---

<margin><unclear>Mr. Lu</unclear></margin>
**T**he place of this preface <note>original: remḟocul</note> was first Druim Cett, for it was there that the great assembly of Druim Cett was held. In another place <note>original: in alio loco</note>, however, the body of the hymn was composed from then on, as will appear later <note>original: ut post apparert</note>. It was composed in the time of Aed, son of Ainmire. The person <note>original: persu</note> who wrote it was Dallán Forgaill of the Masraige of Magh Slecht. The cause <note>original: tucait</note> was for his own attainment of heaven, and for others through him <note>original: per se</note>.

There were, in truth, three causes why Columba <note>original: Colum Cille</note> came from Scotland <note>original: Alba</note> to Ireland at that time: namely, to release Scannlán Mór, son of Cenn Fáelad, King of Ossory, for whom he had stood as a guarantor; and to retain the poets in Ireland (for they were being expelled because of their burdensomeness, as there used to be thirty in the retinue of every master-poet <note>term: ollam</note>, and fifteen in the retinue of every secondary poet <note>term: anrad</note>); and to make peace between the men of Ireland and Scotland concerning the territory of Dal Riata.

And it is said that Columba did not see Ireland at that time, for there used to be a cloth over his eyes. This was the reason for that: because he had promised before going across the sea that he would never look upon Ireland from then on, saying <note>original: dicens</note>:

# PREFACE.

**T**he setting for this preface, firstly, is Druimm Ceta <note>modern: Drumcet, near Limavady</note>, for it was there that the great meeting of Druimm Ceta was held. However, the body of the hymn was composed in a different place from then on, as appears later. It was composed in the time of Aed, son of Anmere <note>Aed mac Ainmuirech, High King of Ireland r. 586–598</note>. The author was Dallan Forgaill of the Masraige of Mag Slecht <note>The Masraige were an ancient tribe in County Cavan</note>. The purpose was to reach heaven for himself and for others through this work. 

Now, there were three reasons why Colum Cille <note>Saint Columba</note> came from Alba <note>Scotland</note> to Eriu <note>Ireland</note> at that time: 
1. For the release of Scanlann Mór, son of Cend Faelad, king of the Osraige <note>Ossory</note>, for whom he had acted as a legal guarantor.
2. For the retention of the poets in Ireland. They were facing banishment because of their burdensomeness; for there used to be thirty people in the company of each *Ollom* <note>term: a master poet of the highest rank</note>, and fifteen in the company of each *Anrad* <note>term: a poet of the second-highest rank</note>.

> The desire of a man of battle is purple spoils;
> He goes for God's sake, certainly not sparingly;
> He placed a firm grip <note>original: "tend"</note> on a spear-shaft of pine;
> A head is usually in the claw of Cú Echtga.

It might be that the example here refers to a physical thing—that is, that man's own head on him and someone else's head in his hand; however, these poetic devices are observed in speech and not in physical objects. > A path from lakes to a pool of drinking
> With bells of fame that is not small:
> Going before horses at the end of the territory—
> Good is the life in which they are there <note>original: "annón"</note>.

### REMARKS ON THE TEXT, ETC.

There are a few complete copies of the "Amra" <note>The Eulogy</note> besides that of the *Lebor na hUidre* <note>The Book of the Dun Cow, a famous 12th-century Irish manuscript</note>, which is the oldest and the best; in Part II, I shall occasionally refer to those copies.

In representing the original, I have made no distinction between uncontracted and contracted syllables <note>referring to the shorthand marks used in medieval Gaelic script</note>, as I could not do so without disfiguring the page with the introduction of either Roman characters or brackets.
