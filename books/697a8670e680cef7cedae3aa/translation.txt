### MAIMONIDES, Moses (Moses ben Maimon) (1139–1204)

Book of the *Guide for the Perplexed* (Hebrew title) *The Teacher of the Perplexed* <note>Latin: *Doctor Perplexorum*</note> ... translated by <note>Latin: *conversus a*</note> Johannes Buxtorf. Basel, 1629. First edition of this version. Small quarto. Index. [40 introductory], 532, [52 index] pages. Slightly browned. Contemporary vellum <note>A durable binding made from treated calfskin</note>.

This greatest of all Jewish doctors lived under the gentle rule of the Muslims of Spain; and the people of all three faiths <note>Judaism, Christianity, and Islam</note> united to weep over his grave and to consecrate his memory. The *Guide for the Perplexed* <note>original Hebrew: *More Nevochim*</note> is his masterpiece, in which he replaces the diverse and often fantastic opinions prevailing in regard to the interpretation of Scripture with a <unclear>xxxxx</unclear> rational and uniform exposition. This Latin version by Buxtorf, one of the most noted Hebrew scholars of his age, follows the Hebrew translation made from the Arabic of Maimonides by Samuel ben Tibbon.

<note>This catalog entry highlights the 17th-century Christian interest in Maimonides, as Johannes Buxtorf the Younger was a famous Christian Hebraist who translated this foundational Jewish philosophical work into Latin to make it accessible to European scholars.</note>
