# Preface.

Since I made the decision to write the secret history of the upright Cedrinus, <note>Cedrinus is a fraternal name or pseudonym, a common practice in 18th-century secret societies to protect members' identities and signify their spiritual rank.</note> I have spared no effort, above all else, to get to know the man himself as intimately as possible. He is of an open character. One gets to know him quickly. But the history of a man's spirit is not so quickly learned; and this was my study. I have seen the man in manifold situations. I have observed him in joy and in sorrow. I am proud to call him my friend.

What Cedrinus did, in deciding to make known his secret history within such an order as that of the Gold and Rosy Cross <note>The Order of the Gold and Rosy Cross (Gold- und Rosenkreuzer) was an influential 18th-century German secret society that blended alchemy, mysticism, and traditional Lutheranism.</note>—to make it known according to the strict truth, without sparing his own weaknesses—truly, that is no ordinary thing!

How much was required for this decision, those may judge who know secret societies of that kind from their own experience.

### Preface

He has entrusted his papers to my faithful hands. I will preserve them, and in due time deposit them in a public library. There is still much within them that can be of use to our contemporaries and descendants. — I did not wish to have the original letters of the persons involved in this history printed. <note>In the 18th century, "printing letters" often caused public scandal, and Albrecht is likely protecting the identities of other high-ranking members of secret orders.</note> — I shall carefully observe how many a person behaves toward Cedrinus.

I still had much to say to the public in this preface; but I must break off. At this very moment, Cedrinus lies dangerously ill. Perhaps he will not live to see his own history fully in print. — Oh! Honest man, wherever you live, whatever you do: beware of societies that shun the light! <note>original: "Gesellschaften, die das Licht scheuen." This phrase was a common Enlightenment-era critique of secret societies (like the Rosicrucians or the Illuminati), suggesting their secrecy was a cover for superstition or political manipulation rather than enlightenment.</note>

Hamburg in May
1792.
