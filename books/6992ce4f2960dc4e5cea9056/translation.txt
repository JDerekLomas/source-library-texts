### Construction of the Multi-Barreled Fire Spear

Use a single length of bamboo with a circumference of <term>five inches<note>original: "五寸圍" (wu cun wei). In the Ming period, a "cun" or Chinese inch was approximately 3.2 centimeters.</note></term>. The pole should be <term>fifteen feet<note>original: "一丈五尺" (yi zhang wu chi). Approximately 4.8 meters.</note></term> in length. At a distance of <term>one and a half feet<note>original: "一尺五寸" (yi chi wu cun). Approximately 48 centimeters.</note></term> from the top end, install the spearhead.

The spearhead is shaped like a **human hand with five tines**. The backward-facing barbs are <term>four inches<note>original: "倒鬚" (dao xu). These "inverted whiskers" acted as hooks to prevent the spear from being easily pulled out of a target or to snag an opponent's clothing or equipment.</note></term> long. Near the tip, attach **wooden fins** on three sides. 

<column-break/>

Bind three sections of large bamboo tubes to the pole. Each tube should have a diameter of **two inches** and a length of **one foot**. Fill these tubes with <term>gunpowder<note>original: "火鎗藥" (huo qiang yao). Specifically, the explosive mixture used to propel the projectiles.</note></term> and insert **iron or lead pellets**. 

<margin>The bamboo must be seasoned and thick-walled to withstand the pressure of the blast.</margin>

Fasten these tubes securely onto the wooden fins. The <term>fuses<note>original: "藥線" (yao xian). Literally "medicine threads," the ignition cords for the gunpowder.</note></term> are all joined together into a single lead. As the fire is unleashed, it **increases the momentum and power** of the weapon's strike.

<gloss>The combination of the multi-pronged head and the simultaneous discharge of three barrels ensured that the weapon remained deadly even if the initial shot missed.</gloss>

Three men are assigned to each gun, and each man also carries one **gun-staff** <note>original: "銃棍" (chonggun). A hybrid weapon featuring a small firearm barrel mounted on a long staff, used for both ranged and melee combat.</note>. This device is modeled after the **Frankish Gun** <note>original: "佛狼機" (Folangji). These were breech-loading swivel guns based on European designs introduced by the Portuguese in the early 16th century.</note>, though it has been slightly modified. 

The original Frankish guns are heavy and massive; they are advantageous for use on ships but disadvantageous for infantry and cavalry. Furthermore, the older **portable breech-blocks** <term>Tipao<note>Small, removable chambers used in earlier breech-loading designs.</note></term> were too short and small, which allowed the explosive gases to leak out, resulting in a shot that lacked power. 

Now, the design has been updated to use **sub-cannons** <note>original: "子砲" (zipao). These are the interchangeable breech blocks that hold the powder and projectile.</note>. Each gun is provided with three sets of sub-cannons (totaling nine units per squad); their bodies are longer, which keeps the pressure contained and makes the shot powerful. 

As one is loaded, another is fired, creating a cycle that continues without end. One must align the **front sight** <note>original: "照星" (zhaoxing), literally 'shining star.'</note> and **rear sight** <note>original: "照門" (zhaomen), literally 'shining gate.'</note> accurately before firing. 

When fired on a level trajectory, the range is over **two hundred paces** <note>Approximately 300 to 320 meters.</note>. Each shot requires **three-tenths of a tael** <note>original: "三錢" (san qian). Approximately 11 grams.</note> of gunpowder and one lead bullet weighing **three-tenths of a tael**. This weapon can provide supporting fire for the **Weiyuan** <note>A "Great Distance" cannon known for its range.</note> and the **Volley Cannon** <note>original: "連砲" (lianpao), a multi-barrel or rapid-fire weapon.</note>.
