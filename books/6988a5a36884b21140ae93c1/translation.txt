# TO THE READER

I would have you advised, reader, that in this edition of Horus <note>original Latin: *Ori*. The author refers to Horapollo, the purported author of this work on Egyptian hieroglyphs.</note>, many things are handled differently than in previous versions. This is because Guillaume Morel <note>Guillaume Morel (1505–1564) was a distinguished French printer and scholar known for his meticulous editions of Greek and Latin texts.</note>, a most learned and diligent man, provided us with an ancient manuscript of this author. From this manuscript, we have restored many passages in the first book (which was the only part contained in that manuscript, and even then, not recorded in its entirety).

Know this, however: that where a different reading occurred in that manuscript, we exercised our judgment and followed those versions which seemed superior. In the meantime, we have added the readings from the ancient codex <term>codex: a manuscript book, typically made of parchment or paper leaves bound together, as opposed to a scroll.</term> to the margin in instances where our own chosen reading was not unsatisfactory. Furthermore, you should be advised that in the ancient manuscript, the titles are not placed before the chapters, but are instead written in the margin. Since this was of little importance, we did not wish to rashly change the custom already established in this edition.

Therefore, kind reader, if you now hold a more corrected <note>original: *castigatiorem*, a technical term in Renaissance editing meaning a text that has been "chastened" or purified of errors through careful comparison of manuscripts.</note> version of Horus, attribute it to venerable antiquity. As for our labor in this matter—and the efforts of others through whose work this more corrected Horapollo now comes to light—we ask that you look upon it with favor.
