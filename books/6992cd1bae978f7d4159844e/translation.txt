# Preface to the Geographical Illustrations

I and Yunming <note>original: *Yunming* (允明). This is the courtesy name of Wang Qi, the lead compiler of this encyclopedia.</note> are brothers of the same generation; today, we are also joined through the marriage of our children. Yunming, however, as the son of a renowned Censor <note>original: *Yushi* (御史). ## The System of Longitude and Latitude

Regarding the lines of longitude and latitude: the east-west lines of <term>latitude<gloss>Literally "weixian" or "woof threads."</gloss></term> measure the world’s length <note>In this period of Chinese cartography, "length" (chang) often referred to the north-south extent or the vertical axis of the map, though the text here associates it with east-west lines.</note>, originating from the <term>Equator<gloss>The "Day-Night Level Line," so called because day and night are of equal length here.</gloss></term> as the central starting point. 

From there, one counts upward to the North Pole and downward to the South Pole. The north-south lines of <term>longitude<gloss>Literally "jingxian" or "warp threads."</gloss></term> measure the world’s width, beginning from the <term>Fortunate Isles<gloss>The Canary Islands, which served as the Prime Meridian in early modern European and Jesuit maps.</gloss></term> at the first degree and continuing to three hundred and sixty degrees until they meet again. All land situated above the central line is oriented toward the North Pole and is truly the Northern region; land below the central line is oriented toward the South Pole and is truly the Southern region. 

The <term>Buddhists<gloss>Literally "The followers of Shakyamuni."</gloss></term> claim that <term>Jambudvipa<gloss>In traditional Buddhist cosmology, the southern continent where humans live.</gloss></term> and <term>Mount Meru<gloss>The sacred mountain at the center of the universe.</gloss></term> account for the measurements of the earth's features, but their calculations of how these landmasses rise from or enter the earth are erroneous. <note>The author is asserting the superiority of spherical, grid-based geography over the traditional "flat earth" or "altar-shaped" cosmology of religious texts.</note>

Furthermore, latitude lines are used to determine the height of the celestial poles above the horizon. The number of degrees a location is distant from the Equator is exactly equal to the number of degrees the pole rises above the horizon at that location. This principle applies in the south regarding the South Pole, and in the north as well. 

Therefore, by observing that <term>Beijing<gloss>The "Northern Capital" of the Ming and Qing dynasties.</gloss></term> is separated from the central line by forty degrees to the north, one knows that the North Pole is forty degrees high at the capital. <term>Nanjing<gloss>The "Southern Capital."</gloss></term> is thirty-two degrees above the central line and one hundred and thirty degrees east of the Fortunate Isles.

**Southern Zhili** <note>Also known as Nanzhili, this was a massive administrative region surrounding the secondary capital, Nanjing. The founding of our current dynasty’s great enterprise was truly rooted in this place.

However, the **Yangtze River** divides the north from the south; since ancient times, it has been relied upon as a "natural moat." 

### Northern Defense and Transport
North of the River, the two prefectures of **Xu** and **Ying** connect several provinces like links in a chain, and both are known as powerful garrison towns. For this reason, **Huai'an** was specially established with high-ranking civil and military offices. Although these officials are primarily tasked with overseeing **transport** <note>Specifically the management of the Grand Canal, which was vital for moving grain to the northern capital, Beijing.</note>, their presence also demonstrates a strategic posture of control and containment.

### Southern Gateways and Coastal Security
South of the River, **Anqing** controls the entirety of the **Chu** region <note>Modern-day Hubei and Hunan provinces</note> to the west, serving as the gateway to the southern lands. 

Along the coast, military deployments were originally established to defend against the **Japanese pirates** <term>Wokou; seafaring raiders who plagued the Chinese coast during the 16th century</term>. However, the people of **Chongming** and **Changshu**, along with those living along the rivers and seas, frequently disappear and reappear amidst the waves to commit acts of plunder themselves. Therefore, river defense and maritime preparation are of primary importance today.

### Economic and Environmental Challenges
As for **Luzhou**, the people are accustomed to idleness and the land is not utilized to its full potential. 

**Fengyang** shares similar customs, but it suffers from the redundant expenses of the **High Wall** <note>A specific imperial prison in Fengyang where members of the Ming royal family who committed crimes or were deposed were kept under house arrest.</note> and the local garrison; consequently, it is prone to reporting famine. 

In **Suzhou** and **Songjiang**, the taxes are heavy and the labor service is frequent, leaving the people increasingly impoverished by the daily demands for supplies. Recently, the **Wusong River** has become silted with mud, causing suffering from both drought and waterlogging. Between **Feng** and **Pei**, the "Muddy River" <note>Referring to the Yellow River, which frequently shifted its course during this period, causing devastating floods.</note> overflows its banks, nearly turning the inhabitants into "fish and turtles" <note>A traditional Chinese idiom for drowning or being overwhelmed by floods.</note>. Truly, the discussion of **water conservancy** <term>Shuili; the science of irrigation, flood control, and canal management</term> has never been more urgent than at this very moment.

Shaanxi occupies the ancient lands of **Yongzhou**. Surrounded on all sides by mountains and rivers, its strategic terrain is the finest under heaven. However, in the region encompassing **Pingliang** and **固原** <note>Guyuan</note>, where livestock flourishes, there is a lack of external natural barriers. Consequently, Mongol <note>original: 胡 "Hu" — a general term for northern nomadic tribes</note> cavalry have gradually encroached upon the territories of **Gongchang** and **Fengxiang**.

In the areas of **Yan'an** and **Qingyang**, the wilderness is desolate, yet the soldiers there are elite and formidable. **Ningxia** possesses the natural defenses of the **Helan Mountains** and the **Yellow River**; because it is difficult for the enemy to maneuver their horses through such terrain, it is twice as easy to defend as other regions. 

Ever since the expansion of the current <term>Ming Dynasty</term>, the ancient city of **Shouxiang** <note>literally "City for Receiving Surrender"</note> has long been abandoned and left unguarded. Since the frontier garrison was moved to **Yulin**, the **Ordos Loop** <note>original: 河套 "Hetao" — the fertile land within the great northern bend of the Yellow River</note> has fallen entirely into the hands of the enemy. Now, the smoke of beacon fires reaches the inner prefectures even in broad daylight.

To the west of **Ganzhou** and **Liangzhou**, flanked by Tibetans <note>original: 番 "Fan" — referring to the various tribes of the West, primarily Tibetans</note> on the left and Mongols <note>original: 達 "Da" — referring to the Tatars or Mongols</note> on the right, **Suzhou** is particularly isolated. Consequently, the **Turpan** <note>the Turpan Khanate</note> frequently launches reckless invasions, posing a constant threat to the **Hexi Corridor**.

In **Taozhou**, **Minzhou**, and **Xining**, the land borders the <term>Western Qiang</term> tribes, sharing the same defensive burdens as **Sichuan**. Furthermore, the rebel leader **Ibula** <note>a Mongol chieftain who fled to the Qinghai region after internal conflicts</note> led his remaining followers to take refuge near the **West Sea** <note>modern-day Lake Qinghai</note>. Since then, the nomadic tribes have gradually migrated southward. The state has already lost the economic benefits of the <term>Tea-Horse trade</term> <note>a vital state-run exchange of Chinese tea for Tibetan horses</note>, while the population of these fugitive bandits grows more numerous by the day. This represents a deep-seated threat to the very heart of our western borders.

### Henan: The Ancient Land of Yuzhou

**Henan**, the ancient land of **Yuzhou** <note>Yuzhou was one of the Nine Provinces of ancient China; the name implies a central, fertile land</note>, occupies the center of **China**, where the four directions converge. 

**Zhangde** <note>Modern Anyang</note> controls the region north of the **Yellow River**. **Mount Song** and **Luoyang** serve as a screen to the south of the mountains. **Nanyang** and **Runing** lead directly to the outskirts of **Xiangyang** and **Huangzhou** <note>regions in modern-day Hubei</note>. 

**Kaifeng** is the provincial metropolis. From Kaifeng, one can sail up the **Wei River** to transport grain to **Shandong**; following the **Bian** and **Si** rivers, one can transport grain to the **Huai** region. Therefore, those who discuss strategic terrain rank this province second only to **Guanzhong** and **Shaanxi**.

However, the establishment of various **Imperial Kin** <note>Members of the Ming royal family granted local fiefs</note> and their official stipends cost nearly a million units of grain; the common people are thus heavily burdened. Furthermore, the region encompassing the three prefectures of **Guide**, **Sui**, and **Chen** borders several other provinces; because the jurisdiction is not unified, wicked men often use this lack of oversight to start uprisings. 

**Zhangde** lies on the road between the ancient states of **Wei** and **Zhao**; the military and civilians there are surrounded by bandits. Although the bandits may hide their tracks, the danger of being threatened and restrained by them is effectively the same as in **Guide**. 

If one examines the land of the **Seven Prefectures** <note>Referring to the primary administrative divisions of Henan at the time</note>, where the mountains and waters intertwine and the people and products are prosperous and abundant, then **Runing** is considered the best. 

For mining in the mountains, hunting in the wilds, and relying on formidable natural defenses, the region of **He-Luo** <note>The area surrounding the Yellow River and the Luo River, centering on Luoyang</note> is the most prominent; strong bows and sharp crossbows are produced there. 

However, much of the land has not yet been reclaimed for farming, and the people have not yet been tamed by instruction and law. Unless the government applies attentive care and soothing governance <note>original: *fuxun*, a term for paternalistic pacification of a restless population</note>, then bandits will continue to commit robberies and plunder.
