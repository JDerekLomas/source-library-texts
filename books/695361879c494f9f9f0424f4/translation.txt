<note>Maslama al-Majriti (d. 1007 AD) was a renowned Andalusian mathematician and astronomer from Madrid. While the text is attributed to him, modern scholars believe it was likely written by a different author in the mid-11th century.</note>

# In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful

Praise be to God, from whose light the veils of concealment are illuminated, and from whose might the wonders of destiny flow. To Him all traces lead, and by His command night and day alternate. He is the bringer of things into existence after their non-existence, the originator of creatures and the changer of their blessings. He is the Ordainer, the Creator of every being; He is neither mingled with things nor distinct from them. Attributes do not encompass Him, darkness does not overtake Him, descriptions do not exhaust Him, and accidents do not befall Him. May God bless the Master of the Messengers and the Seal of the Prophets, upon whom the Book was revealed in a clear Arabic tongue—and indeed, it is found in the scriptures of the ancients—and upon his righteous and pure family, and grant them much peace until the Day of Judgment.

My composition of this book began in the year 343 <note>954-955 CE</note> upon my completion of the revision of the book *The Rank of the Wise*, and I finished it at the end of the year 348 <note>959-960 CE</note>.
