*This most complete history of Cloven-Hoofed Quadrupeds <note>original: "de Quadrupedibus Bisulcis"</note> by the most noble and learned man, Ulisse Aldrovandi, sought out a Prince who was equally noble and learned. It was necessary that this work—which, due to the excellence of its subject matter, is in no way inferior to the other works already published by the same author—should find a patron of no less authority. As I meditated on this in my mind, you, Most August Prince, occurred to me. Your kindness, the sweetness of your character, your purity, and your wisdom—which is far beyond your youthful years—shone so clearly to the City of Bologna during the two years you spent in the College of Nobles, that the city judged you to be born by Nature not only for your current dignity, but for even greater honors. Bologna considers it a great glory to itself that it has given two Prelates from its own fellowship to that most distinguished See in continuous succession. It predicts for your Highness an increase in fame and the highest summits of achievement, so that you may finally rule in that place from which your most noble family draws its origin. Your first ancestor, as far back as memory can be traced, emerged from the darkness of idolatry into the light of the Gospel while holding patrician dignity at Rome. For that reason, having been proscribed by Nero during his rage against the first Christians, he chose Brescia as the seat of his fortunes, and soon after, in the Rhaetian region, he erected the Roman Castle, which they previously called Lodron. From this lineage, an unconquered and long line of descendants has proceeded in an uninterrupted series; nor do I mention what is most well-known...</note>*

...I review the most [noble], but following the highest peaks, I shall survey from the watchtower of true virtue the most illustrious men of this most noble lineage within recent memory—though not all of them. **Paris**, held in love by his neighbors and in terror by his enemies, left as heirs **Peter** and **George**, who added four towns to their ancestral and most ample patrimony; around the year 1442, they were announced as Counts of the Holy Roman Empire. **Ludwig**, having served the Christian Republic <note>The "Respublica Christiana," a term for the collective interests of the Christian world</note> most excellently, was the leader of the German forces for **Ferdinand, King of the Romans** in **Hungary**. During that dismal retreat of the Christians from **Osijek** <note>original: "Exechio"</note>, when the supreme commander deserted the army, he offered himself with an unconquered spirit as a sacrificial victim to the cruelty of the **Turks**. Thus, those whom he could not overcome while living due to adverse fortune, he might surpass by falling most bravely. For, having been hailed as commander of the squadrons that remained, he leaped from his horse to meet the boldness of the common soldiers; exhorting his men to battle, he kept the victory in doubt for a long time until at last, after a great slaughter of the enemy, as if to avenge the death looming over him, he perished—yet he did not truly die. For his head, brought to **Suleiman** <note>original: "Solimano"</note> at **Constantinople** as a trophy of victory, proclaimed in a speaking silence that he, though conquered, had triumphed over the victor. From the same family came **John Baptist**, of the privy chamber to the most invincible **Charles V**; he was a prefect under the same Emperor's auspices in the German war, and when the war moved to **Insubria** <note>The region of Milan/Lombardy</note>, conducting affairs most fiercely, he was killed at **Casale**. The heir not only to his fortune but to his virtue was **Alberic**, renowned in the Belgian war, who closed his final day at **Messina** in **Sicily**. **Hieronymus**, surnamed **Aenobarbus** <note>meaning "Bronze-beard"</note>, prepared various titles for himself through his achievements. Another **Hieronymus**, having earned long-standing pay in **Belgium**, **Insubria**, and **Portugal**, was appointed Prefect of the **Spearmen of His Catholic Majesty** <note>original: "Doriphoris Catholicæ Maiestatis"</note> due to the distinguished commendation of his military fortitude. **Caspar**, Prefect of the Court and also a confidant of the secrets of **Maximilian Augustus** <note>The Emperor Maximilian II</note>, was celebrated for many embassies most excellently performed. His brother **Paris**, who performed outstanding service for **Charles V** in the German war undertaken against the enemies of the Christian religion, possessed a probity so sublime and a faith so blameless that **Maximilian** entrusted to his care his sons **Rudolf** (who later succeeded him in the Empire) and the Archduke **Ernest** when they were setting out for **Spain**, with the title of Grand Chamberlain. He, second to none in the glory of obedience to his Princes, fathered **Nicholas, the parent of Your Highness**. Nicholas, as a youth, served the **Most Serene Princes of Austria** as a cup-bearer <note>original: "ab auro potorio," likely referring to the service of the golden drinking vessels</note>, then became Governor of the County of **Tyrol**; soon, with increasing age and attaining increments of honor, he conducted himself in the **Insubrian**, **Portuguese**, and **Genevan** expeditions in such a way that he attained immortal fame among Spanish historians. Finally, named Colonel of a double German legion by **Philip III**, he received the reward of his virtue from the judgment of the greatest Prince—merits which, while he still survives, promise even greater things daily. But I restrain my pen, which runs toward the praises of others who have flourished most greatly in that family, lest I seem to have woven a history; rather, as an admirer of such vast virtues, and with the sharpness of my eyes dimmed by the intuition of such splendor—like an owl before the sun—I shake off the darkness as if by emitting certain rays, and I congratulate you on the **Church of Salzburg** having been delivered to you, which, if its Prelates [are distinguished] by birth and virtue...

Your past life promises that you are in no way inferior to those most noble men whom this See has always possessed. <image-desc>Decorative drop cap 'A' set within a square frame embellished with floral and scrollwork designs.</image-desc>

*Accept these gifts of praise, owed to you by Bologna <note>original: "Felsineae," an ancient Etruscan name for Bologna</note>,*
*For you are the splendor and honor of the Bolognese home.*
*Our Muses, once devoted to your wisdom <note>original: "Minervae," referring to the goddess of wisdom</note>,*
*Desired to offer such things to your merits.*
*Ulysses <note>referring to Ulisse Aldrovandi, the famous naturalist</note>, renowned for learning, granted these wishes,*
*Having measured both worlds with his genius:*
*It is he who wandered without error through the wide Ocean,*
*Saw the lands, and the path of the Heavens.*
*Yet you are that one, the foster-son in whom we exult;*
*Whom the sacred tiara makes distinguished in worship.*
*This book displays every kind of four-footed wild beast;*
*Which the cloven hoof marks as becoming gentle.*
*From this, may you know how to lead the sheep, for whom you provide the best pastures,*
*To also bear this mark within the human mind.*
*Hence we shall sing to you the vows we have conceived:*
*May God soon grant prosperous words for the future.*
*As he has expressed every kind of sheep in this book;*
*So may you rule as the supreme Pastor in the world.*
