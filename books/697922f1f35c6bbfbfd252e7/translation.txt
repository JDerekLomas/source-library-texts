# PREFACE

## BY JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS

**I**t is the opinion of the wise that the ancient **Philosophers of art and nature**, having discovered secrets, hid them by various means and figures so that they would not fall into the knowledge of wicked men. The more learned among the Jews also confirm that **Moses**, the most famous leader of the Israelite people, revealed the inexpressible mysteries of secrets in simple words within his description of the creation of heaven and earth. Among our own people, the most learned Saint **Jerome** states that almost as many mysteries are hidden in the **Apocalypse** <note>The Book of Revelation</note> as there are words. Furthermore, the Greek sages, held in no small esteem among their own, devoted themselves to writing fables; through a very prudent method, they delivered one meaning to the unlearned and another to the wise within the sequence of a single narrative. These most devoted lovers of wisdom I cannot imitate, owing to the slowness of my own wit; yet I do not fail to admire them and read them with all the diligence I can muster, for as long as [I recognize] that they have produced great works through nighttime study <note>original: "lucubrasse"—to work by candlelight, implying intense scholarly labor</note> beyond other men...

# THE FIRST MODE OF SECRET
## writing is done through the individual first letters of
## single words: of which
## this is the sign: I.

---

The first mode of secret writing is thus: When you wish to write something to a friend secretly, so that no one understands your will except him to whom you write, then take any narrative in Latin <note>original: "narrationem Latinam"</note> you wish which does not need to be hidden. Arrange all its words in such a way that the first letters of each word yield the word you want <note>This is a classic acrostic cipher, where the hidden message is read vertically through the first letter of each word in the "cover" text.</note>, just as I will teach you in the following argument or example. However, this mode is not sufficiently safe, because it could easily be detected by a diligent man, as I have often experienced. It is indeed easy to read, but it is written with difficulty, because it is laborious to always have suitable words at hand for the purpose. Let us set down an example of this mode, whose open text is written in Latin, but the secret meaning <term>tenor</term> <note>original: "tenor" — referring to the underlying intent or hidden message.</note> is found written in German <note>original: "Teutonice"</note>, namely:

# Precautions to be observed.

**I**t must be observed, however, that the aforementioned mode, as we have said, is not safe enough in difficult matters, because as soon as any suspicion begins to be held regarding the letters, the secret could easily be caught by a clever person. The following modes, therefore, are safer, as is evident. At the same time, it must be observed that if you write by this or other modes to someone not sufficiently learned in the Latin language, he should not divide the words, lest you think one is two points if you wish: once any word of your secret is completed, you should make a comma thus (,) as the sentence of the outer text is completed, or conversely as you please; yet it seems safer that neither points nor commas be made, and a sign of a period should rarely be placed, and not in the secret word, nor should it be placed over the first letter nor after the last, just as I did above in the hidden meaning <term>tenor</term> <note>The "tenor" is the underlying intent or true message hidden within the cover text.</note>: *Dear faithful friend, etc.*

# Extension of this first mode.

**T**his first mode of secret writing, just like almost all others, can be varied with manifold diversity, so that he who perfectly understands the aforementioned order of secreting might hardly or with difficulty perceive other modes to be inserted from it. For anyone can vary this mode, as well as the rest, at his own pleasure. Namely, so that the first line contains the secret and the second does not, or if that one did, this one might not, and so consequently the hider of secrets, as he wishes, may use alternate lines, either in whole or in half, descending from the beginning of his letters to the end, or ascending from the end to the beginning. Add also that by descending and ascending from either side, one can hide their meanings in so many various and diverse ways that an Oedipus <note>In classical mythology, Oedipus solved the riddle of the Sphinx. Trithemius uses the name here to mean a master of riddles or an expert cryptanalyst.</note> would be needed to unravel them. However, my purpose now is to show the first simple modes of writing and to provide an opportunity to those who are worthy and fit to know many things, by which they may be able to more elegantly conceive the greatest things from the smallest.

# Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim
## The second mode with alternating words,
## prudently varied in every language,
## easy and very safe.

**T**he second mode of secret writing without raising suspicion is that the words of the narrative text in the open message vary by an alternating position. This means that while the first letter of the first word provides testimony to the hidden intent, the first letter of the following word is free from the duty of the secret office. After that, the third word is not free from service, the fourth remains idle, and so on consequently; words mixed in alternating turns always serve the secret. While they agree with the public speech <note>The "public speech" refers to the visible, "innocent" layer of the text—in this case, a prayer.</note>, they recede from the signaling of hidden things in a prudent order. This mode is easy in every language of the world and seems to me safe enough for announcing whatever secrets you wish; an example of it is as follows.

(Dear...).</note> us, O sweetest Jesus, ultimate salvation of the blessed spirits, and pardon our guilt, and hear the groans of the poor, mitigate our tribulations, renew our old age, heal the diseases of vice, grant virtues, receive the exiles, and absolve the guilty. *It must be observed, however, that the aforementioned mode, as we have said, is not safe enough in difficult matters, because as soon as any suspicion begins to be held regarding the letters, the secret can easily be detected by a clever person. ## Extension of this first mode.

*This first mode of secret writing, just like almost all others, can be varied with manifold diversity. Thus, he who perfectly understands the aforementioned order of secreting can hardly or only with difficulty perceive other modes to be inferred from it. For everyone can vary this mode, as well as the others, at his own pleasure. Namely, so that the first line contains the secret, the second does not; or if the latter contains it, the former does not. Thus the hider of secrets, as he wishes, may use alternate lines, either in whole or in halves, descending from the beginning of his letters to the end, or ascending from the end to the beginning. Add also that by descending and ascending on either side, one can hide their meanings in so many various and diverse ways that an Oedipus <note>A classical reference to Oedipus, the solver of the Sphinx's riddle; used here to mean someone exceptionally clever at solving puzzles.</note> would be needed to unravel them. ## The second mode using alternating words, prudently varied and easy and very safe in every language.

The second method of writing secret messages without exciting suspicion is to vary the words of the narrative text in an alternating arrangement. This is done so that while the first letter of the first word bears witness to the hidden intention <note>meaning it is the first letter of the secret message</note>, the first letter of the following word is free from the duty of the secret task <note>this is a "null" or "filler" word used to deceive anyone trying to crack the code</note>. In this way, words mixed in alternating turns always serve the secret, while simultaneously fitting into a public speech and receding from the disclosure of hidden things through a prudent order. ## The tenth method uses individual significant words, and each letter is transferred into the tenth preceding it.

**T**he tenth method of this chapter, following the manner of the preceding ones in hiding the mystery, uses individual significant words with absolutely no nulls <note>term: nulls (vacua) - meaningless letters or words added to a cipher to confuse a codebreaker</note> mixed in. # The Nature of the Elements

It is necessary first to consider the primary qualities of the four elements, for as the ancient philosophers <note>Likely referring to Aristotle and the Empedoclean tradition</note> have taught, all physical bodies are composed of these fundamental building blocks. 

->**Of Fire and Air**<-

Fire is by its very nature hot and dry, tending always toward the highest spheres. It is the most subtle of all bodies, possessing a transformative power that consumes the grosser parts of matter. <margin>Fire represents the vital heat of the soul</margin> In contrast, the Air is hot and moist; it is the medium of breath and the vehicle of the vital spirits that sustain life in all creeping things.

<column-break/>

->**Of Water and Earth**<-

Water, being cold and moist, seeks the lowest places and flows according to the shape of its container. It is the principle of phlegm <term>Phlegm: one of the four bodily humors believed to cause a calm or stolid temperament</term> within the human frame. Finally, the Earth is cold and dry, the most stable and fixed of the elements, providing the foundation and <insert>the physical weight</insert> of the world.

### The Harmony of Opposites

| Element | Primary Quality | Secondary Quality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fire | Hot | Dry |
| Air | Moist | Hot |
| Water | Cold | Moist |
| Earth | Dry | Cold |

We see, therefore, that each element shares one quality with another, allowing for a constant <unclear>transmutation</unclear> of one into the other. <gloss>This is the cycle of generation and decay</gloss> Without this shared nature, the world would remain static and devoid of change.
