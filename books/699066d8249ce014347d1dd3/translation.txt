->**Chapter One: On Measurements and Origins**<-

The measurement of a village or city, its treasuries and boundaries, is calculated in <term>Yojanas</term> <note>a traditional unit of distance, roughly 8-9 miles</note>. For temples, palaces, and houses, the measurement is by the <term>Hasta</term> <note>cubit; the length from elbow to fingertip</note>. For vehicles, seats, and beds, the measurement is determined by the <term>Angula</term> <note>finger-width</note>. || 34 ||

The science of <term>Vastu</term> <note>architecture/habitation</note> was first revealed by Brahma, then passed to Vishvakarma <note>the divine architect</note>, and then to Vishnu. Sages and gods reside within this knowledge. One must understand the center and the periphery, for in the center lies the victory of the divine. || 35 ||

The winds and the works of Vishvakarma are threefold in their nature and application. <insert>The forms are</insert> Ishana <note>Northeast</note>, Vittapati <note>Lord of Wealth/North</note>, Nishakara <note>Moon/Northwest</note>, Jayanta, Shri, Vasudeva, and Vishnu. These are the twenty-three deities of the directions. || 35 || Now, regarding the afflictions: if the deities are troubled, it leads to disease, loss of wealth, suffering for the subjects, and even death or destruction. One must place the deities in their proper order to avoid such ruin. || 36 ||

The distance between the hand and the sun <note>likely a reference to gnomon shadow measurements</note> must be maintained in the foundation of temples and dwellings. If a <term>Hasta</term> <note>cubit</note> is lost or added suddenly during the work, it manifests as an obstacle to the festival or the completion of the task. || 37 ||

A <term>Tala</term> is twelve <term>Matras</term> in measure. Two <term>Talas</term> make a span. A <term>Danda</term> <note>staff</note> is four cubits. Two thousand <term>Dandas</term> make a <term>Krosha</term>, and four <term>Kroshas</term> make a <term>Yojana</term>. Thus, the wise calculate the extent of the earth in hundreds of <term>Yojanas</term>. || 38 ||

# The Layout of the Divine Grid

In the direction of the sky <note>the Northeast</note>, one finds Jaya, Aditi, and Shikhi. In the direction of the Fire <note>the Southeast</note> are Vitatha and Gruhakshata. In the direction of the Ancestors <note>the Southwest</note> are the deities of the void, and in the direction of the Wind <note>the Northwest</note> are the guardians of the gates. Parjanya and the other directions are established here.

On the right arm sits the Sun <note>Surya</note>, and on the left arm is the Moon. On the right hand is Savita, and on the left is the Earth-holder <note>Dharadhara</note>. In the region of the thighs are the groups of the Maruts and Mitra. In this system, the Creator <note>Brahma</note> is placed in the center, shining with divine light.

### The Outer Deities
Ishana <note>the form of Shiva in the Northeast</note>, Parjanya, Jayanta, Indra, the Sun, Satya, Bhrisha, and the Sky <note>Antariksha</note> are the deities of the eastern side. In the corner of Fire <note>Southeast</note> are Agni, Pusha, Vitatha, and the House-protector <note>Gruhakshata</note>.
<margin>The Hungry Ghost</margin>
Then follows Yama <note>the Lord of Death</note>, Gandharva, Bhringaraja, and the Deer <note>Mriga</note>. In the direction of the Ancestors <note>Southwest</note> are the Pitris, Dauvarika, Sugriva, and Pushpadanta. In the West are Varuna <note>Lord of the Waters</note>, Asura, Shosha, and Disease <note>Papi-yakshma</note>. In the Northwest are Vayu <note>the Wind</note>, the Serpent <note>Naga</note>, and the Main One <note>Mukhya</note>. In the North are Bhallata, Soma <note>the Moon/Kubera</note>, and the Mountain <note>Aditi</note>.

These twenty-one deities are situated on the outer perimeter. Now, the thirteen deities who reside in the inner middle section are described:

### The Inner Deities
Aryama is in the East, Vivasvan is in the South, Mitra is in the West, and the Earth-holder <note>Prithvidhara</note> is in the North. In the very center of all these, Brahma must be placed, for he brings auspiciousness to the entire structure.

Between the corners of the Water-god and the Wind-god, Savitri and Savita are located. In the corner of the Great Indra <note>Mahendra</note>, Jaya is found. In the Southwest, Rudra and the Lotus-born <note>Brahma's aspect</note> are placed alongside Yama.

### The Secondary Spirits and Grid Divisions
In the second ring, Vidarika is placed, and in the Southwest corner is Nirriti. The three types of Sinful Spirits <note>Paparakshasas</note> are in the Northwest corner, as prescribed by the ancient rules.

Brahma occupies the central four squares <note>padas</note>. The deities surrounding him occupy the middle squares. In the corners, the deities are assigned one square each. The Serpents and the Fire-spirits are also given their single-square portions. The deities like Aryama and others are assigned squares according to the proportions of the grid <note>the text refers to the 64 or 81 square divisions</note>.

In the three worlds, these deities are worshipped. In the outer corners, the "Half-Awakened" spirits reside. In the corners of the Creator, there are deities of two squares, while others are assigned only one square as declared by the sages.

The deities starting from Shiva <note>Northeast</note> and the Water-god <note>West</note> are placed with half-square portions in the corners. The horses, elephants, and vehicles of the gods are also assigned their specific spots in the cardinal directions.

Twenty deities are placed in the belly of the grid, starting from Aryama. The groups of Maruts and the celestial beings are honored with their own spaces. The Sun and the Wise Ones are given their portions.

These twenty-one deities, along with the Lotus-seated Brahma and Yama, provide happiness. The Serpents, the Sun, and the Eight Gods are placed with the Serpents on the outer edge. The spirits of Wasting <note>Shosha</note> and the two-fold Vidarika are placed near the Lotus-grid. These are the sequences of the deities, who, when properly awakened and honored, protect the household.

## Chapter 3: On Dimensions and Proportions

Now, the methods for determining the auspicious stars, lunar days, and planetary positions are described, for without these, the building may bring disease or loss of fame. Therefore, the formulas for <term>Aya</term> <note>income/gain</note> and other proportions are told. <gloss>Odd numbers are best</gloss> for the houses of the gods. || 1 || 

In the construction of palaces, temples, and royal residences, the measurements should be taken using the "Hand" <term>Hasta</term> <note>a unit of measure roughly 18-24 inches</note> prescribed in the scriptures. For the houses of laborers, the measurement of the master's own hand may be used. The <term>Aya</term> is calculated by fingers <term>Angula</term> and other units. In the city, the measurement is done by the "Staff" <term>Danda</term>. || 2 ||
