# The Motion of Olympus

...the motion of Olympus, in which the five planets along with the sun and moon are arranged, is called the "Cosmos" <note>original: κόσμον</note>; however, the part below these, which is beneath the moon and near the earth, where the things of ever-changing generation exist, is called the "Heaven" <note>original: οὐρανόν</note>. And while Wisdom <note>original: σοφίαν</note> is concerned with the ordered things of the celestial heights, Virtue <note>original: ἀρετήν</note> is concerned with the things of disorder <note>original: τῆς ἀταξίας</note>; the former being perfect, while the latter is imperfect.

In the center of the universe, therefore, is the so-called **Central Fire**, which by nature is the first: "the first thing joined together, the One in the middle of the sphere" <note>original Greek: τὸ πρᾶτον ἁρμοσθὲν, τὸ ἓν ἐν τῷ μέσῳ τᾶς σφαίρας</note>, which according to the above is called *Hestia* <note>The Greek goddess of the hearth</note>, and here more specifically named the *Hestia of the All*, the Hearth of the Universe, the House of Zeus, and the Mother of the Gods—all of which are mythical names. It is also called the Altar, the Cohesion, and the Measure of Nature. The first of these names alludes to the Altar of Hestia in the center and "navel" of city-states, located in the *Prytaneum* <note>The seat of government in ancient Greek cities</note>, where the eternal fire—the life-flame of the state—blazed. The other two names, however, undoubtedly refer to the fact that this is the Unity in which the world finds its stability, and which simultaneously, as the Unity, gives measure and limitation to everything.
