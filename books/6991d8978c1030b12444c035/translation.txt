# || Salutations to the True Supreme Brahman ||
# The Illustration of the Description of the Six Chakras
# composed by the Venerable 108 Swami Hansaswarup.

> To the non-terrifying, and to the terrifying, and to those more terrifying than the terrifying; to all and every form of the Destroyer, O Rudra, salutations be to Your forms.
> <note>original: "Aghorebhyo'tha ghorebhyo..." from the Taittiriya Aranyaka 10.19.1. This is a traditional Vedic prayer to Shiva/Rudra in his various peaceful and fierce aspects.</note>

> We meditate in our hearts forever upon that Infinite One, Shiva, who is joined with the half-body of His consort, whose ignorance-born diversity appears like the various phases of the moon in water; in whom the world appears like a city reflected in a mirror, and from whose supreme consciousness this world emerges like a silver-mirage on a shell.
> <note>This verse uses the classic Advaita (non-dualist) metaphors of a mirror reflection and a shell appearing like silver to explain how the physical world is a projection of divine consciousness.</note>

Dear readers! Whatever wonderful creation that <term>Supreme Lord of the Universe</term> <note>original: "Parabrahma Jagadishvara"</note> has made in His gross, vast macrocosm—that is, in His Universal Form—He has also created exactly the same, as it is, within this "three-and-a-half cubit" body.
<note>A "cubit" (haath) is a traditional unit of length; "three-and-a-half cubits" is the traditional Indian measure for the height of an average human being.</note>
In other words, the seven upper worlds such as the Earthly, Ethereal, and Heavenly realms, and the seven lower worlds such as the Atala, Vital, and Sutal realms; as well as the Sun, Moon, stars, oceans, mountains, trees, and rivers—whatever is seen manifested in this vast universe—all of them are situated exactly so within this "small universe," which is your body. The point is that the body is a reflection of the entire macrocosm. Just as a <term>Photographer</term> <note>original: "Photo-grapher"</note>, using the lens of his camera, can capture a reflection of a city as large as Mumbai onto a small four-inch plate, in the same way, that extremely clever "Photographer" in the form of the Creator has used the lens of <term>Illusion</term> <note>original: "Maya"; the creative power that makes the one appear as many.</note> to project a universe spanning millions of miles onto the tiny plate of the five elements.

->|| Evidence ||<-

> In this body exists Mount Meru, surrounded by the seven islands; there are rivers, oceans, mountains, fields, and the guardians of those fields. || 1 ||
> All the seers, sages, stars, and planets are here. The holy pilgrimage sites, the seats of power, and the deities of those seats reside here. || 2 ||
> The creators and destroyers of the universe, the sun and moon, move within it. Ether, air, fire, water, and earth are also here. || 3 ||
> All beings that exist in the three worlds are found within the body. Everything functions by surrounding the central axis. || 4 ||
> He who knows all this is a Yogi; of this there is no doubt. In this body, known as the macrocosm, everything is arranged according to its proper place. || 5 ||
> <note>original: "Shiva Samhita, Second Chapter." This is a foundational text of Hatha Yoga.</note>

This means that a person is truly a Yogi—there is no doubt—who, through the instruction of a <term>Teacher</term> <note>Guru</note>, comes to fully know within this body-universe the <term>Spinal Column</term> <note>original: "Meru-danda"; metaphorically identified with the cosmic Mount Meru.</note> and the seven islands, rivers, oceans, mountains, fields, guardians, sages, stars, planets, holy sites, seats of power, deities, the sun and moon (which create and destroy), ether, air, fire, water, and earth.

Dear readers! Not only this, but that "Photographer" has, through His wonderful power, hidden many other types of supernatural structures within this body composed of five elements. To understand these, the seers and great sages of old performed penance for a long time, and when they finally knew them, they were immersed in supreme bliss. As it is written in the "Bhrigu-valli, Chapter Three of the Taittiriya Upanishad":

"Bhrigu, the son of Varuna, approached his father Varuna and said: 'Venerable Sir, teach me about Brahman [the Ultimate Reality].'"

In other words, once Bhrigu, the son of Varuna, went to his father and prayed, "O Father, grant me the knowledge of the Divine." Then his father replied, "Seek to know the Divine through penance, for penance is the Divine." Following this, Bhrigu performed penance and, through that penance, discovered the following hidden layers within this body.

<margin>The scriptures have been summarized here; seekers of knowledge should consult the "Taittiriya Upanishad" for details.</margin>
1. He realized that Food is the Divine...
2. He realized that Breath is the Divine...
3. He realized that the Mind is the Divine...
4. He realized that Knowledge is the Divine...
5. He realized that Bliss is the Divine...
<note>These are the five "Koshas" or sheaths that veil the soul: Annamaya (food), Pranamaya (vitality), Manomaya (mind), Vijnanamaya (wisdom), and Anandamaya (bliss).</note>

It is clearly seen from these scriptures that this body is a storehouse of various wonderful substances, into which the Supreme Soul has entered in the forms of Food, Breath, Mind, Knowledge, and Bliss. That is to say, there are five "sheaths" in this body; by understanding each one thoroughly, the seeker attains the joy of the Divine. Therefore, out of these five, the secret of the first, the <term>Food-composed Sheath</term> <note>Annamaya Kosha; the physical body.</note>, is being explained here.

In this manner, there are many teachings of the saints regarding this "Body-Fort" <note>original: "Kayagarh"</note>; they are not written here for fear of making the book too long.

Now it should be known that this **Fort** has five city walls (fortifications), seven basements (cellars), three and a half million rooms, and seven stories (palaces). On the seventh palace, that King of Kings—the Great King, the Supreme Brahman in the form of Light—is residing. Just as a flag is placed upon the specific building of a fort where the King himself sits, in the same way, a flag in the form of the **shikha** <note>the traditional tuft of hair kept by Hindus at the crown of the head</note> has been placed on this body-fort where the Brahman resides in a hidden form. That is to say, the shikha indicates the location of the <term>Brahmarandhra</term> <note>the "Aperture of Brahman" or the soft spot at the top of the skull</note>. For this reason, the teachers of the Eternal Religion <note>original: "Sanatana Dharma"</note> established the system of "tying the shikha" during the twilight prayers <note>original: "Sandhya"</note> while reciting the Gayatri Mantra.

<note>*Tying the shikha does not merely mean tying the hair; rather, it means tying one's mental focus to the meditation of Brahman near the Brahmarandhra during the time of Sandhya. For this reason, many teachers have given the instruction to merely touch it.</note>

Now, the explanation of the aforementioned five walls, seven basements, etc., is given, and their primary significance is shown.

### Five City Walls (Fortifications)
= 1. Space, 2. Air, 3. Fire, 4. Water, 5. Earth.
**Scriptural Proof:** <note>original: "Om akashadvayuh vayoragnih..." from the Taittiriya Upanishad</note> From the Self, space arose; from space, air; from air, fire; from fire, water; from water, earth.

### Seven Basements (Cellars)
= 1. Hair, 2. Skin, 3. Blood, 4. Flesh, 5. Bone, 6. Marrow, 7. Essence/Seed.
**Scriptural Proof:** <note>original: "Saptatvagastavitaponavakshah..." from the Srimad Bhagavatam</note> The body with seven layers of skin, eight branches, and nine openings.

### Three and a Half Million Rooms
= The three and a half million "nadis" <note>psychic channels or nerves</note> that are within this body.

= The Seven Lotuses:
1. The first palace has four doors; that is, the **Four-petaled Lotus** (Muladhara Chakra).
2. The second palace has 6 doors; that is, the **Six-petaled Lotus** (Manipura Chakra) <note>Note: The author lists Manipura second and Svadhisthana third, which differs from some other tantric traditions</note>.

<term>Pranayama</term> <note>original: "Pranayama"; the practice of regulating the breath</note> and similar practices are performed—meaning it is essential to draw the breath in, hold it, and release it. However, these days, because <term>Brahmacharya</term> <note>original: "Brahmacharya"; refers here to youthful vitality and self-control</note> is often lost during childhood, the vital fluid is weakened. Due to the presence of impurities like phlegm and wind in the channels <note>original: "nadis"</note>, living beings lack the strength to draw and release the breath properly. Consequently, the vital breath <note>original: "Prana-vayu"</note> cannot find its pure path. After practicing for a few days, poor seekers become exhausted and abandon the practice; losing hope in breath control, they simply cry out to "fate." Therefore, to encourage these exhausted seekers and lead them back to the practice, an extremely easy variation called **Mental Breath Control** <note>original: "Manasama Pranayama"</note> is explained. In this practice, one does not physically draw or release the breath; instead, one "ascends" and "descends" through the energy centers <note>original: "chakras"</note> using only the mind while meditating on them. If a seeker practices this Mental Breath Control daily for twelve years while following the rules of conduct and discipline <note>original: "Yama and Niyama"</note>, their practice will reach perfection.

### The Petals of the Lotuses
By "petals," it is not meant that there are literal lotus leaves spread out inside the body. "Petals" actually means a "cluster." Just as a cluster is formed when five or seven fruits grow together on a tree, the "petals" refer to the various directions from which clusters of nerves <note>original: "nadis"</note> branch out at specific locations in the body. For instance, the "four petals" of the Four-petaled Lotus mean that at that location, the nerves branch out into a cluster in four directions. This is why in English they are called a **Plexus**. The other petals should be understood in the same way.

### The Letters of the Petals
It is not as if the letters A, Ā, I, Ī, Ka, Kha, Ga, Gha, etc., are literally engraved on these petals. Rather, the meaning is that when one speaks, the impact of the air causes a specific letter to resonate from a specific cluster; that letter is then assigned to that petal. This is why the fifty letters from 'A' to 'Ha' are shown on the fifty petals of the six centers. In the thousand-petaled lotus, groups of twenty petals produce the same letter. Just as in a **Printing Press**, each **Case** contains many pieces of the same **Type** (letter), and if a word requires the same letter two or four times, they are taken from those cases and assembled; similarly, when speaking, if the same letter appears several times in a word, the petals of the brain provide them—as in the sounds "ka-ka," "cha-cha," "ba-ba," "dan-n," "kak-koo," etc.

### The Elements of the Lotuses
The Earth element is in the four-petaled center, Water in the six-petaled, Fire in the ten-petaled, Air in the twelve-petaled, and Ether in the sixteen-petaled. The meaning of these five elements being in these five centers is like a railway **Engine**: in one place fire is burning, in another water is heating, elsewhere **Steam** is being prepared, and in another place air is providing pressure. By their combination, the train is able to move forward. Similarly, after eating food and drinking water, these five elements are prepared in these five locations in the body, which nourishes the body and enables it to perform all its functions. In the two-petaled center is the *Great Principle <note>original: "Mahat-tattva"</note>, which is the source where all elements manifest, and the thousand-petaled center is beyond the elements—the place of the Supreme Absolute.

"Now, hereafter" <note>original: "atha anantaram"</note>, the Root Lotus <note>original: "Adhara-padma" or "Muladhara"</note> exists—this is the implied meaning. What is it like? It is attached to the mouth of the channel that lies within the center of the spinal column <note>original: "merudanda"</note>. It has four crimson petals <note>original: "shona-patram"</note>. Its face is turned downwards. It is joined with the four letters of the "Veda" <note>the number four is often symbolically called "Veda"</note>. Which letters? These four letters are the color of molten gold and are situated on the four red petals in a clockwise direction. Thus, the Root Lotus exists at the mouth of the <term>Sushumna<gloss>the central energy channel of the subtle body</gloss></term>, between the genitals and the anus, facing downwards, glowing like molten gold, and supporting the four letters *Va, Sha, Sha, Sa*. ## || The Commentary ||

**"The Vermilion..."** <note>original: *Sindureti*—referring to the first word of the verse being commented upon</note> — By "vermilion-filled" is meant a heap of vermilion; like that, it is beautiful and red. That "lotus" is the one named <term>Svadhisthana</term> <note>The second energy center, located near the base of the sexual organs</note>, which is distinct and separate from the <term>Muladhara</term> lotus. What is its nature? It is located in the region of the "root of the banner" <note>original: *dhvaja-mula*, a traditional euphemism for the base of the male organ</note>, arranged and woven within the <term>Sushumna</term> <note>The central energy channel of the subtle body</note>. Furthermore, it is surrounded and enveloped by "limbs" or "covers," which refers to its six petals. What are these petals like? They are joined with letters that have the color and brilliance of lightning. Specifically, they begin with the letter *Ba* and end with the letter *Purandara* (which refers to the letter *La*). This means the letters are: *Ba, Bha, Ma, Ya, Ra, La*. Furthermore, they are "shining with dots," meaning they are adorned with the <term>Bindu</term> <note>The nasalized 'm' sound or anusvara</note>. The essential meaning is that the vermilion-colored Svadhisthana lotus, located at the root of the genitals and surrounded by six petals adorned with the letters *Ba, Bha, Ma, Ya, Ra, La* (each with an anusvara), exists as a separate entity from the Muladhara lotus. <note>Metre: Vasantatilaka. Its definition: "Vasantatilaka is said to have the ganas ta, bha, ja, ja and two gurus."</note> ॥ 1 ॥

**"Of this..."** — "Within this" means in the center of this Svadhisthana lotus. There exists the "lotus-circle" (the water-disk) of **Varuna**, the presiding deity of water. What is this water-circle like? It possesses a shimmering, white, and pure light; it is white in color. Furthermore, within that circle, there is the <term>Bija</term> <note>Seed-syllable</note> *Vam*, which belongs to Varuna. What is this seed-syllable like? It is adorned with the shape of a half-moon. Furthermore, it is as white as the autumnal moon. It is stainless and pure, and it is "mounted on a Makara," meaning its vehicle is the <term>Makara</term> <note>A mythical sea-creature often depicted as a cross between a crocodile and a dolphin</note>. Since Varuna's vehicle is the Makara, it is established that his seed-syllable also rides the Makara. The sense is: inside the Svadhisthana chakra is the water-circle of Varuna, and in the center of this circle resides the seed *Vam*, white as the autumn moon and mounted on a Makara. <note>Metre: Vasantatilaka</note> ॥ 2 ॥

**"His..."** — Within the "lap" or middle region of that seed-syllable *Vam*, may **Hari** (Vishnu) protect you all. What is Hari like? He possesses a beautiful splendor through his "blue light," meaning his body is dark blue in color. Furthermore, he is "clothed in yellow," meaning his garments are yellow. Furthermore, he possesses the "pride of first youth," meaning he is endowed with the vigor and pride of a young man. Furthermore, he bears the <term>Shrivatsa</term> <note>A special mark on the chest of Vishnu</note> and the <term>Kaustubha</term> <note>A divine jewel worn by Vishnu</note>. Furthermore, he "holds four Vedas as arms," meaning he has four arms. The sense is: within the seed *Vam* of the Svadhisthana lotus, there resides the blue-colored, youthful, four-armed Vishnu. <note>Metre: Vasantatilaka</note> ॥ 3 ॥

---

**"Right here..."** — Right here, in the very lap of the seed-syllable *Vam*, the famous Power (Shakti) named **Rakini** truly and constantly shines. What is she like? "Blue lotus-like"—her brilliance and color are like the interior of a blue lotus; she is dark in color. Furthermore, she manifests the beauty of her body through her "uplifted hands" which hold "various weapons." Furthermore, she is adorned with "divine clothes and ornaments," and her "mind is intoxicated," meaning her heart is filled with great joy. The essential meaning is that within this very seed *Vam*, the blue-colored, four-armed Rakini Shakti resides. <note>Metre: Vasantatilaka</note> ॥ 4 ॥

**"Named Svadhisthana..."** — Now the author describes the fruit of meditating on the Svadhisthana lotus. Whosoever (any person) meditates upon this stainless "lotus named Svadhisthana," for that person, the "entirety of enemies"—beginning with the defect of ego and including the six internal enemies <note>Lust, anger, greed, etc.</note>—is instantly destroyed at that very moment. Furthermore, that person becomes a "Lord of Yogis." Additionally, having become like the sun in his brilliance to dispel the "wonderful mass of darkness" that is delusion or ignorance, he composes a "wealth of nectar-like poetry" in the forms of prose, verse, and elaborate literary works. <note>Metre: Sragdhara</note> ॥ 5 ॥

**"In the middle of that"** — In the middle of that region of wind, one should meditate upon the "Seed of the Breeze" <term>Yam</term> <note>original: *pavanaksharam*; the seed-syllable 'Yam' is the sonic vibration of the air element</note>. What is it like? It is "sweet," meaning characterized by a pleasant gentleness. Furthermore, it is "gray like a line of smoke," meaning it has the slightly pale, dusky color of a mass of smoke—a mixture of light white and dark shades. It is "adorned with four hands," meaning it possesses four arms. It is "mounted upon a black antelope," riding the <term>Krishnasara</term> <note>The blackbuck or antelope represents the swift, restless, and light nature of the wind</note>. Here too, as before, one should visualize the seed-syllable as having hands and a vehicle. At its core, within that form of the Wind-Seed 'Yam', one should contemplate the god named **Ishana** <note>A benevolent form of Shiva</note>, who is a "treasure-house of compassion," immaculate and pure, and white in color like a swan. He is "granting fearlessness and boons" to the three worlds—heaven, earth, and the underworld—meaning he offers liberation and fulfills the desires of all beings. In the center of the smoke-colored, four-armed 'Yam' seed riding the black deer, one should meditate on the white-colored Shiva, named Ishana, who grants protection and blessings with his hands. (Poetic Meter: *Shardula-vikridita*) ॥ 2 ॥

**"In this place"** — Here, within this 'Yam' seed or in the presence of the god Ishana, the power known as **Kakini Shakti** certainly resides. What is she like? She is "yellow like fresh lightning," possessing a golden hue like a clear flash of electricity. She is "three-eyed," "auspicious," and "the giver of blessings." She is "endowed with all ornaments," adorned with every kind of jewelry. She is "associated with Yoga," meaning she is beneficial and brings joy and welfare to those who practice yoga. With her "four hands," she is "holding a noose, a skull-bowl, and the gestures of blessing and protection." <note>The noose (*pasha*) represents the binding of desires, while the skull-bowl (*kapala*) represents the transcending of the ego.</note> She is "intoxicated," meaning filled with divine joy. Her "heart is softened by the full essence of nectar," meaning her heart is saturated with the liquid of immortality. Finally, she is "wearing a necklace of skeletons," adorned with a garland of bones. In this 'Yam' seed dwells the four-armed, lightning-bright, three-eyed goddess Kakini. (Poetic Meter: *Shardula-vikridita*) ॥ 3 ॥

**"This"** — Inside the pericarp <note>the central seed-pod</note> of this lotus, there shines a certain power. It is called the **Triangle Shakti**. Within that triangle resides a well-known energy. What is she like? Her "body is as delicate as ten million lightning bolts," meaning her form is as beautiful and tender as hundreds of thousands of flashes of light. There also resides the Shiva-linga known as **Bana**. <note>The *Bana-linga* is a naturally shaped stone found in rivers, but here it is a subtle pillar of light in the heart.</note> Not only is the famous Shakti within it, but the Shiva-linga named Bana is also there; this is the supreme truth. This god named Bana is "resplendent with body-unguents the color of gold," shining with the brilliance of golden saffron. On his head, there is a "subtle opening" or pore, where an eight-petaled lotus—the "abode of Lakshmi" or divine splendor—shines like a radiant jewel. The essence is that within the twelve-petaled lotus resides the Triangle Shakti, and within her is a lightning-like power and the golden-hued Bana-linga, upon whose head sits a jewel-like eight-petaled lotus. (Poetic Meter: *Shardula-vikridita*) ॥ 4 ॥

**"One should meditate"** — The person who meditates upon this lotus, the **Anahata**, in their heart and mind, becomes a "Master of Speech," equal to Brihaspati <note>The preceptor of the gods and master of eloquence</note>. Even as a mortal, they become like God—specifically like Shiva <note>original: *Hara*</note>—and become "capable of protecting the worlds," meaning they gain the power to preserve or dissolve the three realms of heaven, earth, and the underworld. This lotus is like a **Surataru** <note>The Kalpavriksha or wish-fulfilling tree</note> because it fulfills all the desires of the practitioners. It is the "dwelling place of the playful God Shiva" <note>original: *Sharva*</note>. It is further "illuminated by the flame of a lamp in a windless place," representing the individual soul (*Jivatman*) which remains steady and unmoving like a candle flame where there is no breeze. It is "adorned by the orb of the sun," and the center is beautified by the "shining filaments" of the flower. (Poetic Meter: *Shardula-vikridita*) ॥ 5 ॥

**"Lord of Yogis"** — Connecting this with the previous verse: the person who meditates on this lotus becomes a "Lord of Yogis," the greatest among practitioners. He becomes "constantly dear to the hearts of women," loved exceedingly by them. He also becomes a "Lord of the Wise," the foremost among the learned. He is "virtuous," "grateful," and has "conquered his senses." He is "capable of intense concentration in meditation." Through his mastery of "prose and verse," he becomes a "constant stream of the water of poetry," meaning he possesses an extraordinary wealth of eloquent and rasa-filled expression. Finally, becoming "equal to the deity who delights Lakshmi" <note>Narayana or Vishnu</note>, he becomes "capable of entering another’s body" <note>original: *parapura-pravesha*; a legendary yogic power</note> in an instant. (Poetic Meter: *Shardula-vikridita*) ॥ 6 ॥

### || Vernacular Commentary ||

The commentary for verses 1 and 2 is provided together. Above the previously mentioned heart-lotus, in the middle of the throat, there is a sixteen-petaled lotus of pure white color. On its sixteen petals, the sixteen vowels from **A** to **Ah** <note>original: "अ to अः"</note> are glowing in a blood-red color. This is called the **Vishuddha Chakra**. In the center of this lotus, a circular "Space Mandala" or "Void Circle" is shining, filled with the light of the full moon. In this place, the **Seed-Syllable of Ether (Ham)** is present, white in color, four-armed, and riding an elephant. He wears white clothes and his four arms hold a noose, the gesture of fearlessness, an elephant goad, and a boon. In the lap of this "Ham" seed-syllable resides the Lord **Sadashiva**, who is half-Gauri (Parvati), white as snow, famous among the perfected ones, three-eyed, five-faced, and ten-armed, wearing a tiger skin around his waist like a garment. He is always capable of granting various types of welfare and success (siddhis) to his devotees. || 1, 2 ||

Inside this nectar-filled lotus, near Lord Sadashiva, resides the Goddess named **Shakini**. She is of pure white color, wears yellow clothes, and holds an arrow, a bow, a noose, and a goad in her four arms. Furthermore, in the center of this lotus, the full moon mandala with its sixteen phases and no dark spots is shining. This is the gateway to great liberation for those of controlled senses who desire all power and glory. || 3 ||

The practitioner who, at every moment, fixes his mind in this place—that is, restrains the fluctuations of the mind—and takes in the air <note>The practice of *Puraka* or inhalation.</note> to engage in Yoga, if that Yogi should become angry, he could make the entire three worlds tremble. And no one—neither Brahma, Vishnu, Hari-Hara, the Sun, nor Ganesha—would be able to pacify that anger. || 4 ||

The Yogi who masters all the limbs of Yoga <term>Yoga-anga</term> and meditates perfectly on this Vishuddha Chakra becomes capable of composing great poetry, becomes an excellent orator, a man of wisdom, and attains a peaceful mind. He becomes a "seer of the three worlds," knowing the news of all three realms, a well-wisher to all, and free from all sickness and grief. Furthermore, he becomes long-lived and, like the rays of the sun, becomes capable of destroying the darkness of all kinds of calamities. || 5 ||

Between the eyebrows—that is, in the shining space of the forehead between the two brows—there is a two-petaled lotus, white in color like the moon <note>literally: "the frost-maker"</note>. In the center of this lotus, the six-faced goddess named <term>Hakini</term> resides. In this place, the seed of the Vedas, the <term>Pranava</term> (OM), resides in all its glory. Seekers should, with an extremely steady mind, meditate on these elements in sequence: first, the Goddess Hakini in the center of the Ajna lotus; then the Mind; then the Itara Shiva-linga with the Supreme Power; and finally, the OM. By meditating in this way and turning the eyes upward with great stability, one gains awareness of the *Brahma-nadi*, which stretches from the Root Lotus (*Muladhara*) to the Thousand-Petaled Lotus (*Sahasrara*) at the crown ॥ 2 ॥

The soul who meditates in this place in the manner described becomes the greatest among seekers. They gain the power to enter other bodies, become a king among sages, omniscient (all-knowing), a knower of all scriptures, all-seeing, a benefactor to all, a proponent of non-duality (*Advaita*), and famous for possessing extraordinary spiritual perfections (*Siddhis*). It is the pure essence of the syllable OM, endowed with intellect and shaped like the flickering flame of a lamp. By "constructing the city of the sky" <gloss>*</gloss> and merging the mind perfectly, the seeker draws out the inner soul-light (which is like a flash of fire) and, beholding various wondrous forms, begins to see the entire universe—the whole of creation—as being made of that same soul-light ॥ 5 ॥

He is imperishable and holds His full majesty—the power of creation, preservation, and destruction—appearing like the spheres of fire, the moon, and the sun. In this sweet, supremely luminous place—the "City of Vishnu"—filled with supreme bliss (the aforementioned Ajna Chakra), the best of yogis, at the time of death, place their life-breath with great joy. They merge into that Supreme Person who is eternal, imperishable, unborn, the beginning of the three worlds, ancient, timeless, and only knowable through the *Vedanta* <note>the philosophical end-portion of the Vedas</note>. This means that any creature who, at the moment of death, becomes steady-minded and fixes their breath between the brows through the power of yoga and devotion, attains the Supreme Divine ॥ 7 ॥

**Regarding "Above that" (*Tadurdhve*)** — "Above that" means in the upper part, above the Ajna Chakra. At the peak or head of the nerve named <term>Shankhini</term>, and below the <term>Visarga</term> (which is the Shakti), there resides a thousand-petaled lotus. What is it like? It is **shining in the region of the void**—meaning its expansion or blossoming occurs in the "void" of the macrocosm <note>Sanskrit: *Brahmanda*, the egg of Brahma/the universe</note>. Furthermore, what else? It is **white like the very fullest moon**, meaning its color is pure white like an exceptionally full moon. Furthermore, it is **facing downward**. Furthermore, it is **beautiful** and charming. Furthermore, it possesses a **cluster of filaments** <note>Sanskrit: *kinjalka-punja*</note> that is as lovely as the rays of the midday sun. Furthermore, its **body is adorned by the letters beginning with 'A' and the forehead**—meaning its form is beautifully decorated by the letters of the alphabet starting with 'A' <note>The 50 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet are said to be repeated 20 times to fill the 1,000 petals</note>. This means the thousand petals are characterized by the letters beginning with 'A'. Furthermore, it is the **form of absolute bliss**, meaning its nature is eternal bliss. The summary of the meaning is: in the region above the Ajna Chakra, at the peak of the nerve called Shankhini, below the Visarga-Shakti, there shines the downward-facing thousand-petaled lotus, whose petals are decorated with the fifty letters from 'A' to 'Ksha', and whose nature is supreme bliss. (From the *Shobha* commentary) ॥ 1 ॥

**Regarding "Inside dwells" (*Samaste*)** — Within the center of that thousand-petaled lotus, the **pure full moon**, free from the hare-mark (meaning without any stain), resides. What is it like? It has a **shimmering mass of moonlight**, meaning a collection of moonlight that is radiating and beautiful. Furthermore, it is the **soft, spreading luster of a collection of supreme nectar**—meaning its light or radiance is a "smile" or manifestation of the thick, spreading flow of the highest nectar. Inside that moon, a **triangle**—a Shakti in the shape of a triangle—constantly and perpetually flashes. What is the triangle like? It is **the form of a flash of lightning**. Inside that triangle, one should meditate on the famous **Void** (*Shunya*), which is formless. What is that void like? It is the **teacher of all the gods**, the best of all deities. Furthermore, it is **extremely secret**, meaning it should be hidden with great care. The summary: Inside the stainless full moon that exists within the mentioned chakra, within the lightning-like flashing triangle, resides the Void, which is worshipped by all gods and is highly secret...

**"Here resides"—** Here, in the triangle within the Thousand-Petaled Lotus, resides that famous **digit of the moon named Ama**. She is the **sixteenth part** of the moon, which is the "maker of the day" <note>this usually refers to the sun, but here refers to the lunar cycle</note>. She is like the **young morning sun**—meaning she is reddish in color. What is she like? **Pure**, stainless, and changeless. Furthermore, she is **like a single fiber** of a lotus stalk that has been split into a hundred parts; she has a form as subtle as one such fragment. Moreover, she is **Supreme** or excellent. Her **body is as delicate as a flash of lightning**—a soft, glistening body like a streak of lightning. She is **eternally risen**, constantly manifest because she never sets. This means she is eternally luminous. She is **downward-facing**. She is the **bearer of the stream of nectar**—the one who holds the flow of nectar that trickles down from the lineage of full bliss, the totality of universal joy. The meaning is that in this very Void exists the sixteenth lunar digit named Ama, which is as subtle as the hundredth part of a lotus fiber, shines like a garland of lightning, carries the stream of nectar flowing from the place of Brahman, is eternally manifest and downward-facing, and resembles the young sun. (In the **Shardula-vikridita** meter) ॥ 7 ॥

She is the "life-soul" of all living beings, the very essence of their breath. In the minds of the sages <note>original: *munis*</note>, she joyfully brings about the realization of Truth <note>original: *tattva-bodha*</note> or knowledge of Brahman; she is the generator of essential wisdom for those prone to deep reflection. The gist is: within the aforementioned "Liberation-digit," the Power of Liberation shines—radiant like ten million suns, mother of the three worlds, subtle as a hair-tip, extremely hidden, the life-force of beings, and the bringer of divine knowledge to ascetics. It is the "Place of Supreme Power." It is the "Light of Pure Realization," from which the light of stainless knowledge originates. Above the aforementioned Ajna Chakra, at the peak of the channel called Shankhini *, in the region of the void—that is, spread within the crown of the head—below the power named Visarga, there is a white **Thousand-Petaled Lotus** that is exceedingly beautiful and radiant like the full moon. It faces downward †. Within it, filaments of a red color, shining like the rays of the morning sun, are resplendent. Furthermore, the fifty letters of the alphabet from 'A' to 'Ksha' are present on the petals of this lotus—specifically, twenty petals are threaded with each single letter. This lotus is the very embodiment of Eternal Bliss. || 1 ||

In the middle of this thousand-petaled lotus, a perfect full moon, beautified by nectar-filled rays, performs its divine play while spreading its lovely light in all ten directions. In the center of this lunar orb is a triangular yantra <term>yantra|a geometric diagram used for concentration</term> that flashes like lightning. Within this yantra, one should meditate with the utmost secrecy upon the Void-Brahman <note>original: *śūnya-brahma*</note>, the supreme teacher of all the gods. || 2 ||

One must keep this Void-Brahman hidden with great effort; it is extremely subtle, the root of supreme bliss, and radiant like a full moon adorned with sixteen digits <note>original: *kalās*; the phases of the moon</note>. In this same place, the Sky-God, the Supreme Soul known as Supreme Shiva, resides. He is famous among the perfected masters <term>siddhas|spiritually accomplished beings</term>, constantly raining down a stream of nectar and granting self-knowledge to yogis of pure intellect. He is the inner soul of all, an embodiment of the bliss of the union between Shiva and Power <note>original: *śakti*</note>. He is capable of destroying the darkness of ignorance just as the Sun <note>original: *haṃsa*</note> destroys the night. Furthermore, the Lord of all, the ocean of all happiness, the all-blissful Supreme Swan <note>original: *paramahaṃsa*</note> Lord, resides in this place. || 3, 4 ||

Followers of Shiva call this void the Place of Shiva; followers of Vishnu call it the Place of Vishnu; many devotees call it the Place of Hari-Hara <note>the combined form of Vishnu and Shiva</note>; those who serve the feet of the Goddess call it the Place of Power <note>original: *śakti*</note>; and many sages describe it as the Place of Matter and Spirit <note>original: *prakṛti-puruṣa*</note>.
