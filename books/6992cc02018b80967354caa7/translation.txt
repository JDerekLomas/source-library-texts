## Difficult Problems in Proportional Distribution, Section 3

<note>original: "難題衰分" (Nántí Shuāifēn). "Shuaifen" is a classical mathematical method for dividing items or profits according to specific ratios or "weights."</note>

->To the Tune of "The Moon over the West River"<-

<note>Chinese mathematical texts often presented problems in verse to make them easier to memorize. "The Moon over the West River" (Xījiāng Yuè) is a famous poetic meter used here as a mnemonic device.</note>

> Pure, picked cotton is carded fine and mixed together in one basket. 
> <gloss>9 catties and 12 ounces belong to Zhang Chang; Li De has 5</gloss> 
> catties and 4 ounces. Once spun and woven, the cloth measures 108 feet long. 
> <gloss>The distribution between the two parties must be clearly shown;</gloss> 
> There must be no bias toward either side.

**Answer:** Zhang Chang receives 7 <term>zhang</term> and 2 <term>inches</term>. Li De receives 3 <term>zhang</term>, 7 <term>feet</term>, and 8 <term>inches</term>.

<note>In this period, 1 zhang = 10 feet (chi), and 1 foot = 10 inches (cun). Zhang Chang’s portion is 70.2 feet; Li De’s is 37.8 feet.</note>

**Method:** List the respective <term>factors</term> <gloss>Zhang Chang: 9 catties, 12 ounces; Li De: 5 catties, 4 ounces</gloss>. Convert each into ounces using the standard conversion: Zhang obtains 156 <gloss>ounces; Li obtains 84 ounces. Summing these yields 240, which serves as the "divisor" (fa). Take the total cloth</gloss> of 108 feet and multiply it by each individual's factor, then divide by the divisor to obtain each share.

<term>Catty (斤, jin)</term>: A unit of weight. In the Ming Dynasty, 1 catty equaled 16 ounces.

<term>Ounce (兩, liang)</term>: A unit of weight. 16 ounces made 1 catty.

<term>Factor (衰, shuai)</term>: In proportional distribution, this refers to the ratio or "weight" assigned to a participant based on their initial contribution.

<term>Method (法, fa)</term>: In a mathematical context, this word can mean "the rule," "the procedure," or specifically "the divisor" in a division operation.

# Song of Decreasing Steps on a Journey

> Three hundred and seventy-eight *li* to the pass,
> <note>A *li* is a traditional Chinese unit of distance, roughly equal to 500 meters or one-third of an English mile.</note>
> On the first day, his vigorous stride found no difficulty.
> But the next day his legs ached, and his pace was halved,
> Over six days, he traveled slowly to reach that pass.
> If you wish to see the number of *li* traveled each day,
> I ask you, sir, to calculate it carefully in return.

**Answer:** 
On the first day, he traveled 192 *li*.
On the second day, he traveled 96 *li*.
On the third day, he traveled 48 *li*.
On the fourth day, he traveled 24 *li*.
On the fifth day, he traveled 12 *li*.
On the sixth day, he traveled 6 *li*.

**Method:** Set the total of 378 *li* as the <term>dividend</term> <note>original: 實 (shí), the number to be divided</note>. Arrange the <term>proportional factors</term> <note>original: 衰 (shuāi), the relative weights assigned to each part of a distribution</note> as <gloss>1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32</gloss>. Divide the dividend by the divisor to get <gloss>6</gloss> *li*, which is the distance for the <gloss>sixth</gloss> day. Progressively double this value for each preceding day to match the requirements of the question.

# Newly Compiled Direct Pointer to the Systematic Treatise on Computational Methods

## Volume 15

### Folding the rope to measure the pole, it falls short of the pole by one <term>tuo</term> <note>original: "托" (tuo); a unit of length defined by the span of a person's outstretched arms, here specified as 5 chi.</note>.

**Answer:** The pole is one <term>zhang</term> and five <term>chi</term> long <note>approximately 15 feet</note>. The rope is two <term>zhang</term> long <note>approximately 20 feet</note>.

**The method says:** Set down double the shortage <gloss>1 doubled becomes 2</gloss>. Add 1 <gloss>to the 2 to get 3, representing the pole</gloss>. Add another 1 <gloss>to get 4, representing the rope</gloss>. Multiply each of these by the length of one <term>tuo</term> <gloss>which is five chi</gloss> to reach the answer. <note>The logic here assumes the rope is folded in half. If $R$ is rope and $P$ is pole: $R/2 = P - 1$. By using the ratios derived (3 and 4) and multiplying by the unit (5 chi), the solver finds $P = 3 \times 5 = 15$ and $R = 4 \times 5 = 20$.</note>
