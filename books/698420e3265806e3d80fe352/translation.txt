# Secret dealings of the Jesuits.

[A] great and populous city is built (what am I saying, particularly! since none of their Colleges is in a lowly or despised place). When you, I say, enter such a College, look first upon the Porter, who is like the Ferryman of Hell, Charon, or the Three-headed Hell-hound Cerberus. For he is commonly very old, or if he is somewhat young, very discreet, and one of whose loyalty they are certain. If anyone does, this man knows all the secrets and mysteries of the Jesuit <term>Kabata<note>A term derived from "Kabbalah," used here by the author to imply a secret, oral tradition of trickery passed down through generations of Jesuit leaders.</note></term>. <margin>Kabata is the name they give to what their Fathers have left behind by word of mouth, and which they deliver from hand to hand.</margin> With this man lie all sorts of women’s and men’s clothing, with which the Jesuits, being dressed and prepared, commit wonderful deceptions, according to the character each one intends to play. For at one time, equipped as soldiers, they run through the streets and go into the public brothels to whore and banquet. And dressed as citizens, pretending to be Lutherans or Calvinists, they investigate and spy in the inns, at the market, and in outside social circles to hear what is commonly said about them, what reasons are being discussed, and what counsels are being taken. At another time, prepared as Doctors, with rings shining on their hands, and openly calling themselves Papists, they will go where they know the richest people are, who have great sums of money; they will approach them as if they came for some other purpose, and will seek in every way to bring themselves into their fellowship and friendship. Finally, they will exhort them to place their children under the Jesuit discipline, for they too (they say then) had them as masters and made good progress and advancement under them. Then again, dressed as Noblemen, and having hired a post-horse somewhere outside, they go to visit the Courts of Lords and Princes in a manner of friendship and service. Sometimes they also behave as displaced persons and exiles, and request that someone might come to their aid; and this they do especially among the theologians of the Protestants and Calvinists, to find out what they are writing against them. And such, I fully believe, were those who have so miserably deceived you, Honorable Men, until now. But so that you might better guard yourselves against such spies from now on, I will recount what the Jesuits at that time—at this time, I say—took as counsel and decided.

But now, for what purpose do the women’s clothes serve? No <term>pimp<note>original: 'Kofsiaen'. The young Jesuits do not even notice this, for they have secluded, vaulted, and even subterranean places and lofts for this purpose—much like those who in former times in Rome were professed in the Order of Venus <note>original: "Veneri"</note>, who had so beastly abandoned themselves to all kinds of fleshly wantonness and lust that the Roman Council, fearing the wrath and anger of the gods, ordered that house of prostitution to be razed to the ground, as <note>original: "Livius"</note> Livy <note>Titus Livius, the Roman historian.</note> testifies. These things would never have entered my mind, nor would they have come to light, had I not heard these and many other things from the most prominent of this Jesuit rabble and their companions.
