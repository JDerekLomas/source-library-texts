# P R E F A C E .

—edge that the Faithful Frenchman <note>original: "le fidéle François," the pseudonym of the author of the 1609 prophecy.</note> gained of a German Gentleman, called the Imperial Knight, a close friend of Count Bombast.

It appears that the Faithful Frenchman was a person of standing, who perfectly understood the affairs of the world, and who traveled for his pleasure.

He says that since he was not of a mind to follow the Court, nor to remain at home in idleness, he had a desire to see the world. In 1602, he departed from France to go to Spain, Italy, Flanders, Germany, and Poland. He always maintained a very fine retinue and socialized with the highest-ranking Lords.

In the year 1603, while he was in Hamburg, he met the Imperial Knight of whom we have already spoken. This Knight was a very honorable man, civil to foreigners, and particularly to the French; he was generous, virtuous, temperate, and possessed a penetrating, subtle, and learned mind.

The manners of the Faithful Frenchman greatly pleased the Knight. They formed such a close bond that the Knight called the Faithful Frenchman his elder brother. He also secured for him the friendship of Count Bombast, after having portrayed him as an *Adept*, <note>original: "Adepte."
