It seems strange that this idea should enter into the heart of any man; and yet, we are so tied up by previous perceptions <note>original: "apprehensions"</note> and conclusions fixed in our minds that further consideration of it cannot easily take place or sink into us. This may make us pause for a while and examine what the *cause* of this might be.

And then we may observe that our *disposition* is commonly such that we do not love to hear opposition or a different perspective from others, lest we should be led to think and believe something contrary to our previously *framed basis of opinion*, which was *abstracted* from outward senses alone. This is because when that basis is once removed, all conclusions built upon it fall to the ground. Thus we lose all that great labor and are forced to work anew upon a new foundation. Then there is a fear that if this new foundation should be shaken again, there must be another labor with the same *risk of unprofitable success*. 

This is such a massive discouragement to any mind that it eventually becomes *languishing* and, as it were, dead—especially if an assured, infallible, and immovable internal ground is not presented to it, so that it may go on cheerfully to build, hoping to enjoy the fruit of beholding the *beauty* of its *superstructure* <note>The "superstructure" refers to the complex system of beliefs or knowledge built upon a foundational principle.</note>. And when the mind is thus "dead" to its own efforts, it is most capable of being taught by the quickening <note>original: "quickening," meaning life-giving or awakening</note> Spirit of Truth that lies hidden within it, beneath its former but merely supposed truths.
