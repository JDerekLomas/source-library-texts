# Poem

## ON THE WEDDING
## OF THE HONORABLE AND
## LEARNED MAN MASTER JACO-

### bus Mercator of Sindelfingen, a helpful teacher of youth
<note>Jacob Mercator is the Latinized name for Jacob Kauffman. "Mercator" is Latin for merchant or tradesman (Kauffman).</note>
### in the monastery of St. Alban, and the chaste maiden
### Margaretha Ströling of Grüningen. Written on February 7,
<note>original: "Sept: Id: Febr:" refers to the 7th day before the Ides of February, which is February 7th.</note>
### in the year 1564 from the Virgin's birth, by Simon
### Studion of Urach, formerly 
### his 
### student.

---

*O Christ, most holy offspring of the all-hearing Father,*
<note>original: "panomphæi," a Greek-derived term meaning "source of all voices" or "all-hearing," usually applied to God as the source of all prophecy or prayer.</note>
*O Christ, the cause and the head of the marriage bed.*
*Part the sublime walls of the shining heaven,*
*And seek the kingdoms subject to the celestial region.*
*So that new joys may proceed in a happy course,*
*Which this current day brings to the two spouses.*
*Margaret, a model of conduct matching Jacob,*
*Is joined, behold, in the covenant of a lawful marriage bed.*
*Break off long delays, leave your vaulted dwellings,*
*And honor these festive times with your descent.*
*By your divine presence, protect the wedding torches,*
<note>original: "tædas." Torches were a traditional Roman wedding symbol, signifying the light and heat of the new household.</note>
*Which without you are able to have no weight or value.*
*Grant that the spouses be bound in a peace of kinship,*
*And let mutual love enter into their souls.*
*For without you, no one comes to the joys of the bed,*
*And without you, love endures at no time.*
*Instead, each person in turn descends to arms,*
*And forgetting peace, stirs up bitter wars.*
*Finally, send forth your ministers who love peace,*
*Who carry out your heavenly commands by your word.*

<summary>A formal wedding poem written by Simon Studion for his former teacher Jacob Mercator (Kauffman). The poem is a prayer asking Christ to bless the union with peace, mutual love, and divine presence, contrasting a Christ-centered marriage with the strife of worldly conflict.</summary>
