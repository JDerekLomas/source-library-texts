->To the Benevolent Reader<-

Since I have set out to publish in the Latin language, one after another, the writings of the most celebrated German Philosopher <note>This refers to the author, Basil Valentine, whose identity remains a historical mystery, though his works were central to 17th-century science.</note>, a man most experienced in the <term>chemical art</term> <note>original: "arte chymica." In alchemy, "theory" refers to the spiritual and philosophical understanding of how nature works.</note> first. I do this so that you might certainly train yourself in theory before you proceed to the <term>practice</term> <note>original: "praxin." This refers to the actual laboratory work of distilling, heating, and combining substances.</note>. For whoever applies his mind to the work without theory labors in vain. I wished to warn everyone of this, lest anyone take the opportunity—as is the common and detestable modern custom—to interpret everything maliciously and to slander these philosophers who now rest in God. After all, they left their <term>scholarly works</term> <note>original: "lucubrationes," literally meaning works produced by intensive study late into the night by lamplight.</note> to us not for their own gain, but for our sake and for the sake of those yet to come. Farewell in the meantime, and look forward to the remaining works as soon as possible, God willing.
