> Therefore, of those very things which are perceived by sight, no other animal perceives beauty, grace, or the symmetry of parts. Nature and reason, transferring this analogy from the eyes to the mind, think that beauty, consistency, and order should be even more strictly maintained in our decisions and actions. From these elements is forged and fashioned that "moral goodness" <note>original: "honestum"</note> which we seek: which, even if it were not celebrated, would still be honorable; and which, even if it were praised by no one, is by its very nature praiseworthy. You see the very form and, as it were, the face of moral goodness; if it could be seen with the eyes, it would awaken a marvelous love for wisdom. <note>original Latin: "Itaque eorum ipsorum quæ aspectu sentiuntur... mirabiles amores excitaret sapientiæ."
