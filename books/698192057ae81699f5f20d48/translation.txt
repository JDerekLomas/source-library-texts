### *Decree concerning Counterfeiters, issued on July 19, 1793, Year II of the Republic.*
<note>Year II refers to the second year of the French Revolutionary Calendar, which began in September 1792. This specific decree is a landmark in history, as it established the foundation for modern copyright law in France.</note>

Art. III. Peace Officers, Justices of the Peace, or Police Commissioners, shall be required to confiscate, at the request and for the benefit of Authors, Composers, Painters or Draftsmen and others, their Heirs or Assigns, all copies of printed or engraved editions published without the formal and written permission of the Authors.

Art. IV. Every Counterfeiter <note>In this context, "counterfeiter" refers to someone producing "pirated" or unauthorized copies of a book.</note> shall be required to pay the true Owner a sum equivalent to the price of three thousand copies of the original edition.

Art. V. Every Seller of a counterfeit edition, if not recognized as a Counterfeiter, shall be required to pay the true Owner a sum equivalent to the price of five hundred copies of the original edition.

---

I place the present edition under the safeguard of the Laws and the integrity of the Citizens. I declare that I will prosecute before the Courts any Counterfeiter, Distributor, or Seller of a counterfeit edition. I further promise to whoever identifies a Counterfeiter, Distributor, or Seller to me, half of the compensation granted by Law. The two copies, in accordance with the Law, have been deposited at the National Library.
<note>The requirement to deposit copies in a national library—known as "legal deposit"—ensures that a nation's published heritage is preserved and acts as proof of the publisher's claim to the work.</note>
