# THE CALL of GOD
## And the
# REFUSAL of MEN.

In which one sees how God in these last times <note>Bourignon, like many mystics of her era, believed she was living in the "end times" or the final age of the world.</note> calls men, whether to the administration or to the participation of the fullness of his graces & of his divine banquet; what disposition he requires of them for this, and how they make themselves incapable of it by the refusal they make of it: and thus, *that many are called; but few are chosen*. <note>A reference to the Biblical parable of the wedding feast in Matthew 22:14.</note>

*The whole contained in this Collection of the first Letters of*
**Lady ANTOINETTE BOURIGNON,**
*(born in Lille, in Flanders, on January 13, 1616.)*

# TO THE READER.

We begin here, Dear Reader, to share with you one of the first works collected from among several left to us, after her death, by Mademoiselle <term>Antoinette Bourignon</term> <note>A controversial Flemish mystic (1616–1680) who claimed to receive direct revelations from God and gained a significant following across Europe.</note>. Her printed works, some of which have appeared in the past, will undoubtedly have already given you some knowledge of her person and of what you should expect from her. If, however, you have known nothing of her until now, this single piece is capable of forming in you a true idea of her character; especially since everything you will see here consists of <term>familiar letters</term> <note>The term "familiar letters" refers to private correspondence written in an informal, candid style, rather than formal documents intended for public debate.</note>, in which it is customary to reveal, with sincerity, the character of one's heart and one's spiritual state. These are letters written to a person from whom she neither should nor could hide anything, since she was writing them to a theologian who was her pastor and her confessor.
