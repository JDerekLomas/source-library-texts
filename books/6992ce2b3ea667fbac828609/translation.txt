# The Ritual of the Capping Ceremony for Scholars and Commoners

Generally, any male between the ages of fifteen and twenty may undergo the **Capping Ceremony** <note>term: "Guanli," the traditional Confucian rite of passage marking a boy's transition to legal and social adulthood.</note>. Before the capping takes place, one must **divine the date** and the **Guest of Honor** <note>term: "Bin," a respected figure, usually a family friend or elder, invited to perform the ritual act of capping the youth.</note> at the **Ancestral Shrine** <note>The sacred space where the family’s ancestors are honored; major life events must be reported here.</note>. The protocol for notifying the Guest is the same as that used for officials of the same rank.

On the day of the ceremony, rise at dawn and hang curtains to create a private room to the east of the main hall. When the Guest arrives, the **Attendants** <note>Those designated to handle the ritual objects.</note> must all wear their formal dress. They set up a washing basin southeast of the **Eastern Steps** <note>term: "Zuojie," the ceremonial stairs reserved for the host (the father or elder), symbolizing his authority over the household.</note>. The ritual garments are arranged inside the curtained room beneath the western window; they face east, with the items of the highest order placed to the north.

Two mats are placed to the south, and the wine jar is positioned to the north of the clothing. Next are the wine cups and ewers. The **Official Cap** <note>term: "Futou," a black cloth headpiece that signifies adult status.</note>, the hat, and the headcloth are each placed on a tray and covered with a decorative cloth. Three Attendants hold these trays and stand below the hall, southwest of the western steps, facing east, arranged from the north.

The **Host** stands below the Eastern Steps. The younger brothers, sons, and relatives stand to the east of the washing basin. The **Usher** stands outside the gate to wait for the Guest. The **Youth to be Capped**—his hair still styled in **dual topknots** <note>term: "Shuangji," a hairstyle for children; the transition to a single topknot under a cap is the central act of this ritual.</note>, wearing a robe with a silk belt and plain shoes—waits inside the room. When the Guest arrives, the Host goes out to meet him, bows, and they enter and take their seats. Once they are settled, the Youth to be Capped emerges from the room. The Attendant requests permission to begin the proceedings, and the **Youth's Assistant** <note>term: "Zan," a person who assists the candidate with his wardrobe changes during the ritual.</note> prepares to act.
