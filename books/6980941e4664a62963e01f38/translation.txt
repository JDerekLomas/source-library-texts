# Front Cover and Spine

The manuscript is bound in a sturdy, dark brown leather cover, a testament to the durability of historical <term>bookbinding|the process of physically assembling a book from a stack of paper or parchment sheets</term>. The exterior is decorated with <term>blind-tooling|a decorative technique where patterns are pressed into the leather using heated metal tools without the application of gold or color</term>. 

The design features a large central diamond (rhombus) formed by triple-scored lines. This diamond is nested within a rectangular frame, also defined by triple lines, with additional lines extending from the points of the diamond to the corners of the frame, creating a balanced, geometric aesthetic common in early library collections.

The spine of the book, which would have faced outward on a scholar's shelf, features five <term>raised bands|horizontal ridges created by the cords used to sew the pages together, which are then covered by the leather of the spine</term>. These bands divide the spine into six compartments.
