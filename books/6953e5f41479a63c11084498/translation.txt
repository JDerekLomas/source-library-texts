--- Page 8 ---
<meta>This page provides an iconographic explanation of the frontispiece (title image) of the work, likely a volume of Antoni van Leeuwenhoek’s microscopic observations. The text is written by Pieter Rab (Petrus Rabus), a contemporary Dutch scholar and journalist.</meta>

->The images expressed in the title are<-
->explained thus<-

# PIETER RABUS.

**PHILOSOPHY**, the **Queen** of sciences, points with her scepter toward **NATURE**, who was previously veiled but is now clearly visible. Before her are various offspring of Nature, whose birth and procreation are contemplated through glass eyes <note>A reference to the lenses of the microscope</note> by keen **INQUIRY**, who is distinguished by winged temples and a garment covered in eyes. Toward her, eager **DILIGENCE** drags the unwilling **ERROR**, who is deformed by a missing hand, bound eyes, and the ears of a donkey. Three men, who rejoice in being called Philosophers, have not yet reached the threshold: the first a superstitious Jew, the second an overly-credulous Christian, and the third a Pagan from the school of Aristotle (carrying "hidden qualities" <note>original: "occultas qualitates" — a critique of old scholastic physics that explained phenomena by naming mysterious "qualities" rather than observing physical causes</note> upon his shoulders). Upon that threshold sits **TRUTH** herself, requiring no finery or adornment, and trampling the horrific **ENVY** under her feet. A divine light from heaven shines down upon the craftsmanship of Leeuwenhoek.

<summary>This page provides an allegorical interpretation of the book's frontispiece, celebrating the triumph of empirical inquiry and Leeuwenhoek's microscopy over traditional scholastic errors and superstition.</summary>
<keywords>Antoni van Leeuwenhoek, Pieter Rabus, allegory, Philosophy, Nature, Microscopy, Scientific Revolution, Truth vs. Error</keywords>
