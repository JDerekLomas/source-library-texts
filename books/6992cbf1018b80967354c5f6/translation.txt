**The Logic of Positioning:**

**The Dividend** <note>The number to be acted upon</note>
*   From the **First Digit of the Dividend** to the **Last Digit of the Dividend**.
*   The Dividend acts as the <term>Child</term> <note>In Chinese mathematics, the numerator of a fraction or the dividend is called the "child" (zǐ)</note>.
*   It is **Active** <note>Because this number changes as beads are moved during the calculation process</note>.
*   The leading positions are on the left; the next positions follow below.

**The Divisor** <note>The number used to operate on the dividend</note>
*   From the **First Digit of the Divisor** to the **Last Digit of the Divisor**.
*   The Divisor acts as the <term>Mother</term> <note>The denominator or divisor is called the "mother" (mǔ)</note>.
*   It is **Static** <note>This number remains fixed on the abacus as a reference while the dividend is manipulated</note>.

<header>Multiplication by Single Digits</header>

For any multiplier from two to nine units, use this method: place the quantity of goods as the <term>Dividend</term> <note>original: 實 (shí); the number being multiplied</note> and the price as the <term>Multiplier</term> <note>original: 法 (fǎ); the number acting upon the dividend</note>. Recite the "nine-nine" <note>The traditional Chinese multiplication table</note> to combine the numbers. When the result is ten or more, place the "ten" digit at the current position <note>original: 就身 (jiù shēn); a technical instruction for abacus carry-overs</note> as if skipping a space. Begin calculating from the last digit. To restore the number to its original value, use the method of "nine-return" <note>Division by a single digit</note>.

### Addition Method

Whenever using the addition method where the leading digit [of the rate] is "one," use this rule: set the quantity of goods as the **Augend** <term>Shi</term> <note>original: "實" (Shi). A "ten" returns to its own position, while the remainder follows;

### The Method for Simplifying Fractions

Simplifying involves using the **numerator** <note>original: 分子 (fenzi); literally "the son of the fraction"</note> to communicate with the **denominator** <note>original: 分母 (fenmu); literally "the mother of the fraction"</note>. The rule states: if the numbers can be halved, halve them. If they cannot be halved, subtract the smaller number from the larger number, repeating this mutual subtraction to find the "equal" <note>This refers to the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD), found via the Euclidean algorithm.</note>. Use this "equal" number to simplify the fraction.

For example, if a number is one-fourth of a <term>liang</term> <note>a tael or Chinese ounce</note>, it is 2 <term>qian</term> and 5 <term>fen</term> <note>mace and candareens; 1/4 of 10 is 2.5</note>. This is called a "terminating" or "finite" number. If a number is one-third of a <term>liang</term>, it is 3 <term>mace</term>, 3 <term>candareens</term>, 3 <term>li</term>, and 3 <term>hao</term>, with a remainder. This is what is called "non-terminating" or "infinite," and for this, the method of simplifying fractions must be used.

The explanation says: Simplifying fractions refers to instances when using the division method results in many odd remainders that do not divide evenly, carrying on for thousands...

### Systematic Treatise on Computational Methods: Volume 20

**Treating fractions** <note>original: 百分 (baifen); while this modernly means "percentage," here it refers to the general management of fractional parts</note> is the process of removing complexity to arrive at simplicity. However, there are some fractions that cannot be easily simplified <gloss>by simple halving</gloss>.

**The Rule states:** Designate the larger number as the **denominator** <note>original: 母 (mu); literally "mother"</note> and the smaller number as the **numerator** <note>original: 子 (zi); literally "son"</note>. Place the values of the "son" and "mother" in two columns. Mutually subtract the smaller from the larger <note>This describes the Euclidean Algorithm: repeatedly subtracting the smaller number from the larger until the remainders are equal</note> until they reach the same value. Use this resulting number as the **divisor** <term>fa</term>. Divide both the original numerator and denominator by this divisor, and there will be no remainder. This is what is meant by "regulating the irregular to achieve uniformity." For example, when people divide silver and the numbers do not come out even, or when objects cannot be physically split, one cannot name the resulting quantity without using this method of simplification.

### Reducing Fractions <term>tongfen</term> <note>In this context, *tongfen* refers to the process of converting whole numbers or mixed numbers into improper fractions to simplify multiplication and division.</note>

Reducing fractions means converting the whole number by the **denominator** <term>fenmu</term> <note>Literally "the mother of the fraction."</note> and incorporating the **numerator** <term>fenzi</term> <note>Literally "the child of the fraction."</note>. If a number is finite <note>That is, it can be expressed as a terminating decimal or a simple whole number.</note>, there is no need to reduce it. However, if there are fractional remainders <term>jiling</term> that are infinite, and one does not reduce them, how can the values be placed on the counting board <term>zhiwei</term> to be calculated? This is the reason the method of reducing fractions was established. 

For example, one-fourth of a **tael** <term>liang</term> is equivalent to 2 **mace** <term>qian</term> and 5 **fen** <note>Traditional Chinese currency and weights: 1 tael = 10 mace, 1 mace = 10 fen. Thus, 1/4 tael is exactly 0.25 taels.</note>. This is what is meant by a "finite number." However, if you have one-third of a tael, it is 3 mace, 3 fen, and 3 **li**, continuing with 3s infinitely. This is what is meant by an "infinite number." One must use the fraction to reduce the values before a calculation can be performed. Otherwise, those infinite fractional remainders can never be assigned a fixed position for calculation.

### Logic of Simultaneous Multiplication and Division

Suppose originally 8 geese could be exchanged for 20 chickens; for every 30 chickens, one could exchange them for 90 ducks; and for every 60 ducks, one could exchange them for 2 sheep. If you now have 5 sheep to exchange for geese, how many should you receive?

->Answer: 20 geese<-

**Method:** Using the <gloss>Method of Different Multiplication and Same Division</gloss> <note>This is a technique for managing complex ratios by grouping all multipliers and all divisors to perform the calculation in two large steps.</note>, set down the original <gloss>8 geese</gloss> and multiply them by the <gloss>30 chickens</gloss> to get <gloss>240</gloss>. Then, multiply this by the <gloss>60 ducks</gloss> to get <gloss>14,400</gloss>. Finally, multiply this by the <gloss>5 sheep</gloss> currently held to get <gloss>72,000</gloss>, which serves as the <term>shi; the dividend, or "solid" number</term>. 

Then, using the <gloss>Method of Different Multiplication and Same Division</gloss>, take the <gloss>20 chickens</gloss> received in exchange and multiply them by the <gloss>90 ducks</gloss> to get <gloss>1,800</gloss>. Multiply this by the <gloss>2 sheep</gloss> received in exchange to get <gloss>3,600</gloss>, which serves as the <term>fa; the divisor</term>. Divide the dividend by the divisor to obtain <gloss>20 geese</gloss>, which matches the question.

**Instruction:** It is noted that in the past, one would perform a sequence of individual divisions and multiplications, which often resulted in awkward remainders or fractional figures.
