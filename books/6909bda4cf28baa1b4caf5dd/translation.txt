There is no beauty that one does not make ugly; no modesty that one does not seek to ruin; no virtue that one does not shame; no body or soul that one does not mock, which one does not deform and make unseemly or ridiculous. The best people are the least loved and esteemed. Their seclusion is called melancholy; their reasoning is called extravagance; their godliness is called hypocrisy; their gentleness is called cowardice; their erudition is called insignificance; their patience is called timidity; their zeal is called madness; their joy is called wild frenzy <note>original: "uyt-geestery"; this refers to a state of being "out of one's spirit," implying a fanatical or irrational enthusiasm.</note>; their love is called lust; their freedom is called licentiousness; all their actions are called foolishness.

But how shall we leave the honor of men untouched when we do not do so for God's honor? With your permission, how often is His name and His attributes despised, and His word handled poorly? How often does one turn His holy word into a mockery? How many impieties are uttered against His mysteries? And how often does one make of the whole of religion nothing but a theatrical play? And do the taverns not echo with Gospel words taken in a perverted sense? And does one leave anything holy alone without defiling it, whether through superstition or blasphemy?

The Master having been treated thus <note>A reference to Matthew 10:25: "If they have called the master of the house Beelzebub, how much more shall they call them of his household?"</note>, what must the servant expect? And God not being loved, what must man be? No faith, no love, no sincerity.

# Binding Description

->**Front Cover**<-

This volume is bound in a traditional **half-binding** style <note>A binding technique where the spine and corners are protected by a durable material like vellum or leather, while the rest of the cover is finished with decorative paper.</note>. The spine and the protective corners are fashioned from a plain, light-colored material, likely **vellum** <note>A high-quality parchment made from animal skin, prized for its durability in bookbinding.</note>.

The central panels of the boards are decorated with **marbled paper** featuring a **combed** or **fan** pattern. This decorative effect was achieved by floating pigments on a liquid surface, "combing" them into rhythmic waves, and then transferring the design to paper. The pattern consists of repeating rows of multi-colored semicircular waves in vibrant shades of:
*   **Deep Red**
*   **Cerulean Blue**
*   **Yellow-Tan**

<margin>The marbled pattern suggests a production date in the late 18th or early 19th century, consistent with the "combed" style popular in European workshops.</margin>
