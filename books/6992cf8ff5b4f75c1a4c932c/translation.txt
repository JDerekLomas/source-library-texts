# General Introduction to the Various Nations of Europe in the Atlantic Ocean

The narrative says: I respectfully read that in the 50th year of the Kangxi reign [1711], in the tenth month on the *Renzi* day, <gloss>The Sacred Ancestor <note>The posthumous title for the Kangxi Emperor (reigned 1661–1722), considered one of China's greatest monarchs.</note> issued an edict saying:</gloss>

"Regarding overseas countries such as those of the Western Ocean <note>The term *Da Xiyang* literally means 'Great Western Ocean' or 'Atlantic,' but in this context, it refers broadly to the European powers.</note>: in a hundred or a thousand years, I fear China may suffer on their account. These are words I have predicted beforehand."

Now, in the era of Kangxi, Holland and England had established regular tribute, and the nation of Italy provided officials for the observatory <note>Specifically the Terrace of the Imperial Bureau of Astronomy.</note> to assist with astronomical calculations. While the four seas were in submission, how is it that the Great Sage <note>A respectful reference to the Emperor.</note> already possessed wisdom spanning the six directions <note>A term for the universe: North, South, East, West, Heaven, and Earth.</note> and concerns reaching across ten thousand generations?

That the terrestrial essence moves from south to north is known to those who observe the migration of birds; that the celestial essence moves from west to east is verified by those who cross the seas. The name of **Da Qin** <note>The ancient Chinese name for the Roman Empire, often associated with a utopian vision of a Western civilization equal to China.</note> has been heard in China since the Han Dynasty; however, the actual communication between **Da Qin** and China began during the Wanli era of the Ming Dynasty [1572–1620] with **Matteo Ricci** <note>original: "Li Madou." Ricci was an Italian Jesuit priest and one of the founding figures of the Jesuit China missions.</note>.

**Da Qin** is the nation of **Italy** in the Western Ocean. Generally, those known as **Franks** <note>original: "Folangji," a term originally derived from "Farang" or "Frank," used by the Chinese to refer to the Portuguese and later the Spanish or French.</note> and **Portuguese** who reside in **Macau** or enter the **Imperial Bureau of Astronomy** <term>Qin Tian Jian: the government office responsible for the calendar and astronomical observations</term> are all Italian...

According to the *Book of the Later Han* <note>original: *Hou Hanshu*. Along their roads, they establish postal stations and rest pavilions at regular intervals, all of which are plastered white. The customs of the people involve diligent farming; they plant many varieties of trees and practice sericulture <note>The raising of silkworms for silk production.</note>. When entering or leaving a place, they beat drums and hoist banners, flags, and streamers. 

The capital city where they reside is over a hundred *li* in circumference. Within the city are five palaces, each situated ten *li* apart. The pillars of the palace buildings are all made of crystal <note>original: *shuijing*. Their king visits one of the five palaces each day...

[The Roman armies] pursued [the northern tribes] deep into the deserts and as far as the **North Sea**, <term>razing their strongholds and sweeping their dens</term> <note>Original: 犁庭掃穴 (li ting sao xue). A traditional Chinese military idiom meaning to utterly destroy an enemy's home territory by "plowing their courtyards and sweeping out their caves."</note>. Only after a million corpses had been laid low were they able to achieve conquest.

However, the territory they opened was so vast that "the whip could not reach" the distant borders. Furthermore, habits of depravity, lawlessness, and extravagance had become common throughout the nation. To this was added the problem of the surrendered kings of the northern lands; having been settled in the Roman interior, their populations gradually flourished. They never forgot their desire for revenge. Each of them eventually returned to their original tribes and reported on the magnificent wealth of the Roman people and the fertility of their lands. They recounted the tragic deaths of their kinsmen and friends to incite the fury of the northern tribes. 

Consequently, these entire nations swore to seek vengeance. "Sleeping upon their weapons," <note>A reference to the idiom "sleeping on spears and tasting gall," signifying a determined wait for revenge.</note> they brought along their wives, children, slaves, livestock, and household goods, swarming in from all directions like bees. Wherever they passed, they swept the land clean; they burned every house they encountered and slaughtered everyone they met, until "livers and brains were smeared upon the ground." 

By the time of <term>Emperor Hui of the Jin Dynasty</term> <note>A Chinese chronological anchor referring to the reign of Sima Zhong (290–307 AD). This allows the Chinese reader to synchronize these events with the era of the "Rebellion of the Eight Princes" in China and the "Crisis of the Third Century" in Rome.</note>, the succeeding King <note>Referring to the Roman Emperor.</note> died in battle while resisting the northern tribes. The nation was greatly shaken. Out of necessity, they sued for peace and moved the capital from the **Western Capital of Rome** to the **Eastern Capital of Byzantium** <note>Original: 伯拉 (Bela). Likely a phonetic rendering of Byzantium.</note> to avoid the brunt of the invasions. They also transferred the troops who had been guarding the rivers of the Western Capital to the East to serve as guards. In doing so, they dismantled their own "fences and hedges" <note>A metaphor for border defenses.</note>, which ultimately led to the fall of the old capital.

# General Overview of the Atlantic Ocean

Their defenses were weak and solitary. In the twenty-second year of the reign of **Emperor Xiaowu of the Eastern Jin** <note>Approximately 395 AD. The author uses Chinese imperial calendars to date Western historical events.</note>, the nation was again divided between two sovereigns to rule the Eastern and Western capitals <note>Refers to the division of the Roman Empire into the Western Roman Empire (Rome) and the Eastern Roman Empire (Constantinople) after the death of Theodosius I.</note>. Afterward, the Western capital was repeatedly invaded by the **Goth** tribes. In the third year of the **Yuanhui** era of the **Song** dynasty <note>Approximately 475 AD. This refers to the Liu Song dynasty of China's Southern and Northern periods.</note>, the Goths plotted against Rome; the King of the Western capital came out and surrendered. From then on, the Western capital was occupied by the Goths. Only the Eastern capital continued to be called the **Roman** state.

A barbarian chief named **Odoacer** <note>Original: 科多亞塞 (Keduoyasai). The Germanic soldier who became the first King of Italy after deposing Romulus Augustulus in 476 AD.</note> subsequently seized the Western capital and ascended the throne as king. The palaces were burned and destroyed, and the treasuries were emptied and scattered. After several hundred years, the collapse of that realm was total.

At the beginning of the **Tang Dynasty** <note>Early 7th century AD.</note>, the religion of **Islam** <term>Islam</term> <note>Original: 回敎 (Huijiao), the traditional Chinese term for Islam.</note>, founded by **Muhammad**, arose in **Arabia** <term>Arabia</term> <note>Original: 天方 (Tianfang), literally "The Heavenly Direction," a historical Chinese name for Arabia, particularly Mecca.</note>. They annexed **Persia**. Their power grew so strong that they attempted to force Rome to enter the Islamic faith, but Rome would not follow. Because of this, the Islamic armies of Persia repeatedly invaded the eastern borders of Rome. The regions of **Judea** and **Egypt** <note>Original: 麥西 (Maixi), likely a transliteration of "Misr," the Arabic name for Egypt.</note> all fell. The lands east of the strait <note>The Bosphorus or Dardanelles.</note> fell entirely to Islam; only a small fragment of **Greek** land remained.

At the end of the **Yuan Dynasty** <note>The mid-14th century AD.</note>, the lands east of the strait fell entirely to the **Turks**. They repeatedly crossed the sea strait to invade Rome, swallowing up almost all the surrounding towns. The fall of the capital, **Constantinople**, marked the final end of Rome. The Roman military was once renowned for its peerless discipline, which remains a model for military law even to this day. However, the empire fell because later generations failed to uphold the "Way of the Former Kings" or focus on diligent statecraft. Instead, they obsessed over territorial expansion and militarism. When prosperity reaches its peak, a collapse must follow. Foreign threats struck suddenly, humiliating the state and eroding its prestige. A millennium of opulence was cast into the wilderness beyond civilization. How truly lamentable!
