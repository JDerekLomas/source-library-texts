## Schumontu.

If one wishes to know the essence <note>original: "Wesen"; here meaning the fundamental nature or "being" of a thing</note> of every thing precisely, then seven points <note>original: "Stücke," meaning parts or aspects of an argument</note> primarily come into consideration. This knowledge leads us imperceptibly and surely to that of the true God. Above all things, one must consider the being in itself, seek to research its nature, and then its characteristics and properties: whether it be capable of action <note>original: "Handlung"</note> or not, whether it be composite or simple; in what it agrees with other beings; or what unique qualities it possesses. One counts in general nine categories <note>original: "Gattungen"</note> of beings: the earth, the water, the light, the wind, the air, the time, the regions of the world <note>Refers to the cardinal directions or space</note>, the soul, and the will. Then the characteristics of each being are considered: whether it is sentient or not; whether it constitutes only one or several in number; whether it is composed of several inseparable or separable parts; what it has in common with others, or what is peculiar to it; how large it is, and finally whether it is capable of intellect <note>original: "Verstand"</note>, joy, pain, desire, hate, reflection, and memory.
