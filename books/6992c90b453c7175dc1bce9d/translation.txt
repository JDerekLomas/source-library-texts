# Designs and Patterns for Decorative Carved Woodwork <note>The term "Diao muzuo" refers specifically to the craft of ornamental carving on wood, distinguishing it from general construction carpentry.</note>

1. Carving in the Round <note>original: "混作" (hunzuo); this refers to fully three-dimensional sculptural carving rather than low-relief or flat designs.</note>

2. Carved Inserts within the Spandrels of the Brackets <note>original: "栱眼" (gongyan); these are the triangular or rectangular spaces formed within the complex wooden bracket sets that support the roof. These spaces were often filled with decorative openwork or carved inserts.</note>

3. Decorative Waist Panels for Lattice Doors and Related Structures <term>huaban</term> <note>Lattice doors (gezimen) often featured a solid horizontal section at waist-height known as the "waist panel" (yaohuaban), which was a prime location for intricate floral or geometric carvings.</note>

4. Floral Medallions for Coffered Ceilings <term>pingqi</term> <note>The "pingqi" is a high-status ceiling composed of flat, square panels. Each panel often featured a central decorative "flower plate" (huapan) or medallion.</note>

5. Miscellaneous Designs, Including Cloud-Brackets and Other Forms <note>Cloud-brackets (yungong) are bracket components shaped like auspicious swirling clouds, often used in ceremonial or highly decorated buildings.</note>
