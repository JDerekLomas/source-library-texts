*The nature of Empire is by its own accord incited toward expansion:* <note>original: "amplificationem"</note> *because, the more august, extensive, and powerful any Kingdom is, the more firmly, safely, and durably it is held.* Bodin <note>Jean Bodin (1530–1596), a French jurist and political philosopher who pioneered the concept of sovereignty.</note> no. 717. *And for this reason, we believe that the zeal for increasing and expanding the Commonwealth <note>original: "Reip." 3. *The **Natural Expansion of the Commonwealth** is thought to occur either through the propagation of the People, or when men from foreign States are enticed by various allurements to join us. To this same category I refer the cultivation of barren places, and any similar means by which we improve the character of the soil through our own ingenuity.*

4. *The **Industrial Growth of the Commonwealth** consists both in the height and dignity of the State, and in the abundance of resources and wealth, both public and private. To this also belong the **Wonders** of buildings and foundations, which generally follow the magnitude of the Commonwealth. <note>See Kirchner, Thesis 3, point 3. 19. *Thus Lithuania was once united with Poland (see the **Body of Polish History**, folio 171, volume 1). More recently, the remnants of the Kingdom of Navarre were united with France (as noted by Botereus, 14, folio 355), and Scotland was united with England (see the **Speech of the King of England**, March 19, 1603, and Pierre Matthieu, book 7, folio 652).*

20. *The enlargement of a Republic, or the expansion of territory by force, occurs through the <term>right of war</term> <note>original: "jure belli"</note>: specifically through surrender or by storming. The law of surrender dictates that a People—along with their city, land, water, borders, shrines, and all their possessions—come under the power and dominion of the victor, just as the formula for Surrender in Livy (Book 28) demonstrates. Sometimes a surrender is made with a treaty; although such a thing is done through force and fear, it must be honored no less than a voluntary agreement. Jean Bodin argues this (Book 5, *On the Republic*, number 596) against Bartolus <note>Bartolus de Saxoferrato, a highly influential 14th-century legal scholar.</note> and other interpreters of great name.*

21. *Things taken by storm fall absolutely to the <term>right of Mars</term> <note>original: "Martis juri"; a legal reference to the god of war, signifying the absolute rights of a conqueror over the conquered.</note>. From this, the possession of things acquired in a "just war" becomes most lawful, and they can never be reclaimed for the same cause. Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities*, Book 3.*

22. *It is much more difficult to protect a recently gained Kingdom than it is to acquire one. Indeed, if any Prince should occupy either a city or a region, Machiavelli teaches in his **Commentaries** (Book 1, Chapter 26) that he must establish an entirely new form of government for that State.*

->**Decorative Marbled Paper**<-

*This page consists of a full-sheet decorative pattern known as **marbling**. Swirling, organic shapes in shades of ochre, muted purple, and dark green flow across a tan background, creating a textured, stone-like appearance.*

<note>Marbling (original: "ebru" or "marbling") is a method of aqueous surface design. To create these patterns, a craftsman floats pigments on a bath of thickened water (often using carrageenan or tragacanth gum) and then carefully lays a sheet of paper onto the surface to "pick up" the colors. This specific style, with its rounded, pebble-like forms, is often referred to as a **Turkish** or **Stone** pattern.</note>

<note>In historical <term>codicology</term>—the study of books as physical objects—these papers served both aesthetic and practical purposes. Visually, they provided a grand opening to the volume. Practically, the busy patterns were excellent at hiding the "turn-ins" (the edges of the leather or fabric cover folded over the inside of the boards) and any adhesive stains or structural irregularities in the binding.</note>
