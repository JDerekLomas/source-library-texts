## BOOK ONE
# ON MAGIC AND ITS MOST NOBLE SUBJECT
### TREATISE.

<margin>1 Corinthians 2:9-10</margin>
<margin>Introduction of the Arbatel,</margin>
It is the common opinion that *Magic* is a Persian word; for <term>Porphyry<gloss>A 3rd-century Neoplatonist philosopher.</gloss></term> and <term>Apuleius<gloss>A 2nd-century Roman writer and philosopher, author of "The Golden Ass."</gloss></term> seem to support this assertion, and they say that in the Persian language it means the same thing as *Wisdom*, *Philosophy*, and *Priesthood*. Consequently, a <term>Magus</term> is the same as a Wise Man, a Philosopher, and a Priest. But indeed, regarding its general scope, *Magic* is said to be divided into two parts, categorized according to the nature of **Wisdom** described by the **Apostle** <note>Referring to St. Paul the Apostle, specifically his distinctions in 1 Corinthians.</note>. One part he makes **human** or **worldly**, which he says is mere foolishness; the other, however, is **divine**. This divine wisdom is further extended into **natural** wisdom—insofar as that Wisdom works within nature and causes the creatures (according to the sentiment of the **Royal Psalmist** <note>Referring to King David, traditional author of the Psalms; specifically Psalm 19:1: "The heavens declare the glory of God."</note>) to declare the glory of the creator <note>original: "enarrare gloriam creatoris"</note>—and another part is **supernatural and mystical**. It is this latter type which the Apostle intends when he says, *We speak the Wisdom of God in a mystery* <note>original: "Sapientiam Dei loquimur in mysterio"</note>, which God has revealed to us through his Spirit. That magic which is human and worldly, which others also call **ceremonial magic**, is impious and hateful to both God and pious men. But so that you may better understand these matters, **Wisdom** in general is described in this manner according to the opinion of the <term>Arbatel<gloss>A reference to the "Arbatel de Magia Veterum," a famous 16th-century manual of spiritual magic.</gloss></term> in his highest study concerning Wisdom.

Magic, or Wisdom in general, is a science either...

<margin>Aphorisms of the Arbatel <note>The *Arbatel de Magia Veterum* is a 1575 book of spiritual aphorisms that promotes a "pious" form of magic based on the relationship between man and God's celestial hierarchy.</note></margin>

* **Of the Good**, and it is True Wisdom, one species of which is:
    * **Theosophy** <gloss>Literally "God-wisdom"; the study of divine things.</gloss>
        * Knowledge of the Word of God, and the establishment of a life according to the Word of God.
        * Knowledge of God's governance through Angels (whom Holy Scripture calls "watchers"), and the understanding of the mysteries of Angels.
        * The science of natural things and the wonders contained within them; this belongs to natural Philosophy.
    * **Anthroposophy** <gloss>Literally "human-wisdom"; knowledge granted to man by God.</gloss>
        * Prudence in human affairs, which is the subject of moral and political Philosophy.

* **Of the Evil**, and it is Pseudo-wisdom, or that human wisdom which is false and fraudulent; and this is twofold:
    * **Cacosophy** <gloss>Literally "bad-wisdom" or wicked knowledge.</gloss>
        * Contempt for the Word of God and living according to the will of the Devil.
        * Ignorance of God's governance through Angels.
        * To despise the protection of Angels and to be companions of Devils.
        * Idolatry and Atheism.
    * **Cacodaemony** <gloss>Literally "evil-spirit worship" or wicked demonry.</gloss> which deals with the cleverness of the human race; its species are:
        * **Sorcery** <term>Veneficium <gloss>The practice of "poisoning" or using harmful magic through physical substances.</gloss></term>, which deals with drugs, potions, and love-charms <note>original: "philtris"</note> prepared for human harm.
        * **Necromancy**, composed of dealings with unclean Spirits through rites of ill-omened and nefarious curiosity, illicit charms, and conjurations.
        * **Theurgy** <note>Historically, this meant "high magic" or "god-working." The author here argues it is a deceptive trap.</note>, which appears to be ruled by good Angels and divine power, though it is actually bound by the evil deceptions of Demons under the names of God and Angels.
        * **The Illusory Science** <term>Scientia præstigiatrix <gloss>The art of creating illusions or "prestige" to deceive the senses.</gloss></term>, through which Mages produce illusions and even many miracles, partly through fumigations, lights, love-charms, rings, images, and mirrors; with this are also mixed necromantic incantations, curses, and the deceptions of Demons.

### Concerning the Highest Good, etc.

By these things it seems clearly argued to the Wise, that Magic as a whole is not to be repudiated by Christians simply because some portion or species of it deals with the worst things, abominable to a Christian soul. If, I say, worldly Wisdom—which is the fruit of human invention—is foolish according to the words of the Apostle <note>A reference to St. Paul in 1 Corinthians 3:19: "For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God."</note> and leads to the worst outcomes; then that true Wisdom, sent into the world by divine inspiration from God for the benefit of His creatures, is not to be rejected or consigned to perpetual oblivion by Christian men on account of the diabolical follies of that *other* kind. Indeed, if it were taken away, nothing of Wisdom, whether divine or natural, would be found on the face of the earth. As for that knowledge of evil which caused the fall of Adam (who, having had dealings with the serpent, was deceived and thus tasted of the tree of both evil and good), I desire from my heart, along with all Christians engaged in the true and sincere Wisdom of God, that it should be thrust down into the abyss to its master the Devil. For it was originally inspired by the cunning of said master into the world for human ruin and destruction—rising upward like a foul vapor—so that no trace of such a mystery of iniquity would be left to human posterity.

For a better distinction between the two species, authors have called the good science *Magic*, and the evil science directly *Cacomagic* <note>original: "Cacomagia," from the Greek 'kakos' (evil) and 'magia' (magic).</note>.

That which is called "Natural Magic" is destined for revealing and investigating the mysteries of nature, both celestial and terrestrial. Therefore, by means of this science, the Magi—the most accurate explorers of nature—by applying active agents to passive subjects <note>A technical concept in Renaissance magic where the practitioner "marries" active celestial forces to passive terrestrial materials to produce effects.</note> with a learned and expert hand, finally produced things for the world that the common people regarded as miracles. They were accustomed to perform works that sometimes surpassed all admiration, as authors worthy of trust have committed to writing. Sometimes this art even leads to a supernatural mystery, bringing the incorruptible Spirit of the creature—or the very <term>quintessence</term><note>The "fifth element" or "aether," thought to be the pure, celestial substance that permeates all things.</note>, most full of the divine ray—out of the darkness into the sight of mortals through true and non-spurious <term>alchemy</term>. By means of this science, the *ancient Magi* (not *Cacomagi*, as the ignorant suppose, who are practiced in the worst "Cacomagic" wisdom) brought so many and such great things to light. These were truly Wise men; namely, those who received the gift of Wisdom from God, such as those three *Magi* or Wise Men who followed his star from the East at the birth of Christ. Those three kings, I say, who foretold that Christ, the Savior of the world, would be born. 

It should be known, however, that there were anciently four schools of this natural Magic famous throughout the whole world: the *Indian*, the *Persian*, the *Chaldean*, and the *Ethiopian*. In the *Indian* school among the <term>Brahmins</term><note>original: "Brachmanas"</note>, the fame of *Iarchas* was greatest; of *Thespesion* among the <term>Gymnosophists</term><note>Meaning "naked philosophers," a group of ancient Indian philosophers known for their asceticism.</note>; of *Buddha* among the Babylonians; of *Numa Pompilius* among the Romans; of *Zamolxis* among the Thracians; of *Abaris* among the Hyperboreans; of *Hermes* among the Egyptians; and of *Zoroaster* (son of Oromasus) among the Persians—from which part of the world came those three kings seeking the newborn Christ to adore Him with gifts. Interpreters of the Gospels explain these men to be *Chaldean Philosophers*, of the same kind as *Iarchas the Indian*, *Buddha the Babylonian*, and *Zoroaster the Persian*. And these are called kings because Plato testifies in the *Alcibiades* that the sons of Persian kings were instructed in the mysteries of this kind of natural magic, so that they might learn to administer and distribute the republic according to the image of the worldly Republic <note>A reference to the "macrocosm/microcosm" theory where a state should be governed in harmony with the laws of the universe.</note>. Cicero seems to agree with this opinion of Plato, saying in his books on divination: *no one among the Persians was decorated with the royal crown who had not first learned Magic.* And this seems to be the reason why these Eastern kings were most learned in Wisdom, or "Good Magic," seeking the name of MAGI and WISE MEN, which is why they are described by the HOLY SPIRIT in the Gospel as "Magi."

# Treatise

Is the name of Magic or of the Magi to be held in such hatred among mortals, when the Holy Scriptures seem to celebrate and remember it with such honor? Or should anyone be believed who, because of one evil branch of Magic, attempts to uproot even the good from human memory? As if, because of the sins of the worst men, even the good should be utterly extirpated and fundamentally removed along with the wicked?

But there are those who use the word "Magic" without any distinction throughout the entire thread of their works in the worst sense—that is, they take it for Cacomagia <note>original: "Cacomagia," meaning "evil magic" or "black magic."</note>—hardly understanding that there is no difference between Natural Philosophy and Natural Magic, and consequently no difference between a Magician and a Philosopher. Even Jerome, in his letter to *Paulinus*, did not disdain to mention this kind of Magic, where he says that Apollonius of Tyana was a Magician or a Pythagorean philosopher.

Can you not also perceive with open eyes, contrary to their opinion and based on the branch of the preceding distinction, that True Wisdom (which is the science of the good) has in its Theosophical <note>Theo-sophical: literally "God-wisdom," referring here to divine knowledge or mystical insight.</note> nature the task of teaching not only the knowledge of the Word of God—that is, Eternal Wisdom—but also that by which we are taught how God governs all things through the mediation of Angels? For this reason, they are called "Watchers" by the holy page, and through it, we are made participants in angelic mysteries. Is it not also through a single branch of Cacomagia that those are included who, out of ignorance of God's governance through Angels, are swept away, and, despising the protection of Angels, willfully or unwillingly make themselves associates of Devils? But we also have this confirmed by the Holy Scriptures: namely, that God brings about His Word through the mediation of His Angels, who are most mighty in strength, and that it is the office of Angels to carry out the pleasure of God, and that everyone has their own good Angel before the face of the Father who is in heaven.

<margin>Psalm 103:4. <note>In the author's citation system; modern Bibles often cite this as Psalm 103:20: "Bless the Lord, O you his angels... that do his commandments."</note></margin>
But why should we doubt further in this matter, when the Holy Bible itself seems to echo this subject everywhere? And yet a certain monk called Mersenne <note>Marin Mersenne (1588–1648), a French polymath and theologian known for his intense criticism of alchemy and the occult.</note> has, out of ignorance, I know, fallen into that pit of *Cacosophia* <note>Cacosophia: "Evil wisdom" or "false wisdom," used here as a pun to contrast with Theosophia.</note>. For he seems to deny the powers of Angels, and their governance of the world under God, and the protection assigned to us by God; he seems to call those "impious magicians" who think thus, and by this reasoning—in this denial of the protection of good Angels—he makes himself an associate of Devils.

But since this "good" author, according to his own malicious whim, sharply rebukes European men who are most famous for their learning and even immaculate in the Roman religion—being clothed in religious habits and untouched in the honesty of their lives—falsely accusing them of "impious magic" (which is truly called *cacomagia*), let the readers see through these following pages whether these men are truly such as they are accused of being by Marinus <note>Another reference to Marin Mersenne.</note>. Let their writings be produced before the throne of judgment, so that you may understand what they can bring forward in their masters' cause, by which they may say their masters are either innocent or guilty of Cacomagia. In this way, you may conclude whether they are truly to be held as impious black magicians or as natural magicians, not at all inconsistent with divine laws. And first, I will explain the accusation against Roger Bacon <note>Roger Bacon (c. 1214–1292), an English Franciscan friar and philosopher whose interest in nature and optics led to long-standing legends that he was a sorcerer.</note> introduced by Marin Mersenne; then I will give his response, and so I will proceed with the others.

->From Column 971 of Mersenne.<-

*It should be noted, however, that Roger Bacon wrote one book on Necromantic <note>Nigromantia: black magic or communication with the dead.</note> images, another on incantations, two on Alchemy, and countless others on Astrological mysteries. So cautious is the rival of the human race <note>A traditional term for the Devil.</note> that he even mocks and leads into error men who are otherwise most learned; certainly, familiarity with them is all the more dangerous because they better cover the poison of their insane doctrine with diverse conjectures and the tricks of reasoning. By their habit, even the elect, if it were possible, are cast into the danger of their salvation, not to say their destruction.*

# On the Highest Good, etc.

## Robert Bacon in his own defense, from the booklet on the power of Art and Nature.

<note>original: "Robert. Bacon," referring to the 13th-century English philosopher Roger Bacon. The text uses "Robert," a common contemporary variation or error.</note>

*Although nature is powerful and wonderful, nevertheless Art, using nature as an instrument, is more powerful than natural virtue, as we have seen in many things. However, whatever exists outside the operation of Nature or Art is either not human, or it is a fiction and occupied with frauds. Because of this, it is a most wicked occupation when a man despises the laws of Philosophy and, against all reason, invokes nefarious Spirits so that through them he may fulfill his will. And in this lies the error: that they believe Spirits are subjected to them, and that Spirits are compelled by human will. This is impossible, for human power is far inferior to that of the Spirits. Or they wander even further in this: that they believe through certain natural things which they use, evil Spirits are either summoned or put to flight. And they err still, when through invocations, prayers, and sacrifices, men strive to appease them and bring them forth for the benefit of men; for without comparison, it would be easier to obtain from God or from good Spirits whatever a man ought to consider useful. But neither do the evil Spirits assist favorably in these useless matters, except insofar as it is permitted by God because of the sins of men—He who rules and governs the human race. Therefore, these ways are outside the teachings of Wisdom; indeed, they work better to the contrary, nor have those who truly practice Philosophy ever cared for these methods.*

*And elsewhere: And thus many secrets of Nature and Art are thought to be Magic, and Magicians foolishly trust in charms and characters <note>In this context, "characters" refers to talismans, symbols, or encoded sigils.</note>, thinking that they provide power to them. For the sake of pursuing these, they abandon the work of nature and art because of the error of charms and characters, and thus both kinds of these men are deprived of the utility of Wisdom, compelled by their own foolishness. Therefore, those things contained in the books of Magicians are all to be kept away; although they may contain something true, they abuse so many falsehoods that the truth cannot be discerned from the false.*

These things he says for himself, and there throughout his entire admirable booklet, he declares the power of art and nature—that is, the mysteries of natural Magic.

What do you say, (just judges)? Is this religious man, according to the opinion of that Monk <note>Likely referring to a critic or the specific accuser mentioned in the previous context.</note>, guilty or not guilty? I shall say no more on this matter, since the integrity of this good man shines forth more clearly than the midday light (despite his slanderer). But now let another be produced: and let him be an Abbot—a man of no small esteem in the Roman Church—Trithemius, I say, the Abbot, a man known to all the learned and most celebrated for his profound knowledge, who has not only himself but also other religious men speaking on his behalf. His accusation—or rather, truly his judgment—is such:

->From Column 42 of Mersenne, Volume 1.<-
<note>Marin Mersenne (1588–1648) was a French mathematician and theologian who famously attacked "magical" works.</note>

I gladly leave the other rhapsodies and "name-makings" <note>original: "Onomatopœias," likely referring to the creation of spirit names.</note> of the Rabbis, just as those things which Cornelius <note>Cornelius Agrippa.</note> has in the cited place, and Trithemius in his *Steganographia*, which is most worthy of the flames, etc.

### Trithemius in his own defense, in his letter to Cornelius Agrippa.

*We ask and pray that you always exercise yourself through your labor toward better things, and show the light of true Wisdom—with which you are especially and divinely illuminated—even to the ignorant. Nor let the consideration of any scoundrels (take note, Mersenne!) pull you back from your purpose, of whom it is truly said: "the weary ox plants its foot firmly," etc.*

These things he says for himself, by which he wishes to indicate to the world that the entire effort of a Christian man is in the acquisition of the light of true Wisdom, by which he may be divinely illuminated, so that in that light, he may see the light.

### Abbot Sigismund appears on behalf of Trithemius, as in Column 440 of Marin’s work.

# Treatise

Thus says Sigismund: Judge therefore whether this Abbot <note>Referring to Johannes Trithemius, discussed on the previous page.</note> is guilty of evil magic <note>original: "Cacomagiæ," a term for black magic or sorcery.</note> or not.

Let us proceed to the third, who is called into question as a wicked Magician, and that is Marsilio Ficino the Priest, whom he pursues with this kind of accusation.

->From Column 1704 of Mersenne.<- <note>Referring to Marin Mersenne’s work *Questions on Genesis* (1623), where he attacked many Renaissance occultists.</note>

> He objects that Marsilio Ficino, a Catholic and most learned man, attributes the powers of Music to the stars, so that through songs the higher influences might be attracted. I respond indeed that Ficino is not a Catholic where he brings forth and approves those Magical and Astrological trifles.

## **Marsilio Ficino, in his Apology at the end of his life, responds for himself from heaven thus.** <note>The author uses "from heaven" (cœlitus) rhetorically, as if the deceased Ficino is speaking from the celestial realm through his writings.</note>

> Magic or images are not indeed approved by Marsilio, but he is recounting Plotinus <note>Plotinus was a major Neoplatonic philosopher whose works Ficino translated.</note> himself while interpreting him. This his writings clearly declare, if they are read with a fair mind: Nor have I asserted a single word here about profane Magic, which relies on the worship of Demons, but I have made mention of natural magic, <term>natural magic</term> <note>Natural magic (magia naturalis) was considered a branch of science that used the hidden properties of plants, stones, and stars for healing and benefit, without involving spirits.</note> which through natural things captures the benefits of the heavens for the prosperous health of bodies; which faculty surely seems as much to be granted to those using their talents legitimately as medicine and agriculture are granted by right—and all the more so, the more perfect it is, an industry coupling celestial things to earthly ones. And from this workshop, the first of all the Magi immediately adored the newborn Christ. Why then do you dread the name of Magus so fearfully? A name favored by the Gospel, which does not sound of sorcery and poisoning, but of the Wise Man and the Priest, etc.

This is the rock upon which Guicciardini <note>Likely referring to a defender or commentator on Ficino's work.</note> stands to defend the reputation of his Master against the detractor.

Whether therefore this good man is worthy of such great accusation, I leave to your judgments.

But now, the writings of that "Arch-Magician" (as he calls him), Cornelius Agrippa, are produced for accusation and judgment. He who is the accuser of his brothers <note>A reference to Mersenne, whom the author views as attacking fellow Christians.</note> proceeds thus:

->From Mersenne's Column 39, verso.<-

His own writings are produced, excusing their master.

But perhaps curious readers will desire to know the reason why these authors were accustomed to mixing spurious, superfluous, and wondrous or necromantic things with true Wisdom and the naked truth. To them I respond: because they did not wish, at first glance, to "cast pearls before swine," nor did they wish for their "roses to be found without thorns." They wrote, therefore, in such a way that they might be understood only by the wise; for the wise man reads one thing and understands another. Whence says **Abbot Sigismund**:

<margin>From column 470.</margin>
*Although Trithemius possesses characters, numbers, and various modes of conjuration by which Demonic Magic seems to be propagated: nevertheless, under those names he only hides natural secrets, etc.* This is also what Agrippa seems to intend: by confessing that although his Philosophy contains vain and superstitious things alongside the truth, he nevertheless warns us to leave the vain things behind, with the caution that we should by no means be ignorant of their hidden causes. To come to the sayings of **Roger Bacon** (whom Mersenne makes a defendant of wicked magic): Bacon himself calls that man insane who wishes to disclose any secret to the world except through riddles and enigmas, or to commit them to writings by which they might be hidden from the world and scarcely understood by the studious and the wise. And **Solomon** says: *It is the glory of a wise man to conceal a matter.* And **Christ** says: *Speak to them in parables,* etc.

Here, therefore, judge, O Readers, whether so great a crime is to be imposed upon these good men as **Marin Mersenne** has published to the world with his outcries, and whether the word "magic" is to be held in such great hatred, if it be considered in its sincere and simple nature.

# Treatise

Just as it was said regarding Magic—that there is a divine wisdom upon which true sciences depend, and another that is worldly, which the Apostle <note>Referring to St. Paul, who often contrasted worldly wisdom with divine wisdom.</note> calls vanity and folly—so it must be said of the art of **Cabala** <note>Also spelled Kabbalah; a Jewish mystical tradition for interpreting Scripture and understanding the divine.</note>. One type comes from the true wisdom sent from heaven, while the other comes from the deceptive and unstable invention of men, being fashioned and refashioned. For the gift of that true wisdom is the subject of the first kind of Cabala, which is revealed to men either by immediate inspiration or by having its Kabbalistic mystery uncovered through the teaching of a faithful master. In contrast, the second kind, derived from human invention, is vain, uncertain, and inconsistent, fashioned and produced according to the imagination of the inventor.

And although the inventor may have understood the reasoning behind his own invention quite well, and may have attempted to hide and conceal the truth of the mystery beneath it—like a spirit beneath the literal text—he nevertheless acted most wickedly in this: by such superstitious ceremonies, he led man away from the truth toward a certain species of **Theurgy** <note>Theurgy: rituals intended to invoke the presence or action of divine beings or spirits.</note>, rather than directing them toward the desired goal, as will be discussed more broadly below. The species of true and essential Cabala known as **Cosmological** deals with natural things, both of heaven and earth, and is accustomed to explaining and uncovering the laws and secrets of the Holy Bible through philosophical reasoning. From this, it is clear that the subject of Cosmological Cabala and Natural Magic is the same—of which we have spoken at length in our preceding discourse on Magic. 

However, that species called **Marcava** <note>original: "Marcaua"; referring to Merkavah or "Chariot" mysticism, focused on the throne of God and celestial hierarchies.</note> deals with mysteries far more sublime: namely, those which pertain to the divine nature and the condition of angels, as well as the secrets of characters and formal, fiery, or luminous elements. A branch of this, **Notariaca** <note>A branch of Kabbalah involving the use of letters and names to interact with spiritual forces.</note>, deals with the virtues, names, and seals of angels, and also reveals the conditions of demons and souls. So too does the **Theomantic** branch <note>Divine divination or the study of God's names.</note> search out the mysteries of the divine majesty and various emanations according to their properties and the sacred names of God. Those Kabbalists who truly know these things say they possess such wonderful powers that whenever one wishes, he may foreknow all future things, command all of nature, have authority over demons and angels, and perform miracles. Indeed, it is believed that through this species, Moses performed those signs recorded in Exodus. Its entire and unique subject is that Spirit of Wisdom who (as the Holy Scripture testifies) is the worker of all wonders; Solomon confessed that he possessed perfection by the assistance of this Spirit in these two complete species of true Cabala. For he acknowledges the entire power of **Cosmology** in these words:

<margin>Wisdom 7:17, 18, 19.</margin>
> Wisdom has given me the science of those things that exist; from her, a certain knowledge was given to me of the structure of the world and the powers of the elements; the beginning, the end, and the midpoint of times; the changes of the solstices and the varieties of seasons, the circuit of the year and the positions of the stars, etc.

By these words, he proves that through true wisdom he possessed knowledge of the natural mysteries of heaven, and consequently was most perfect in his understanding of the higher cosmological parts. Then he continues thus:

<margin>Wisdom 7:20.</margin>
> Wisdom was given to me to know the natures of living creatures and the rages of wild beasts, and the differences between plants and the medicinal powers of roots, etc.

By which he concludes that he had certain knowledge of nature, both of the lower (earthly) and the higher (heavenly) things. In these, one may clearly see that Solomon acquired the most perfect Cosmological science in the first species of essential true Cabala.

### Treatise on the first Question.

Mersenne is of the opinion that Alchemists hide their secret for this reason: lest, by revealing such a fabulous thing to the world, they should eventually prove themselves to be the greatest of fools. But truly, why would so many Wise Men occupy themselves with such a trifling business (if it were a fable or a fiction), when it is their primary duty to distinguish truth from fiction, and to separate the pure from the impure? For this alone is the business of the true Chemist. How much "chemical purging" does this bastard chemist, Mersenne, require? He ignores the central duty of the true Alchemist, yet judges him to be hiding a fable or fiction to avoid being thought a fool—when in reality, the Alchemist is the one attempting to distinguish fiction from truth! Indeed, let Mersenne understand that he is the one conscious of his own folly; he should be judged a fool by all because, while he claims to know that the subject of Chemistry is a fiction, he nevertheless persists in it stubbornly and never ceases to trumpet its praises to the world every year. For what benefit, I ask, could it bring to Wise Men—both Heathen and Christian—to celebrate Alchemy with such great honor if the art were fake and of no importance? Indeed, they would rather earn the title of "fools" than "Wise Men," as this man Mersenne seems to assert.

What, then, does Mersenne think of those Heathens held to be Wise Men not only by the followers of Christ, but by the Pagans themselves? Specifically: Hermes <note>Hermes Trismegistus, the legendary Hellenistic figure to whom the Hermetic corpus is attributed.</note>, Mary the Prophetess <note>An early alchemist, often identified as Miriam the sister of Moses.</note>, Geber, Calid, Democritus, Avicenna, Pythagoras, Socrates, Plato, Parmenides, Zeno, Arisleus, Anaxagoras, and many other Philosophers highly skilled in the Chemical art? Mersenne calls them fools and regards them as tellers of fables because they were involved in Alchemy. And in truth, if Alchemy were as vain as he drones on about, they would be manifest fools to occupy themselves in it with such devotion. But in truth, the primary goal of the upright Philosophers is that **Highest Good** <note>original: "summum illud bonum"</note>, toward which they direct all their desires with such seriousness and diligence. 

But if that man replies that they were all Pagans, I answer: it would be a greater disgrace to us who assume the title of "Christians" (perhaps unjustly) to be ignorant of that true mystery—by which we might more worthily deserve the Christian name—than to those who, though never clothed in the name of Christian, understood and possessed the secret of Wisdom. Albert the Great <note>Albertus Magnus (c. 1200–1280), a Dominican friar and bishop.</note>, so called for the depth of his science; Thomas Aquinas; Synesius; Pico della Mirandola; Roger Bacon, a monk; Raymond Lull, a monk; Arnold of Villanova; Abbot Cremer; Basil Valentine, a monk; George Ripley, a monk; Bernard Trevisan, a Count; Morienus the hermit; Richard the Englishman; Norton; and very many others were most highly skilled in the art of Alchemy, and they announced its praises to the world like the sound of a trumpet. All of this occurs through the conversion or relation of the elements—the transmutation of the composite into the simple, and the impure into the pure. This, then, is the work of true and divine Alchemy, through which an entrance is made into the paradise of pleasure for those of this world, so that we may gather that red rose with the lilies of the valley and eat from the tree of life. Does not the Apostle teach us this same path in these words: 

> *That you may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the width and length and height and depth; and to know the love of Christ which surpasses knowledge, that you may be filled with all the fullness of God.* <margin>Ephesians 3:18</margin>

This is done to find the true cornerstone which God threw into the base or foundation of the earth, so that he may know the surpassing love of the knowledge of Christ and be filled with all the fullness of God.

We conclude, therefore, that this Rosy Cross, or that mystic blood of the cross (of which a certain divine Poet says: *The sacred form whose revered cloak shines*), is to be sought out by knocking strenuously and by patiently expecting and imploring the divine blessing, even "unto blood," according to that teaching of the Apostle: 

> <margin>Hebrews 12:4</margin> *You have not yet resisted unto blood, striving against sin, etc.*

In that divine light, he may see the light and finally taste happily of the fruit of his labors, according to the words of the Apostle concerning a Farmer of this kind of understanding:

Therefore, when these things are well weighed, it will be clear how parabolic and mystical this blood of the cross and the cross of Christ is, which every true Christian ought to investigate with the greatest effort until he finds that most red Rose or that most precious blood of the cross fixed and permanent there. Thus, at last, he may deservedly be called a Brother of the Rosy Cross and be rewarded by the true fraternity, verifying the axiom of the royal Psalmist: 

> <margin>Psalm 133:1</margin> *How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell in unity.*

...is discerned in being explained, for it belongs to man—inasmuch as he is the image of the divine mind—above all to ascend to the Father, the source of his Light, by transcending worldly things with the most accurate keenness of his mind, and to be transformed into that same image with Christ, and through Christ. Hence it was that the Mosaic Decalogue <note>The Ten Commandments given to Moses</note> was divided by our Savior into only two parts: one of which regards God—namely, that we should seek him with fear and reverence, and find the secret mysteries of his wisdom, according to that saying of the Wise Man: "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of the acquisition of wisdom" <note>original: "Timor Dei est principium acquisitionis sapientiæ"</note>, which reveals all things, both hidden and manifest, and makes men friends of God and Prophets. The other part regards the neighbor, or man, which pertains to tropology <term>a method of interpreting scripture for moral or ethical lessons</term> or morality; specifically, the ethical order and disposition of one man toward another. Of these two, the former is by no means to be broadcast indiscriminately, for the Savior of the world teaches us that sacred things are not to be mixed with the profane. Indeed, he argues this in these words:

> "Do not," he said, "throw pearls before swine, or the bread of children to dogs, but speak to them in parables." <note>A paraphrase combining Matthew 7:6 and Matthew 15:26</note>

And Paul confessed that he was caught up into the third heaven, where he recognized that he saw things that should by no means be made known in speech. Similarly, we read that of the books written by Esdras <note>A reference to the apocryphal 2 Esdras 14:44–46</note>, the first ones were made public by his command so that both the worthy and unworthy among the people might read them; but he advised that the last 70 were to be preserved and held in secret, so that they might be handed down to the wise among the people. By these examples, it seems to be argued that both the mysteries and the moral lessons of the Holy Bible are to be embraced by men; however, those mysteries are to be revealed to and explained by only a few who are worthy and wise, while the moral lessons are to be published for both the worthy and unworthy, so that they may learn the morality of life and action through reading them.

From this it is made manifest that although the text of sacred letters—and consequently theology—may be turned into a moral sense through reading and preaching, and thus published in a tropological and historical manner, it does not follow from this that its mystical sense, hidden under the letter, should not be drawn out for both the metaphysical and physical salvation of men. Even though this species of Theology might seem at first glance varied and different from that moral sense, and for that reason appear more foreign and almost new, nevertheless it is the part of a wise man (according to the Savior’s intention) to embrace both these members: namely, that which is depicted in moral form from the letter for both the wise and the foolish, or the worthy and unworthy; as well as the other which is contained under the letter—namely the allegorical, mystical, and spiritual sense—which is to be opened only to the few who are wise.

Hence it is that (if it is asked, to which religion such wise men belong who excel in this mystical interpretation of the scriptures—namely whether they are Roman Catholic, Lutheran, Calvinist, etc., or whether they have some religion of their own, peculiar to themselves and divided from others) it will be very easy for them (in my opinion) to answer. For since all Christians, of whatever religion, tend toward one and the same goal—namely Christ himself, who is the only truth—they all indeed meet in this one unanimous consensus.

<margin>1 Corinthians 8:3.
1 Corinthians 8:13.</margin>
But in truth, insofar as these religions seem to differ both in their formula of morality and in the external ceremonies of the Church—which are human inventions, such as the various habits of monks and Popes, the adoration of the cross, the approval or rejection of images, the various disputes about the "real presence" <note>The theological debate over whether the bread and wine of the Eucharist literally become the body and blood of Christ</note>, the lighting of candles at night, and infinite other things (which disputes are outside the essential laws of truly mystical wisdom)—we believe that such men of both lower and higher condition are scattered throughout every religion. These men are so well-versed in mystical Theology that after their victory or acquisition of knowledge, they are deservedly to be called **Brothers of the True Rose Cross** <note>original: "veræ crucis Rosæ fratres"</note>. These men, seriously applying the keenness of their minds to the point of truth and unity, think of such external matters as things applied for the benefit of the common people in a moralizing way, and they willingly submit themselves to them according to the laws of morality. Nevertheless, they revolve these words of the Apostle in their minds:

> "If anyone loves God, he is known by him. Regarding foods sacrificed to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in this world, and that there is no God except he who is the only truth."

Wherefore, in respect to external ceremonies, they conduct themselves (as I believe) just as St. Paul says he conducted himself toward both Jews and Gentiles in 1 Corinthians 9:19: devoted, namely, with their whole effort to the fear of the one God, and to his good pleasure, without giving offense to their brothers. "Therefore," says the Apostle,

> "if food causes my brother to stumble, I will never eat meat again, so that I may not cause my brother to stumble."

I conclude, therefore, that these profound men observe both the moral laws of the Holy Bible (as I believe) and the mystical ones, diligently in any Christian sect. And following the rule of the Savior of the world, they are most busily engaged both in fearing and revering God, and in loving and embracing their neighbors with every respect for morality.
