# Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms, Volume 32
## Small Western Ocean: Various Countries of West Africa

### Volume 32

The latter people <note>referring to the indigenous inhabitants of the West African coast</note> are black in color with curly hair. They do not use elaborate ornamentation and lack formal arts, crafts, or literature. Though they are fierce in battle, in their daily lives they remain simple and unrefined. They do not follow the established religions <note>referring to organized faiths like Islam or Christianity, which the author contrasts with local practices</note> but exclusively worship the gods of the **Fetish** <term>fetish: Wei Yuan uses the phonetic transcription 匪底祗 (fěidǐzhī), likely from the Portuguese 'feitiço', to describe the practice of attributing supernatural powers to physical objects</term>. These may be large trees, great stones, ivory, dog teeth, tiger teeth, sheep heads, fish bones, or bundles of branches and ropes; they designate such items at will as their **Fetish** gods. Some are enshrined within the home, while others are placed in villages. Shrines are erected and offerings are laid out, with prayers offered whenever matters of importance arise.

When a person dies, it is common to burn wealth and silks, which is called "providing for use in the underworld." They also sacrifice humans as livestock to offer to the spirits of their ancestors. They are fond of music and enjoy singing and dancing. They keep many wives and concubines; commoners may have as many as ten or twenty, while kings count theirs by the hundreds. The chieftains of the two kingdoms of **Ashanti** <note>original: 阿寒底 (Āhándǐ)</note> and **Dahomey** <note>original: 那和彌 (Nàhémí)</note> may even have several thousand.

The dwellings of the people are low and small, with doors only three feet high, requiring one to bow to enter. A house typically consists of a single room with a single door, surrounded by an outer wall; this is true for both the upper and lower classes. Only the palaces and government offices are somewhat more spacious and situated on higher, drier ground, occasionally featuring **carved decorations** <term>carved decorations: 斲飾 (zhuóshì); referring to wood-carving or architectural ornamentation used to denote social status</term> to distinguish between different social ranks.

The **Iya** tribe <term>Iya: Likely referring to a riverine settlement or district along the Niger, such as Ejya</term> is also situated along the riverbank, stretching for over four *li* <note>approximately 2 kilometers</note> and crowded with many merchant vessels.

# Central African Nations in the Lesser Western Ocean

The above entries describe the various tribes of the Central African <term>中阿非里加 (Zhong-Afeilijia): Central Africa</term> interior. There are also several nations located along the upper reaches of the **Niger River** <term>尼阨河 (Ni’e He): The Niger River</term>, where the soil is fertile and the environment is generally similar. Many travelers and explorers, such as **Park** <note>original: 巴客 (Bake), referring to Mungo Park (1771–1806), the Scottish explorer who died while navigating the Niger River</note>, died during their journeys and were unable to return to their home countries. Consequently, the full details of their experiences are not known, but some information regarding the larger tribes has been heard and is recorded below.

**Timbuktu** <term>丁麻杜 (Dingmadu): Timbuktu, the historic trading center in present-day Mali</term> is located on the borders of Central Africa. It produces gold of such brilliance that its light dazzles the eyes. **Europeans** <note>original: 歐羅巴人 (Ouluoba ren)</note> often risk their lives to reach this land. Its territory is vast, but the specific details of its situation remain unknown. During its height of power, the various nations of **Gao** <note>original: 葛司沙 (Gesisha), likely referring to the Gao or Songhai Empire</note> were all under its jurisdiction. However, in recent centuries, the country itself became a dependency of **Morocco** <term>摩羅果 (Moluoguo): Morocco</term>. Currently, it is ruled by a Black king. The palaces and commoners' houses are mostly round and pointed at the top, shaped like beehives. The soil there is dry and barren, and local crops are insufficient to feed the population; they rely on supplies from neighboring countries, which are transported along the Niger River. The port of **Kabara** <note>original: 加墨臘 (Jiamola), the river port serving Timbuktu</note> can be reached in a single day. Because the gold mines are so productive, merchants and travelers flock there in great numbers like ducks; many among them are from Morocco.
