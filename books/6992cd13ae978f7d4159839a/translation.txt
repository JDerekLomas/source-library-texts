# The Well Mansion (24 Degrees)

Master Gan says: The eight stars of the **Well** are located within the River <note>original: "河中" - referring to the Heavenly River, or the Milky Way</note>. They govern wells and springs. The Sun, Moon, and the five planets pass through them, making this the "Middle Path" <note>a reference to the Ecliptic, the apparent path of the Sun across the sky</note>.

Master Shi calls it the **Eastern Well**, and also the **Celestial Well**. It governs the positions of the feudal lords, imperial relatives, and the <term>Three Ducal Ministers<note>The San Gong, the highest-ranking civil officials in the imperial government.</note></term>. Therefore, if these stars are bright and large, it signifies the enfeoffment of lords and the founding of states.

If they tremble or lose their color, it portends the execution of lords and relatives, the deposing and slaughter of the Three Ducal Ministers, and calamity for the Imperial Preceptors.

Zhang Heng says: This is the Southern Gate of Heaven. It is the area traversed by the **Ecliptic**. It serves as the pavilion-watch of the heavens and governs the affairs of the <term>Superintendent of Waters<note>Shuiheng; an official post managing royal parks, granaries, and the coinage of money.</note></term>. It is the source from which the equity of laws and regulations is derived. When a Ruler applies the law with fairness, the stars of the Well will be bright and appear in an orderly alignment. When the Moon lodges within this mansion, there will be responses of wind and rain.

It is also said: The Well is the treasury of the Son of Heaven. <note>The text here is slightly obscured or contains rare technical terms regarding astronomical measurements.</note> If the Sun or Moon are eclipsed here, or if the five planets retrograde and "infringe" upon the mansion, high officials will plot rebellion and war will arise.

The inner six stars should not be overly bright; if they appear too bright, there will be disasters involving water.

The star **Yue** <note>the Battle-Axe</note> is attached to the front of the Well. it governs the monitoring of luxury and debauchery and their subsequent punishment. Therefore, it is undesirable for this star to be as bright or as large as the stars of the Well itself. If it trembles, the Son of Heaven will "use the axe" upon his high officials <note>meaning he will carry out executions of high-ranking members of the court</note>.

### The Imperial Attendant Stars

The primary **Emperor Stars** are five in number. The first is called the **Emperor’s Teacher**, or sometimes the **Emperor’s Heart**. The second is the **Emperor’s Friend**. The third is the **Three Dukes** <note>The highest-ranking officials in the ancient Chinese government</note>. The fourth is the **Erudite** <note>A scholar-official or 'Doctor' of the classics</note>. The fifth is the **Grand Historian**, also known as the **Five Grand Masters**. These five stars are constant companions to the Emperor, serving to resolve his doubts and guide his deliberations. 

In the practice of divination, if these stars appear bright, large, and "moist" in luster, and if they are equal in size to one another, then the world will be well-governed; this is a great blessing for the state. However, if they appear with sharp rays or "horns" <note>scintillating excessively</note>, it indicates that calamity is brewing within the court. If they are dim or unclear, it suggests that the high and low officials will become suspicious of one another, and loyal, virtuous men will not be employed. **Zhang Heng** <note>The famous Han dynasty astronomer and mathematician (78–139 CE)</note> also stated: "If they appear red, there will be a bountiful harvest; if they are dark, it portends misfortune."

### The Celestial Stores and Water Officials

The star **Accumulated Water** <note>original: 薪積水, likely referring to the star *Jishui*</note> is located to the north of the **North River** asterism. It is responsible for providing the wine used in ceremonies. In divination, if this star cannot be seen, it is a sign of impending disaster. It also serves as a sentinel for observing flood-related calamities.

The star **Accumulated Firewood** <term>Jixin<note>A single star in the Gemini constellation representing the storage of fuel for the imperial kitchens.</note></term> is located to the east of **Accumulated Water**. Its purpose is to provide for the needs of the imperial kitchens. If it shines brightly, the sovereign will enjoy good health; if it is faint, there will be blame and misfortune. If the planet **Mars** <note>original: 火 (Fire Star)</note> "guards" or lingers near this star, a great drought is foretold.

The **Water Treasury** consists of four stars located to the southwest of the **Well** mansion. These are the celestial water officials. Their divinatory significance is the same as that of the **Water Level**.

The **Water Level** consists of four stars to the east of the **Well**. This asterism governs the "Water Balance" <note>an official responsible for canal and dam maintenance</note> and also oversees the discharge, overflow, and flow of currents. Regarding this, the Master **Wu Xian** <note>A legendary astronomer of the Shang Dynasty</note> offered this praise: 

> "The four stars of the **Water Level** govern discharge and overflow. If they shift their position to move close to the **North River**, then the kingdom will be submerged and turned into rivers and streams, or suffer the duality of flood and fire."

### The Four Waterways and the Military Market

The **Four Channels** <note>original: 四瀆, Sidu; representing the four great rivers of ancient China: the Yangtze, Yellow, Huai, and Ji</note> consist of four stars located to the south of the **Well** mansion and east of **Xuanyuan** <note>The constellation Leo</note>—though some records place them to the west of Xuanyuan. They are the celestial essence of the Yangtze, Yellow, Huai, and Ji rivers. In the practice of divination, if these stars appear bright and large, it indicates that the earthly waters will rise and flood.

The **Military Market** <note>original: 軍市, Junshi</note> is a group of thirteen stars arranged in a circular shape like a string of coins. They are located southeast of the constellation **Orion** <note>original: 參, Shen</note>, or according to some, south of the **South River**. This asterism represents the marketplace where the Heavenly Army trades its goods. If a **Guest Star** enters this area, or if the **Metal Star** <term>Venus</term> or the **Fire Star** <term>Mars</term> lingers there, the army will suffer a great famine. Some say that if these stars appear to shake or change their color, it also portends a great famine for the troops.

The **Wild Pheasant** <note>original: 野雞, Yeji</note> is a single star located in the center of the Military Market. It governs strange omens and supernatural transformations. In divination, if it appears blurry with sharp rays <note>original: 茫角, mangjiao; indicating excessive twinkling</note> or seems to tremble, it is a sign of impending military disaster. If the star moves out of its usual position, it indicates that the regional lords will raise their armies in rebellion.

### The Ancestral Lineage and the Palace Gates

To the southwest of the Military Market are two stars called the **Old Man** <note>original: 丈人, Zhangren</note>. To the east of the Old Man are two stars called the **Son** <note>original: 子, Zi</note>, and to the east of the Son are two stars called the **Grandson** <note>original: 孫, Sun</note>. 

The **Old Man** governs the elderly and venerable ministers of the state. If these stars cannot be seen, it signifies that the ministers are being blocked and cannot communicate with the Emperor. The **Son** and **Grandson** stars are positioned to "attend" at the side of the Old Man, dwelling together in a supportive manner. If they become invisible, it portends a disaster for the lineage; however, as long as they maintain their constant, normal appearance, there is no cause for blame.

The **Palace Gates** <note>original: 闕丘, Queqiu</note> are three stars located to the southeast of the South River. They represent the ceremonial gate towers of the Emperor's palace and the dual watchtowers of the regional lords' estates. If **Venus** or **Mars** lingers within this asterism, it is a sign of impending battle at the very gates of the palace.

### The Wild General and the Heavenly Bow

One star, located to the southeast of the **Well** mansion, is large and bright. It is known as the **Wild General** <note>original: 野將, Yejiang; possibly the star Beta Canis Majoris or a similar star within the Military Market asterism</note>. It governs matters of killing and looting. Its color should remain constant; it is undesirable for it to change its appearance or shift its position.

If this star wavers or shakes, it portends the rise of bandits and the rebellion of **foreign troops** <note>original: 胡兵, Hubing; historically referring to nomadic groups from the north or west</note>, leading to times so desperate that people resort to cannibalism. If the star appears agitated, the sovereign will find no peace and will be forced to gallop restlessly across the empire.

**Zhang Heng** <note>78–139 CE; a renowned Chinese polymath, astronomer, and inventor</note> says: "If the star dwells out of its proper place, people will eat one another. If its color is yellow-white and bright, it is a sign of good fortune. Black is ominous. If it appears red with sharp, scintillating rays <note>original: 茫角, mangjiao</note>, war will break out. If the **Metal Star** <term>Venus</term> or the **Fire Star** <term>Mars</term> lingers near it, the omens are the same."

The **Bow and Arrow** <note>original: 弧矢, Hushi; an asterism in the constellations Canis Major and Puppis</note> consists of nine stars located to the southeast of the **Wolf** <note>The star Sirius</note>. It represents the "Heavenly Bow," prepared as a defense against bandits. The bow must always point toward the Wolf.

If the Bow and Arrow shifts or moves irregularly, there will be many bandits. If it appears exceptionally bright, a great army will arise. When both the Wolf and the Bow and Arrow are prominently visible and "tense," the empire is on the verge of chaos. It is further said: "When the Heavenly Bow is drawn tight, the entire world will be consumed by war, and the ruler and his ministers will plot against one another." Zhang Heng also notes: "If the bow appears drawn to its full extent, war will break out across the realm."

### The Star of Longevity

The **Old Man** <note>original: 老人星, Laorenxing; the star Canopus</note> is a single star located to the south of the Bow. It is also known as the **South Pole** <note>original: 南極, Nanji; not the geographic pole, but the southern celestial pole star of the Chinese system</note>. This star governs longevity and old age. 

It typically becomes visible in the direction of **Bing** <note>South-Southeast</note> on the day of the **Spring Equinox** <term>the moment in March when day and night are of equal length</term>. On the evening of the Spring Equinox, it sets in the direction of **Ding** <note>South-Southwest</note>. On the evening of the **Autumn Equinox** <term>the equivalent moment in September</term>, it appears again at the position of *Ding*. To observe it, one should go to the southern suburbs of the city.

If this star appears large and bright, the sovereign will enjoy a long life and the empire will be peaceful and serene. However, if the star is not visible when it should be, it is a cause for great concern for the sovereign.

### The Ghost Mansion

The **Ghost Mansion** <note>original: 輿鬼 (Yugui). The 23rd of the 28 Lunar Mansions, corresponding to the constellation Cancer.</note> spans two degrees and serves as the central path for the Sun, Moon, and the Five Planets. It governs matters of death, misfortune, and disease. **Zhang Heng** <note>A renowned Han Dynasty astronomer and polymath (78–139 CE).</note> states that it presides over ancestral sacrifices and is considered the **"Eye of Heaven."** It also oversees keen observation, the detection of treacherous plots, and the carrying out of executions.

*   The **Northeast star** governs the accumulation of horses.
*   The **Southeast star** governs the accumulation of weaponry and soldiers.
*   The **Southwest star** governs the accumulation of cloth and silk.
*   The **Northwest star** governs the accumulation of gold and jade.

Divination is performed by observing the changes in these four stars. 

The single star in the center is named the **Cumulative Corpse-Gas** <note>original: 積尸氣 (Jishiqi). This refers to the Beehive Cluster (Messier 44). To the naked eye, it appears as a faint, cloudy nebula.</note>. One interpretation says it represents a heap of corpses where only the "vapor" is visible; it governs death, mourning, and sacrificial rites. Another interpretation calls it the **Iron Lock**, governing executions; it is also known as the **Ghost’s Execution Block Star**.

When the stars of the Ghost Mansion are bright, the "five grains" <note>A traditional grouping of staple crops, signifying a successful harvest.</note> will ripen. If they are dim, the populace will scatter. If they flicker and shine intensely, taxes and forced labor will increase. If the stars shift their positions, the people will be filled with sorrow and government decrees will become harsh and urgent. For the Ghost Mansion, it is actually a sign of good fortune to be hazy and dim. If it appears bright, war will break out, high ministers will conspire against the throne, and the people will become refugees—a dire omen.

**Master Gan** <note>Gan De, a famous astronomer from the 4th century BCE.</note> says: If the "Cumulative Corpse-Gas" flickers or loses its color, there will be epidemics, ghosts will be heard wailing, and the land will fall into famine.

These represent the lighting of fires to warn of emergencies. In divination, it is a favorable sign when they are dim and still. If they appear large and bright, then...

### The Heavenly Dog
The **Heavenly Dog** <note>original: 天狗 (Tiangou). ### The Outer Kitchen
The **Outer Kitchen** <note>original: 外厨 (Waichu).</note>, consisting of six stars, is located to the south of the **Willow Mansion** <note>original: 柳 (Liu). The 24th Lunar Mansion.</note>. It serves as the "outer kitchen" for the **Son of Heaven** <note>The Emperor.</note>. The divinatory interpretations for this constellation are the same as those for the **Celestial Kitchen** <note>original: 天厨 (Tianchu). A constellation in Draco responsible for the imperial dietary needs.</note>.

### The Celestial Altar
To the south of the **Bow** <note>original: 弧 (Hu). An asterism in Canis Major and Puppis.</note> are the six stars of the **Celestial Altar** <note>original: 天社 (Tianshe). Located in the constellation Vela.</note>, situated to the southeast of the **Old Man Star** <note>original: 老人 (Laoren). Known in the West as Canopus.</note>.
*   If the stars are straight and bright, it is auspicious, and the **Altars of Soil and Grain** <note>original: 社稷 (Sheji). A metaphor for the state itself.</note> will be peaceful.
*   If they flicker or shake, officials are plotting against the sovereign.
*   If they are slightly dim, the state is in peril.

The **Treatise of the Sui Dynasty** <note>original: 隋志 (Sui Zhi). Referring to the "Treatise on Astronomy" within the *Book of Sui*.</note> states that **Goulóng** <note>original: 句龍.</note>, the son of the deity **Gònggōng**, was able to level the waters and the earth; therefore, he is sacrificed to alongside the Altar of the Soil. His spirit became this constellation.

### The Celestial Record
To the south of the **Outer Kitchen** is a single star called the **Celestial Record** <note>original: 天紀 (Tianji).</note>. It governs the knowledge of the "teeth and years" <note>An ancient way of referring to the age and lifespan.</note> of birds and beasts. If Venus or Mars "guards" this star <note>i.e., lingers near it in retrograde or conjunction.</note>, or if the star becomes invisible, then many birds and beasts will die.

The essence of the **Great Yang** <note>original: 太陽 *Taiyang*; the Sun</note> follows the heavens, rotating to the left. The heavens travel one circuit every day, which is consistently 365 and 1/4 degrees <note>In traditional Chinese astronomy, a degree was defined by the Sun's daily motion, making a full circle 365.25 degrees to match the days in a year</note>, plus an additional degree. The Sun also completes one circuit in a single day, but compared to the movement of the heavens, it falls short by one degree. After a total of 365 and 1/4 days, it meets again with the heavens <note>this describes the completion of one solar year</note>.

The essence of the **Great Yin** <note>original: 太陰 *Taiyin*; the Moon</note> also follows the heavens, rotating to the left. In one day, it consistently falls short of the heavens by thirteen degrees and a fraction <note>original: 有奇 *you qi*; meaning "and a remainder" or "some more"</note>, and it falls short of the Sun’s position by twelve degrees and a fraction. After a total of twenty-seven days and a fraction, it meets with the heavens <note>a sidereal month, the time it takes the moon to return to the same place among the stars</note>. After a total of twenty-nine days and a fraction, it meets with the Sun <note>a synodic month, the time between two new moons</note>.

### Governance and Records of the Seven Luminaries

The Sun follows the Yellow Path <note>original: 黃道 (Huangdao), the ecliptic or the sun's apparent path across the sky</note>, moving eastward one degree every day and night. It completes a full circuit of the heavens in three hundred sixty-five and a fraction days. Its transit through the Eastern Lands is called spring; its transit through the Southern Lands is summer; its transit through the Western Lands is autumn; and its transit through the Northern Lands is winter. Through these movements, the cycles of **Yin and Yang** and the seasonal rhythms of cold and heat are established.

Therefore, the traditions say: the Sun is the essence of Great *Yang* <term>Taiyang: the supreme active, masculine, and solar principle in Chinese cosmology</term>. It presides over the nourishment of life and the bestowal of benevolent grace; it is the symbolic representation of the Sovereign. If the Sovereign has flaws, the Sun will inevitably reveal these hidden faults as a warning to him. 

For this reason, when the Sun shines upon a nation that possesses the "Way" <note>the *Dao*, or the proper moral and cosmic order</note>, its light is brilliant. The Sovereign enjoys auspicious prosperity, and the common people live in peace and tranquility. When a Sovereign occupies his rightful place and his governance achieves Great Peace <term>Taiping: a state of perfect social and cosmic harmony</term>, the Sun will manifest in five colors <note>an auspicious rainbow-like solar phenomenon</note>.

Any celestial anomalies or strange variations are entirely due to a deficiency in the Sovereign’s virtue; thus, the changes in the heavens correspond to his actions. If the Sun’s color changes and a halo appears, it signifies the defeat of an army. If there is no halo during a color change, it portends mourning and illness. If the Sovereign lacks virtue and his ministers plot rebellion, the Sun will turn red and lose its radiance. If the Sun’s proper color is lost, the nation over which it shines will not prosper, and the day will turn dark as it travels.

If people cast no shadows and this lasts until evening, it indicates that punishments from above are harsh and the people below find life unbearable; within a year, there will be great floods. If the Sun is dim during the day and flocks of birds cry out, the state has lost its proper governance. 

If a bird is seen within the Sun <note>This likely refers to sunspots, traditionally personified as a three-legged crow in Chinese mythology</note>, the Sovereign is unenlightened and his governance is in chaos. There will be a "Gathering of White Clothes" <note>original: 白衣之會 (baiyi zhi hui); white is the color of mourning, but also represents commoners or rebels who lack official rank</note>, and generals will set out with banners raised for war. 

If there are **sunspots** <term>heizi: literally "black seeds," the historical term for solar spots</term> within the Sun, it means ministers are usurping authority and monopolizing blessings, thereby harming the "Way of the Sovereign." 

If black vapors or black clouds appear—sometimes three, sometimes five—it is a sign of a minister deposing his lord. This represents **Yin encroaching upon Yang** <note>original: 陰侵陽 (yin qin yang)</note>, a symbol of a minister overshadowing the Sovereign. This portends the fall of a nation or the death of a ruler. If a solar eclipse occurs and stars become visible during the day, there will be the assassination of a ruler and great floods; the realm will be split asunder. In such times, the King should **cultivate his virtue** <note>original: 修德 (xiu de)</note> to perform the **rituals of averting calamity** <term>rang: a ritual intended to expel evil or prevent a predicted disaster</term>.

### The Nature of the Moon

The Moon is the essence of the **Great Yin** <term>Taiyin: the supreme passive, feminine, and lunar principle, the counterpart to the Sun</term>. Its shape is perfectly round and its substance is pure. When the sunlight shines upon it, we see its brightness. The parts where the sunlight does not reach are called the *po* <note>original: 魄 (po); in Chinese cosmology, this refers to the dark, receptive "soul" or the unlit portion of the lunar disk</note>. 

Therefore, on the day of the **Full Moon** <note>original: 望 (wang), the 15th day of the lunar month</note>, the Sun and the Moon face one another, and so its shape appears perfectly round to us. On the days of the **two quarter moons** <note>original: 二弦 (er xian), literally "the two bowstrings"</note>, the Sun shines upon its side, and thus it appears as a "half-darkness." On the days of the **new moon and the month's end** <term>huishuo: the transition between the last day of the old month and the first of the new</term>, the Sun shines only upon its outer surface <note>the side facing away from the Earth</note>, and therefore it cannot be seen at all.

### The Year Star (Jupiter)

The **Year Star** <term>Suixing: the planet Jupiter, so named because its roughly 12-year orbit was used to track the years</term> is associated with the East, the season of Spring, and the element of Wood. Among the **Five Constants** <note>the five core Confucian virtues: Benevolence, Righteousness, Propriety, Wisdom, and Trustworthiness</note> of humanity, it represents **Benevolence** <note>original: 仁 (ren)</note>. Among the **Five Matters** <note>five aspects of royal conduct: appearance, speech, vision, hearing, and thought</note>, it represents Appearance. 

If benevolence is lacking and the ruler’s appearance is unseemly, defying the seasonal ordinances of Spring and injuring the vital energy of Wood, then punishment is manifested through the Year Star. The acceleration or deceleration of the Year Star determines the fate of a nation based on the **lunar mansion** <term>xiu: one of the 28 divisions of the sky through which the planets pass</term> in which it resides. Wherever the star dwells for a long duration, that state possesses deep virtue; the five grains will be abundant and prosperous, and that state must not be attacked. 

The position directly opposite the star is known as the "Clash." In that direction, the year will bring calamity. If the Year Star is tranquil and moves according to its proper degree, it is an auspicious sign. If its advancement or regression loses its sequence, the corresponding state will suffer disaster, and one must not initiate great affairs or deploy the military. 

It is also said: The Year Star is the image of the Sovereign. Its color should be bright, clear, and lustrous, which signifies that the ruler's virtue is in harmony with the cosmos. It is further said: If its advancement and retreat follow the proper degree, then treacherous and evil...

### The Sparkling Delusion (Mars)

The **Sparkling Delusion** <term>Yinghuo: the planet Mars, so named because its reddish color and retrograding motion seemed to "shimmer" and "delude" observers</term> represents the South, the season of Summer, and the element of Fire. It corresponds to the virtue of **Propriety** <note>original: 禮 (li)</note> and the sense of **Vision**. When propriety is deficient or lost, or when the commands of Summer are violated and the **Fire Qi** <note>the vital energy associated with heat, growth, and the heart</note> is harmed, then punishment is manifested through the appearance of Mars.

Mars acts as a celestial messenger; its movements are without constant rule. When it appears, there is war; when it retreats, the armies disperse. It issues fates according to the Lunar Mansions it occupies. If it lingers in a particular region's celestial station for a long time, that state will suffer calamity. It brings about chaos, rebellion, illness, death, warfare, and famine.
