### TO THE KIND READER.

**U**ust as Lipsius <note>Justus Lipsius (1547–1606), a renowned Flemish humanist who produced definitive editions of Roman authors.</note>, a great ornament of his age, successfully used the rejection of a certain manuscript from Mirandola <note>Referring to the *Codex Mirandulanus*, a manuscript Lipsius used to correct the text of the Roman historian Tacitus.</note> to restore the works of Cornelius Tacitus; so I have acquired three manuscript volumes <note>original: *MSS. voluminum*</note> that the "Phoenix of geniuses," Pico della Mirandola <note>Giovanni Pico della Mirandola (1463–1494), a brilliant Renaissance philosopher and pioneer of "Christian Kabbalah."</note>, once diligently studied. These were offered to me by chance in Venice, and I judged that they should be bought back at no small price. I have decided to use the benefit of the index found within them for the illustration of the hidden mysteries of the Kabbalah <term>Kabbalah: a traditional system of Jewish mysticism that seeks to explain the relationship between the eternal God and the mortal universe</term>.

That these volumes are the very same ones that the recently praised Count of Mirandola testified so often were acquired by him at an enormous price can surely be doubted by no one. In the margin of the first volume, he himself wrote brief notes with his own hand, from which he later established those famous Kabbalistic Conclusions <note>original: *conclusiones Cabalisticas*.
