# A MOST LUCID EXPLANATION OF THE EMBLEM
## AT THE BEGINNING OF THIS VOLUME.

**A**<note>The ornamental 'A' marks the beginning of the philosophical discourse.</note>t the beginning of the creation of all things, the incomprehensible and almighty Creator of the **Macrocosm** <note>The "Great World" or the entire universe, as opposed to the "Microcosm" or man.</note> divided the entire mass into three parts differing in purity by His immense power. From the purest part, He ordained that the Cherubim, Seraphim, Archangels, and all orders of other Angels be created; from the less pure, the heavens and the ethereal regions; and from the most impure, the elements and all sublunary <note>Things existing beneath the moon, subject to change and decay.</note> things, by saying: *Let them be made*, and they were made. However, because of the simple purity of its substance, He placed the first division of matter beyond the comprehension of natural things; for it is metaphysical and supernatural, which modern writers of Philosophy confirm is bounded by the **Empyrean Heaven** <note>original: "cœlo Empyreo" — the highest heaven, believed to be composed of pure light or fire.</note>, that is, the burning and fiery realm.

The remaining parts of the less pure mass, namely the ethereal and the elemental, are rightly called "natural" because they are subject to Nature (the governess of the **Microcosm** <note>The "Little World," referring to humanity as a reflection of the universe.</note> under GOD). Furthermore, two efficient causes contained within this fabric of the Macrocosm are enumerated: namely, **Nature**, and her **Ape**, which we call **Art**. The Father and Lord of Nature is GOD, the Best and Greatest; she is closest to Him, and we recognize that all her virtues and effects are derived from Him. She is, as it were, the knot and bond of the elements, and she possesses the power of rightly tempering the mixture of elements in their due proportions, mixing them aptly in every compound, and impressing upon each species its congruent form.

Nature is also a certain infinite force procreating like from like, as she is the fertile mother of all qualities and things, which she increases and nourishes. And to describe her in a single word according to the opinions of **Zoroaster** and **Heraclitus**, she is an **invisible fire**; which they themselves call the **Soul of the World** <note>original: "animam mundi" — the vital force connecting all living beings on Earth.</note>. We, however, have depicted her visibly in almost the same manner as some of the more recent Philosophers; we have done this with the intention that her power might be better understood by the reader's mind.

Thus, we have fashioned a naked virgin of tender and blooming age, praiseworthy for her star-bearing and most beautifully gilded hair and her most clear eyesight, and lovely for the blushing color of her cheeks. The virgin was so remarkable in the whiteness of her whole body, and so sweetly adorned with all the gifts of the greatest Creator, that the Poets could not prefer **Pallas**, nor **Venus**, nor **Juno**, nor any other goddess, however charming, to her beauty. The mobile world is governed, and the eighth sphere <note>The sphere of the fixed stars in traditional Ptolemaic astronomy</note> adorned with stars is handled and turned by her hands; and by her fingers, the influence of that same sphere is daily prepared and arranged for the generation of elementary things. The bodies of the Planets also serve as instruments for the works of Nature; they are, as it were, little hammers by which metals are produced in the terrestrial mines. The region of the heart and breast of this virgin is the true seat and matrix <note>womb or source</note> of the celestial Sun, and her belly is filled with the lunar body. From her breasts, she perpetually instills and suckles all the creatures of the elements with innate heat and radical moisture <note>The essential fluids believed to sustain life in medieval medicine</note>, from which their life and growth arise. By a certain golden splendor of her heart, the stars—both fixed and wandering <note>planets</note>—are illuminated, and they borrow their souls, lives, and beauties from it, as it were. Their imperceptible influences, infused into her matrix by the Mercurial Spirit (which the Philosophers call the Spirit of the Moon), are carried downward to the earth, penetrating even to its center; by these impressions, diverse generations, differing in both place and species, are continually made. By the actions of this Nature, the Planets come together, and by this joining, they produce various things differing in species in the animal, vegetable, and mineral kingdoms. Starting from the lower parts, from the loins, the four Elements are encompassed; for the right foot occupies the earth, and the left the water, signifying the conjunction of her sulfurous and mercurial nature: by which it is argued that nothing can be generated or grow without the union of both.

This Nature, I say—not a Goddess, but the closest minister of God—has beneath her a handmaid or follower who, by imitating her Mistress and impressing the likenesses of things produced by her into herself through consensus, follows and imitates her footsteps and outlines in a marvelous way. And it is for this reason that we have depicted her in the form and habit of an Ape <note>The "Ape of Nature" is a common Renaissance metaphor for Art or Craft</note> beneath the feet of Nature; which name we also call by the more dignified title of **Art**. This beast (I mean Art), arising from human ingenuity, learns many secrets, beautiful and notable, from the diligent observation of her Mistress, with which she is accustomed to enrich men in every kind of learning; so also, conversely, she brings no small utility and profit to that same teacher and Mistress. For unless Art, produced by human ingenuity, sometimes gives aid in certain matters, Nature herself would soon fail in the perfection of her operation. For Art sometimes corrects and supplies Nature, and indeed in the discourse of mineral things, she even seems to surpass her—if only we give some faith to those Chemical Philosophers <note>Alchemists</note>; especially since it is certain and proven by experience that in the animal circle, life can sprout more vigorously, silkworms can be multiplied, and by their growth be transformed into diverse shapes in the manner of Proteus <note>A Greek sea-god capable of changing his shape</note>, and bees can be procreated from the head of an ox; in the vegetable circle, crops grow and multiply by the artifice of the plow (by which fields are tilled and harrowed), trees by grafting, and herbs and other vegetables by planting; and in the mineral circle, metallic bodies and lesser minerals are prepared for medicine as well as for other uses, and cleansed of their dregs, and the Physician’s Stone <note>The Philosopher's Stone</note>, whose praises the Philosophers have so greatly preached, is forged. It is therefore our purpose in this volume to discuss these two effecting causes of the Macrocosm both clearly and copiously.

Furthermore, I do not believe this subject of ours should be submitted to the judgments of those who claim it is too profound and divine for human grasp, and for that reason decree that these sacred secrets <note>original: "arcana sacra"</note> must in no way be investigated by us. They constantly insist upon that old axiom: *What is above us, is nothing to us.* Indeed, by attempting to withdraw us entirely from the contemplation of divine matters in this way, they seem to suggest quite clearly that humans—made in the image of the immense Creator—differ little or not at all from brute beasts devoid of all reason. # PREFACE,
## IN WHICH IT IS DEMONSTRATED,
### HOW CAUSES ARE INVESTIGATED THROUGH THEIR EFFECTS, THAT IS, BY DEMONSTRATION *A POSTERIORI*. <note>The author uses the logical terms "a priori" (from cause to effect) and "a posteriori" (from effect back to cause) to explain his scientific method.</note>

Since it is impossible for even the wisest or most ingenious person to demonstrate or know God himself—the author of the fabric of the **Macrocosm** <note>The "Great World" or the universe as a whole.</note>—through an *a priori* path, and since it has never been granted to anyone to define Him essentially (not even to the divine leader Moses), nor shall it ever be granted in any age (so omnipotent and incomprehensible is He in His very being!), it is therefore necessary that He be sought *a posteriori*. In this way, our knowledge may approach Him through His more excellent effects. The Psalmist <note>Referring to King David in Psalm 19:1, though the text cites 19:4.</note> teaches us to behold His more noble effects in the heavens, and His glory in the firmament, as if in a highly polished mirror, with universal eyes. Because these things are placed above us, it will be necessary to ascend toward a knowledge of them through the elementary stages, as if moving from more complex things toward the simpler through the process of **resolution** <note>The chemical or logical process of breaking a substance down into its component parts.</note>. No rest in this ascent is to be allowed until those first principles are reached, which, on account of their simplicity, cannot undergo further resolution. This journey is therefore immense, exceedingly thorny, and dangerous; and truly, it is not easy to find by which ladders we might reach the summit of this invisible height without the aid of divine light and the illumination of the Spirit.

->*In how many ways is GOD said to be in a place, how is He present everywhere and filling all things? And concerning His principal seat in this world?*<-

BEFORE we treat of the proper seat or dwelling-place of *Natura naturans* <note>original: "Natura naturans" — literally "Nature naturing," a philosophical term for God as the creative force behind the universe, as opposed to "Natura naturata" or created nature.</note>, we must ask whether He can be in any place, since He is infinite in His essence? To this doubt, the entire School of Theologians responds by making a distinction: He is in a place, *not definitively, like intelligences or spiritual creatures, nor circumscriptively, in the manner of bodily things, but only by presence;* although according to **Gregory**, **GOD** is said to be in all things in a *threefold* way: namely, *either by presence*, as He bestows good upon them; *or by power*, namely through influence; *or by essence*, that is, through the intimacy of His essence. Moreover, **GOD** is said to be everywhere insofar as His divine power permeates all beings, through which He defines the relationship of the entire universe's potentiality to Himself. Thus He is also said to fill all things, not so that He is contained by them, but rather so that they are contained by Him. *For it is*, says **Ambrose**, *a property of the Deity to be everywhere and in all things.* It is manifest, therefore, that **GOD** is not in all things in such a way that any object can contain Him according to the measure of its own portion—as larger things contain smaller—but He is said to fill all things in such a way that there is nothing where He Himself is not. We understand, therefore, that **GOD** is within all things, but not enclosed, because He is infinite and immeasurable; and outside all things, but not excluded, insofar as He encompasses all created things by His uncircumscribed greatness. And it is from this that all things created by **GOD** are called "good" by Him through participation—that is, through His presence, or according to the degrees of essence by which angels are preferred to souls, and souls to animals. And yet, insofar as any creature has obtained more or less dignity according to the beauty of its form proceeding from the first nature or Being, to that extent, as **Iamblichus** <note>A Neoplatonist philosopher who wrote on theurgy and the soul.</note> testifies, it will possess a higher or lower seat in goodness acquired through participation. But truly, as "good" is understood absolutely and simply, and—to use the words of **Iamblichus**—*above essence*, to that extent it is taken for the cause of good, that is, for the principle of essential good; concerning which **Hermes Trismegistus** <note>original: "Mercurius Trismegistus" — the legendary Hellenistic figure associated with the Hermetic writings.</note> treats in that place where he warns us to beware of calling anything "good" except **GOD** alone. > *A shadow,* he says, *infinite in the abyss, etc.*

We conclude, therefore, from all the writings of both ancient and modern philosophers, that this first matter is the primordial Being, infinite, unformed, in potentiality as much toward something as toward nothing; of no quantity or dimension, since it can be called neither small nor large; of no quality, since it is neither subtle, nor thick, nor perceptible; of no property or inclination, neither moving nor resting, without any color or elementary quality. ->*In how many ways is GOD said to be in a place, how is He present everywhere and filling all things? And concerning His principal seat in this world?*<-

Before we treat of the proper seat or dwelling of "Nurturing Nature" <note>original: *Natura naturans*; a technical term for God as the creative force behind the natural world</note>, we must ask whether He can be in any specific place, since He is infinite in His essence? To this doubt, the entire School of Theologians responds by making a distinction: He is in a place *not definitively*, like intelligences or spiritual creatures, nor *circumscriptively* <note>meaning limited by boundaries or physical edges</note>, like bodily things; *but only by presence*. However, according to **Gregory** <note>likely Pope Gregory the Great</note>, GOD is said to be in all things in a *threefold way*: namely, either *by presence*, as He lays open what is good to them; or *potentially*, namely through influence; or *essentially*, that is, through the intimacy of His essence. Moreover, GOD is said to be everywhere insofar as His divine power permeates all beings, through which He defines the relationship of the potentiality of the entire universe to Himself. Thus He is also said to fill all things, not so that He is contained by them, but rather so that they are contained by Him. *For it is*, says **Ambrose**, *a property of the Deity to be everywhere and in all things*. It is clear, therefore, that GOD is not in all things in such a way that any object can contain Him according to the measure of its own portion (as larger things contain smaller ones); rather, He is said to fill all things in such a way that there is nothing where He Himself is not. We understand, therefore, that GOD is within all things but not enclosed, because He is infinite and immeasurable; and He is outside all things but not excluded, insofar as He encompasses all created things by His uncircumscribed greatness. And it is from this that all things created by GOD are called "good" by Him through participation—that is, through His presence, or according to the grades of essence by which angels are preferred over souls, and souls over animals. For when it is considered by itself—that is, with respect to its own proper matter—it is called the **middle spirit** after the dispersal of the darkness. However, with respect to the superior heaven—that is, the lucid substance—and its mixing with the spirit of that realm, it is called **ether** <note>original: "æther"</note>. Furthermore, insofar as it relates to the four inferior elements appearing and distinguished on the third day, it is called the **Fifth Essence** <term>Quinta essentia: the "quintessence," a celestial substance distinct from earth, water, air, and fire</term>. Therefore, by itself—that is, in its own substance revealed on the second day and cleansed of its gloom—it displays the form of the most subtle waters, and of a certain vaporous and never-resting smoke. Hence, among the *Platonists*, it is called the **middle spirit of the world**, which we called the receptacle, bond, and vehicle of the essential light and the **soul of the world** <note>original: "anima mundi"</note> above in Chapter 5 of Book 1; where we also demonstrated the reasoning for that name. 

But insofar as this spirit is the bond of the light descending from the spiritual fountain, by whose action a life-giving and natural heat is excited, it is to that extent called **ether**, *that is, shining*, according to *Isidore* <note>Isidore of Seville, an influential medieval encyclopedist</note>; because there is perpetual clarity and brightness there. Wherefore *Orpheus* calls it *Pyripnon* <note>original: "Pyripnon", from Greek πῦρ (fire) and πνοή (breath)</note>, *that is, a fiery breath*, and *Porphyry* with the other Platonists call it the spirit in the middle of which they placed the soul of the world. 

And hence it is that, according to *Marianus*, in the ether there is perpetual day, because darkness never approaches that place; although its simple substance, divided from the stellar bodies, is invisible—that is, not perceivable by mortal eyes—as we shall declare more extensively in the following chapter. Finally, it is held to be a single element, just as *Aristotle* wishes in his book *On Meteors* <note>original: "Meteorōn"</note>, where in that place he asserts that the ether is nothing other than a single element. Whence it is necessary that this single element be outside the number of the four inferior essences soon to be produced in the lowest region. And for this reason it is also called the **fifth essence** by the Philosophers, in which nothing of corruption is found, since two contraries inducing putrefaction are not found in the same element. 

From the reasons for these names, therefore, it is manifest that the matter of this region—formerly primary, but now made secondary through its reduction into action—is nothing other than a most pure vapor, or a certain most subtle mist, generally called **spirit**; and its form, by whose presence it is brought into action, is that light or clarity flowing essentially from the perennial fountain of the divine and supracelestial spirit, totally filling the appetite of this middle spiritual matter, as will be declared below.

Therefore, by two united forces—namely the natural and the adventitious <note>adventitious: coming from without; added or external</note>—the matter, which is rebellious on account of its coldness, is powerfully beaten back by them. This matter, which by a certain natural instinct fled from those forces emitted from the periphery—which, pursuing their cold nature, chased both the primary and secondary effects of light even to the seat of a place most remote from the circumference of that same light—shrank itself into the form of a dreadful and oblique revolution of a gliding shadow around the center or middle point of the spherical machine of the universe, forming a hard, solid, and opaque globe. For in this place, it is much more secure from the actions of its adversary, namely heat, and far more remote from the domicile of light, since it is from the act of light that that inimical heat arises; which motion of matter was indeed not stirred up by itself, but by that aforementioned double action of light. For just as without the manifestation of light the matter of darkness was only something potential; so also without the presence of heat, which is the essential quality of light, that natural disposition of matter, namely coldness, was indeed nothing in act; and consequently, it produces no changes by itself. Therefore, the coldness of matter is driven downward, both by the active virtue of the primary light and by the element of fire produced by the motion of matters; for without these, nothing at all is done. Moreover, that coldness, by suffering the repercussions of heat, is seen to resist in a certain way, and by resisting, to react. By these actions and passions, other grades are produced in this lowest heaven, and intermediate regions differing in disposition are established, which Philosophers call the lower elements. From these things, therefore, that principal reason is explained: why heavy things cannot fall beyond the center of the world, and for what reason they rest in it perpetually; namely, because gravity exists as the companion of denser and more compact matter. The cause of this thickness was coldness, contracting the dissipated and thin matter toward the center of the world away from the rage of penetrating heat, and constricting it in that place, and defending it against the invasions of the rays of light. Since, however, the action of heat pursues the cold dispersed in every part of a thick thing, and especially around the center throughout the whole mass of its body, and the rays of light (whose nature it is to penetrate bodies most easily) besiege and surround it everywhere as it were, it follows from this that heavy things can by no means proceed beyond that center toward the natural habitation of light, unless that cold enclosed by nature in heavy things is oppressed and violated by the violence of heat. And hence it is that any heavy thing violently cast upward returns naturally and of its own accord downward to the center or the seat of coldness. It is manifest, therefore, that all the substance of darkness, by whose presence the spiritual matter of the whole world was hidden and formless, is now at last, by the virtue of light, reduced and gathered into a dense and opaque mass around the center of the world, which is indeed called the element of earth. But the remaining spiritual mass of the whole of matter, now formed, diaphanous and wonderfully transparent, extending from the borders of the earth even to the convex surface of the spiritual world, is called by the general name "heaven." ## *On the reasoning of worldly proportions.*

From the preceding chapters, it is evident that just as the Empyrean Heaven <note>The highest, most spiritual part of the universe, believed by ancient and Renaissance scholars to be composed of pure light or fire.</note> is the home and seat of supernatural fire and light—from which natural fire proceeds directly, and that other more accidental fire proceeds indirectly—so also its opposite place, which terminates and ends the motion of its rays, is the receptacle of matter and darkness. Since, moreover, the relationship between opposites is the same, it is necessary for the continuity of the world that the disposition of the moist nature of darkness relates to that fiery nature of light by the same degrees and the same proportion. Hence, as we have declared above that the physical center <note>The Earth.</note> is naturally cold in the fourth degree, most dense, most opaque, and most heavy, and by its nature seeks rest; so also the ultimate region of the world-machine <term>machina mundi: the universe viewed as a complex, divinely designed mechanism</term> is without doubt opposed to it in all things, not only in location but also in condition. Thus, by the equal action of the one and the proportional passion <note>In the sense of "being acted upon."</note> of the other, all intermediate bodies are constituted; and according to the nature of the subject—that is, according to the abundance or lack of the light predominating in it—they either resist deprivation for a long time or more quickly yield and are subjected to it.

Who, therefore, will doubt that the supreme region of the Empyrean heaven is free and immune from every invasion of coldness (the property of which is to thicken), since it is the most subtle, most pure, most light, transparent in the highest degree of clarity, and is almost nothing else than fire sublimated to the ultimate peak of most sweet and amorous heat? Undoubtedly, therefore, those proportions of things induce a most noble worldly harmony <term>harmonia mundana: the "music of the spheres" or the mathematical order of the cosmos</term>, through which, by the virtue of the primary light working upon the middle matter, an indissoluble concord of things is won, and the ears of the intellect are soothed by a certain inexplicable music.

For the instrument of this melody—namely, the machine of the world—is like a monochord <note>A single-stringed instrument used since antiquity to demonstrate mathematical ratios in music.</note>, whose string, through which the consensus of the parts is introduced, is the intermediate matter of the whole world. The Author in this music is the Soul of the World or the essential light <term>lux essentifica: light that gives essence or being to things</term>; not otherwise than in that instrumental music of man, in which **Γ** (that is, Gamma) is taken as the beginning from the lower part of the system, ascending with deep, excellent, and super-excellent voices. Such a progress surely occurs also in the worldly monochord: namely, ascending proportionally from the Earth to its *ee la* <note>The highest note in the medieval "Guidonian Hand" musical system.</note>, which is the limit of the ultimate periphery. And as in Gamma the sound is heavier and deeper because of its extreme distance from *ee la*, from which the striking of the voice becomes more intense by ascending; so also the higher one ascends from the Earth toward the Empyrean heaven, the stronger the effect of light and heat is found, and the worldly spirit <term>spiritus mundanus: the subtle "breath" or ether that permeates the universe</term> is rendered thinner by its virtue. Just as a greater force, and therefore a greater virtue of the shining soul, is required for the raising of the human voice, while in its lowering, altogether less is desired.

Then, let a second string be stretched from another point to its opposite, and likewise a third and fourth, proceeding in this way with the rest until the thickness of the entire pyramidal instrument is filled with strings differing proportionally among themselves. We say, therefore, that these same strings, when stretched with equal force, maintain the intervals of Whole Tones and Semitones, so that from the base of the pyramid toward its peak <note>original: "conum"</note>, we shall always ascend from deeper sounds to sharper ones through those musical scales (which we more rightly call intervals), until the peak is reached. Beyond this, to ascend higher will be no less impossible than trying to penetrate further by moving beyond an Arithmetic unit or a Geometric point.
