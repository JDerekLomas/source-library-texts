# THE BRITISH HISTORY OF THE HEAVENS
## VOLUME THREE.

->Containing<-

A spacious **PREFACE** (or **PROLEGOMENA** to the *Catalog of* **Fixed Stars**) which provides a brief *History* of **ASTRONOMY**, as well as a Description of the *Observations* performed and the *Instruments* employed both by the astronomers of old, and

->In the Royal OBSERVATORY at GREENWICH.<-

->Next<-

A **Catalog of FIXED STARS** constructed by *Ptolemy, Ulugh Beg, Tycho Brahe, William Landgrave of Hesse, and Hevelius.* <note>These represent the primary astronomical authorities from Antiquity through the 17th century.</note>

->Finally<-

A **New and most ample CATALOG** of the *Right Ascensions, Distances from the Pole, Longitudes, Latitudes,* and *Magnitudes of the Fixed Stars:* together with the *Variations of Right Ascensions* and *Polar Distances* while they change their *Longitudes* by one Degree. <note>Right Ascension and Polar Distance are the coordinates used to map the sky, similar to longitude and latitude on Earth.</note>

->To which is annexed<-

A Catalog of certain *Southern* **FIXED STARS**, not visible in our **Hemisphere**.

->Lastly<-

Most ample **TABLES** for finding the *Longitudes and Latitudes* of the **FIXED STARS** and **PLANETS** by *Inspection* <note>meaning by looking them up in the table rather than calculating by hand</note>, given only their *Right Ascensions* and *Distances* from the POLE.

# PREFACE TO THE READER:
### OR,
## PROLEGOMENA <note>A formal introductory essay or preliminary remarks to a book.</note> to the Catalogue of FIXED STARS.

KEPLER, a man of keen intellect (in the first part of his *Commentaries on the Motions of the Star* <note>original: "Stellæ"</note> MARS), demonstrated to us how those men who first closely observed the motions of the celestial bodies explored the inequality of planetary movements, how these motions are carried out, and how they constructed their theories upon them. Ismael BULLIALDUS <note>Ismaël Boulliau (1605–1694), a French astronomer and defender of Copernicus.</note>, a most learned man, constructed a history of astronomy in the introduction to his *Philolaic Astronomy*; as did also *Lucius BARRETTUS* <note>The pseudonym of Albert Curtz (1600–1671), a Jesuit astronomer who edited Tycho Brahe's observations.</note> in the introduction to his book on the *Observations* of TYCHO BRAHE, which he published in a large volume under the title *Danish Celestial History*. Those who desire to be instructed in either of these particular matters should consult the authors who discuss this subject at length. I shall say little of what has been handed down by the aforementioned authors, unless something remains to be expanded or corrected, adding certain things perhaps not devised by them, or which have become apparent to us since their works were published.

<sig>A</sig>
