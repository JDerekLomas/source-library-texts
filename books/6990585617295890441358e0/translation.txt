# PREFATORY TREATISE.

IN this brief present treatise, a small part of the ancient <term>arithmetic science</term> <note>The study of number theory as it relates to music and geometry</note>, it is assumed that the common operations of adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing ratios are already known. The immediate and direct foundation of this section depends upon the demonstration provided in the third Chapter, sixth Proposition, Figure VI, to prove that within the <term>sextuple</term> <note>A 6:1 ratio</note> is found the period and completion of the integral extension of the harmonic system, by virtue of the four means of the <term>duple ratio</term> <note>The 2:1 ratio, or the octave</note>, which is demonstrated to be the first universal principle of the system. It being a matter of common agreement that there are four means for any given ratio—the <term>harmonic</term>, <term>geometric</term>, <term>arithmetic</term>, and <term>sub-contrary</term> <note>original: "contrarmonico"</note>; by common demonstration, given the duple ratio of 120 to 60, the harmonic mean being 80, the arithmetic 90, and the sub-contrary 100; and since in the aforementioned demonstration there results, beyond the three assigned, another mean which is 84; if the three assigned means are true, then the fourth must also be a true mean. But given the three means—harmonic, arithmetic, and sub-contrary—nothing is missing except for the geometric. Therefore, the result of 84 is the missing geometric mean: all the more so because it results between the harmonic mean of 80 and the arithmetic mean of 90. Since 84 is neither one nor the other of the two aforementioned means, and since it results demonstratively between them—where the geometric mean must be found—84 shall be the geometric mean respective to the harmonic system. This mean, when reduced with its relative extremes of 120 and 60 to <term>prime numbers</term> <note>In this context, the lowest whole-number terms of the ratio</note>, is 10, 7, 5. Of such a mean, as a geometric one, there is not, nor can there be, a clear idea, because it is contrary to common definition and understanding.
