# In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful

Praise be to God, from whose light the veils of secrecy are illuminated, and from whom the wonders of destiny flow. To Him all traces return, and by His command night and day alternate. He is the bringer of things into existence after their non-existence. Praise be to God, the Creator of all beings and the Changer of their blessings. He is the Ordainer and the Originator of every being; He is neither mingled with them nor distinct from them. Attributes do not encompass Him, injustice does not touch Him, descriptions do not exhaust Him, and accidents <note>In philosophy, "accidents" refers to non-essential properties that change, unlike the eternal essence.</note> do not affect Him. May God bless the Master of Messengers and the Seal of the Prophets, upon whom the Book was revealed in a clear Arabic tongue, and who is mentioned in the scriptures of the ancients, and upon his virtuous, good, and pure family, and grant them peace.

## Know

O student, who are enticed to dive into the sciences of the philosophers, to look into their secrets, and to search for the wonders they have immortalized in their books: know that the motive that moved me to compose this book—which I have named *The Goal of the Wise* <note>Original: "Ghayat al-Hakim." This is the famous "Picatrix."</note> and the more deserving of the two results to be presented first—is what occurred to me during the composition of the book I wrote previously, which I named *The Rank of the Wise* <note>Original: "Rutbat al-Hakim." This was the author's work on alchemy.</note>. My composition of that book began in the year 343 <note>954-955 CE</note> and I completed it at the end of the year 348 <note>959-960 CE</note>.

What moved me to compose this is what I have seen most people of our time searching for regarding the matter of <term>talismans</term> <note>Objects inscribed with astrological symbols to capture celestial power.</note> and the various arts of magic, while they do not know what they seek nor which path they intend. Their lives have been wasted in a pursuit that has been shielded from them because the Sages concealed it and declined to reveal it, due to the destruction and ruin it could bring to the world. God forbids that, for the welfare of His world. For this reason, they erected the structures of the <term>temples</term> <note>Original: "Barabi," referring specifically to ancient Egyptian temples believed to hold hidden alchemical and magical secrets.</note> and placed what they immortalized in books using symbols and enigmas that none but a Sage like them can understand. Yet, they conveyed the truth in between those lines and explained the secrets to those who could comprehend them.

I saw fit to disseminate to the people and explain to them the obscured path of this result called <term>Simiya</term> <note>Natural or letter magic; here referring to the art of enchanting the senses or the soul.</note>, and to clarify what the Sages hid regarding this magical result, just as we did with the <term>alchemical result</term> <note>Original: "al-natija al-san'awiyya," referring to the "Art" of alchemy.</note>. I have divided this book into four treatises, each treatise being useful and summarized into chapters.

**The First Treatise** has seven chapters, intentionally corresponding to the seven fast-moving planets. The first chapter is on the nobility of wisdom. In this first treatise, I mention the celestial proportions and the forms of the spheres' proportions when constructing talismans, and how the fixed stars cast their rays upon the planets. I have deposited within it obscure meanings that the Sages kept hidden out of stinginess and greed at the beginning of the treatise.

**The Second Treatise** is on the celestial images <note>The constellations and decans.</note> and the explanation of the secrets the Sages obscured, and examples of how magic is borrowed into this world—called the <term>World of Generation and Corruption</term> <note>The sublunary world where things are born and die, as opposed to the eternal heavens.</note>—from the world of the ether. I also discuss what led Plato to speak of the "unification of forms."

**The Third Treatise** is on the properties of the planets within the <term>three kingdoms</term> <note>Original: "al-muwalladat al-thalath," meaning minerals, plants, and animals.</note>, for there is nothing in the world of generation and corruption that accepts magical work except these. The <term>elements</term> <note>Original: "al-istiqassat," from the Greek "stoicheia."</note> are impossible to work with directly as they are fluid and do not hold impressions; therefore, only these three remain. I shall mention the mixing of some with others to achieve the desired magical effects through the influence of elemental or natural heat, whether carried by incense to be burned, or by things intended for the stomach, such as food or drink.

**The Fourth Treatise** is on the magic of the Kurds, the Nabataeans, and the Abyssinians, and examples of the works of magical stratagems, which are the finest types of magic. I shall complete this purpose as required, without stinginess or concealment. I ask God for help in completing what we have intended; He is the Master of that, and with His help, I begin.

# The First Treatise

Know, O brother—may God enlighten your intellect—that wisdom is the most sublime of gifts and the best of acquisitions. For wisdom is the knowledge of the remote causes <note>original: "al-asbab al-ba'ida" - in Aristotelian philosophy, these are the ultimate or primary principles of existence</note> by which all existing things came to be, and the knowledge of the immediate causes for those things that possess them. This is achieved by demonstrating their existence and knowing what they are and how they are.

Though these causes are many, they ascend in an orderly fashion to a single Existent who is the cause for the existence of those remote things and the immediate things beneath them. That One is the First in reality; His subsistence does not depend on anything else. Rather, He is self-sufficient, needing no other to grant Him existence, nor is it possible for Him to receive existence from another at all. He cannot be a body <note>material substance</note> in any way, nor can He reside within a body. His existence is of a different kind, distinct from the existence of all other beings, sharing no common meaning with them at all. If they share a description, it is in name only, not in the actual meaning understood from that name.

There can only be one such Being. He is the One in truth, and He is the one who bestowed upon all other existents the unity by which we say they "exist." He exists without a cause or reason; He is the First Truth who bestows reality upon others, while His own reality is sufficient unto itself. It is impossible to imagine a perfection greater than His perfection—exalted be He—let alone for such a thing to exist. There is no existence more complete than His, no reality more absolute than His, and no unity more perfect than His.

Furthermore, one should know how all other beings derived existence and reality from Him, and what the portion <note>allotment or share</note> of existence, reality, and unity is for each one of them. One should understand how all other caused causes derived from Him, and know the ranks of all existents: some are first, some are intermediate, and some are final. The final ones have causes but are not causes for anything below them. The intermediate ones have a cause before them and are causes for things below them. The First is the cause for all that is below Him and has no cause above Him. One must know how the final things ascend toward one another until they reach the First, and how the order begins from the First and descends through the existents until it reaches the last of them.

This is wisdom in truth. So, what do you think of such a path, O seeker? Wisdom—may God strengthen you—is vast and majestic; seeking it is both a virtue and a religious duty. This is because it illuminates the intellect and the soul with eternal, beautiful light when they seek it, and it makes them renounce this decaying world once its meaning and essence are understood. It inspires a longing to occupy oneself with that high and sublime world, which is their origin and their point of return, and wherein is their resurrection. There, God shall comfort them and make known to them the cause of the world and its effect, and the reason for manifesting the "cause of the effect" <note>likely referring to the intermediary or Logos</note> which stands between them. Thus, they may know with necessary knowledge that God Almighty is the Cause of the world, the world is His effect, and the reason [for existence] is His service—that they might glorify and worship Him, as He announced: *I have not created jinn and mankind except to worship Me* <note>A quote from the Quran, Surah Adh-Dhariyat 51:56</note>. He sustains them and provides for them so they may thank and praise Him; whoever He wills shall be wretched, and whoever He intends shall be happy, remaining with Him in the bliss that never perishes.

->( Wisdom possesses three specific characteristics )<-

These are essential qualities: it grows and does not vanish; it shines and does not fade; and it reveals itself to be gazed upon and does not withdraw. It also possesses three powers of governance: it restrains, it disciplines, and it does not turn toward those who have no desire for it. Know that this "Result" <note>term: "al-Natija" - The Result. Here referring to the specific philosophical conclusion or the title of a section</note> which we are about to uncover could not exist without the existence of wisdom. The sages rightly called it a "Result," for among the masters of logic, the result is the fruit of the syllogism <note>term: "al-qiyas" - the logical process of deduction</note> and the finest part of the premises. The purpose of setting down these two results was nothing other than to incite the quest for knowledge; therefore, none shall attain it except the wise.

One who has encompassed knowledge of all branches of wisdom <term>Wisdom: here referring to *Hikma*, a blend of philosophy and science</term> according to their ranks, will find that the status of this final stage of wisdom is like the status of a conclusion in a syllogism <term>Syllogism: a form of logical reasoning where a conclusion is drawn from two premises</term>, which is the finest part of the premises. Understand this, for I have explained to you a wondrous secret. Know that the conclusion is the fruit of the two premises, which is called a "couple" <note>original: *qarinah*</note>, and when it produces a result, it is called a "collector" <note>original: *jami'ah*</note>, or in the language of the Greeks, *syllogismos* <note>original: *sumjus*, a transliteration of the Greek *syllogismos*</note>.

A premise consists of a subject and a predicate. The subject is the "starting noun" according to the grammarians, and the predicate is the "report" or "news." The "report" is that which can be characterized by truth or falsehood. The subject and the predicate—which is that which is predicated—is a statement that is neither limited nor defined, because all statements are of this kind (some statements are not limited or defined, while others are limited and others are defined). I mean that the informative statement is what is used in logical propositions. Other types of speech, according to the scholars, are not used [in logic], such as commands, inquiries, questions, and vocatives, because they do not yield truth or falsehood. To explain what we have mentioned would require a lengthy commentary that would deviate from our purpose; let whoever wishes seek it in its proper places.

## Chapter Two

Know that the "conclusion" is what is expressed by the term Magic <note>original: *Sihr*</note>. Magic is an absolute reality, just as it enchants the minds and as souls from all corners of existence submit to it. Actions [of magic] imply wonder, submission, listening, and approval. It is that which is difficult for the intellect to perceive, and its causes are hidden from the dull-witted. This is because it is a divine power with pre-existing causes set toward your perception; it is a science of obscure comprehension. 

Some of it is also practical, because its subject is "spirit within spirit"—this is *Niranj* <term>Niranj: a type of white magic or sorcery involving spells and illusions</term> and phantasmagoria—just as the subject of the Talisman is "spirit within a body." The subject of Alchemy is likewise "body within a body." In short, magic is that whose cause is mostly hidden and whose derivation is difficult to deduce. 

The reality of the Talisman <note>original: *Talsam*</note> is found in the reversal of its name: it is the "Ruler" <note>The author suggests a linguistic play where *Talsam* reversed sounds like *Musallat* (Ruler/Empowered)</note>, because it is the essence of the Moon. Ruling acts upon that which is composed of it through an act of dominance and subjugation, using numerous proportions, established celestial secrets, and specific bodies at favorable times, along with strengthening incenses that draw the spiritualities of that talisman. Its state is like that of the "Second Conclusion," which is expressed as the Elixir <note>original: *al-Iksir*</note>, which transforms bodies into its own likeness and stabilizes them. For it is a potent leaven that transforms things through their senses. It is a ruler, like a poison that flows through its own kind of bodies and transforms them into itself, in order to flip one person [or substance] into its own person by a power placed within it.

<insert>Know, my brother, that the reality of the leaven</insert> is an elixir composed of earth, air, water, and fire. When contained in its refined vessel, it directs whatever it is placed in toward its own essence and turns it into its own form. It acts upon its contents through the expansion and fragility it has acquired, until its digestion settles in the stomach and it is quickly transformed into nourishment.

Likewise, the Alchemical Elixir acts in this way: the body is quickly transformed into it, and it changes it from one nature to a more noble nature, clothing it in spirit, soul, and solidity, and removing from it corrosion <note>original: *tazanjur*, specifically verdigris or rust</note> and corruption. Such was its secret among the ancients. The word "Elixir" means the power that breaks <note>The author links *Iksir* to the Arabic root *K-S-R*, meaning to break</note> other powers, changing them through dominance and transforming them into its own essence until they become similar to it.

The Elixir only exists within the totality of animals and plants in a certain respect, as they constitute a "world" according to the sages. For through their union, the world exists and one part rectifies the other. Plants cannot subsist by themselves, nor can animals subsist by themselves; rather, they are in need of plants and other things. The mineral is in need of "cooking" and fieriness along with mercurial moisture; only then is it perfected. This is a secret we overlooked in the *Book of the Rank* <note>original: *Kitab al-Rutba*, a famous alchemical work attributed to Maslama al-Majriti</note>. Now I return to our purpose and say:

### ### Magic is Divided into the Theoretical and the Practical

The **theoretical** <note>original: *al-ilmi* - literally "scientific" or "knowledge-based"</note> consists of knowing the positions of the fixed stars, as their positions are the locations of the "forms," and understanding how they cast their rays upon the wandering stars <note>the planets</note>, as well as the state of the celestial proportions when seeking a desired outcome. Under this category falls everything established by the ancients regarding elections <note>choosing auspicious times</note> and talismans.

Know that for whoever chooses to bind a talisman, this knowledge is a necessity. The finest type of theoretical magic is **speech**, and similarly, **gesture**. Indeed, "some speech is surely magic" <note>A famous Hadith: *Inna min al-bayani la-sihra*</note>. Among the proofs of this is the saying of the Supporter, Plato, in the *Book of Aphorisms*: "Just as a friend turns into an enemy through evil speech, so too does an enemy turn into a friend through good speech." Is this not a species of magic?

The **practical** <note>original: *al-amali*</note> refers to the subtle arts dependent on the "generated beings" <note>minerals, plants, and animals</note> and the planetary powers infused within them. These are expressed as "properties" <note><term>khawass: occult properties inherent in natural substances</term></note> by those who advocate for them, though they may not know their cause or reality, nor have the need to uncover the secrets of the ancients. Furthermore, it involves the mixing of some substances with others through labor, seeking thereby an elemental heat—this belongs to the category of **fumigations**, used to aid complete powers over deficient ones. Or, one seeks thereby a natural heat—this belongs to the category of **edibles**. In both cases, they do not exceed these limits and are only assisted by the human or animal soul.

The tricks called *Niranjat* <note><term>Niranjat: a Persian-derived term for white magic, sleight of hand, or illusions produced by natural means</term></note> are the finest types of this practical magic. Know that magic is of two kinds: that which is **acquired** and that which is **mechanical**.

*   **The Acquired:** This is what the Sage of the Lunar Cycle used to support, and it is alluded to by the words of the Almighty: *"Take four birds"* <note>A reference to Quran 2:260 regarding Abraham</note>.
*   **The Mechanical:** This is what the Sage of the Saturnian Cycle used to perform, as well as what the Sage of the Venusian Cycle performed.

The ancient Greeks used to designate *Niranjat* and "the transformation of the eye" <note>optical illusions or glamours</note> with the name *Tarjih* <note>preponderance</note>, and they called the talisman *Saljamus* <note><term>Saljamus: likely a corruption of a Greek term, possibly related to the summoning of spirits</term></note>, which is the drawing down of the powers of the higher spirits. They applied the name "Magic" to all of these. These sages had no power over this science except through the knowledge of astronomy.

### Chapter Three

Know, O observer, that the celestial sphere is a solid orb in the ultimate state of roundness. Likewise, everything within it, in all its states and at all times, is perfectly circular. Some people have imagined that states occur within the sphere that remove it from its circular shape at certain times, but it is impossible for anything of that sort to exist at all. This is because the shape of the sphere is the shape of its Cause. This is because the Soul—this is its form—is the "First Thing Sent Forth." This is because the First Thing, in which there is no corruption, possesses a perfect form. The perfect form is the **circle**, and for this reason, it is constituted from a single line, because it is a First Cause. This subject has a hidden proof.

The sphere, moreover, is an essence, and the degrees <note>of the zodiac</note> within it are merely positional. If we begin with the positional degrees, we ascend from them to the "Necessary Compulsion." The path of instruction for this matter is actually the reverse. Furthermore, the teaching of the path leads to the teaching of the states of the sphere. It is impossible for any of the bodies of the "World of Generation and Corruption" <note>the sublunary realm/Earth</note> to be in the place of the sphere, nor can any part of the sphere be in the place of the World of Generation and Corruption, unless by force.

The sphere, as we have said, is a smooth orb from all sides in the ultimate health of its roundness. The circle is formed from a single curved line, inside of which is a point. All lines extending from this point to the circumference are equal. That point is the **Center**. The reference to these lines is to the "casting of the rays" of the planets upon the World of the Center <note>Earth</note>. This is the influence of talismans; it is like a limit or a design that has been agreed upon. Since the sphere is an orb encompassing the entire world, there is neither void nor plenum beyond it.

The **Ether** is a still form, and it is that which is pointed to... those planets are within its interior, deviating from its center toward the side of the Earth's center, as has been said regarding the inclination. The nature of the sphere is a single nature.

This is the necessary knowledge of the science of the spheres, and it is found in the books of the ancients, so let it be taken from them. > I am the one who was raised above the seven spheres.

And similar to it. By saying "I was raised," he intended the comprehension of them through the knowledge of his intellectual power, similar to the allusion in the verse: "And We raised him to a high station" <note>Quran 19:57, referring to the Prophet Idris, who is traditionally identified with Hermes</note>.

## Chapter Four

Know, O observer—may the world be your model—that Saturn moves things characterized by coldness and dryness. Jupiter moves things characterized by coldness and moisture <note>Note: Traditionally Jupiter is hot and moist; the author may be following a specific variant tradition or emphasizing its tempering effect</note>. Mars moves things characterized by heat and dryness. Venus moves things characterized by weak heat and dominant moisture. Mercury moves things characterized by weak heat and much dryness. The Moon moves things characterized by coldness and moisture... and the fixed stars are likewise. Whenever a degree is under the influence of a planet that is purely hot but weak in its secondary qualities of dryness and moisture, and the Sun is in that place alone, you should judge according to that matter and its increase. Likewise, if it happens that they act with their combined power, growth will be greater. This is a difficult and highly obscure point among the early sages; for "obscurity" to them in speech meant to veil the literal meaning and hide the inner meaning—this is what they called obscurity. So know this.

What one performs should be done without any doubt entering into the work, nor any hesitation, so that the action of the *rational soul* <note>The "rational soul" (al-nafs al-natiqa) refers to the highest part of the human psyche in Aristotelian-Islamic philosophy, responsible for intellect and will.</note> may be strengthened by it. ->### The Lunar Mansions<-

**The first of them is the mansion of al-Sharatan** <note>The "Two Signs," identified with the horns of Aries (Beta and Gamma Arietis).</note> (and it extends from the beginning of Aries to twelve degrees, fifty-one minutes, and twenty-four seconds). The people of India, when the Moon is in this mansion, undertake travel and drink purgative medicine. Therefore, make it a rule for a traveler to have a <term>talisman</term> <note>original: "tilsam"; an object inscribed with astrological symbols to harness celestial power.</note> made for them here for safety during their journey. Also made in it is a talisman for the escape of a slave, or for binding a slave for whomever you wish and intend, and for ruining a partnership between partners, because it is of a **fiery, inauspicious nature**. And I mention a rule for you: always ensure in works of good that the Moon is safe and free from <term>malefics</term> <note>original: "al-nuhus"; refers to the influence of planets like Mars or Saturn.</note> and combustion <note>Combustion occurs when a planet is too close to the Sun, weakening its power.</note>. ->### The Second is the mansion of al-Butayn<-

Also made in it is the corruption of marriage between those intended before they unite, and talismans for arousal, because it is of a **fiery, auspicious nature**. It is also used for the escape of a slave, and for tightening the bonds of a prisoner if one intends to harm him.

->### The Third is the mansion of al-Thurayya<-

(And it extends from twenty-five degrees, forty-two minutes, and twelve seconds of Aries, to eight degrees, thirty-four minutes, and two seconds of Taurus). In it is made a talisman for one who travels by water, for he shall be saved. And a talisman suitable for ruining a partnership; and a talisman for loosening the bonds of a prisoner and his release. In it is made a talisman for the successful pursuit of <term>alchemy</term> <note>original: "al-kimiya"; here referring to the practical and spiritual art of transformation involving fire.</note> and the service of fire. And a talisman for land hunting. And a talisman for affection between a married couple. And a talisman for the ruin of sheep, cattle, and slaves against their owner so that they do not remain in his possession, because it is of a **mixed, auspicious nature**; so know this.

->### The Fourth is the mansion of al-Dabaran<-

(And it extends from eight degrees, thirty-four minutes, and two seconds of Taurus, to twenty-one degrees, twenty-five minutes, and forty-four seconds of Taurus). In it is made a talisman for the ruin of a city's condition. And a talisman for a building whose survival is not hoped for and whose condition will not be good. And a talisman for the destruction of crops. And a talisman for the protection of slaves for their owner. And a talisman for ruining the relationship between a married couple and casting separation between them. And talismans for the fear of misfortune for those who dig wells and seek buried treasures. And for the destruction of whomever you wish, and for the binding of snakes and scorpions.
