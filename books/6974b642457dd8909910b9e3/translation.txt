### Georgius Benignus de Salviatis, Archbishop of Nazareth, to the reader.

How truthful it is, honest reader, and of what great authority is this work by Pietro Galatino—a man of profound learning and master of four languages <note>Latin, Greek, Hebrew, and Aramaic.</note>—concerning the secrets of Catholic truth. This work, excerpted from Hebrew books with marvelous talent and not without divine inspiration, is not only proven by the fact that certain Most Reverend Lords, the Cardinals—men outstanding in divine and human wisdom—and several other Prelates of the Church, most excellent in every kind of literature, have praised it with wonderful accolades in my presence when the work was sent to them for examination. Indeed, I myself, when I had seen and read it all, praised it most highly as something that would be of great benefit to the Christian Commonwealth. But it is also proven by this: that the Hebrews themselves, compelled by the truth alone, have published beautiful poems in the Hebrew language in praise of the same work and its author. This is the greatest argument for the secrets of Catholic truth against them; for in proving the truth, nothing is more effective than the testimony of one's adversaries. ->Farewell.<-

### ¶ Maximilian, by the favor of divine mercy, Emperor of the Romans, forever august. To the honorable and religious Petrus Galatinus, of the Order of Friars Minor <note>The Franciscans.</note>, Professor of Sacred Theology.

<image-desc>Decorative woodcut initial 'H' featuring white interlaced knotwork on a dark background within a square frame.</image-desc>

Honorable, religious, and beloved to us in devotion: We have learned from the letters of our solicitor, Stephanus Rosinus, that you have embraced with all zeal the defense of the cause of our counselor, Johannes Capnio <note>original: "Iohannis Capnionis." Capnio is the Greek-derived name for Johannes Reuchlin (1455–1522), a famous humanist scholar who was then under fire for defending Jewish books against destruction.</note>. We have also learned that you intend to demonstrate through a singular work that nothing he asserted was reckless or scandalous—a fact that has been most pleasing to us above all things. We have heard of your great erudition in the three languages <note>Latin, Greek, and Hebrew—the "trilingual" ideal of the Renaissance scholar.</note>, joined with a singular integrity of mind and religious devotion. Therefore, we foresee that his reputation—which is currently being sharply attacked by the biting words and tearing of rivals—will be safe. Thus, we urge you, out of regard for us, to publish more quickly this work of yours, which is already expected with open hands by many. In the meantime, continue to defend Capnio with the zeal you have begun. In this, you will not only employ the labors of your talent most excellently, but you will also perform a most grateful service for us, which we in turn will acknowledge with all our mercy toward you. Given in our town of Innsbruck, on the Calends of September <note>September 1st.</note>, in the year of our Lord 1515, and the 30th year of our Roman reign.

### ¶ To the Most Invincible Maximilian Caesar, Emperor of the Romans, forever august, a letter from Petrus Galatinus of the Order of Friars Minor.

<image-desc>Decorative woodcut initial 'N' featuring white interlaced knotwork on a dark background within a square frame.</image-desc>

**NOT** long ago, I was distressed with no small anxiety of mind, O **MAXIMILIAN** August Caesar, as I saw the manifold and extraordinary learning of Capnio being so unworthily torn apart by the pestilential and poisonous habits of envious men. I saw that some even dared to falsely slander many of his sayings—which are most consistent with the orthodox faith—with the mark of heresy. And although I noticed this was being done as a bad, or rather the worst, example against all learned men (should such unjust gratitude be repaid after so many exhausted labors and vigils), I nevertheless did not dare to write anything, nor even to open my mouth, to attempt to defend a man so learned and outstanding in every kind of virtue against the reckless ignorance of the wicked. I feared that the fault of recklessness and arrogance might be attributed to me if I—endowed with barely mediocre talent and not equipped with enough learning—should wish to voluntarily and uninvited act as an advocate for a man so skilled and excellent in the triple learning of languages, who is therefore suited beyond what can be said to defend himself along with the truth itself. But truly, since my most Reverend Lord, Lord L. Pucci <note>Lorenzo Pucci (1458–1531), an Italian cardinal who protected Galatinus and Reuchlin.</note>...

<meta>This page continues the introductory front matter of Pietro Galatino’s "On the Secrets of Catholic Truth." Having received the Emperor’s endorsement (shown on the previous page), Galatino now formally responds, explaining the high-level support he has received from the Papal court and his motivations for using Jewish texts like the Talmud to defend the scholar Johannes Reuchlin.</meta>

The Cardinal of the Four Crowned Saints <note>Lorenzo Pucci (1458–1531), a powerful cardinal and protector of the Reuchlin cause at the Roman Curia.</note>, a most keen investigator of the Christian faith and (as is right) most observant of your Majesty, had urged me all the more sharply to undertake a defense of this kind with a spirited mind. He did so because he believed your Majesty’s own honor was at stake if even the slightest suspicion of the crime charged against your counselor <note>Johannes Reuchlin (Capnio).</note> were to touch him. This forced me to take up a heavy burden, a task unequal to my shoulders. 

Added to this was the will of the Most Holy Pope LEO X, which moved me toward this goal above all else. For I was well aware that the Pope himself—as a singular cultivator of truth and wisdom—greatly desired both that Reuchlin be freed from these slanders and that your Majesty's wishes be honored in this matter, as he hoped your prayers would be most vehemently satisfied. 

While I was uniquely intent on this matter—sweating with all my strength to draw out the secrets found in the Talmud <note>Galatino believed the Talmud contained hidden "secrets" (arcana) that, if interpreted correctly, proved the truth of the Christian faith.</note>, which contribute in a wonderful way both to the confirmation of the Catholic faith and to the protection of Reuchlin's cause—your Majesty’s letters suddenly arrived. They most kindly urged me to finish this work as quickly as possible. Along with them came several other letters written most graciously in my commendation to the Pope, the Catholic King <note>Charles I of Spain, the future Emperor Charles V.</note>, and several Cardinals, the fathers of the Christian aristocracy. 

Moved by this extraordinary and almost incredible kindness, I began not only to press my ongoing nighttime labors <note>original: "vigilias." These were the "watches" or late-night study sessions common to dedicated scholars.</note> more urgently and ardently so they might hurry to a conclusion, but I also decided to consecrate whatever the results might be to your divine power <note>original: "tuo numini." A high-flown, classical way of addressing the Emperor's authority.</note>. I do this so that they may come forth into the light under better omens and, having shaken off the darkness of errors, may reveal the light of truth much more confidently. 

Indeed, it was not fitting that a work concerning the secrets of Catholic truth, completed for the benefit of the Christian commonwealth, should be dedicated to anyone other than the best preserver, defender, and promoter of both that Catholic truth and the entire Christian state. 

If this dedication of mine does not quite match the height of your Majesty’s Highness, yet, because you will be able to understand that it comes from a most devoted soul, you will deign to consider it well and receive it with a calm face (as you have always been accustomed to do). Receive it now, therefore, most Sacred Emperor; and as I labor unceasingly in the vineyard of the Lord <note>A common metaphor for the Church and theological scholarship.</note>, receive, protect, and cherish me among the least of your Majesty’s servants (though I am unworthy) according to your clemency.
