## Description of the Temple of the South Sea God

The **Temple of the South Sea God** is located twenty <term>li<note>A traditional Chinese unit of distance, approximately 500 meters or one-third of a mile.</note></term> south of the Guangzhou provincial capital at the mouth of the sea. The temple halls and pavilions are grand and magnificent. A stone stele with an inscription by **Han Changli** <note>original: 韓昌黎. Also known as Han Yu (768–824), a famous Tang Dynasty poet and official whose "Inscription for the Temple of the South Sea God" is a landmark of classical prose.</note> is preserved there.

There is a very high peak upon which sits a pavilion called **"Sun Bathing"** <note>original: 浴日 (Yuri). So named because it offered a spectacular view of the sun rising out of the ocean.</note>. From this vantage point, one looks out upon the vast, boundless ocean. In the distance, **Mount Ya** <note>original: 崖山 (Yashan). The site of a famous historical naval battle.</note> appears like a patch of floating foam. Within the shimmering light of the sky, one can spot dark specks the size of small beans; as they ride the wind and approach the temple's base, they reveal themselves to be massive ships of <term>ten thousand hu<note>A traditional unit of dry measure; "ten thousand hu" was a common literary way to describe a ship of enormous cargo capacity.</note></term>.

Outside the central gate stands a statue of **Bodhidharma** <note>original: 達磨 (Damo). The Indian monk who founded Chan (Zen) Buddhism in China.</note> that is exceptionally imposing. His clothing and headgear are all fashioned according to the regulations of the Tang Dynasty. In the courtyard, there is a tree with a verdant trunk and lush leaves whose species is unknown. Local tradition holds that Bodhidharma himself brought it from the **Western Regions** <note>A historical term referring to Central Asia or the Indian subcontinent.</note>.

Within the temple are several bronze drums belonging to **Ma Yuan** <note>14 BC – 49 AD. A celebrated Han Dynasty general known as the "General who Calms the Waves." Bronze drums are frequently found in Southern China and associated with his military campaigns.</note>. The largest of these has a diameter of four or five feet, while the smaller ones are two feet across.

### The Seven Star Crags

The **Seven Star Crags** are located to the north of the prefectural city of Zhaoqing. Its peaks are jagged <note>original: 嶙峋 (lín xún); describing the rugged, bony appearance of the limestone.</note> and lush with vegetation. Among them, one peak opens wide to reveal a valley of caves. This space is hollow, bright, and vast, capable of holding several hundred people. The fine stones form a vaulted dome above, while clear springs wind around the base like ribbons; it is truly a magnificent sight of this region. Outside the crags, several pavilions have been constructed, with a central plaque reading **"Overlooking the Ravine."** Stone steps have been laid out to serve as a ladder, leading the visitor directly to the site of the crags.

Inside the caves, a platform has been built, three feet high and covering an area of several <term>zhang<note>A traditional Chinese unit of length; one zhang is approximately 3.3 meters or 11 feet.</note></term>.
