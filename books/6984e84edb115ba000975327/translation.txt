# P R O L O G U E .

We have no doubt that as soon as theologians cast their eyes upon the title and name of this <term>little book</term> <note>Latin: *libelli*</note>, they will not be kindly or fairly disposed toward its author; indeed, for some, their bile will be stirred. For their minds have become so hardened and secure in their preconceived opinions—or those received from their teachers—that they think their views are entirely in harmony with the truth and should be held by everyone as if they were divine Oracles. Therefore, they take it very badly and are greatly indignant if anyone dares to think differently from them, to profess it openly, or to enter the arena to challenge their views.

But such is the happiness of the age we live in that we need care little for the anger and indignation of these men. If they are of such a weak mind as to allow themselves to be carried away, they can do nothing else than slander the author's name among the common folk and the unlearned rabble to make him hated, should his identity become known. To keep ourselves safe and sound from this trouble, we have thought it wise to step onto the stage <term>wearing a mask</term> <note>The author is explaining their choice to publish anonymously to avoid personal persecution.</note>.

And although by this means not every opportunity for slander is taken away from them—indeed, it gives them a handle to attack us with insults, calling us a <term>shunner of light</term> <note>Latin: *Lucifugæ*</note> or a <term>dweller in shadows</term> <note>Latin: *Tenebrionis*</note>, as if we were someone condemned by our own conscience who dared not reveal our name because we could expect no honor from this writing nor any praise from the pious. ...[not so that we might] capture a little glory, since we have devoted our labor in this work solely to Truth. From this, we are more than sufficiently conscious that if our intention had been to pursue good fame and reputation, we would undoubtedly have obtained it—if not among those [critics] and the common folk who depend on their every nod, then at least among the wise, who care for the truth at heart, however paradoxical it may be (and whom alone we judge worthy of the name of the truly pious). But that we have set as our goal in this treatise not the pursuit of vain glory, nor the hunting for human approval, but the propagation of truth, the benefit of our neighbor, and the reconciliation of a divided and disagreeing Christianity, will be clearer than the noonday sun to any candid reader who does not intend to interpret our efforts perversely, both from the whole context of the work and from what we are about to say.

...seemed established. For just as all the philosophers who once existed can be most conveniently divided into two classes—the first being the **Dogmatists**, who claimed to teach and hand down to their disciples a certain and indubitable knowledge of things drawn from true principles; and the second being the **Skeptics**, who, on the contrary, calling everything into doubt, pronounced that nothing could be known for certain—so also in this century, two similar kinds of **Theologians** are found, where one corresponds to the former group and the other to the latter. 

For the greatest part of them boldly proclaim that they possess a true and evident understanding of theological matters drawn from the **Holy Scriptures**, and they preach that they are teaching this to others; whereas others, on the contrary, ingenuously confess that if not all, then at least as many as possible of their views concerning the Christian religion are only *probably* sought and deduced from the Scriptures. Therefore, they submit these views, as if they were uncertain and doubtful, to the better and healthier judgment of others, speaking, as they say, *under correction* <note>original: "sub correctione" — a humble scholarly formula indicating the author is open to being corrected by higher authorities.</note>, as if proposing them to be amended by those others.

And just as in ancient times the whole class of **Dogmatic** philosophers did not unite into one body, but split into diverse, disagreeing, and clashing opinions and sects—such as the **Pythagoreans**, **Platonists**, **Epicureans**, **Stoics**, **Peripatetics** <note>These represent the major schools of Greek philosophy: followers of Pythagoras (mathematics/mysticism), Plato (ideal forms), Epicurus (atomism/pleasure), the Porch/Zeno (duty/logic), and Aristotle (observation/logic).</note>, etc.—similarly in our own time, the Dogmatic theologians differ much among themselves regarding the main points of the Christian religion. They have forged, and even now cherish, various and contrary opinions, and this with such heat of spirit and fervor that the Christian world is, as it were, torn and distracted into different factions and diverse Churches. Its inhabitants are not only discordant in spirit and customs, but some have become mortal enemies—such as there are in Europe (to say nothing of those in Asia): **Catholics** <note>original: "Pontificii" — literally "those following the Pontiff/Pope."</note>, **Reformed**, **Lutherans**, **Anabaptists**, **Socinians** <note>A group that rejected the Trinity and emphasized rational interpretation of the Bible.</note>, **Arminians** <note>Followers of Jacobus Arminius who argued against strict predestination in favor of free will.</note>, and others of that kind. 

And finally, just as those ancient philosophers did, so also do these theologians; although any given sect of either side boasts and commends itself above the others, and [claims to rest] upon unshakable truth...

-> # P R O L O G U E . <-

...glory and promise that they possess a well-founded knowledge and teach it; if not all, they nevertheless profess as many things as possible, and thrust upon their followers dogmas which either are not built upon the firm and stable foundation of Truth, or, if perhaps they are, they are by no means able to demonstrate or certainly show them to be so.

That this is true of **Theologians** (to say nothing of **Philosophers**, since they do not pertain so much to the plan of our undertaking) will be clear to anyone who wishes to look more deeply with us into their **Method** and way of demonstrating. For when some controversy arises among them concerning the establishment of a dogma of **Faith** and **Morals**, they immediately—as befits men of such a profession—betake themselves and flee to the **Holy Scriptures**, the infallible Word of God, as if to a "sacred anchor" <note>original: "sacram anchoram" — a common classical and biblical metaphor (Hebrews 6:19) for a final refuge or ultimate security.</note>. From there they summon and pile up as many passages as possible to support their opinion, and they busy themselves showing that these passages mean what they contend; but almost always with such trivial little reasons and petty interpretations <note>original: "ratiunculis interpretatiunculisque" — the author uses these diminutive Latin forms to mock the perceived weakness and insignificance of his opponents' arguments.</note> that an adversary is soon able to refute them with the greatest of ease, either by adapting the cited passages to strengthen his own opinion or by twisting them into another sense. Then another person, equipped with the same weapons, later evades and turns aside the force and strength of that argument with equal facility: so much so that everyone fights, but no one conquers or carries off a victory over the Enemy.

When I observed that **Theology**, the queen of the sciences <note>original: "disciplinarum principe" — literally "the leader of disciplines," reflecting the traditional view of theology as the highest field of academic study.</note>, was in such a wretched state, and that its Doctors, practitioners, and students were reduced to such straits—and that I myself, along with them, was immersed in such waves of uncertainty and doubt—I began to turn over in my mind whether the source of these evils could be uncovered, and whether some hope of emerging from them might shine forth, or a way of escape might be found anywhere. While I was fixing all my thought on investigating these things, there suddenly occurred to me that most successful and excellent **Method** by which that most noble and incomparable man, **René Descartes**,
