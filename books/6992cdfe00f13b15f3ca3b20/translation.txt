Now, if we use the character for "One" and the character for "Cross" as metaphors, the principle will become clear. <note>The author is using the shapes of Chinese characters—the horizontal line "一" (*yī*) and the intersecting strokes of "乂" (*yì*)—to explain the geometry of combat.</note>

If an opponent thrusts at my face, their staff follows this diagonal line: ＼. I then lower my rear hand slightly so that the head of my staff is raised, forming this diagonal line: ／. When these two "One" lines cross each other, they form the character "Cross": 乂.

If the opponent thrusts at my midsection <term>zhongping (中平): the "middle-level" or horizontal thrust, often aimed at the solar plexus</term>, their staff follows this straight line: 一. I again lower my rear hand slightly to create this diagonal line: ／. These two lines crossing also form the character "Cross": 乂.

If the opponent thrusts at my feet, their staff follows this downward diagonal line: ／. I raise my rear hand slightly so the head of my staff points downward, creating this diagonal line: ＼. These two lines crossing also form the character "Cross": 乂.

Regardless of whether you use the methods of **Seizing**, **Intercepting**, **Chopping**, **Catching**, **Hooking**, or **Lifting**, within every stance and every movement, the intersection of the opponent’s attack and my own technique should always form this "Cross" 乂. How could there ever be a moment where you strike the air and fail to connect? <note>The "Cross" represents the "sweet spot" of a parry where the defender's staff successfully meets and redirects the attacker's weapon.</note>

->The **Lower Insertion Stance** <term>Lower Insertion Stance: *Xià chā shì*; a low-profile posture designed to dive beneath an opponent's guard or neutralize high kicks.</term> is specifically designed to subdue those with "fast legs."<-
->Once you have gained ground, execute the **Stir-and-Lean** <note>original: 攪靠, *jiǎo kào*; a grappling maneuver involving the entangling of an opponent's limb followed by a forceful shoulder or torso strike.</note> without hesitation.<-
->Hook the foot and lock the arm, refusing to let them break away;<-
->distract them with a feint above to strike below—a single throw ends the exchange.<-

->The **Ambush Stance** <term>Ambush Stance: *Máifú shì*; a coiled, deceptive posture where the practitioner drops low to the ground, hiding their reach and intentions.</term> is like a drawn bow waiting for a tiger <note>original: 窩弓待虎, *wō gōng dài hǔ*; a metaphor for a spring-loaded trap used by hunters for dangerous prey.</note>.<-
->Should the opponent fall into this trap, they will find it impossible to move even an inch.<-
->Seize the opportunity to launch a rapid succession of kicks;<-
->once struck, the opponent will surely be dazed and in grave danger.<-

->In the **Throwing the Frame** <term>Throwing the Frame: *Pāo jiàzi*; a deceptive opening maneuver where the practitioner "throws" their posture forward to break the opponent’s rhythm.</term> technique, use charging steps and the **Spreading Hanger** <note>original: 披掛, *pīguà*; a long-range, swinging arm strike intended to bypass a guard.</note> followed immediately by a supplemental kick.<-
->Even if the opponent meets you with a firm right-hand horizontal strike, pluck it away with your left as fast as if you were in flight.<-
->When you prop them up with a single palm strike, they will be left completely disoriented, "not knowing heaven from earth" <note>original: 不知天地, *bùzhī tiāndì*; an idiom describing a state of total confusion or being overwhelmed by a superior force.</note>.<-

->The **Pinching the Elbow Stance** <term>Pinching the Elbow Stance: *Niān zhǒu shì*; a specialized grappling posture used to seize and manipulate the opponent's elbow joint.</term> is used to defend against their leg maneuvers.<-
->I intercept their movement with "short" power, making sure to distinguish between high and low threats.<-
->Chopping, striking, pushing, and pressing must all follow these core principles;<-
->above all, ensure that your hands and feet never become hurried or frantic.<-
