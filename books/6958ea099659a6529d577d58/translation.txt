### CONCERNING THE ILLYRIAN IRIS

The Illyrian Iris has leaves similar to the *silphium* plant <note>a medicinal plant, possibly extinct, highly prized in antiquity</note> but larger, broader, and thicker. It also has flowers on the upper part of various colors; that is, white or honey-colored or purple or a fine red. It has roots underneath that are knotty and curved, full of a good scent. These roots should be gathered and threaded onto a linen cord, and thus hung up to dry. However, the best Irises are those from Macedonia or Illyria, which are especially knotty, shorter, and reddish in color.

### II. CONCERNING ACORUS.

**A**corus <note>Sweet Flag (Acorus calamus)</note> has leaves similar to an iris, but narrow, and the roots are different; they are twisted and not straight, but jointed and knotty. They are whitish underneath, with a pungent bite and a sweet odor. However, the better kind is white and hard, not scorched, but sturdy on the outside.
