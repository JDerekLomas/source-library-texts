### The System for Establishing a Level

Once the four cardinal directions have been correctly determined, and according to their designated positions, erect a **sighting post** <term>Biao (表): A vertical pole used as a reference point for visual alignment or measuring shadows.</term> at each of the four corners.

In the center, place the **leveling board** <term>Shuiping (水平): A horizontal wooden crossbeam used in conjunction with water to determine a level plane.</term>. This leveling board is two *chi* four *cun* long, two *cun* five *fen* wide, and two *cun* high. <note>In the Song Dynasty, 1 *chi* was approximately 31.2 cm (12.3 inches). It was divided into 10 *cun*, and each *cun* into 10 *fen*.</note>

Below the board, install a **supporting post** <term>Chun (椿): A vertical stake that holds the leveling board at a working height.</term> that is four *chi* long <gloss>this includes the length of the iron spike fixed at the bottom for stability</gloss>. The leveling board sits horizontally upon the top of this post. At both ends of the board’s top surface, carve out a **square reservoir** <term>Chi (池): Literally "pool"; these reservoirs hold the water that establishes the level.</term> measuring one *cun* seven *fen* square and one *cun* three *fen* deep. <gloss>In some cases, an additional reservoir is carved in the center with the same dimensions.</gloss>

Inside the body of the board, carve a **channel** <term>Caozi (槽子): A narrow groove connecting the reservoirs.</term> five *fen* wide and five *fen* deep to allow water to flow freely between the reservoirs at each end. 

Within each reservoir, place one **water-float** <term>Shuifuzi (水浮子): Lightweight blocks that float on the water's surface to provide a precise sighting line.</term>. <gloss>If three reservoirs are used, then three water-floats may also be used.</gloss> Each float is one *cun* five *fen* square and one *cun* two *fen* high. Carve the top of the float so that the edges are thin—only one *fen* in thickness. While these floats are buoyant within the reservoirs, one sights across the tops of the two floats toward the distant **sighting posts**. Where the line of sight meets the post, draw a mark on the body of the post.

<note>This device functions on the principle of communicating vessels; the water level in the connected reservoirs will always be perfectly horizontal. By sighting across the tops of the floats, the builder can project this level line over a distance to ensure the entire foundation is uniform.</note>

# By Imperial Decree: The Complete Library of the Four Treasuries <note>original: 欽定四庫全書 (*Qinding Siku Quanshu*). This was a massive collection of Chinese literature commissioned by the Qianlong Emperor in the 18th century.</note>

<meta>Catchword: Supplement</meta>

The text consists of 3,555 articles in total. Among these, 49 chapters comprising 283 articles were compiled through the investigation and research of the **Classics and Histories** <note>original: 經史 (*Jingshi*). These refer to the foundational Confucian texts and official dynastic records.</note> and various other books. In some cases, the designs and regulations <term>regulations: *zhidu*, the formal rules governing dimensions and style in state architecture.</term> were found to align with the Classics and their Commentaries; in other cases, it was discovered that a single object was known by several different names. Beyond the sections already detailed and established in the preceding text, the remaining 308 chapters, containing 3,272 articles, consist of methods that have been passed down through generations of professional practice. 

These are durable methods that can be put into practical use. They have been thoroughly discussed, and their rules and standards compared, with experienced **craftsmen** <note>original: 工匠 (*gongjiang*)</note> from various trades and guilds. By comparing the advantages and disadvantages of different construction methods, we have established rules for increasing or decreasing specifications based on the size and scale of the object.

<gloss>For instance, in the regulations for solid-board doors, one foot of height is used as the base unit, which can be scaled up to twenty-four feet.
