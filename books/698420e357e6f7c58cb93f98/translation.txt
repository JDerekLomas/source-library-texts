# Chapter 1.
## That it is useful for every person
### to consider upon what the world stands, so that
### it does not fall.

Although this visible world, composed of heaven and earth, stands in no physical place according to its outward part (for it floats within itself in the nothingness of the expanse or the infinite depth), nonetheless, locations within the world are considered here and there—wherever every land, city, or person lies, stands, and walks. And because man is made from the <term>clod of earth: a literal translation of the German 'Erdenkloß,' referring to the biblical creation of Adam from the dust of the ground</term> of this world, and is placed in the midst of the world to dwell therein until his appointed time, it is very fitting for him to look upon and consider his location or fatherland where he has his home during this time. 

This consideration of the mortal body serves to remind him that he has no permanent city <note>original: "bleibende Statt," a reference to the New Testament verse Hebrews 13:14: "For here we have no continuing city, but we seek one to come."</note> in this world; he must soon depart and leave this world behind, for he belongs to another fatherland which is eternal in heaven, for which he was originally created. Man is composed of two parts—so that he might be and remain not two, but one single person—of flesh and spirit, of body and soul. According to the body, he requires in this time a place or position, be it on land or on sea; he must be in a specific place and cannot do without a location for his stay. But according to the spirit, he requires no place; for the spirit occupies no physical city, takes up no space, and does not allow itself to be enclosed or locked into any location. He who considers and recognizes this well strives to walk according to the spirit in Christ.
