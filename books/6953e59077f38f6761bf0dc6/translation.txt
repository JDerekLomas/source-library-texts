->This book is divided into five parts,<-
->of which<-

The First *contains the Description of the* **OSCILLATING CLOCK.** <note>original: "Horologium Oscillatorium." This refers to the pendulum clock, which Huygens invented in 1656.</note>

The Second *deals with the* Fall of heavy bodies, *and* *their motion in a* Cycloid. <note>A cycloid is the curve traced by a point on the rim of a circular wheel as it rolls along a straight line; Huygens discovered it was the ideal path for a pendulum.</note>

The Third *concerns the* Evolution *and* *Measurement of curved lines.* <note>Huygens developed the theory of "evolutes" to ensure the pendulum followed the necessary cycloidal path.</note>

The Fourth *concerns the* Center of Oscillation or Agitation. <note>This section addresses the mathematical point in a pendulum where its entire mass can be considered concentrated.</note>

The Fifth *presents the construction of another Clock, in which the motion of the pendulum is circular, and* Theorems *on* Centrifugal Force. <note>original: "Vi Centrifuga." This is the first formal mathematical treatment of the force that acts on an object moving in a circular path.</note>

# TO LOUIS XIV,

## OF FRANCE AND NAVARRE

### ILLUSTRIOUS KING.

WE owe the rebirth and restoration of Geometry in this century, greatest King, chiefly to France. For from here arose those who first restored it—when it was lost in its greatest and better part and, as it were, buried—and brought it back into the light. Following in their footsteps, men of the most subtle intellect throughout all Europe have since so cultivated it, that now few things seem left by them for the industry of posterity; indeed, they have far surpassed the discoveries of the ancients. In this science, which I have always admired and loved most dearly, whenever I have applied my mind to it, I have set before myself for investigation those things above all others which, once discovered, might prove useful either for the conveniences of life or for the knowledge of Nature. Then, truly, I considered my labor best spent when I happened upon those matters in which utility was joined with the difficulty of discovery and some degree of subtlety...

Indeed, there is no need for me to labor at length, most powerful King <note>original: "Rex potentissime"</note>, to show you the utility in these things. For you have not only discovered through long experience, since our Automata <note>self-moving machines, specifically pendulum clocks</note> deserved to be received into the inner chambers of your palace, how much they excel other machines of this kind in the steady demonstration of the hours: but you are also aware of their more important uses, those for which they were destined by me from the very beginning. Namely, those uses which they are fit to provide both in Celestial observations and in measuring the Longitudes of places while navigating. For by your command, our Clocks have been carried across the sea more than once. Under your auspices, many of these same clocks, dedicated to the uses of Astronomy, are seen in that famous citadel of Urania <note>The Paris Observatory, named for the Muse of Astronomy</note>, which you recently cared to have built with such remarkable magnificence, and on a scale greater than any king before.
