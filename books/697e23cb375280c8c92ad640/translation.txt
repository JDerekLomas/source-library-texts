# Light and Heat System
## in Nature.

Nature is an active whole of causes, effects, and consequences, or—what amounts to the same thing—of forces, organs, and forms.

Everywhere there is something that acts, or the <term>stimulating force <note>original: "reizende Kraft". In the late 18th and early 19th centuries, "stimulus" or "irritation" was a foundational concept used to explain how life responds to the environment.</note></term>; something that is acted upon, or the receptive matter, and a medium between both, through which the interaction occurs.

Nature can thus be viewed as a continuous influence that constantly stimulates the <term>receptivity to stimuli <note>original: "Reizempfänglichkeit". In the late 18th century, this term described the capacity of matter—both living and non-living—to react to external forces.</note></term> in matter. As has been stated, wherever there is life and movement, there is a "something" that acts and a "something" that is acted upon—an active and a passive being.

All existing things can therefore only be viewed in three ways, because they only exist in three ways:

1) In their forces, or stimulus-generating principles.
2) In their substances, or <term>excitabilities <note>original: "Erregbarkeiten". This refers to the inherent quality of a substance that allows it to be moved or changed by an external force.</note></term>.
3) In their <term>influences <note>original: "Einwirkungen". This describes the actualized effects or the interaction between a force and a substance.</note></term>.

If we dissect a body in descending order, we find in the absolute innermost part—or in the <term>counterpoint of the extension <note>original: "Kontrapunkt des Extensums". This uses musical and philosophical terminology (Spinoza’s *extensio*) to describe the material world's most concentrated physical point.</note></term>—the highest, most fixed being known to us as **carbon** *).

The most volatile is therefore **nitrogen** <note>original: "Stickgas". Literally "choking gas," a common historical term for nitrogen.</note>, the principle that generates stimuli.

The most fixed is **carbon**, the being susceptible to <term>excitability <note>original: "Erregbarkeit". A central concept in late 18th-century "Brownian" medicine, referring to a living tissue's ability to react to external stimuli.</note></term>.

**Oxygen** <note>original: "Lebensluft," literally "life-air."</note> is the medium in which the interaction of the stimulus and the receptivity of the matter occurs, and which produces all physical phenomena.

### Observation.

The effect of nitrogen on carbon extracts a very fine, **soapy** being, and this soapy being is actual **light matter**.
