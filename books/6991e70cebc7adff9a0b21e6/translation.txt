# ON THE WEIGHT OF BODIES #

<margin>The center of gravity</margin>
The center of every weight shall be found upon the line that divides it into two equal parts. <note>Leonardo is discussing the fundamental principle of equilibrium: for an object to be balanced, its center of mass must align with its point of support.</note>

### Of Local Motion ###

If a power moves a body for a certain time over a certain distance, the same power will move half of that body in the same time over twice the distance. <insert>This is always true if the resistance of the air does not interfere.</insert>

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| 1 unit | 0.5 unit | 2 units | 1 unit |
| 2 units | 1 unit | 2 units | 1 unit |

<gloss>The movement is swifter where the resistance is less.</gloss>

Every heavy body desires to fall toward the center of the world by the shortest possible path. <note>original: "Ogni peso desidera cadere al centro del mondo." In the Renaissance, the "center of the world" was understood to be the center of the Earth.</note>

# On the Nature of Sound and its Velocity

Every motion that a voice <note>original: "uoce"; Leonardo uses "voice" here to refer to any sound or acoustic vibration.</note> makes through the air is of one same equality of velocity.

---

Every voice that spreads through the air shall be of as much greater velocity as it is of greater force, and of as much less as it is of lesser force; and this voice is caused by the percussion <note>The physical striking or compression of a medium.</note> of the air, so that the air struck shall be of as much velocity through the percussion as has been stated; and while the voice spreads through the air, it loses its force. <note>Leonardo is grappling with the distinction between the speed of sound (which is constant) and the force/intensity of the sound (which diminishes over distance).</note>

---

The whole quantity of the voice shall be in every part of its space, and all of it in every part of that space, just like the vision of seeing. <note>Leonardo compares the propagation of sound to light (sight), noting that a sound or an image can be perceived in its entirety from any point within the field of its expansion.</note>
