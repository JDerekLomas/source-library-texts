<margin>1. Year 1 of the war (535/6 AD)</margin>
<margin>27</margin>
He [the Emperor Justinian] ordered Belisarius to proceed to Italy with all haste and to treat the Goths as enemies. Constantianus, meanwhile, arrived at Epidamnus <note>modern-day Durrës, Albania</note> and, after spending
<margin>B 37</margin>
<margin>5</margin> some time there, gathered an army. During this time the Goths, led by Gripas, arrived in Dalmatia with another army and occupied Salona <note>the capital of the Roman province of Dalmatia, near modern Split</note>.
<margin>28</margin>
Constantianus, when his preparations were at their best, set out from Epidamnus with his entire fleet and anchored at Epidaurus <note>modern-day Cavtat, Croatia</note>, which is on the right as one sails into the Ionian Gulf <note>In antiquity, this term often included the Adriatic Sea</note>. 
<margin>10</margin> There, it happened that men whom Gripas had sent on a scouting mission were present.
<margin>29</margin>
As they observed the ships and Constantianus's encampment, it seemed to them that the sea and all the land were full of soldiers.
<margin>15</margin> Returning to Gripas, they insisted that Constantianus was bringing with him many tens of thousands <term>myriads</term> <note>original: μυριάδας; while literally 10,000, it often implies a vast, uncountable number</note> of men. 
<margin>30</margin>
He [Gripas], falling into a great fear, thought it disadvantageous to meet the attackers, and he had no desire to be besieged by the Emperor's army, especially since they held such command of the sea <term>thalassocracy</term> <note>original: θαλασσοκρατοῦντος; meaning "ruling the sea"</note>.
