### To Those Who Scorn

---

Even if my health and condition suffer no loss at all if someone holds or makes what I have written contemptible; nevertheless, to everyone whose mind this might not suit (as it is impossible to please all <term>complexions</term> <note>In 17th-century thought, "complexions" refers to a person's physical and psychological temperament, governed by the four humors.</note> at once with a single work), my friendly admonition is that he should not display his partisan mind against it. Rather, he should at least show <term>humanity</term> <note>original: *humanitæt*</note> and not hinder others who might view it more favorably, so that it might reach their hands without his opposition. This, to my knowledge, does him no harm, unless it be that he enjoys some easily disrupted private profit and fears that such gain might be hindered by this work.

But to the others, to whom this is not only pleasing but who also feel a joint witness in their conscience that it is the truth, I ask—through our dear God and Lord, who provides for all people in the entire world with equal love—that they would, with all the power of their understanding and ability, contemplate and strive for the means and ways by which love among men, one for another, and also for God, might be more greatly kindled. For at this time, love is almost entirely extinguished and has been transformed into an inhuman cruelty of one brother against another. 

In my estimation, the priests and teachers of all religions and sects, with none excepted, bear no small amount of blame for this. They keep the hearts of their listeners constantly <term>preoccupied</term> <note>original: *præoccupirt*; meaning biased or filled with prejudice beforehand</note> and filled with hatred and disgust toward others who are not of their <term>confession</term> <note>A "confession" is a formal statement of religious doctrine; the author is criticizing the rigid boundaries between denominations like Lutheran, Reformed, and Catholic.</note>. If this were not so, a better and more friendly spirit would sometimes prevail among men. 

Nature itself teaches almost all nations this, as they allow the crops, wares, and manufactures of their land to be shared with other nations, regardless of what religion they are. Likewise, they gladly accept and use the gifts of God that come to them from other countries. Indeed, many quarrelsome teachers themselves drink the wine and beverage of that very kingdom whose inhabitants they consider damned because of an "incorrect religion." Nor do they despise a people’s minted currency for their own needs, though they recognize those people as <term>heretics</term> <note>original: *Ketzer*</note>. Nevertheless, they are sometimes so fierce and embittered against those fellow human beings who seem heretical to them—whose country's wine, money, and cloth they themselves use—that they would not be unwilling to see them completely rooted out. This, however, is entirely against God. Those who wish to be born of Him must also be full of divine mercy toward friends and enemies alike; let those who love the truth strive to help spread this mercy.
