# TO THE SPECTATOR.

<image-desc>A large ornamental woodcut initial letter 'E' featuring intricate floral and scrollwork patterns within a square frame.</image-desc>

WE present to you through these many pages the GARDEN OF EICHSTÄTT <note>original: "HORTVM EYSTETTENSEM"</note>, constructed some years ago at the very roots of WILLIBALD’S CASTLE <note>The Willibaldsburg, the bishop's residence in Eichstätt</note> during a GOLDEN AGE, by royal daring, at the greatest expense, and with incomparable success, under the auspices of the Most Reverend and Illustrious PRINCE JOHANN KONRAD <note>Johann Konrad von Gemmingen, Prince-Bishop of Eichstätt (1593–1612), who commissioned the original garden and book</note>. But, such are human affairs: a STORM recently arriving from the NORTH <note>A reference to the Swedish intervention in the Thirty Years' War</note> has shaken off the flowers and overturned the disturbed beds along with their little plants; so that now, in the place where once the Gentlest Species grew, nothing else remains but the fierce and grim Face of BELLONA <note>The Roman goddess of war</note>. In one place, ramparts stand over threatening rocks; in another, cliffs have been cast down to defend the mounds, with lightning-bolts <note>A metaphor for cannons or artillery</note> peering through the slanting crags, and trunks and protruding stakes, and baskets trembling with rough gravel; and whatever else the Ingenuity of the modern Age has devised for the destruction of warring men.

However, lest all Memory of this Immortal Undertaking should fade for Posterity, the Most Reverend and Illustrious PRINCE AND LORD, LORD MARQUARD <note>Marquard II Schenk von Castell, Prince-Bishop of Eichstätt (1637–1685)</note>, Bishop of Eichstätt—whom a petitioned GOD has granted again for the Mitigating of the Calamities of these Times—has acted. By His Gift and Expense, you have these things which we have elaborated in these Second Labors <note>Referring to this new edition or printing</note> according to the vow and opinion of those who seemed to desire this work; of whom there were many in FRANCE, BELGIUM, and BRITAIN.

We have followed the order which the first examples led, except that we believe it unnecessary to divide this harvest into so many small bundles; nevertheless, they come forth in a Three-part Series, so that the First Class displays the Spring Species, the Second the Summer ones, and the Last those which can scarcely be ripened among Us as Autumn rushes headlong.

The descriptions—which some deemed insufficiently accurate, and others insufficiently clever or unable to satisfy the impatience of this Century—we have omitted; especially so that we might not sin by the example of ancient artists, who found it necessary to aid the simplicity of their art by adding Indexes in good faith, so that the People might be reminded, for instance, that it is an Ox being looked at, and not a Man.

As for the rest that could be added here: look, judge, and run your eyes over it, and Give Thanks to the MOST ILLUSTRIOUS PRINCE MARQUARD, whose Honors deserve to be Crowned with even more FLOWERS than those you shall Read of here.
