# Records of Military Preparation
<term>Wubizhi</term> <note>original: "武備志". This is the most comprehensive military encyclopedia in Chinese history. Compiled by the scholar-official Mao Yuanyi in 1621, it spans 240 volumes and covers everything from strategy and tactics to weapons technology and maritime charts.</note>

<note>The title slip shown on the cover serves as a formal introduction to this monumental work of the Ming dynasty, reflecting a period of intense military innovation and the need for organized defense strategies against domestic and foreign threats.</note>

## A Study of the Northern Barbarians
<term>Northern Barbarians</term> <note>original: "北虜" (Beilu). A historical term used during the Ming dynasty to refer to the various Mongol tribes and northern nomadic peoples.</note>

<insert>Collection of the National Central Library</insert>

**Master Mao** <note>Mao Yuanyi, the compiler of this encyclopedia.</note> says:

The tribes of the **Northern Barbarians** are scattered throughout the <term>Nine Frontiers</term> <note>original: "九邊" (Jiubian). These were the nine key military districts along the Great Wall established by the Ming to defend against northern incursions.</note>. I will no longer list the details of the era when they ruled over the **Central Plains** <note>original: "中國" (Zhongguo). Here referring to the period of the Mongol-led Yuan Dynasty (1271–1368).</note>. They have their "national language" and they have the **Han (Chinese)** language. What is called their "national language" is simply their local dialect.

Emperor **Taizu** <note>The founding emperor of the Ming dynasty, reigned 1368–1398.</note> established the <term>Bureau of the Four Barbarians</term> <note>original: "四夷館" (Siyi Guan). An imperial institution dedicated to translating diplomatic correspondence and training interpreters in foreign languages.</note> to translate the languages of the various border peoples. These were identified as the <term>Jurchen</term> <note>original: "女直" (Nüzhi). The Tungusic people from Northeast Asia who would later establish the Qing dynasty.</note>, the <term>Huihui</term> <note>original: "回回". A term referring to Muslims and people from Central Asia or Persia.</note>, and the **Yunnan** tribes...

While this usually refers to the Central Chinese province, in these catalogs it occasionally refers to specific southern maritime regions or remains an administrative anomaly.</note>, **Sumatra**, the **Uyghurs**, **Melaka**, **Siam** <note>Thailand.</note>, **Champa** <note>A kingdom in what is now central and southern Vietnam.</note>, **Hexi** <note>The Gansu Corridor.</note>, **Myanmar**, and the **Dada**. Other regions are not listed in full detail; however, because the affairs of the **Northern Barbarians** are the most urgent, they are specially noted here.

## A Study of the Jurchens

The Jurchens <note>original: "Nüzhi" (女直). In the Ming dynasty, this term referred to the various Tungusic-speaking tribes of Manchuria.</note> were the ancient Sushen people. They are located to the east of the Huntong River <note>Now known as the Songhua or Amur River.</note>. To their east, they border the sea; to their west, they connect with the Uriankhai <note>A Mongol-speaking group in the northeast.</note>; and to the south, they neighbor Korea <note>original: "Chaoxian" (朝鮮), referring to the Joseon Kingdom.</note>. 

During the Han dynasty, they were known as the Yilou. During the Northern Wei dynasty, they were known as the Wuji. During the Tang dynasty, they were known as the Black Water Mohe <note>A prominent tribal confederation in Manchuria.</note>. Their tribal name was the Nüzhen <note>original: 女真. This is the older form of the name "Jurchen."</note>, who were the ancestors of the Jin dynasty <note>The "Great Jin" (1115–1234) which conquered northern China.</note>. 

Later, to avoid a naming taboo of the Liao dynasty, they were called the Nüzhi <note>Naming taboos were a common Chinese and Inner Asian practice where certain characters were forbidden if they appeared in an Emperor's name. The "Zhen" in Nüzhen was changed because it was part of the personal name of Emperor Xingzong of Liao.</note>. The Yuan dynasty eventually destroyed the Jin...

In these lands, Military-Civilian Myriarchy Offices <term>Wanhu Fu (萬戶府); an administrative unit theoretically overseeing ten thousand households</term> were established. During our current dynasty <note>The Ming Dynasty (1368–1644).</note>, they are divided into several groups. 

Those residing in the Haixi region <note>The area of the Songhua River.</note> are called the **Haixi Jurchens**. Those residing in **Jianzhou**, **Maolian**, and other such places are called the **Jianzhou Jurchens**. Those in the extreme east, the most distant, are called the **Wild Jurchens** <note>original: "Yeren Nüzhi" (野人女直). These were the most northern and eastern tribes, often perceived as less "civilized" by the Ming court than their southern neighbors.</note>. 

There are also many smaller tribes. All are brave and fierce, skilled in archery, able to endure hunger and thirst, and fond of battle. The Jianzhou Jurchens occupy the central position and act as the preeminent leaders; their territory is of the highest strategic importance. These people understand farming and weaving; their dwellings, food, and drink possess a considerable degree of **Chinese style** <note>original: "Hua Feng" (華風). This indicates that the Jianzhou Jurchens had adopted many cultural and agricultural practices from the Ming, distinguishing them from the more nomadic or "wild" tribes.</note>.

In the first year of the **Yongle** era <note>1403 CE, during the reign of the Emperor Zhu Di.</note>, the chieftains of the Wild Jurchens came to the imperial court. Subsequently, the entire territory submitted to the Ming throne. Over time, there were established 184 **Guards** <term>Wei (衛); the primary Ming military administrative unit, usually composed of 5,600 men</term> such as Jianzhou, and 20 **Posts** <term>Suo (所); a subdivision of a Guard</term> such as **Wuzhe**. One **Regional Military Commission** <term>Dusi (都司)</term> was established, called **Nurgan**. 

The Ming commissioned their chieftains as Commanders-in-Chief, Regional Commanders, Chiliarchs, Centurions, and Pacification Officers, allowing them to lead their own tribes. Additionally, seven **Stations** and seven **Districts** <note>original: "Zhan" (站) and "Dimian" (地面). These were specialized administrative units for logistics and local management.</note> were created, which are not administered by the Guards and Posts. All of these groups were contracted to provide **tribute** <term>Chaogong (朝貢); a ritualized system of trade and diplomatic recognition where local goods were exchanged for imperial gifts</term> once a year.
