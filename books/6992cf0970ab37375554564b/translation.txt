# Coastal Guard Posts and Warships
<note>The Weisuo (Guard-Post) system was the military backbone of the Ming Dynasty, consisting of fortified garrisons where soldiers both farmed and trained for defense.</note>

Under the old regulations, during the "wind season" <note>original: 風汛 (Fengxun); referring to the seasonal monsoons when pirate raids were most frequent due to favorable sailing conditions</note> at the end of spring and the beginning of summer, officials from the prefectural, guard, and county levels—including those tasked with capturing outlaws and preparing for maritime incursions—would be deployed to sea. Their mission was to defend against Japanese pirates <note>original: 倭寇 (Wokou); maritime raiders of various ethnicities who plagued the Chinese coast</note> and foreign vessels.

Funding was drawn from the Provincial Administration Commission’s military pay to recruit "brave soldiers and laborers" from places such as Nantou, as well as young men skilled in handling ships. Each ship was assigned fifty men, and every four ships were commanded by a single officer.

The "three-stepped" troop ships <note>original: 三踏兵船 (Santa bingchuan); likely referring to vessels with three levels of oars or decks</note> were organized by ship-masters and deputies and assigned specific identification numbers. Sailors were instructed in the methods of timing the tides and catching the wind. Soldiers armed with longbows, short bows, and crossbows were regularly drilled, ensuring their movements at sea were as swift and coordinated as if guided by spirits. Any vessel found without an identification number would be pursued by the masters and deputies in "Bird-ships" <note>original: 鳥艓 (Niaodie/Niaochuan); fast, agile vessels used for pursuit and reconnaissance</note>. These operations continued until the end of autumn, after which the vessels were withdrawn once the seasonal threat had passed.

<margin>Eight large warships; twelve Bird-ships</margin>
