*Truly remarkable and profound is that admonition of the Apostle <note>Referring to St. Paul the Apostle.</note>, commanding that among Christians prayers, supplications, and thanksgivings be made for all those established in high positions; with the weighty reason added, that we may lead a calm and quiet life, with all godliness and honesty. 1 Timothy 2, verse 1. *For we see the Christian world moved and entangled by such great and pestilential turmoils, that a land lacking a legitimate and godly magistrate <term>magistrate: In this period, "magistrate" (magistratus) referred not just to a judge, but to any high-ranking civil authority or ruler, such as a Prince or Elector.</term> is nothing but a sheep in the midst of wolves, deprived of its shepherd; or a ship on the high seas destitute of a pilot; that is, it lies most clearly open to plundering, violence, and every kind of injury.*

*This is so clear, and proven by the truth of experience, that even at the present time, groans, tears, and infinite wailings are seen and heard everywhere on this very account. By contrast, those who enjoy a Christian magistrate experience—not without the most pleasant joy of soul—that such misery is turned away and kept from them and their families; and for that reason, they are deservedly numbered among the most fortunate. Although, meanwhile, no one can deny that even in these lands of ours, many can be found so hindered by a dangerous sleep of negligence and a certain reckless sluggishness, that they can scarcely ever bring their minds to value the greatness of this divine benefit, or even to hold it of any importance; so far are they from laboring to proclaim and cherish it in the proper manner.*

In their temples of old, the Pagans <note>Latin: *Ethnici*. The author refers to the ancient Greeks.</note> engraved that famous saying of theirs, **Know Thyself** <note>original: γνῶθι σεαυτόν (*gnōthi seauton*)</note>, in golden letters, and they sang it until they were hoarse. Because they believed it was sent down from heaven, they thought it should be raised back to heaven with highest praises. Now, I certainly do not intend to look down upon or belittle the excellence of that sentiment.

However, if someone were to weigh the **Elector’s Motto** <term>Motto: The Latin *Symbolum* here refers to a personal device or "word" chosen by a prince to represent his guiding principles.</term> for me—if he were to draw out the marrow of its most pious meaning and compare it with the divine words of the Bible from which it flowed—he would find it necessary to call that Pagan saying a mere trifle, or a shell empty of its kernel, compared to this.

Do you desire to know where that name and dignity of yours comes from, so that you may be called a Christian? Look! The Elector’s Motto shows you: ***From Christ***. Do you strive to learn how all the affairs of your life ought to be arranged so that they are holy and in harmony with the divine will? The Elector’s Motto teaches you: ***Serve Christ***.

Do you seek to know (which is the most important point and essence of Christianity) where you can look while living and while dying, so that after death you may obtain the life that is truly life? The Elector’s Motto informs you: ***To Christ***.

O, therefore, a voice of a Prince that is truly princely! O, a voice to be inscribed not on paper, but on cedar—and what do I say, cedar? Rather, it should be written in Heaven! Could you even doubt whether to offer vows for such a pious and wise **Jehoshaphat** <note>A biblical King of Judah known for his piety and for turning the people back to God; here used as a flattering comparison for the Elector.</note>, who breathes nothing but Christ, or to intercede for him with serious sighs before God?

Indeed, I affirm simply that I cannot express in words here how great an incentive the meditation on this very motto is, and has been for me, toward a serious devotion in prayer (and I wish this to be said **simply and without exaggeration** <note>original: ἀβασανíστως (*abasanistōs*)</note>). Yet I gladly confess, in honor of this Electoral aphorism, that when I thought to clothe it in a poetic paraphrase and thus recommend it to others as much as possible, I felt its truth most deeply.
