The exterior of this volume features a <term>binding</term><note>The protective cover of a book, consisting of the boards and the spine.</note> crafted from dark brown leather along the spine and decorative paper on the front and back covers. The <term>leather spine</term> is reinforced with raised horizontal bands, which were traditionally used to cover the cords where the pages are sewn together. A small, red leather title label is affixed to the spine, bordered with intricate <term>gold-tooled decoration</term><note>A process where gold leaf is pressed into the leather using heated brass stamps.</note> in a zig-zag pattern. 

The gilt-stamped text on the label has faded significantly over time, leaving only a few fragments of what was likely a formal title in Latin:

Such decorative papers were a popular and elegant choice for high-quality bookbindings in the 18th and 19th centuries.

To you, MOST SERENE PRINCE, I offer this book, which—to confess frankly—I had decided to dedicate openly to no mortal, but silently to Him alone <note>original: "Soli Illi"; a reference to God</note> to whom all people owe everything. For throughout the entire time I spent composing it, and even beyond that, I very truly intended in my mind to keep my name hidden. This is even evident from the writing style itself: since it is expressed in the first person as if [the soul] were speaking about itself (in the manner of those in meditation <note>The author refers to a tradition of philosophical or spiritual "meditations," where the "I" represents a universal human soul rather than a specific individual.</note>), it does not seem to allow the reader to modestly form an idea of a specific person identifying themselves by name.
