# Complete Library in the Four Treasuries <note>original: "四庫全書" (Siku Quanshu). Commissioned by the Qianlong Emperor in 1772, this is the largest collection of books in Chinese history. The "Four Treasuries" refer to the four main branches of knowledge in the traditional Chinese library system.</note>

## The Masters Section <note>original: "子部" (Zi Bu). This is the third of the four main categories of the imperial library. It includes works on philosophy (outside of the Confucian classics), religion, arts, and sciences such as agriculture, medicine, and mathematics.</note>

<term>Complete Library in the Four Treasuries (Siku Quanshu)</term>: A monumental project of the Qing dynasty that sought to compile and preserve the entirety of Chinese literature and knowledge into a single collection.

<term>The Masters Section (Zi Bu)</term>: One of the four traditional divisions of Chinese bibliography. While the other sections cover Confucian classics, history, and literature, the "Masters" section is a diverse repository for philosophical thought and practical technical manuals.
