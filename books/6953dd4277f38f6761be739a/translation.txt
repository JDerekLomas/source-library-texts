He is the Judge <note>Arabic: al-Qadi</note> Abu al-Walid Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Rushd <note>Known in the West as Averroes (1126–1198), a preeminent philosopher and jurist of Islamic Spain</note>. He was born and raised in Cordoba, and was renowned for his virtue and his dedication to the acquisition of knowledge. He was peerless in the science of jurisprudence <note>Arabic: fiqh</note> and legal theory, having studied under the jurist and memorizer of traditions Abu Muhammad ibn Rizq. He was also distinguished in the science of medicine, being an excellent writer with a refined clarity of expression. In medicine, he authored the book *The General Principles* <note>Arabic: Kitab al-Kulliyat; famously known in Latin as the 'Colliget'</note>, which he composed with great mastery. There existed a deep friendship between him and Abu Marwan ibn Zuhr <note>Known in the West as Avenzoar, a famous physician of the same era</note>. When Ibn Rushd composed this book on general medical matters, he intended for Ibn Zuhr to compose a book on specific, localized ailments so that their two works combined would serve as a complete manual for the craft of medicine. For this reason, Ibn Rushd says at the end of his book the following:

> This concludes the discussion on the treatment of all categories of diseases in the briefest and clearest manner possible. There remains for us in this section the discussion on the healing of each individual symptom of the diseases that affect each specific organ. Although this is not strictly necessary—as it is already implicitly contained within the general principles previously discussed—it serves as a completion and a practical exercise. In it, we descend to the treatments of diseases according to each organ, which is the method followed by the authors of the *Medical Compendiums* <note>Arabic: Kunanish; a term for medical handbooks or pharmacopoeias</note>. Thus, we combine in our discussions the general principles with the specific applications, for this craft is the most deserving of crafts in which one should descend to specific details as much as possible. However, we shall delay this until a time when we are less occupied, due to our current concern with other pressing matters.

Therefore, whoever comes into possession of this book without that specific section, and wishes thereafter to consult the *Compendiums*, the most suitable of them is the book titled *The Facilitation* <note>Arabic: al-Taysir</note>, which was composed in our time by Abu Marwan ibn Zuhr. I myself requested this book from him and transcribed a copy of it.

# Biography of the Author

The year five hundred and ninety-five <note>1198 CE</note>, at the beginning of the reign of al-Nasir <note>The Almohad Caliph Muhammad al-Nasir</note>. Ibn Rushd <note>Known in the West as Averroes</note> had lived a long life. He left behind a son who was a physician, learned in the craft, called Abu Muhammad Abd Allah. He also left behind sons who occupied themselves with jurisprudence and served as judges in the provinces. Among the sayings of Abu al-Walid ibn Rushd is: "Whoever occupies himself with the science of anatomy increases his faith in God."

Abu al-Walid ibn Rushd authored many books, including:
*   **The Book of Attainment** (*Kitab al-Tahsil*), in which he collected the differing opinions of the scholars among the Companions <note>of the Prophet Muhammad</note>, the Successors, and those who followed them; he supported their schools of thought and clarified the points of ambiguity that are the source of disagreement.
*   **The Book of Introductions** on jurisprudence.
*   **The Distinguished Jurist’s Primer** (*Bidayat al-Mujtahid*) <note>original: "Nihayat al-Mujtahid"</note> on jurisprudence.
*   **The Book of Generalities** (*Kitab al-Kulliyat*) <note>A famous medical encyclopedia known in Latin as the *Colliget*</note>.
*   **Commentary on the Poem** attributed to the Chief Sheikh, Ibn Sina <note>Avicenna</note>, on medicine.
*   **The Book of Animals**.
*   **Compendia of the Books of Aristotle** on physics and metaphysics.
*   **The Book of Essentials** on logic, which is appended to them.
*   **Summaries of the Books of Aristotle**, which he summarized completely and exhaustively.
*   **Summary of the Metaphysics** by Nicolaus <note>Nicolaus of Damascus</note>.
*   **Summary of the Metaphysics** by Aristotle.
*   **Summary of the Ethics** by Aristotle.
*   **Summary of the Posterior Analytics** <note>original: "Kitab al-Burhan"</note> by Aristotle.
*   **Summary of the Physics** <note>original: "al-Sama' al-Tabi'i"</note> by Aristotle.
*   **Commentary on the Book of the Soul** (*De Anima*) by Aristotle.
*   **Summary of the Book of Elements** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the Book of Temperaments** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the Book of Natural Faculties** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the Book of Causes and Symptoms** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the Book of Recognition** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the Book of Fevers** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the First Book of Simple Drugs** by Galen.
*   **Summary of the Second Half of the Method of Healing** by Galen.
*   **The Incoherence of the Incoherence** (*Tahafut al-Tahafut*), in which he responds to the book *The Incoherence [of the Philosophers]* by al-Ghazali.
*   **The Methodology of Proofs** on the science of theology <note>original: "ilm al-usul"</note>.
