# Exterior Binding and Spine (1682 Edition)

This 17th-century volume is bound in light brown leather, showcasing a high level of craftsmanship through various decorative techniques. The binding utilizes <term>blind-tooling</term> <note>a technique where decorative patterns are impressed into leather using heated tools without the use of gold or pigment, creating a subtle, indented design</note> and <term>gold-tooling</term> <note>a process where gold leaf is pressed into the leather with heated tools to create a shimmering, decorative effect</note>.

The front cover is dominated by a large, central <term>lozenge</term>-shaped medallion <note>a diamond-shaped decorative ornament</note>. This central feature is blind-stamped with a symmetrical pattern of foliage and scrolls. A double-line blind-tooled border frames the entire cover. At each corner of the inner rectangle and at the midpoints of the vertical lines, the binder has placed small decorative <term>palmettes</term> or <term>fleurons</term> <note>small, flower-like or leaf-like stamps used to accent corners and borders</note>.

The spine is constructed with five prominent <term>raised bands</term> <note>the horizontal ridges on a book's spine where the leather covers the cords or "supports" used to sew the pages together</note>. These bands divide the spine into six compartments. 

*   The second compartment from the top contains a <term>spine label</term> <note>a piece of leather, often in a contrasting color, used to display the book's title</note> decorated with gold-tooling. 
*   The first, third, fifth, and sixth compartments are embellished with identical gold-tooled floral motifs.

The edges of the boards also show traces of decorative tooling, indicating the thoroughness of the book’s decoration.
