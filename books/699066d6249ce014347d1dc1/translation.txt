# The Marvels of Geomancy

**Salutations to the glorious Lord Ganesha.** I bow to the lotus feet of the Lord of Ganas (Ganesha). His temples are adorned with swarms of bees, attracted by the fragrance of ichor <note>A nectar-like secretion from the foreheads of elephants in rut, symbolizing power and abundance.</note>, hovering and striking repeatedly. May the Great Goddess Kalika protect you; she who performs her great dance with her mind fixed on the jingling sound of her anklets, her feet rising slightly and bestowing a beautiful brilliance.

He who is joined with the moon of wisdom should not trust the world that is released from that forest of delusion. This primordial house leads to victory and produces merit. May Shiva grant you auspiciousness.

I, the scholar **Ramadeva**, residing in the beautiful city of Jaipur, compose this most delightful and marvelous treatise on the <term>science of geomancy</term> <note>original Sanskrit: "Ramala Shastra." This is a system of divination using patterns of dots and lines, originally adapted from Arabic and Persian sources (Raml) into the Sanskrit tradition.</note> as instructed by the learned.

---

Having first prostrated before the deities of my own family and then at the feet of the twice-born <note>Brahmins, or the learned priestly class.</note> and the elders, I have consulted the experts of the Western <note>original: "Paschatya." In this context, it refers to the Islamic or Persian scholars from whom this specific science originated.</note> science of geomancy to accurately determine the outcomes of the patterns.

In the art of Ramala, a single dot is known as a **Bindu** <note>a "point" or single dot</note>; two dots together form a **Rekha** <note>a "line" or dash</note>. One must understand the four primary positions or "houses" according to the left-hand path.

A single point arises, and a single line likewise arises. From the specific union of lines and points, a "zero" or empty space is also formed. 

From these four initial positions, the fifth is generated in an upward sequence, then the sixth, and following those, the eighth is described.
