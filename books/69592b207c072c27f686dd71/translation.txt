# Extract of the King’s Privilege

*BY the grace and privilege of the King, Gilles Gorbin, bookseller at the University of Paris, is permitted to print or have printed a book entitled Platonic Theology on the Immortality of Souls, by Marsilio Ficino <note>Ficino (1433–1499) was a central figure of the Italian Renaissance who sought to harmonize Platonic philosophy with Christian theology</note>. And the said Lord <note>The King</note> forbids all other booksellers, printers, and any other persons whatsoever of this Kingdom from printing or having printed from this copy, or selling the said book without the will and consent of the said Gorbin, under penalty of confiscation of the said books found printed, and an arbitrary fine. This shall remain in effect until the time and term of ten years, finished and completed, counting from the day and date that the printing of the said book is finished, as is more fully contained and declared by the letters patent <note>A public decree or legal document issued by a monarch</note> granted at Villers-Cotterêts on the seventeenth day of April, in the year of grace one thousand five hundred and fifty-seven, and the eleventh year of our reign.*

We have taken care to restore this work—so distinguished, yet for so long struggling against moths and bookworms, and nearly obscured by the foggy darkness of errors—to its proper light and dignity. We judged it unworthy that while so many writings of modern authors <note>original: "neotericorum"; referring to contemporary 16th-century writers</note> are spread upon pages, handled, read, and loved—some of which are certainly trivial, and others even destructive and impious—the most serious, useful, and devout monuments of ancient authors are despised and neglected, treated as if they had never been written. Therefore, kind reader, look favorably upon this effort of ours, and show a sign of a grateful mind; you will gather a harvest from this work that is greater than that from the "know-it-alls" <note>original: "sciolis"; a derogatory term for those with superficial knowledge</note> of our own age, just as that Golden Age, so fertile with noble and heroic geniuses, was always held to be superior to this Iron Age of ours.

**T**he third book descends from God: and compares in turn the levels of things to the middle level: and this level to the others. The descent occurs through five levels: through which the ascent was made: which levels are compared harmoniously to one another: chapter one, letter A, page 39 <note>Ficino uses a system of letters (A, B, C) to mark specific sections within a chapter for easy reference.</note>

The Soul is the middle level of things: and it connects all levels, both the higher and the lower, into one: while it both ascends to the higher beings and descends to the lower: chapter 2, letter D, page 43

### Chapter 4 of the book.

**T**he fourth book divides the middle level of things—that is, the soul—into its own species.

There are three levels of rational souls. In the first is the Soul of the World <note>original: "anima mundi"; the Neoplatonic concept of an animating spirit infused within the entire universe.</note>. In the second are the souls of the spheres. In the third are the souls of the living beings: which are contained within the individual spheres: chapter one, letter B, page 46

The souls of the spheres move the spheres through the law of fate: and they move in a circle: because they themselves are circles: chapter 2, letter F, page 55
