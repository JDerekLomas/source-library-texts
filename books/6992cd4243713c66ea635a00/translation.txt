->**Mount Wudan**<-

**Mount Wudan** is located to the north of the **Chengdu** prefectural government office. According to the *Records of Shu*, a spirit from Mount Wudu transformed into a woman, whom **King Kaiming** of Shu took as his consort. She died because she was not accustomed to the local climate. The King then dispatched the <term>Five Strong Men</term><note>original: "五丁" (Wuding); five legendary giants of ancient Shu known for their Herculean strength, often credited with carving roads through mountains.</note> to Mount Wudu to carry earth back to build her a tomb. It covers several acres of land and stands seventy feet high. At the top, there is a stone five inches thick and five feet in diameter, lustrous and pure, called the **"Stone Mirror,"** which was used to mark the entrance to the tomb. It is also rumored that within Chengdu and near **Pi Bridge**, there is a split stone thirty feet long, which is said to have been dropped by the **Five Strong Men** while they were carrying the earth. <term>Emperor Zhaolie</term><note>original: "漢昭烈" (Han Zhaolie); referring to Liu Bei (161–223 AD), the founder of the Shu Han kingdom during the Three Kingdoms period.</note> of the Han Dynasty ascended the throne to the south of **Mount Wudan**, referring to this very site.

->**The Min River**<-

The **Min River** is one of the <term>Four Great Rivers</term><note>original: "四瀆" (Sidu); a traditional grouping of the four most important rivers in China that flow into the sea: the Yangtze, Yellow, Huai, and Ji rivers. Here, the Min is given this prestigious status as the primary source/tributary of the Yangtze in the region.</note>. It is commonly known as the **Wen River**. Its source emerges from the **Min Mountains**, passing beneath the city of **Maozhou**, traveling southwest to **Weizhou**, and then through **Wenchuan**. It turns southeast to **Guan County**, passes the **Golden Horse Entrance**, and reaches **Xinjin County**, where it merges with the waters of the **Zao River**.

*Recovering from illness, I still cherish the wine shop of Zhuo Wenjun;*

**Ten Thousand Mile Bridge (Wanli Qiao)** is located outside the Zhonghe Gate of the prefectural capital. According to the *Records of the Universe* <note>original: "寰宇記"; referring to the *Taiping Huanyu Ji*, a famous 10th-century geographic work.</note>, it is recorded that when **Zhuge Liang** <note>The legendary strategist of the Three Kingdoms period.</note> saw off **Fei Yi** <note>A high-ranking official of the state of Shu Han.</note> on a diplomatic mission to the Kingdom of Wu, he arrived at this spot and remarked, "The journey of ten thousand <term>li</term> <note>A traditional unit of distance, roughly 500 meters.</note> begins here." Furthermore, the *History of Tang* records that when **Emperor Xuanzong** sought refuge in the region of Shu <note>During the An Lushan Rebellion (755–763 AD), the Emperor was forced to flee the capital, Chang'an, for the safety of Sichuan.</note>, he passed this bridge and asked its name. Those around him replied, "Ten Thousand Miles." **Xuanzong** sighed and said, "At the end of the Kaiyuan era, the monk **Yi Xing** <note>A famous Tang dynasty astronomer, mathematician, and Buddhist monk.</note> told me that in twenty years, the country would face a great hardship and I would travel ten thousand miles away. This must be what he meant." He then established his temporary imperial residence in Chengdu.

**Jinjiang Bridge** is located on Jinjiang Street, to the east of the prefectural capital.

**Ascending Immortal Bridge (Shengxian Qiao)** is located seven <term>li</term> north of the prefectural capital. In the Han dynasty, **Sima Xiangru** <note>One of the most famous poets and statesmen of the Han Dynasty (c. 179–117 BC).</note> once wrote upon a pillar of this bridge: "If a great man does not ride in a carriage drawn by four horses, he shall never cross this bridge again." <note>This was a vow of ambition; Sima Xiangru left Chengdu as a poor scholar but returned in glory as an imperial official, fulfilling his promise to cross the bridge in a "four-horse carriage," a symbol of high status.</note>

**Daci Temple** (Temple of Great Compassion) is located east of the prefecture. It was built during the Zhide era of the Tang dynasty. The four characters **"Great Temple of Sagely Compassion"** <note>original: "大聖慈寺"</note>, written in the calligraphy of **Emperor Xuanzong**, are still preserved there today.

# Mount Qionglai

Mount Qionglai is located forty *li* <note>A *li* is a traditional unit of distance, approximately 500 meters or one-third of a mile.</note> east of Rongjing County in Ya Prefecture <note>Modern-day Ya'an, Sichuan Province.</note>. ## The Nine-Fold Slope

The Nine-Fold Slope is located on the path up Mount Qionglai. The road is so steep and winding that one must make nine sharp turns to reach the summit. During the Han Dynasty, when the official Wang Yang was serving as the Governor of Yi Prefecture, his tour of duty brought him to this place. He sighed and said, "I have inherited this body from my ancestors; how can I justify repeatedly risking it on such dangerous heights?" He later resigned from his post, citing illness.

When Wang Zun later became the Governor and arrived at this same spot, he asked his subordinates, "Is this not the road that Wang Yang feared so much?" He then shouted to his carriage driver, "Drive on!" History regards Wang Yang as a **filial son** <note>Because he honored the Confucian duty to protect the physical body given to him by his parents.</note> and Wang Zun as a **loyal official** <note>Because he placed his duty to the Emperor and the administration above his personal safety.</note>.

# The Records of Binglu Mountain

**Binglu Mountain** <note>original: 栟櫚山 (*Bīnglǘ Shān*). Also known as Fan Palm Mountain.</note> is located in the 27th District, north of the seat of **Yong’an County** in **Yanping Prefecture**. The mountain produces an abundance of **fan palm trees** <note>original: 栟櫚木 (*bīnglǘ mù*), the *Trachycarpus fortunei*</note>, which is how it earned its name. 

During the **Song Dynasty**, the scholar **Deng Su** <note>Deng Su (1091–1132) was a noted poet and official known for his integrity</note> lived at the foot of the mountain and gave himself the sobriquet "The Fan Palm Lay Scholar." Local tradition holds that immortals and divine beings have long frequented this realm. Its peaks, ridges, crags, and caves are too numerous to count; they appear as if "carved by gods and hewn by ghosts" <note>An idiom meaning the natural formations are so spectacular they seem supernatural</note>, with heights and depths connecting one to the next. 

Mists and clouds emerge and vanish at no fixed time, and the vegetation is so lush and thick that it remains a single shade of vibrant green throughout all four seasons. **Li Gang** <note>A famous Song Dynasty statesman and military leader</note> of the Song once remarked that this place was like a "Little Wuyi Mountain" <note>referring to the famous Wuyi Mountains, a UNESCO World Heritage site known for its dramatic karst landscape</note>. 

**Heavenly Pillar Peak** and **Lion Crag** tower together over a hundred *zhang* high <note>approximately 330 meters or 1,100 feet</note>, appearing as sheer as if they had been sliced by a blade. There is also **Archery Target Peak**. Legend says that in ancient times, immortals practiced archery from the **Immortal Platform**, using this specific peak as their target. During the **Mingdao era** of the Song Dynasty <note>1032–1033 AD</note>, farmers once discovered several dozen arrows near the side of the platform.

**Guanyin Crag** <note>named after the Bodhisattva of Compassion</note> sits beside the **Wild Cloud Cave**, featuring stone walls that rise ten thousand *ren* <note>an ancient measure of height; "ten thousand" is used figuratively for immense height</note>. There are also the **Gathering Immortals Crag** and the **Ascetic Monk Crag**. 

Regarding the latter, tradition holds that during the **Tang Dynasty**, there was a monk named **Pei the Ascetic** <note>term: 頭陀 (*tóutuó*), a Buddhist monk who practices asceticism</note>. It is said he crossed the river wearing iron shoes and came to live on this mountain. He cleared away the thorns and brambles to build a small thatched hut. While he sat in meditation, a crack in the rock beside him would daily overflow with two pints of rice to provide for his needs. If guests arrived, the amount of rice would increase accordingly. Later, someone <note>presumably out of greed</note> enlarged the hole, after which the flow of rice ceased forever.

**The Sword Ford** <note>original: 劍津 (*Jiànjīn*)</note> is located east of the Nanping county seat in Yanping Prefecture, at the point where the Jianning and Shaowu rivers merge. During the Jin Dynasty, a man named Lei Huan discovered two legendary swords in Fengcheng. He gave one to the statesman Zhang Hua and kept the other for himself. After Zhang Hua passed away, the location of his sword was lost. Later, Lei Huan’s son was carrying his father’s sword while crossing the Yanping Ford when the blade suddenly leaped from its scabbard into the water and transformed into a dragon. Because of this miracle, the site was named the Sword Ford; it is also known as the Sword Pool. A poem by the Tang Dynasty scholar Ouyang Zhan describes the site:

->Imagining those spirits, yet finding them hard to see,<-
->Once they depart for the Great Ford, the waters stretch endlessly.<-
->All that remains of the past is their frost-cutting sheen,<-
->Forever chilling the depths of the pool, clear and serene.<-
<note>Ouyang Zhan (755–800 AD) was the first scholar from Fujian to pass the highest imperial examination. His poem evokes the "frost-cutting" chill of the legendary blades that still seems to emanate from the water.</note>

**The Yanping Academy** <note>original: 延平書院 (*Yánpíng Shūyuàn*)</note> is situated on the sunny northern bank of the Sword Ford. During the Jiading era of the Song Dynasty (1208–1224), the prefectural governor Chen Mi established the school at the foot of Nan Mountain. It was named in honor of the Neo-Confucian philosopher Li Tong, who was known by the respectful title "Master Yanping." Later, the governor Dong Hong moved the academy to its current location. The complex contains the Hall of Ritual, a memorial shrine, and four study halls named **Sincerity**, **Loyalty and Reciprocity**, **Equilibrium and Harmony**, and **Reverence and Righteousness** <note>original: 明誠 (*Míngchéng*), 忠恕 (*Zhōngshù*), 中和 (*Zhōnghé*), and 敬義 (*Jìngyì*). These are foundational virtues in Confucian philosophy.</note>. It also features the **Bright Breeze and Clear Moon Pavilion**. The academy was rebuilt during the early years of the Yongle era of the current dynasty <note>The Ming Dynasty, specifically the reign of the Yongle Emperor (1402–1424).</note>.

# Mount Hugong

Mount Hugong is located in Putian County of Xinghua Prefecture. While most mountains are described as having four sides, this mountain alone possesses eight. It towers over a thousand *ren* <note>An ancient unit of measurement, roughly equivalent to 7 or 8 feet; used here to denote great height.</note> high, standing directly across from the prefectural seat. The mountain's shape is square and sharp, resembling the pointed head of a ceremonial jade tablet <note>original: *gui* (圭), a flat jade baton held by officials during imperial ceremonies, symbolizing status and authority.</note>. It stands tall like a decorative screen—elegant, singular, upright, and imposing. It serves as the <term>Guardian Mountain</term> <note>original: *zhenshan* (鎮山). In traditional Chinese geography, a "Guardian Mountain" is a primary peak believed to anchor the spiritual energy and stability of a specific region.</note> of the prefecture.

Ancient records state that in the past, a hermit encountered an old man at the absolute summit. Suddenly, they beheld palaces, towers, and grand halls that did not seem to belong to the world of men. When the hermit asked the old man about them, he replied: "This is the <term>Sun and Moon within a Gourd</term>." <note>original: *huzhong riyue* (壺中日月). A Daoist concept referring to a mystical paradise or miniature universe contained within a gourd, where time flows differently and immortals dwell.</note> Later generations therefore named the mountain "Hugong" [Gourd Father] after him.

During the Song Dynasty, Master Zhu Wengong <note>original: *Zhu Wengong* (朱文公), the posthumous name of Zhu Xi (1130–1200), the most influential Neo-Confucian philosopher in Chinese history.</note> passed through Putian. Upon gazing at Mount Hugong, he noted that the southern face was particularly exquisite and remarked: "The abundance of great figures and talents in Putian is entirely due to the concentrated elegance of this mountain; this 'Old Man' is truly working wonders!"

A prophetic saying once stated: "If the ridges of Mount Hugong were severed, half of Putian’s <term>Vermilion and Purple</term> <note>original: *zhuzi* (朱紫). This refers to the red and purple robes worn by high-ranking imperial officials; the term is used here to represent scholarly and political success.</note> would lose their connection. Only when Mount Hugong is whole is it truly a sight to behold." Later, Cai Junmo <note>original: *Cai Junmo* (蔡君謨), the courtesy name of Cai Xiang (1012–1067), a famous official, calligrapher, and structural engineer from Putian.</note> developed water conservancy works, leading the water to encircle Mount Hugong. Consequently, it was said that half the imperial court was filled with officials from Putian.

A poem by Liu Kezhuang <note>A famous Song Dynasty poet and literary critic from Putian (1187–1269).</note> of the Song Dynasty describes the mountain:

So many people chop wood that the mountain grows thin,
The monk who built the hut has gone, and the stones lie in deep sleep.
Birds return to the mid-ridge, carrying the setting sun in their beaks,
Woodcutters return to the thinning forest as evening mist rises.
