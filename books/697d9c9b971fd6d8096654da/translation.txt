# The Pagan World

## And the initial origin of their gods

Through what delusions <note>original: "verwhänungen"; refers to the false beliefs or presumptions of the ancient world</note> a certain imagined power was attributed to them, for which reason they were honored by the ancients. Thus, shrines, temples, images, and altars were erected for them, and innumerable names were given to them. Also, how certain **kings, emperors, cities, and estates** supported them with great wealth and riches, or at other times robbed, burned, tore down, and completely destroyed them. What quarrels, wars, and bloodshed arose on this account, gathered together from the writings of many highly esteemed and learned men (whose names are listed on the reverse side of this page).

### **Six books by Diodorus the Sicilian, the most**
renowned historian among the Greeks, the contents of which show the imagined beginning of the world, as well as its first habitation and the rule over all lands, with many stories, customs, laws, and practices regarded as unbelievable, which occurred up until the Trojan War. <note>Diodorus Siculus was a 1st-century BCE Greek historian; his "Bibliotheca historica" was a massive universal history.</note>

**The truthful description of the Trojan War by Dictys of Crete,** in which he himself was a commander over many people and recorded the events as they actually happened. <note>Dictys Cretensis was long believed to be an eyewitness to the Trojan War, though scholars now view the text as a later Roman-era invention.</note>

**The sculpted symbols of Horus—a King and Priest in Egypt three thousand years ago—** through which, before the invention of letters, all the secrets of the clergy and the worldly-learned were made known and displayed. <note>Refers to the "Hieroglyphica" of Horapollo, an attempt to explain Egyptian hieroglyphs which the Renaissance saw as mystical allegories.</note>

**Planetary Tables, in which those aforementioned gods are named in place of the seven** wandering stars <note>The "wandering stars" are the planets of the classical solar system: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, plus the Sun and Moon.</note>; according to the findings of the astronomers, what they effect in the births of humans, in the character and inclinations of their lives, and what power they have over the weather and the growth of crops, presented here in a very brief form.
