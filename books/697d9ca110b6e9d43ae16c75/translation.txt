# Preface.

<meta>catchword: Dar- (The beginning of the word "Darzu," meaning "thereto," continuing from the previous page's final sentence.)</meta>

...to the daughters. For just as a little rose is a lovely and laudable creature and a hearty creation of God—which delights, cools, strengthens, and refreshes humanity through its beautiful form, pleasant scent, and special operation and effect, as the natural philosophers and physicians <note>original Latin: "Physici & Medici"</note> write regarding it: <note>The author cites classical and Renaissance authorities: Dioscorides, Book 1, Chapter 13; Galen, Book 8, "On the Power of Simple Medicines"; Pliny, Book 21, Chapter 4; Serapion the Younger; and Leonhart Fuchs.</note> It is likewise the same with pious Christians in this valley of tears <note>original: "Jammerthal"; a common Baroque expression for the earthly world of suffering</note>, as David calls it in Psalm 84. Since God the LORD has created and made us into the people and the sheep of His heath and pasture (Psalms 95 and 100), that we should be His own, serving Him in holiness and righteousness that is pleasing to Him (Luke 1). He Himself has planted us, so that we should be trees of righteousness and seedlings of the Lord for His praise (Isaiah 61). He has ransomed <note>original: "ranzionirt," from the French "rançonner"</note> and redeemed us at a very great price (1 Corinthians 6), not with perishable gold or silver, but with His own precious blood and with His innocent suffering and death (1 Peter 1). Thus, with special honor and renown, we may well be titled and named the most hearty creatures and the noblest of God's creations, because the LORD has His highest and greatest delight in us, the children of men (Proverbs 8).

He loves us so fervently that His love is stronger than death, which many waters cannot quench nor the floods drown (Song of Solomon 8). For He did not spare His only Son (Romans 8), even when we were yet His enemies (Romans 5), but gave Him up for our sins (Romans 4)—and not only for ours, but for the sins of the whole world (1 John 2). This was so that all who believe in Him and persevere steadfastly in faith should not be lost, but have eternal life (John 3, Matthew 10). We should forever use this for our true heart's refreshment <note>original: "herzlabung"</note> and, out of a wonder born of strong faith, say with Moses: how the LORD loves the people so much; all His saints are in His hand; they shall sit at His feet and shall learn from His words (Deuteronomy 33). Taste and see how friendly the LORD is (Psalm 39 <note>In the German Bible of the time, this is often Psalm 34</note>); His mercy is as great as He Himself is (Sirach 18), and His love is so fervent that He cannot act according to His righteous anger (Hosea 11). Just as a father has mercy on his children, so the LORD has mercy on those who fear Him (Psalm 103).

# Preface.

<meta>catchword: ders (this catchword refers to the beginning of the word "derselbigen" or "derselben" on the following page)</meta>

...so that they may recognize themselves, <note>original: "ipsi se agnoscent"</note> as Primasius says on 2 Corinthians 1, or as Jerome says in Book 6 of his *Commentary on Isaiah*: "God strikes so that He may improve." <note>original: "Deus percutit ut emendet"</note> But the **scoundrel’s luck** <term>Bubenglück: a derogatory term for the undeserved prosperity of the wicked</term> of the godless grieves us greatly when we are envied and persecuted by them; yet we should not let such "heat of temptation" <note>original: "hitze der anfechtung"</note> alienate us, *1 Peter 4.* For it is thus decreed that all who wish to live godly lives in Christ Jesus must suffer persecution, *2 Timothy 3,* and we must enter the Kingdom of God through much cross-bearing and tribulation, *Acts 14.*

Wherefore Solon rightly says in Herodotus, page 2: "Man is entirely a misfortune" <note>original: "πᾶν ἐστ’ ἄνθρωπος συμφορὴ homo totum quod est, obnoxium est calamitatibus"</note>; man is subject to all kinds of misery and wretchedness. And if the Lord were not with us when men set themselves against us, they would swallow us alive; when their anger rages against us, the waters would drown us, streams would go over our soul, the waters would go far too high over our soul, *Psalm 124.* In this same way, man is called **Enosh** <note>original: אֱנֹושׁ</note> in Hebrew because he must dwell here in misery and walk through the valley of sorrows. The word "human" <note>original: "Mensch"</note> also aims at and reflects this: as if one said "man of ash." <note>The author is using a folk etymology, linking the German word "Mensch" to "Man" and "Asche" (ash), suggesting human frailty.</note> In this fashion, the Patriarchs of the Old Testament gave names to their children by which they remembered their state of distress; as Moses named his first son **Gershom**, *Exodus 2,* from the root meaning "to be a stranger," <note>original: גֵּרְשֹׁום (gershom) / "peregrinari"</note> because he had been a stranger in a foreign land. And the word **German** <note>Germanus</note> also has its origin and beginning from "stranger" and "wretched." <note>original: גֵּרִים (gerim) and עֲנִי (ani), "hoc est, advenæ miseri." The author is claiming the word "German" comes from Hebrew roots meaning "miserable strangers."</note>

For the Patriarchs and Prophets were certainly plagued people, and the Apostles also had to experience all kinds of need and death, and **confirm** and seal their confession of Christ Jesus with their own blood. Oh, how unmercifully they were often torn apart and scourged, and martyred and butchered in the most gruesome ways! As the Church Histories state and show, the persecution did not end with them but has landed upon us as well, and lasts forever and ever until the beloved Last Day. Thus, Bernard <note>Saint Bernard of Clairvaux</note> in his *Book of Meditations* may well lament and say: "Man is nothing other than foul seed, a juice of dung, food for worms; after man, a worm; after the worm, stench and horror." <note>original: "homo nihil aliud est quàm sperma fœtidum, succus stercorum, cibus vermium, post hominem vermis, post vermem fætor & horror"</note> We are nothing else...
