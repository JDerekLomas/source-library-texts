<note>original: 八仙花 (Baxianhua), the hydrangea. The name refers to the Eight Immortals of Chinese mythology, suggesting a gathering of legendary figures.</note>

# Powder Clusters
<note>original: 粉團 (Fentuan). This name refers to *Viburnum macrocephalum*, a species of Viburnum characterized by its large, globose white flower heads, often confused with hydrangeas.</note>

In the *Mirror of Flowers*, the **Powder Cluster** is also known as the **Embroidered Ball**. The bark of the tree has a rough, chapped texture <note>original: 皴 (cun). There are two varieties: a large and a small kind. The leaves resemble those of hemp, and while the individual flowers are small, hundreds of them cluster together on a single stem to form a dense bunch. They are round and white, resembling silk tassels <note>original: 流蘇 (liusu). Ornamental tassels used in traditional decor.</note>. These blossoms begin with a green hue and later turn pure white, appearing exactly like a solid ball. 

The variety with a purple halo or "blush" around the edges of the petals is considered the most precious. Traditionally, the larger variety is called the **Powder Cluster**, while the smaller one is called the **Embroidered Ball**. In the region of Fujian <note>original: 閩 (Min). The historical and cultural name for Fujian Province.</note>, there is a variety known as the **Red Embroidered Ball**, though its characteristics do not truly align with the name "Powder Cluster." Both the Rose of Sharon <note>original: 蕣毬 (shunqiu). Likely *Hibiscus syriacus*.</note> and the Japanese Cheesewood <note>original: 海桐 (haitong). *Pittosporum tobira*.</note> can serve as rootstock for grafting the Embroidered Ball.

The **Powder Cluster** originates in Fujian, which is why the common people often call it the "Foreign Embroidered Ball" <note>This name suggests the plant may have been associated with trade or coastal regions where foreign goods entered.</note>. Its flowers are initially green, then turn a powdered pink; some varieties even transform into a brilliant azure blue before eventually fading back toward green. A single blossom can endure for several months. However, the plant is sensitive: it wilts as soon as it sees the sun, yet remains sturdy and resilient when met with rain. If placed in shady, damp, or even nitrogen-rich, "foul" areas <note>original: 穢溷 (huihun). To confine them to decorative <term>Basins<gloss>original: 盆盎 (pen’ang). Shallow ceramic pots or trays used for ornamental gardening.</gloss></term> is to go against their natural temperament.
