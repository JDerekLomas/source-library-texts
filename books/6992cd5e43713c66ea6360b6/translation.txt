# The Complete Library in the Four Sections
<note>original: "Siku Quanshu." This was a monumental archival project commissioned by the Qianlong Emperor of the Qing dynasty in 1772. It aimed to collect and preserve all significant Chinese literature and knowledge, eventually comprising nearly 800 million characters.</note>

## The Masters Section
<note>original: "Zibu." This is the third of the four main divisions of the imperial library. It contains the writings of various "Masters"—thinkers from philosophical schools (other than Confucianism), as well as works on military strategy, medicine, astronomy, mathematics, and the arts.</note>

<term>The Complete Library in the Four Sections, the Masters Section, Siku Quanshu</term> <note>These terms denote the standardized hierarchical classification used for the library's organization.</note>

<summary>This title page marks a volume as part of the "Masters Section" of the Siku Quanshu, the largest collection of books in Chinese history. It reflects the 18th-century Qing dynasty's efforts to systematically organize all human knowledge.</summary>
