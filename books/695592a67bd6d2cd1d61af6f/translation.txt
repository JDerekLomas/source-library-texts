# The Order of the Celestial Spheres

<note>The symbols above represent the seven classical planets of antiquity, arranged in the "Chaldean order" based on their perceived distance from the Earth and the speed of their orbits.</note>

### The Seven Governors

The heavens are divided into seven distinct realms, each presided over by a wandering star <note>The term "planet" comes from the Greek *planētēs*, meaning "wanderer."</note>. From the highest heights of the firmament to the lowest sphere of the air, the order is established thus:

1. **Saturn** <note>original: "Saturnus"</note> — The most distant and slowest of the spheres, associated with time and melancholy.
2. **Jupiter** <note>original: "Iuppiter"</note> — The great benefic, representing justice and expansion.
3. **Mars** <note>original: "Mars"</note> — The red planet, signifying heat, conflict, and vital force.
4. **The Sun** <note>original: "Sol"</note> — The central light and heart of the cosmos.
5. **Venus** <note>original: "Venus"</note> — The morning and evening star, governing harmony and attraction.
6. **Mercury** <note>original: "Mercurius"</note> — The swift messenger, presiding over intellect and commerce.
7. **The Moon** <note>original: "Luna"</note> — The closest neighbor to our earthly realm, governing the tides and the changing seasons.

<margin>The Moon is the gateway between the corruptible Earth and the eternal heavens.</margin>

# PREFACE TO THE KING

*...though they speak the absolute truth. But those who have diligently and more accurately contemplated these same things in person will confess that they suffer from a great lack and poverty of speech and expression as soon as they desire to pour themselves out oratorically into the full grandeur of these matters. I myself, while lingering for some time this past September in Bratislava <note>original: "Posonio," the Latin name for modern-day Bratislava, then the capital of Royal Hungary</note> in your Hungarian Kingdom, recognized the most brilliant reasons for this, having explored them in various ways as an eyewitness.*

*Regarding the rarity of this gift (which is indeed small in its physical bulk), I shall speak in as few words as possible. As I search with every effort of my mind, there occurs to me a twofold course of human life, tending toward different purposes, which must be considered in this way (and one of which almost everyone enters). As soon as the race of infancy and childhood is finished, and the stage of youth—which [is the remainder] of life...*

# THE MAXIMILIAN

...they shall enter into the race of life; then Choice <note>original: "Optio"</note> begins to twist the mind. At that point, hesitating somewhat in the uncertainty of judgment, they stand at a crossroads <note>original: "Bivio"; a reference to the "Choice of Hercules" between Virtue and Vice</note>. Finally, they decide: either they must strain every nerve toward the study of philosophy <note>original: "Philosophandum"</note> for the entire remainder of their lives (these are the ones captured by the love of Truth and Virtue); or, they must labor anxiously by all means to lead a life of luxury or profit (these, certainly, are the ones ensnared by worldly enticements or burning with a desire for riches). And of the latter, you may indeed very easily find a thousand; whereas of the former—those who more sincerely devote their efforts to philosophy—you can scarcely point out one who has tasted even the first and true foundations of physics <note>original: "Physicae"; here meaning the study of the natural world and its laws</note>. But as for one who has more deeply and fully scrutinized the causes of celestial forces and actions <note>original: "Caelestium virium & Actionum"</note>, and the origins, states, and deaths of all other things: you could not bring forth even the thousandth part of those who have turned themselves entirely to the studies of wisdom <note>original: "sapientiæ studia"</note>.

<summary>The author describes the moral crossroads faced by young men, contrasting the many who seek wealth and luxury with the rare few who truly master the study of natural philosophy and celestial causes.</summary>
