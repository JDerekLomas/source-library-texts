Just recently, and specifically at the end of last year, the famous and modest Royal Danish Councilor of State, Mr. Johann Jacob Moser <note>Johann Jacob Moser (1701–1785) was a renowned German jurist and a leading authority on the constitutional law of the Holy Roman Empire.</note>, published a small treatise consisting of three sheets <note>A "sheet" (Bogen) was a printer's measure; three sheets would be a pamphlet of roughly 48 pages.</note> under the title: *On the Toleration of Freemason Societies; Particularly in Regard to the Peace of Westphalia*. In this writing, he examines the following four questions:

1) Are Freemason lodges contrary to the Peace of Westphalia <note>The Peace of Westphalia (1648) was the foundational legal treaty of the Holy Roman Empire, which regulated which religious and social groups were legally permitted to exist.</note>, and if so, why?

2) Is a German sovereign <term>Landesherr: the ruling prince or lord of a specific territory</term> obligated to abolish them? or

3) Can he at least abolish them whenever he wishes?

4) Can the estates <term>Landstände: the representative bodies of a territory, usually comprised of the nobility, clergy, and cities</term> make a formal grievance of it if a sovereign tolerates them in his lands?

And it must be confessed that he answers these questions with much caution and moderation.
