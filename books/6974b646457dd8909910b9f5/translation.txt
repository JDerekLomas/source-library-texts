# Preface.

Without deciding here whether <term>Freemasonry</term> <note>a fraternal organization that evolved from medieval stonemasons' guilds into a system of morality and philosophy</note> is truly connected with magic, <term>theurgy</term> <note>a form of ritual magic intended to invoke divine or supernatural powers</note>, and <term>Kabbalah</term> <note>an ancient Jewish tradition of mystical interpretation of the Bible</note>, the interest that Freemasons and <term>Rosicrucians</term> <note>members of a secret society claiming to possess esoteric wisdom passed down from antiquity</note> take in one another is well known. The writings emerging from both branches are always interesting, even if discord reigns among the <term>systems</term> <note>the various organizational structures, rites, and degrees of Masonry, which often disagreed on their origins and purposes</note> of their authors. However, it is precisely this diversity of systems that makes it so difficult to find common ground of general interest. For this reason, it is believed that a collection of scholarly and historical essays—intended for all systems and branches of Freemasonry—which contains significant material from old and new works, impartially announces and judges all recent writings, and subsequently recounts the latest events faithfully, would surely find approval and support.

# Preface.

This is the intention of the editor of this <term>Archive</term> <note>referring to the publication itself, which serves as a repository for documents and essays</note>, though only in part; it cannot be fully explained here, nor can it be judged based on a single installment. One can only remark that its continuation alone will and can provide perfection and completeness.

In order to achieve this more and more, we request, on the one hand, suggestions for improvement according to the taste of discerning readers; and on the other, reports of new and remarkable occurrences in <term>Masonry</term> <note>original: "Maurerei"</note>, both public and private; biographies or materials for such concerning notable men; interesting anecdotes; and new <term>Masonic</term> and <term>Rosicrucian</term> writings; sent to the address of the publisher.

It is noted here that in the future, we will primarily work toward providing a productive <term>critique</term> <note>original: "Kritik"; here meaning a scholarly review or analytical evaluation</note> of everything that has been written in older and newer times regarding the subjects pertaining to this field. Furthermore, new essays will also appear, which we flatter ourselves will attract attention and be pleasing to most readers.

## What is a Freemason?

If someone were presented with the questions: What is a legal scholar? <term>an advocate</term> <note>original: "Sachwalter"; a representative in legal matters, similar to an attorney</note>? a physician? a philosopher? then in the answer one would certainly expect to know how all these men, in their individual circumstances, differ from all other scholars and <term>estates</term> <note>original: "Stände"; referring to the social classes or professional ranks that defined 18th-century social structure</note>; yet the questioner's intention is likely primarily this: that one makes known to him the essential purpose of the profession, the essential quality of the man, so that he may, when the occasion arises, test his people, and not remain in uncertainty whether the man who presents himself as a member of one of the named estates is truly what he claims to be, or whether he has only adopted the name, while possessing hardly even the most insignificant of the essential qualities.
