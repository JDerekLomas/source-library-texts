# On the MOST ILLUSTRIOUS MAN
## Master MICHAEL PRAETORIUS.
### His harmonic compositions, which are truly divine.
### An Epode by GEORG REMUS.

<image-desc>Decorative woodcut initial T featuring floral and vine motifs.</image-desc>
AMPHION, the founder of the city of Thebes,
(If faith is to be had in the old stories)
Drew stones along by the sound of his Lute; and the Thracian ORPHEUS
Tamed the wild Lions with his learned lyre.
LET US NOT MARVEL at these deeds. But let us marvel at this:
That while our bodies remain here below,
PRAETORIUS, by the sweetness of his song,
Lifts our souls UP TO THE STARS.
For who among mortals is so dull, so foolish, so slow-witted and senseless,
Who is so leaden of heart,
That he would not think himself to be among the blessed inhabitants of heaven;
And feel himself added to the Choirs of Angels,
As soon as Polyhymnia <note>The Muse of sacred poetry and hymn-singing.</note> begins to sing in the temple,
And soothes the depths of his breast?
You shall be called the SAXON ORPHEUS; nay, to me, PRAETORIUS,
You are the GERMAN ORPHEUS:
Indeed, I believe you are a second Son of Jesse <note>original: "JESSAEuS"; a reference to King David, the biblical psalmist and harpist.</note> given to our shores
From the high Pole of heaven.
Live on, a guest-friend to the earth: but after death, in heaven,
You shall compete with the Angels upon your Lyre <note>original: "Chely"; a term for a lyre or lute made from a tortoise shell.</note>.

->Written on the spur of the moment<- <note>original: "ἐκ τοῦ παραχρῆμα"</note>

->Composed at Nuremberg on the day before the Kalends of May,<- <note>April 30th.</note>

# To all distinguished Musicians, Chapel Masters,
### and Vocal Teachers <note>original: "Phonaséis"</note> of the German Nation, the Author wishes,
### alongside proper greetings and due respect for their station,
### his service, God’s blessing, and all
### prosperity:

**S**ince the most prominent matters in this Third Volume <note>original: "Tomo"</note> of the *Musical Treatise* <note>original: "Syntagmatis Musici"</note> are for the most part the Author’s own thoughts and inventions—which he particularly observed and diligently noted while organizing various Electoral and Princely concert music in different places, and through further reflection and thought, transferred into many other concerts for practical use, and finally set down upon paper: Except only that he has incorporated and placed into this volume certain information from the Italians <note>original: "Italorum Information"</note>. This he found partly in several prefaces of their printed concerts (just as his own draft and work were nearly finished) and partly received through oral reports from good people who have traveled in Italy. And yet, being taught by experience <note>original: "experientiâ edocto"</note>, he is not at all unaware that, especially during the publication <note>original: "evulgation"</note> of new books, detractors are always found. Out of an innate envious malice, these people strive to the utmost to criticize well-intentioned work and to make it appear trivial or even hateful to everyone. Therefore, he asks all those experienced in this art that they might interpret this work of his in the best possible light <note>original: "in optimam partem interpretiren"</note>, judge it candidly, and whatever is to be improved therein, either communicate it to him and all music-lovers for their particular benefit and profit, or release it publicly through the press: Since many eyes always see more than one <note>original: "Siquidem oculi semper plus vident, quàm oculus"</note>.
