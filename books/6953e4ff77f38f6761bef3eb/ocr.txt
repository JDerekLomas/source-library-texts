# Books recommended by Newton himself to be read before Principia:—

<margin>Geometry</margin>Euclids Elements
Elements of Conic Sections.
Elements of Curves, either de Witt, or de la Hire's lat. treatise of
Conic Sections, or Dr Barrow's Epitome of Apollonius.

<margin>Algebra</margin>Bartholinus's introduction — then peruse such <unclear>Ps.</unclear> as are <unclear>yet</unclear>
put scattered up and down in Cartes's Geometry & other Algebraic
writings of Francis Schooten — with all the Commentaries and Solu-
tions of the Problems.

<margin>Astronomy</margin>Short account of Copernican System, and of Gassendus's Astronomy
So much of Mercator's as relates to same system & newer dis-
coveries by Telescopes.
these sufficient for understanding Newton's Book: but Hugenius
Horologium Oscillatorium, will make one much better.

First perusal — understand the propositions with some of the
<gloss>Definitions</gloss> Demonstrations which are easier than the <unclear>rest</unclear>:
Proceed from easier to harder — after first 60 pages, pass
on to III^d Book. When design of that is seen then turn back to
other propos. at pleasure.
vid: Newton Corr: in Cotes
