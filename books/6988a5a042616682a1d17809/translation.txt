One can never wonder enough that almost all nations have finally, by this path, come into experience since the very beginning of the world—from the year Adam first committed sin—up until the exact times of the Roman Emperors Frederick and Henry. <note>Likely referring to the medieval Hohenstaufen Emperors Frederick I (Barbarossa) and Henry VI, often cited in historical political treatises of this era.</note> [It is wondered] whether it ought to be known if it will last through 7,000 years of Christ, or why it has not been reached for so many years. Although they did come (as is evident in the *Scenography* <note>original: "Scenographia," referring to the art of perspective or world-description</note> of Alexander, who reduced the whole world to his fingers) to one another in time of peace—into Turkey, Egypt, and Ethiopia—at which time they admit that there might be peace for the world.

But—in which they are swept along—so that in time of war they may use the arts of war, to render justice (if possible) and wage war, as was seen under Henry V. <note>Refers to Henry V of England, famously successful in the Hundred Years' War.</note> Then finally they came with Philip, Duke of Burgundy, who reduced everything to a narrow strait, until they finally reached the point of asking: from where did it come, and in what way did it come? Namely, from those neighboring kings; from which it can be known from these things where or what it is.

And for this reason, it is strange that no one has yet come to provide instruction on it, even though almost everyone desires it. And it is said that if someone finally does not know these things, it may truly be said of him that it is due to either ignorance or neglect. Indeed, a few years ago it became known to all that they were thinking about this. It also became known to other Kings; but in the end, they have not yet reached where they wished.

But it is said: if someone finally comes by the right way—even if he does not wish it to be immediately known to everyone—he will nonetheless open the way. And in no matter will so much damage ever be done, no matter how small the thing done, because finally it is possible to have it: since at last, he found that part of the work which is true in <term>science</term> <note>original: "scientia," meaning structured knowledge or skill</note>. If ever there was a potentate and lord who has experimented with and experienced the instability and fickleness of friends, it is certainly Frederick, King of Bohemia, Count Palatine of the Rhine, and Prince-Elector, etc. This was proven by both high- and low-ranking persons. The fickleness of several of his Majesty's friends (among whom I mean those who always wish to be the very best in times of peace <note>original: "tempore pacis"</note> and prosperity) became known to the whole world through their abandonment of him when it mattered: namely, whether they would assist and stand by his Majesty in the misfortune of wartime <note>original: "tempore belli"</note>. How some of his servants, bound to him by duty and oath, behaved—partly out of untimely and premature fear, and how they claimed they had to provide for their wives and children, oh, **what a lame excuse** <note>original: "qualis calva excusatio"</note>—toward his Majesty was like a hare caught by a drum, as is evident to the whole world. I will say nothing of his Majesty’s blood-relatives, who did not exercise their kinship but allowed themselves to be persuaded and talked out of it by **Spanish-aligned** <note>original: "Spanniolo-sirten," referring to those influenced by or favoring Spanish/Habsburg interests</note> individuals through their highly damaging and deceitful **assurances of sincerity** <note>original: "Sincerationes"</note>. Thus, they left him standing in a most difficult position, like Christ on the **Mount of Olives** <note>A biblical reference to Gethsemane, where Jesus was abandoned by his disciples before his arrest.</note>, as is plain for all people to see. Indeed, just as Christ was forsaken by all his disciples and delivered to the Jews, so was King Frederick left as a prey to the Spanish-aligned even by those he presumed to be his most faithful. Truly, a fine "Passion" <note>A play on the "Passion of Christ," suggesting Frederick’s suffering follows a similar narrative of betrayal.</note> could be written about it. Therefore, his Majesty may well say with **Tarquin the Proud** <note>The legendary last king of Rome, who was exiled.</note>: **that he finally knew who his faithful friends were and who were unfaithful, since he could reward neither** <note>original Latin: "se demum cognovisse, quos fidos amicos, quos infidos habuisset, cum neutris gratiam referre posset."</note>. This abandonment by such **fair-weather friends** <note>original: "Maul- vnnd Tellerfreunden," literally "mouth- and plate-friends," meaning those who are only friends for the sake of talk and food.</note> has brought the pious Frederick into such a **labyrinth** that his House will have plenty to chew and ruminate on for a good while. He has truly experienced the saying: **If the times are cloudy, you will be alone.** <note>original Latin: "Tempora si fuerint nubila, solus eris," a famous line from Ovid's "Tristia" regarding the loss of friends after one falls from favor.</note>
