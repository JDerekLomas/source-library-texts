For just as here, through the power of God's word, so many fish are miraculously gathered and caught—just as elsewhere in the Gospel, by the command and powerful <margin>Matthew 8.</margin> speaking of the word of God in Christ, the raging sea <note>original: "Möhr," an archaic term for *Meer* (sea).</note> is stilled and is obedient to the word—so too should many people be spiritually caught by Christ through the "fishers." ## Summary of this Gospel.

1 That we should apply ourselves with all seriousness to hearing God’s word, not only outwardly according to the letter, but inwardly with our hearts. For this is the fishing, as well as the catch, the <term>conversion</term> <note>original: "bekörung"; in this context, it refers to the spiritual "luring" or drawing of the soul toward God</note>, the food, and the nourishment of our souls for eternal life.

2 That without Christ—without his grace and illumination—there is nothing but vain darkness, vain error, and damnation in all of a person's life, teaching, and conduct.

3 That our hearts and thoughts should always be ordered in God through Christ, and the little boats of our minds should be guided into the heights, so that in all our doing and letting, we intend only the Lord God with a pure and sincere heart.

4 That Christ unexpectedly gives Peter and his companions so many fish is an indication of the riches of his grace and divine providence over all those who love him and obey his word.

5 That a person should ever learn to know himself more and more, and often confess and acknowledge his sins in the humility of his heart to the High Priest, Christ. If he wishes, like the dear Peter, to draw Christ closer to himself, he must hear the voice: "Do not be afraid," from the heart of Christ.

6 The summary concerns the power and efficacy of Jesus Christ, the living Word of God: Whoever truly recognizes Christ will give leave to all other things in his heart and will diligently follow him into the Kingdom of Heaven.
