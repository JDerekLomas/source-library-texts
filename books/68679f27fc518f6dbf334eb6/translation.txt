The learned men <note>original: "Literati"</note> in the universities might wonder why I would write such a strange book, namely, *Theologized Astrology* <note>original: "Astrologiam Theologizatam"</note>, and especially those blind theologians who reject astronomy and astrology as a useless art. Just as, on the other hand, some astronomers do not think much of theology, believing they may well study astronomy without theology. But this shall not be; they must both be together. One discipline <note>original: "facultet"</note> explains the other.

I am writing nothing new, then, but that which God Himself wrote with His finger in the beginning, and He placed them together within man. For since man has within himself that from which he is made—and is that very thing—he therefore has nothing else to study and learn than precisely that which he has within him; and he must become it himself, so that he may become perfect. Thus, he has his **SOUL** <note>original: "ANIMAM"</note> from the firmament <note>The "firmament" refers to the celestial sphere of stars and planets, believed to influence human nature</note>, which is not only a life for the body and dwells in the body, but is also the entire firmament itself. # That Man, with his Conduct
## and Life, Beginning and End, Action and Omission, Lies within the Firmament like a Child in the Mother's Womb.

The astronomers, when they erect a figure of the heavens <note>original: "Figuram Cœli Erigiren," meaning to cast an astrological chart</note> according to the hour of birth—that is, when they set a nativity <note>a horoscope</note> for someone—they divide the entire sky into twelve houses. To the **First House**, they assign the head and the face.
To the **2nd**, the neck and the throat.
To the **3rd**, the shoulders, arms, and hands.
To the **4th**, the chest, lungs, and diaphragm.
To the **5th**, the stomach, liver, heart, sides, and the back.

To the **11th**, the shins down to the ankles or knuckles.

For they do not contemplate God’s Word; they engage in useless chatter, go to the tavern, to the beer, etc. (To the wine).
