The ridge, however, which is gripped at the top by the aforementioned solid upright timbers <note>original: "monoxylis," referring to beams made of a single piece of wood</note>, must be positioned forward facing the wall. This ensures there is a structural support to receive the projectiles or counter-measures sent against the ram. For if very large stones cast down from the wall, or transverse beams descending with equal weight and balance, should happen to strike the ram while it is uncovered as it strikes the wall with force, one of two things will happen: either the ram itself will be broken as it is forced downward, or it will strike and kill those moving it.

Such is the front Tortoise, which also contains the supports for the ram according to the required distance. There is then a second Tortoise, which is lower in height and smaller, and two others behind it that are smaller still; these are necessary to protect the entrance of the machine. It is better that there be several smaller units (as we have already said) so that they may be brought forward without exhaustion and be built from small timbers. One should avoid building a single massive unit, as it would require enormous timbers that are difficult to find, and it would be moved only slowly and with great labor. These structures are instead arranged in a functional sequence.
