Having offered salutations to **Brahma** <note>The Creator deity</note>, **Shiva**, the **Sun**, the **Moon**, **Mars**, **Mercury**, **Jupiter**, **Venus**, **Saturn**, the **Lord of the Ganas** <note>Ganesha</note>, and the **Guru** <note>The teacher</note>; I shall now explain this work. In this Kali Yuga <note>In Hindu cosmology, the "Age of Darkness" or the current era where spiritual knowledge is diminished</note>, many people find themselves confused by contradictory or overly brief teachings; therefore, to bring delight to the world, I clarify this work, which was originally composed with great skill by the sage **Varahamihira** <note>A 6th-century polymath and the most famous astronomer-astrologer of ancient India</note>. Salutations to that radiant Sun, who is the soul of all!

In this science, there are four essential prerequisites that must be understood: the **Subject Matter**, the **Purpose**, the **Relationship**, and the **Audience**. 

The **Relationship** <term>sambandha: the logical link between the text and its meaning</term> consists of the connection between the "expresser" (the words) and the "expressed" (the meaning), or rather, the relationship between the "means" and the "end." Here, the **Means** is this sacred scripture, and the **End** is the attainment of knowledge. This relationship is also established because this work is considered an limb of the **Vedas** <note>Specifically *Jyotisha* or astronomy/astrology, which is one of the six *Vedangas* or "limbs" of Vedic study</note>. 

The **Subject Matter** <term>abhidheya: what the book is actually about</term> to be understood here includes: 
*   **The Ascendant** <note>original: *lagna*. The sign of the zodiac rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth.</note>
*   **The Hora** <note>A division of a zodiac sign into two halves (15 degrees each).</note>
*   **The Decans** <note>original: *drekkana*. A division of a zodiac sign into three parts (10 degrees each).</note>
*   **The Ninth-Part** <note>original: *navamsha*. A crucial 3.20-degree division used to determine the hidden strength of planets.</note>
*   **The Twelfth-Part and the Thirty-Part** <note>original: *dvadasamsha* and *trimshamsha*. Further mathematical subdivisions of the zodiac.</note>
*   **Planetary Alignments** <note>original: *graha-kundalika-yoga*. The specific patterns and "yokes" formed by planets in a birth chart.</note>

The **Purpose** <term>prayojana: the reason for studying the text</term> is to enable the declaration of the "fruits" of one's past actions—both auspicious and inauspicious. By following the teachings of sages like **Satyacharya**, one achieves success in both this world and the next. Without stating such a purpose, those who listen would not find a reason to engage with the text.

Regarding the authority over this science: it is said that the **Vedas** <note>The most ancient and sacred scriptures of Hinduism</note> must be studied along with their six "limbs" or auxiliary sciences. This is not a mere suggestion, but a command for those who seek spiritual merit. Thus, the great teacher from the city of Avanti, **Varahamihira**, a descendant of the Magadha Brahmins who obtained the grace of a boon from the **Sun**, intending to compile a compendium of the **Science of Light** <term>Jyotisha-shastra</term>, composes this work focusing on the **Horary** <term>Hora: the branch of astrology dealing with individual birth charts and timing</term> branch. ...the Sun, the source of all majesty and the enemy of darkness; may he grant us the power of speech. What kind of Sun is he? He is the one with many rays <note>original: "aneka-kirana"</note>, which means he is the "thousand-rayed one" <note>A traditional title for the Sun, signifying his infinite reach</note>. He is the **lamp of the three worlds** <term>trailokya-dipa: the light that illuminates the heavens, the earth, and the lower realms</term>, existing for the sake of illumination. 

Furthermore, he is envisioned as one who possesses a form; he supports and holds the moon, which is marked with the sign of the hare <note>original: "shashanka," a common Sanskrit poetic name for the moon, where the craters are seen as a hare</note>. Because the moon is essentially made of water, the light of the Sun's rays is reflected in it. These spreading rays of the Sun, when falling upon the watery moon, dispel the darkness of the night, appearing as if they are a secondary light. As it is stated in the **Vedas** <note>The primary sacred scriptures</note>: "The solar ray named *Sushumna* is the moon."

Alternatively, the "Moon-bearer" may refer to the Great God **Shiva** <note>original: "Mahadeva"</note>, who is envisioned in this form. This is because the Lord is said to have an **eight-fold form** <term>ashtamurti: the eight manifestations of Shiva as the five elements, the sun, the moon, and the conscious soul</term>. Within him, this entire universe—the crown of the world consisting of earth, water, and the other elements—is contained as his body.

The Sun is also described as the "path of those who will not be born again" <note>original: "apunarjanmanah," referring to those who have achieved liberation from the cycle of rebirth</note>. He is the **gateway to liberation** <term>moksha-dvara</term>. According to tradition, there are two paths for the soul: the "Way of the Gods" <term>devayana</term> and the "Way of the Ancestors" <term>pitriyana</term>. The moon is the gateway for the "Way of the Ancestors," through which one reaches the heavenly realms. However, the wise know the gateway to final liberation to be through the Sun; for it is said that those entitled to liberation pass through the **Solar Orb** <term>surya-mandala</term> to reach the ultimate state. Thus, the venerable **Vyasa** <note>The legendary sage and author of the Mahabharata and Puranas</note> has also spoken of the "gate of heaven"...

<margin>The Sun as the Gateway to Liberation</margin>

He is the **gateway for all beings** <note>original: "praja-dvaram," meaning the door through which life enters the world</note>, the **gateway to liberation** <term>moksha-dvaram: the path by which the soul escapes the cycle of rebirth</term>, and the **third heaven** <note>original: "trivistapa," the celestial abode of the gods</note>. Indeed, those who know the Self through the fire of knowledge, as well as those who do not, find that his light resides within the heart <note>The text suggests the Sun's essence is identical to the inner light of the human soul</note>.

Thus says the **Veda** <term>Shruti: "that which is heard," referring to the authoritative revealed scriptures</term>: "**The Sun is the Soul of all that moves and all that is stationary**" <note>This is a famous quote from the Rigveda, 1.115.1</note>. He is the Soul of the Vedic tradition and of the fixed earth. To him, the sacrificers offer their worship. As it is said, the offering made into the fire reaches the Sun; from the Sun, rain is born; from rain comes food; and from food, all living beings arise. 

He is the protector, the Lord, and the primary essence of all things. Because he makes himself seen as the one Self in all things, he is the guardian of the universe. In the place where he dwells, there is no <unclear>rising or setting</unclear>. 

As it has been stated, the Sun is the protector of the world. The Sun's rays are like a house for the inhabitants of the spheres. It is seen that the dissolution and stable existence of the worlds are distinguished by these solar rays. The **All-Pervasive Lord** <term>Vibhu: a title for a deity that is eternal and exists everywhere</term> is the cause of the dissolution, destruction, and the origin or birth of the various worlds. He is the master of their maintenance. He is the Lord who is liberated from the divisions of the three periods of time: **past, present, and future**. 

This Truth is described in many ways in the **Vedas** <note>The sacred knowledge</note>. As it is written: "They call him Indra, Mitra, Varuna, and Agni; he is the celestial, well-winged Garuda. **Truth is one, but the wise speak of it in many ways**; they call it Agni, Yama, and Matarishvan" <note>A central tenet of Hindu philosophy from Rigveda 1.164.46, asserting that different gods are manifestations of a single Reality</note>. १

Some scholars desire a specific interpretation of the science of *Hora*. They argue that the term is formed through the **dropping of the first and last letters** <note>original: "paravarna-lopatta." It is formed through the dropping of letters from the word **Day and Night** <term>Ahoratra: the Sanskrit compound for a full 24-hour period, from 'Ahas' (day) and 'Ratra' (night)</term>. When the first letter 'A' and the last syllable 'tra' are removed from *Aho-ra-tra*, the word *Hora* remains as the distinguished term. 

But why is the word *Hora* extracted from "day and night" in this way? It is because knowledge of auspicious and inauspicious results is gained through the power of the **ascendant** <term>Lagna: the specific point of the zodiac rising on the eastern horizon at the time of an event or birth</term> and the specific passage of time. Therefore, in the science of astrology, the term *Hora* is derived in this specific manner. This is the intended meaning of the **Teacher** <note>original: "Acharya." Likely referring to Varahamihira or a similar foundational authority in the field.</note>, as this perspective is not contradicted by other schools of thought.

### The Origin and Purpose of the Term "Hora"

And so it is stated in the **Saravali** <term>Saravali: a foundational 8th-century astrological treatise by Kalyanavarman</term>: "In the common world, the word *Hora* is formed by dropping the first and last letters [of *Ahoratra*]. Within it, the entire assembly of planets and zodiacal signs are considered." 

What then is the purpose of this science? To this, the text replies: It reveals the **karma earned in a previous life** <note>original: "karmarjitam purvabhave"</note>, whether it be good or bad.

In this current life, the science fully manifests the **ripening** <note>original: "paktim," literally a row or a cooking/ripening process</note> of that karma. Whether the deeds of a previous birth were auspicious, inauspicious, or mixed, this science reveals their "cooking" or maturation. 

As stated in the **Laghu Jataka** <note>The OCR reads "Lapattatake," which is a corruption of Varahamihira's "Laghu Jataka," a famous 6th-century primer on astrology</note>: "This science reveals the attainment of the fruits of those actions, good or bad, which were accumulated in another birth."

### On Fate and Human Effort

Now, a question arises: "If good or bad results are inevitable, what is the point of revealing them?" 

To this, it is said that good and bad results are of two kinds: those earned through **visible actions** <term>Drishta-karma: actions whose causes are observable in this life</term> and those earned through **unseen actions** <term>Adrishta-karma: latent potential or merit/demerit from past lives</term>.

Among these, the results of **visible actions** manifest through the sequence of ripening of skillful efforts. For example, if a person desires the fruit of a successful journey, they must act through practical means such as civic planning or military expeditions. When a certain auspicious time for a journey is determined, the results of **unseen karma** manifest in a specific category.

Likewise, if an inauspicious fate is indicated by the stars, one should bring about **pacification** through rites such as **Shanti** <term>Shanti: propitiatory rituals or prayers intended to calm negative planetary influences</term>.

As **Yavaneshvara** <term>Yavaneshvara: "Lord of the Greeks," the author of the Yavanajataka, representing the early influence of Greek horoscopic astrology on Indian thought</term> says:

> "Whatever destiny is fixed for people, arising from the planetary combinations at their birth, it should be met by the wise with their own efforts toward prosperity."

Through various explanations regarding the movement of time and portents, the experience of karma that has arrived is said to be made known by the **seven planets** <note>The Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn</note>.

In that system, **Aries, Gemini, Leo, Libra, Sagittarius, and Aquarius** are known as the **odd-numbered signs** <term>viṣama-rāśi: signs corresponding to the numbers 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, and 11</term>. For the **even-numbered signs** <term>sama-rāśi: Taurus, Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio, Capricorn, and Pisces</term>, the rules are applied in the reverse order.

In the **even signs**, the first **five degrees** belong to **Venus** <note>original: "śukra"</note>. Following this, the next **seven degrees** belong to **Mercury** <note>original: "budha"</note>, making a total of twelve degrees. Then, the next **eight degrees** belong to **Jupiter** <note>original: "guru"</note>, totaling twenty. After that, the next **five degrees** belong to **Saturn** <note>original: "vṛśaneḥ," a likely scribal error for "shaneḥ"</note>, totaling twenty-five. Finally, the last **five degrees** belong to **Mars** <note>original: "kuja"</note>, completing the thirty degrees of the sign.

In contrast, for the **odd-numbered signs**, the five planetary rulers—Mars, Saturn, Jupiter, Mercury, and Venus—are arranged differently. It is stated in the sacred tradition <note>original: "śrutaḥ"</note> that in **odd signs**, the portions are assigned as follows:
*   **Five degrees** for the **Son of Earth** <note>Mars; original: "dharaṇisuta"</note>
*   **Five degrees** for the **Son of the Sun** <note>Saturn; original: "saura"</note>
*   **Eight degrees** for the **Teacher** <note>Jupiter; original: "guru"</note>
*   **Seven degrees** for **Mercury** <note>original: "budha"</note>
*   **Five degrees** for the **Descendant of Bhrigu** <note>Venus; original: "śukra"</note>

In the **even signs**, the sequence of degrees is reversed, belonging to **Venus**, the **Son of the Moon** <note>Mercury; original: "śaśisuta"</note>, **Jupiter**, the **Son of the Sun** <note>Saturn</note>, and the **Son of the Earth** <note>Mars</note>.

### The Junction Points of the Signs

The text now addresses the **junction points** <term>sandhi: a transition zone or "joint" between two astrological signs</term>. Specifically, the end of the **House of the Moon** <note>Cancer; original: "śaśibhavana"</note>, the end of **Scorpio** <note>original: "ali"</note>, and the end of **Pisces** <note>original: "mīna"</note> are of particular importance. These are the three points where a Water sign ends and a Fire sign begins.

The very end of these signs—**Cancer, Scorpio, and Pisces**—is known in the world of astrology as the **Knot's End** <note>original: "gaṃśatam," likely a corruption of "gaṇḍānta," a critical transition point in a horoscope</note>. The sages have taught that at these specific junctions—where **Cancer** meets **Leo**, **Scorpio** meets **Sagittarius**, and **Pisces** meets **Aries**—there is a sensitive overlap.

### The Six-Fold Divisions (Ṣaḍvarga)

Now, the technical names for the groups of divisions—the **Drekkāṇa**, **Horā**, **Navāṃśaka**, **Dvādaśabhāga**, and **Triṃśāṃśa**—are explained for practical use. These are the categories where a planet resides in its own territory.

The definitions are as follows:
*   **Drekkāṇa**: This is the **one-third** part of a sign <note>10 degrees</note>.
*   **Horā**: This is **half** of a sign <note>15 degrees</note>.
*   **Navāṃśaka**: This is the **ninth part** of a sign <note>3 degrees and 20 minutes</note>.
*   **Dvādaśabhāga**: This is the **twelfth part** of a sign <note>2 degrees and 30 minutes</note>.
*   **Triṃśāṃśa**: This is the **thirtieth part** of a sign <note>1 degree</note>.
*   **Kṣetra**: This refers to the **entire sign** or the "field" ruled by a planet.

All of these are known as the **Varga** <term>varga: divisional category</term> of the planetary lords. In this way, when a planet is in its own division within these six categories, it is said to be in its own "varga."

However, it must be noted that for certain planets, all six divisions are not possible. For the **Sun** and the **Moon**, there is an absence of **Triṃśāṃśa** portions <note>only the five "star planets" rule these 30 subdivisions</note>. Conversely, for **Mars and the others**, the **Horā** divisions are not applicable in the same way <note>traditionally, only the Sun and Moon own the two Horas of a sign</note>. Therefore, the "six-fold division" is applied as is possible for each specific planet. As the tradition says, the sign is the field, the half is the Horā, and the third is the Drekkāṇa...

### The Classification of Mobility

Likewise, the "bodies" or natures of these signs are categorized as movable and so forth. Starting from the Ram, they are designated in sequential order as follows:

*   **Movable** <term>Chara: signs characterized by change, movement, and initiative</term>: The Ram <note>Aries</note> is movable.
*   **Fixed** <term>Sthira: signs characterized by stability, persistence, and resistance to change</term>: The Bull <note>Taurus</note> is fixed.
*   **Dual-natured** <term>Dvisvabhava: signs that share both movable and fixed qualities; "common" signs</term>: The Twins <note>Gemini</note> and those following are dual-natured.

This pattern should be understood for the Crab <note>Cancer</note> and the rest as well. Therefore:
1.  **Movable signs:** Ram, Crab, Balance, and Horned Goat <note>Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn</note>.
2.  **Fixed signs:** Bull, Lion, Scorpion, and Water-Bearer <note>Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius</note>.
3.  **Dual signs:** Twins, Maiden, Archer, and Fish <note>Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces</note>.

The practical application <note>original: "prayojane"</note> is that those born in movable signs have a restless or wandering nature; those born in fixed signs have a stable and steady nature; and those born in dual signs possess a mixed temperament.

### The Lords of the Directions

Furthermore, the signs are categorized by their natural directions. The Ram, the Bull, the Twins, and the Crab, along with their respective trines <note>signs located in the 5th and 9th positions from them</note>, are the lords of the four directions starting with the East.

*   **East:** The Ram, the Lion, and the Archer <note>Aries, Leo, Sagittarius—the Fire trine</note>.
*   **South:** The Bull, the Maiden, and the Horned Goat <note>Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn—the Earth trine</note>.
*   **West:** The Twins, the Balance, and the Water-Bearer <note>Gemini, Libra, Aquarius—the Air trine</note>.
*   **North:** The Crab, the Scorpion, and the Fish <note>Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces—the Water trine</note>.

The use of these directions is essential for **Lost Horoscopy** <term>Naṣṭajātaka: the branch of astrology used to reconstruct a birth chart when the birth time is unknown</term> and for the "Science of Secret Thefts" <note>original: "hṛtikā rahasya vijñānam"</note>, which helps in determining the direction in which a thief has fled or where a lost object is located.
