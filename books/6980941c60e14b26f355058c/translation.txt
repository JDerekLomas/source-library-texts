The Rules of Dialectics <note>original: "Dialecticorum Præcepta." In this context, dialectics refers to the formal logic and structured methods of debate used in traditional academic philosophy.</note> prescribe certain forms of debating which conclude so necessarily that reason—trusting in them—can still conclude something certain by the power of the logical form alone, even if it is somehow distracted from a clear and attentive consideration of the deduction <note>original: "illationis"</note> itself. Yet, we notice that truth often slips away from these chains, while those who use them remain entangled within them. This happens less frequently to others; indeed, we find through experience that even the most subtle fallacies <note>original: "sophismata"</note> hardly ever deceive anyone using a pure mind <note>original: "pura mente." This refers to the natural, unclouded capacity for human reason that functions without being forced into artificial structures.</note>, but instead usually deceive the sophists themselves.
