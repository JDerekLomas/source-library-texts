# THE GREAT BOOK OF NATURE

## or the Philosophical and Hermetic Apocalypse.

A curious work, in which one treats of occult Philosophy, the understanding of the hieroglyphs of the ancients, the Society of the Brothers of the Rose-Cross <note>The Rosicrucians</note>, the Transmutation of metals, and the communication of man with superior and intermediary beings between himself and the <term>Grand Architect<note>A Masonic term for God or a supreme creative force.</note></term>. Seen by a Society of <term>Unknown Philosophers<note>Original: "Ph ∴ Inc ∴" (Philosophes Inconnus); a title used in Martinism and certain Masonic grades.</note></term> and published by D.... From year I, until the year 1790. At the South <note>Masonic terminology indicating the location of the Lodge or publisher.</note>, and from the *Printing House of Truth* (1790), octavo, half-cloth (*Extremely rare*). 40 francs.

> "This work was found — says Brother Tacxsi, <term>Unknown Philosopher</term> — among the old manuscripts of the library of the monks of the Convent of Saint Vincent in Lisbon in 1780. It was copied exactly, translated from Portuguese and Latin into French, and copied under the faithful guard of a truly zealous Mason, at the risk of being hanged at any moment."

Indeed, it contains the deepest initiatory teaching. A detailed analysis can be found in "*The Masonic Universe*," 1837, pp. 573 to 575. The mysteries of Initiation are described therein at length in an engaging and deeply esoteric form, in nine chapters divided by verses. He who can grasp the entire intimate meaning of this work will arrive, if he is worthy, into the sanctuary of the true Rose-Cross, and all the secrets of Nature will be revealed to him. Here is, moreover, what the author himself says of it: "One finds, in the Hermetic Apocalypse, an exact account of the reception and conduct of the <term>Unknown Philosophers</term>; all the secrets of the Brother Masons are revealed therein. The Transmutation of metals and the universal medicine are shown there in their full light. In short, it is truly an adept's manual." The second part contains a most precious work entitled "The Language of Adepts, or Abridged Dictionary of Philosophy, with the explanation of words and hieroglyphs"; a collection of secrets extracted from an Arabic manuscript, of which the following is a list: Composition of philosophical vinegar — Water to soften all metals — Various chemical secrets — Human tincture or the antidote of the ancients — The Lamp of Life. A mystical description of the <term>Great Work<note>Original: "Grand-Œuvre"; the alchemical process of creating the Philosopher's Stone.</note></term>, etc... It is the key to Paracelsus, Saint-Martin, Van Helmont, and all the authors who have written on hermetic philosophy, and the signal rarity of this work makes it the object of ardent and almost always fruitless research.

### IX.

#### Libavius's opinion on potable gold.
<term>Potable gold: (Latin: *aurum potabile*) a legendary medicinal elixir believed by many alchemists to be a universal cure-all made from dissolved gold.</term>

This author <note>Andreas Libavius (c. 1555–1616), a German physician and chemist known for writing one of the first systematic chemistry textbooks.</note> says, in *Book Two*, page 79, that the ancients who spoke so much of **potable gold** did not mean by this name a liquid drawn from gold. He asserts that this metal played no part in the preparations that bore this name. By potable gold, they meant a liquid *par excellence*, a rare, expensive, and precious liquid. They gave it the name of gold for the reason that the latter has always been precious and sought after.

He supports his opinion with the fact that the ancients regarded the internal use of metals as a **poison**; it is therefore certain, he adds, that if gold was regarded as harmful, they would not have made it into a liquid to be taken internally at that time.

# PREFACE (*)
