# Ritual for the Falling of Lizards

In front of the person [affected by the omen], one should place a <term>Kalasha|A ceremonial copper or brass water pot used in Vedic rituals</term> filled with water from sacred pilgrimage sites. Having decorated it with cloths and garlands, place it upon a bed of uncooked rice. Inside, place <term>Panchamrita|The 'five nectars': a mixture of milk, curd, ghee, honey, and sugar</term>, the five gems, and the barks and leaves of five sacred trees <note>original: *pañca-tvak-pallavāni*; typically refers to the Peepal, Gular, Pilkhan, Banyan, and Mango trees</note>. **|| 51 ||** One should deposit the branches and the five gems—everything—into the vessel. One should then worship the <term>Lokapalas|The 'World Protectors' or deities guarding the eight cardinal directions</term> in their proper order using incense, flowers, and other offerings. **|| 51 ||** <note>The verse numbering repeats in the original manuscript.</note>

Having established the ritual fire in front, one should perform the <term>Homa-karma|A fire sacrifice where offerings are made into the consecrated flames</term>. A person who is composed and focused should perform one hundred and eight oblations. **|| 52 ||**

Alternatively, one may perform a fire sacrifice of one thousand and eight oblations using sesame seeds or unbroken rice, reciting the <term>Mrityunjaya|The 'Death-Conquering' mantra dedicated to Lord Shiva</term> while using sacrificial wood such as <term>Khadira|The wood of the Acacia catechu tree, prized for its durability in ritual fires</term>. **|| 53 ||**

O King! One should have the Mrityunjaya mantra recited according to one's capacity. At the conclusion of the pacification rite, one should offer oblations of <term>Masha|Black gram lentils</term>. **|| 54 ||**

Then, one should perform the <term>Abhisheka|A ritual sprinkling or pouring of sanctified water</term> upon the head of the <term>Yajamana|The patron or person for whom the ritual is being performed</term>. This should be done according to the traditional rules using the Vedic hymns dedicated to <term>Varuna|The ancient deity of the celestial waters and cosmic order</term>. One should then give gifts of gold, silver, and clothing according to one's financial ability. **|| 55 ||** Following this, one should conclude by providing a feast [for the priests and the community]. **|| 56 ||**

In this manner, one should perform the pacification ritual according to the <term>Shaunaka-vidhana|The ritual procedures established by the sage Shaunaka</term>. By carrying this out, protection, prosperity, and an increase in fame are attained. **|| 57 ||**
