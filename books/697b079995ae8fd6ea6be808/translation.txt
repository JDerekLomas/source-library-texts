## To the Gracious Reader.

<image-desc>A large, ornate woodcut initial 'D' decorated with complex intertwining scrollwork and floral motifs.</image-desc>
That the <term>knowledge of nature<note>During this period, the study of the physical world (natural philosophy) was considered a "second book" of revelation, alongside the Bible.</note></term> leads to the knowledge of God: and that the knowledge of God is the human being’s only and most certain path to attaining the <term>salvation of the soul<note>original: "Seelen seeligkeit"</note></term>—no sensible person could deny this. If, then, the knowledge of nature is such a magnificent essence, through which a person can reach temporal well-being here and eternal welfare there: who could then be so brutish as to not zealously seek and love the great omnipotence and <term>miraculous works<note>original: "Wunder-werck"</note></term> of God to be fathomed therein? For nature is God’s right hand; what she shows or prescribes to us is, as it were, written by God Himself; it has a profound weight, is and remains constant for all time, and no one can contradict her as they might the other writings of fallible men.
