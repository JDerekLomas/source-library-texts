->17 March 1900.<-

Dear Wilson King,
Thanks for the book, which now I return. I had not seen it before. There is a good deal of sense in it.
You will observe that its back is broken. I think it was so when I received it.
The book I mentioned in my last letter was a much bigger one, not so serious, and with lots of illustrations — a very curious production.
I see that many of the anecdotes in this book of yours correspond to <insert>like those</insert> events and details, which happen now, and which I suppose happened in all ages.

->Yours very truly,<-
->Oliver Lodge<-

## Apparitions, Witchcrafts,
### and other Magical Practices.
->CONTAINING<-
**An Account** of the *Genii* or *Familiar Spirits*, both Good and Bad, that are said to attend Men in this Life; and what sensible Perceptions some Persons have had of them: (particularly the Author's own Experience for many Years.)
**Also** of Appearances of Spirits after Death; Divine Dreams, Divinations, Second Sighted Persons, *&c.*
**Likewise** the Power of Witches, and the reality of other Magical Operations, clearly asserted. With a Refutation of Dr. *Bekker's World bewitch'd*; and other Authors that have opposed the Belief of them.

->By *JONH BEAUMONT*, Gent.<-

# READER.

Having long promis'd the World this Book, perhaps, the Jocose part of Mankind may now be apt to say,

> *Quid ferat hic tanto dignum promissor hiatu?*
> *Parturiunt montes, &c.*

Be it as it may, I have here made good my Promise; and though I may not perswade my self, that all Men will acquiesce in what I here deliver, I not pretending, in this Subject, to Mathematical Demonstration;
