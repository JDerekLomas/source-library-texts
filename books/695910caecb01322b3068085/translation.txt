<image-desc>Ornate decorative woodcut initial letter 'A' featuring elaborate foliage, floral patterns, and scrollwork.</image-desc>Natural History is a majestic subject, <note>original: "Avgusta res est Historia Naturalis"</note> insofar as it is contained within the same boundaries as this very Universe. By this very fact, I believe it is clear how great a burden of labor they place upon their shoulders who undertake to hand it down to posterity. Pliny the Elder <note>Gaius Plinius Secundus (23–79 AD), author of the encyclopedic Naturalis Historia</note> did this once, with a success as happy as his daring was astounding. But did he truly complete it? Certainly he could not: not because of a failing in the greatness of his mind—which could easily have embraced even a second world—but because the material evidence of things was lacking. The cause of that deficiency was not one alone; yet two reasons were more significant. The first was that the Indies, though already known at that time, were not yet sufficiently understood, much less thoroughly examined. This was because they were usually approached not by sea, nor in peace, but by land and through war. ->E P I S T O L A<-
-><note>original: "EPISTOLA"</note> THE EPISTLE<-

The histories of all ages—not to mention daily experience—speak of how armies have been destroyed by the vastness of solitudes and the lack of all necessities, or what is more hostile to the advancement of the sciences. Another reason is that the greater part of the earth, namely the whole of America, lay hidden as if in Chaos, enveloped in the thick darkness of ignorance. Discovered in the times of our grandfathers, revealed in the time of our fathers, and surveyed in our own, it has provided a most fertile horn of plenty <note>original: "cornu," referring to the Cornucopia</note> to be added to the knowledge of the ancients. Because I was myself a witness and recorder of a not insignificant part of these things, under the auspices of the Most Illustrious John Maurice, Prince of Nassau <note>Johan Maurits van Nassau-Siegen (1604–1679), Governor of Dutch Brazil</note>, Governor of Brazil by land and sea, and now Your Highness’s Ambassador to the Imperial Diet, I certainly ought not to hide this talent which God entrusted to me for the purpose of declaring His glory, lest I make myself a useless servant. Striving to distance myself from such a crime, I began to arrange a History—both Natural and Medical—from the observations collected at that time, concerning those things which the West Indies <note>The Americas</note> cherish in their bosom for the benefit of human health. Now, as this work longs to come forth into the light together with the long-desired treasures of Nature from the East Indies, it has deemed it necessary to approach Your Serene Highness. Thus, under the auspices of such a great name, it may not only protect whatever honor it possesses, but, safe under your patronage and favor, it may more easily and effectively prove its worth to others. Indeed, it judges that it must venture this all the more confidently because, in the calamity of the present age and with the storms of war roaring on every side, few secure and tranquil retreats are granted to these Muses <note>The personification of the arts and sciences</note>. These Muses show themselves to be primarily of use to health and—if it is permitted to say so—they seem to wish to extend the fated and decreed spans of living by, as it were, new methods. For this reason also, and because it does not present itself as an interpreter of things devised for the charms of the senses or the allurements of pleasure, or for pomp...
