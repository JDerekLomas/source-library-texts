## In this book is treated:

1. What **GOD** is in His essence. <note>The original German *Wesen* refers to the fundamental nature, being, or substance of God, a central concern of mystical theology.</note> Also
2. How the Son of God was begotten of the Father in a Divine manner.
3. How and what He is. Also
4. How He came into the world for the first time.
5. And in what form He shall appear on the <term>Last Day</term> <note>original: *Jüngsten Tage*; the day of final judgment at the end of the world.</note>.

### Likewise further:

1. What the soul, or the <term>inward invisible man</term> <note>A term used in Christian mysticism to distinguish the spiritual, eternal part of a human from the physical, "outward" body.</note> is in its essence.
2. How he was before the Fall.
3. How he is now, even after the Fall, in this earthly life.
4. And what his form is after death; also
5. How he shall regain his former glory in the life to come and what his form shall be.
6. What kind of new body he shall also receive,
7. From what materials the same [is composed], and
8. What its glory, state, and property shall be.

In all knowledge, when we know God, a certain likeness of God is formed within us. <note>original: "In omni cognitione, cum Deum novimus, fit aliqua similitudo Dei in nobis." ### *Bernard on the Song of Songs,*
### *Sermon 37.*

No one is saved without the knowledge of Self and of God, at least those who have the age and the faculty for knowing. Therefore, know Yourself, so that you may fear God: know Him, so that you may love Him just as much. In the one, you are initiated into Wisdom; in the other, you are perfected. For the fear of the Lord is the beginning of Wisdom, and the fullness of the law is <term>Charity</term> <note>original: *Charitas*; in this context, it refers to the highest form of Christian love—selfless, divine love for God and others.</note>. Thus, you must beware of ignorance of either, just as you cannot be saved without the fear and love of God.

### Augustine, *On the Utility of Believing.*

> No one doubts that all men are either Fools or Wise. However, I now call "Wise" not those who are merely shrewd and clever, but those who possess—as much as a human can—the most firmly perceived **KNOWLEDGE** of Man himself and of God, and whose life and character are in harmony with this knowledge. <note>original: "de utilitate credendi." St. Augustine (354–430 AD) argues here that true wisdom is not just intellectual "cleverness" but a deep realization of the relationship between the human and the divine that changes how one lives.</note>

### Augustine in the book *On Order*, Chapter 8.

> The inquiry of Philosophy is twofold. One part concerns the Soul; the other concerns God. The first causes us to know ourselves; the second, our origin. The former is sweeter to us; the latter is more precious. The former makes us worthy of a blessed life; the latter actually makes us blessed. The first is for those learning; the latter is for those already taught. This is the order of the Study of Wisdom, through which everyone becomes fit to understand the order of things. <note>original: "de Ordine." In this early work, Augustine explores how the study of the liberal arts and the soul prepares the mind to contemplate God, the source of all order.</note>
