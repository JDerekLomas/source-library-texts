### CASSIODORUS of Ticino, <note>original: "Ticinensis"</note> to the most eloquent master of both languages <note>A common Renaissance compliment referring to proficiency in both Latin and Greek.</note> and most distinguished cultivator of the Muses, Lord Bernardino of Padua, <note>original: "Patavino"</note> wishes Everlasting Happiness. Dedicated.

---

Many and many days have passed, my Bernardino, since I was requested by Master Nicolo Zopino <note>Niccolò Zoppino (active 1503–1544) was a prominent Venetian printer known for publishing vernacular Italian literature.</note> to diligently review and correct the present work, so that, once corrected, he might release it once again into the light. He affirmed that much profit for him and no small praise for me would result from this endeavor. Being unable to rightfully refuse his honest request—not for the fleeting honor that I might gain from my commendable labor, but to satisfy the reasonable prayers of a friend—I took up the task (laborious though it was) to uproot the errors that had sprouted in the aforementioned work. Previously, it lay in print in a state far from well-corrected, no differently than a glittering gem lying in the vilest filth; because of this, it brought much more dishonor than praise to its excellent author. I, nevertheless, as far as the powers of my weak intellect allowed, have restored it to its original brilliance with accurate vigilance, while not neglecting the observation of the rules of the most polished vernacular <term>volgar: the Italian language as spoken and written by the people, as opposed to Latin</term> tongue. This shall be a cause of no small delight to everyone who, like you, is a follower of such refinement.
