# The Stele of Hammurabi: Law 196

This photograph is a composite image showing the famous relief from the top of the **Stele of Hammurabi** (top) and a modern digital rendering of a specific section of its text (bottom). The text corresponds to the beginning of **Law 196**, one of the most famous provisions of the code, establishing the principle of <term>lex talionis: the law of retaliation, commonly known as "an eye for an eye."</term>

->**The Relief Description**<-

The upper portion of the monument depicts King Hammurabi (standing) in a gesture of prayer or respect before the sun god Shamash (seated). Shamash—the god of justice—is identified by the rays of light emanating from his shoulders and his four-tiered horned crown. He hands the "rod and ring"—divine symbols of authority, measurement, and the power to maintain order—to the king, signifying that the laws below are divinely sanctioned.

### The Cuneiform Text: Law 196

1. If a <term>citizen (awīlum): a free member of the upper social class</term>
2. the eye
3. of a <term>fellow citizen (DUMU awīlim): literally "the son of a man," referring to a member of the same social class</term>
4. should
5. destroy <note>original: "uhtappid" (blinded or gouged out)</note>,
6. his eye
7. they shall destroy <note>original: "uhappadū" (they shall blind)</note>.

### Translator’s Notes

The digital graphic in the image clarifies the layout of the cuneiform signs found in Column XXV (on the reverse of the stele), lines 40–44. 

*   **Social Class:** It is important to note that this "eye for an eye" justice applied specifically to the *awīlum* class. If a citizen blinded a member of a lower class (a commoner or a slave), the punishment was typically a financial fine rather than physical mutilation.
*   **Sign Reconstruction:** In line 7, the digital rendering only shows the beginning of the word; the full legal consequence is known from the complete text of the diorite stele held in the Louvre Museum.
