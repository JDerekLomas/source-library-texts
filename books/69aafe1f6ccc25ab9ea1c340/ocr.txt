This photograph is a composite image showing the famous relief from the top of the **Stele of Hammurabi** (top) and a modern digital rendering of a specific section of its text (bottom). The text corresponds to the beginning of **Law 196**, one of the most famous provisions of the code, establishing the principle of *lex talionis* ("an eye for an eye").

<notes>
- **Text Identification:** This is the opening of Law 196 from the Code of Hammurabi. On the original stele, this text is located in Column XXV (reverse), lines 40–44.
- **Line 1:** The signs are clearly `šum` (BE+MA in some conventions, but here the distinct `šum` sign), `ma`, `a`, `wi`, `lum`.
- **Line 3:** `DUMU` is the Sumerian logogram for "son" or "descendant," used here in the context of "a member of the same social class" (a fellow citizen).
- **Line 4-5:** The graphic splits the word `uh-tap-pí-id` (he destroyed/gouged out) across two lines for stylistic reasons.
- **Line 6:** `i-in-šu` (his eye). The graphic shows `i` and `in` on the left and `šu` on the right.
- **Line 7:** Only the beginning of the final verb `ú-ha-ap-pa-du` (they shall destroy [his eye]) is clearly visible in this specific graphic layout; the final signs are inferred from the standard text of the law.
- **Relief Description:** The upper portion depicts King Hammurabi (standing) in a gesture of prayer or respect before the sun god Shamash (seated). Shamash, identified by the rays emanating from his shoulders and his four-tiered horned crown, hands the "rod and ring"—symbols of divine authority and justice—to the king.
</notes>

<vocab>
- **šumma**: "if" (standard opening for laws)
- **awīlum**: "man" or "free citizen"
- **īnum (i-in)**: "eye"
- **DUMU (mārum)**: "son"; here "fellow citizen"
- **uhtappid**: "he destroyed/blinded" (from *huppudu*)
- **īnišu (i-in-šu)**: "his eye"
- **uhappadū**: "they shall destroy/blind"
</vocab>
