## Preface.

<margin>(1)</margin> Having prostrated before the feet of Parvati, Saraswati <note>original: "Vaani"</note>, Lakshmi, Shiva <note>original: "Paramesan"</note>, Brahma, and Vishnu, as well as the five-handed Ganesha <note>original: "Kaarmukan"</note> and the great Sages; having sought their vision and grace, and with a clear mind arising, this youth <note>The author refers to himself in the third person</note> began to compose this work.

<margin>(2)</margin> This is the scripture spoken by the son of the Vedic Lord <note>Likely referring to a divine or sage-like lineage</note>. It was originally a vast work, but for the benefit of this great world, it has been separated and refined into beautiful Tamil. This is the decorative arrangement of the astrological science as set forth by the Vedic master.

## Benefits of the Work.

<margin>(3)</margin> To see the course of one's birth in this world—the wealth and the enmities—and to see the great yogas <note><term>Yoga</term>: specific planetary combinations that result in fortune or misfortune</note> arising amidst great sorrows, we shall explain in detail the nature of life as created by Brahma <note>original: "Vedan"</note>.

## Section on Afflictions.

### Afflictions in Childhood.
<note><term>Balarishta</term>: Specifically refers to astrological "evils" or health risks affecting infants and young children.</note>

<margin>(1)</margin> If the bright Moon and the Sun <note>original: "Parasan"</note> are joined together in the 12th or 8th house from the ascendant, and if they are in the same lunar day <note>original: "Tithi"</note>, the strength of the Moon is diminished. In such a state, if they occupy the 8th house, a severe affliction <note>original: "Kandam"</note> exists. By the decree of fate, a great danger will manifest within two years.

<margin>(2)</margin> If Saturn <note>original: "Mandhai"</note> and Mars <note>original: "Sei"</note> are joined together in a house, that place is harmed. If they are in the opposite house, a certain fire <note>or fever</note> will occur. If they aspect <note>look upon</note> the 4th, 3rd, 8th, or 10th houses, consider it a fatal affliction that will occur within twelve months.

If Rahu <note>the shadow planet, original: "Aravakkari"</note> is positioned on the right, that place is harmed. It will cause the lineage to diminish and the household to suffer. The evil results of this will be seen in the twelfth year; it will bring sorrow like a flood, causing much distress to one's existence.

## As Spoken by the Guru.

### Royal Yoga <note>Raja Yoga: A planetary combination indicating high status, wealth, and power.</note>

<margin>(1)</margin> If the Moon is in the Ascendant <note>Udayam</note> and the exalted Jupiter is in the seventh house, and if the malefic planets and Venus are in the tenth house, this configuration is known as the Kari-Giri Royal Yoga <note>A yoga as stable and powerful as an elephant on a mountain</note>.

## First Question.
### Whether man possesses Free Will?

**S**o that we may more easily pass from those things which have been said concerning vices and sins to the virtues and grace by which sins are atoned for and avoided, we must discuss free will, which is, as it were, the foundation of all these matters: first, indeed, whether it exists; second, what it is; and third, what its power is in works of piety <note>The term "piety" here refers to religious duties and acts of devotion performed out of love for God</note>.

<margin>Heretics deny free will.</margin>
The Manichæans <note>A dualistic religious movement that taught that the soul is trapped in an evil material world</note> denied free will, concerning whom Saint Augustine wrote in his books *On the Two Souls* and *Against Fortunatus*. Likewise, Wycliffe and Hus, who were condemned in the Council of Constance, and in our own age, Luther and Calvin; who, although they sometimes seem to admit in words that man is not lacking in free will, nevertheless in reality they overthrow it when they teach that man is moved toward good or evil only out of necessity, and that in his actions he behaves only passively, no differently than a stone or a tree trunk.

<margin>Explanation of the question.</margin>
To correctly understand this question, it must be noted that there is a threefold liberty. The first is called *Liberty from coercion*, which is nothing other than immunity from external force; and this not only men, but also brute animals possess, which move of their own accord and without any compulsion. The second is *Liberty from necessity*, by which someone is so moved toward something that they are also able not to be moved, or to do something else; and this is proper to man, and is called Free Will <term>Liberum arbitrium: the capacity of the mind to choose between different possible courses of action</term>. The third is *Liberty from sin*, by which someone wills so rightly that they can only will the good; such as exists in the Blessed <note>Those in Heaven</note>, and in the highest degree in God.

<summary>This page introduces the theological debate over free will, defining it against the views of the Reformers and categorizing liberty into three distinct types: freedom from force, freedom from necessity, and freedom from sin.</summary>
