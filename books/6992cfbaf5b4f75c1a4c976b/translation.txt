<note>This cover introduces one of the most influential works of the 19th century in East Asia. Compiled by the scholar Wei Yuan (1794–1857) and first published in 1842, the *Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms* was a direct response to China's defeat in the First Opium War. Wei Yuan famously advocated for "learning the superior techniques of the barbarians to control them," making this book a cornerstone of the Qing Dynasty's attempts at modernization and maritime defense.</note>

## Northern Oceans: Europe

The five countries in this volume are all located in the northern part of the continent. Because they are close to the Frozen Sea <note>original: 冰海 (*Binghai*), referring to the Arctic Ocean</note>, they are separately categorized as the "Northern Oceans."

The Kingdom of Prussia <note>original: 普魯社 (*Pulushe*)</note> is also written as *Beilusi*, *Polusi*, or *Pulu*. In various records and maps, it appears as *Ya’ermiya* <note>likely a transliteration of "Almania" or Germany</note> or *Tuliya* <note>likely "Thuringia"</note>; these are simply different phonetic transliterations. In foreign accounts, it is also called the Kingdom of *Kongge’er* <note>possibly a phonetic rendering of "Kingdom"</note> or *Maxigabi*.

---

**The Kingdom of Prussia** is the "Single Eagle" nation <note>original: 單鷹 (*Danying*)</note> that comes to Guangdong to trade. <note>Prussia was identified by the black eagle on its national ensign, distinguishing it from the "Double Eagle" of the Austrian or Russian empires.</note> It is located in the northern part of central Europe.

Its territory was formerly small, with various tribes scattered like stars. In the year 1800 of the Christian Era <gloss>the 5th year of the Jiaqing reign</gloss>, King Frederick <note>original: 富律達利 (*Fulu’idali*)</note> seized the region of **Silesia** <note>original: 西里西阿 (*Xilixi’a*)</note> from **Austria** <note>original: 歐塞特厘 (*Ousaiteli*)</note>, along with portions of **Poland**.

**Prussia** then ascended to the rank of the Great Powers. Only six years later, it fought with **France** <note>original: 佛蘭西 (*Folansi*)</note> and suffered a crushing defeat at the **Battle of Jena** <note>original: 支那 (*Zhina*). While this term usually refers to China in other contexts, here it is a phonetic rendering of Jena, the site of Napoleon’s decisive 1806 victory over the Prussians.</note>.

The kingdom was nearly lost. Recently, taking advantage of **France's** defeat by **Russia**, **Prussia** raised troops to seek revenge. They rallied the surrounding frontier tribes <note>referring to the various German states</note> to assist in the attack on **France**, promising that once their territories were reclaimed, each would be granted its own land. The various peoples were eager and competed to be the first to break the enemy. After the victory, the King regretted his earlier promises and made excuses for several years; it was only much later that he began to fulfill the agreement, and even to this day, it has not been fully realized. 

In political affairs, they have appointed four "Wise Officials" <term>Councilors: *Xianguan*, likely referring to high-ranking ministers or the State Council</term>. At every assembly, the Wise Officials of various nations gather in **Germany** <note>original: 耶麻尼 (*Yemani*), referring to the German Confederation established at the Congress of Vienna</note>.

The military is divided into three classes:
1. The **Standing Army** <note>original: 士丹定阿彌 (*Shidanding’ami*), a phonetic rendering of "Standing Army"</note>
2. The **Landwehr** <note>original: 蘭威阿 (*Lanwei’a*), the national militia or secondary force</note>
3. The **Landsturm** <note>original: 蘭士端 (*Lanshiduan*), the final reserve or local defense force</note>

The troops of the **Landsturm** are used only to guard the capital and cities; they do not go on foreign expeditions, and their total number is not precisely known. The established quotas for the **Standing Army** are as follows:

# The Kingdom of Sweden (Continued)

The regions of **Pomerania** <note>original: 波墨臘厘 (Bōmòlàlí)</note> and various other tribes were once so powerful that whenever the nations of Europe had business to conduct, they all followed the commands of Sweden. However, in the year 1700 <gloss>the 39th year of the Kangxi reign</gloss>, the Swedes were defeated by the **Tsar** <note>original: 渣爾 (Zhā'ěr); Peter the Great of Russia</note>. Turkey successfully brokered a peace, but as a result, Sweden had to cede the vast territories of **Livonia** <note>original: 厘阿厘委阿 (Lí'ālǐwěi'ā)</note> and several other large tribal lands as a bribe to the Tsar to finally stop the fighting. Several decades passed until the reign of **Gustavus** <note>likely referring to the era of Gustav III and IV</note>, during which time the internal administration underwent further changes. Bernadotte feared that the former King's son might return to challenge him, so he worked diligently to win the hearts of the common people and establish his own independent authority.

In the central regions, the day lasts 82 *ke* <note>approximately 19.6 hours</note>. In the north, during the summer solstice, the solar disk travels horizontally along the horizon; for half the year it is a single day, and for the other half, a single night <note>a description of the "Midnight Sun" and the Polar Night north of the Arctic Circle</note>.

The land is filled with mountains and forests. It produces wild beasts and sea fish of immense size, quite different from those found in other regions. In the Kingdom of **Denmark** <note>original: 大泥亞 (Dàníyà); Dania</note>, the coastal areas produce legumes, wheat, cattle, and sheep in great abundance. The cattle are exported to other countries.

# Records of Various Nations ... Volume I

...the yield often amounts to fifty thousand units annually. In the sea, fish cover the surface of the water; boats are buoyed up by the swarming fish so that they often cannot move. To catch these fish, one does not even require nets or traps, as they can be taken by hand without end.

Within the last twenty years, there was a scholar of this nation named **Tycho Brahe** <note>original: 地谷白剌格 (Dìgǔ Báilágé); the famous Danish astronomer (1546–1601)</note> who was deeply devoted to the study of **Mathematics** <note>original: 瑪得瑪第加 (Mǎdé mǎdìjiā); a transliteration of the Latin *mathematica*</note>. He built an observatory atop a high mountain <note>likely referring to Uraniborg on the island of Hven</note> to exhaustively observe celestial phenomena. He devoted his mind to this for thirty years, and his calculations were accurate down to a "grain of millet" <note>original: 累黍不爽 (lěishǔ bù shuǎng); a Chinese idiom meaning mathematically precise to the smallest degree</note>. The astronomical instruments he crafted reached the very limits of intricacy and subtlety. Later, the king of a great nation <note>referring to Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II</note> invited him to his kingdom to transmit his learning. Today, he is considered the founding master of Western astronomy and calendrical methods.

Regarding **Norway** <note>original: 諾而勿惹亞 (Nuò'érwùrěyà); Latin: *Norvegia*</note>, the land lacks the five cereal grains. However, the mountains and forests abound in timber, birds, and beasts, while the sea is rich in fish and turtles. The people are gentle and honest, and they delight in welcoming guests and travelers from distant lands. In former times, they never asked for payment from travelers who stayed in their homes; even now, if a small amount is required, they are easily satisfied. For this reason, the land is entirely free of thieves.

**Sweden** <note>original: 雪際亞 (Xuějìyà); Latin: *Suecia*</note> is divided into seven provinces and has twelve dependency states. In the north of **Europe** <note>original: 歐邏巴 (Ōuluóbā)</note>, it is said to be the most prosperous and populous.
