## Preface to the Tenth Edition.

---

I have all the more gladly followed the publisher's wish to add an index to the new edition of Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason*, as I know how much such a tool can serve to facilitate the understanding of this difficult work. Kant has his own language; and even if he explains almost every concept <term>concept: "Begriff" in German; in Kantian philosophy, this refers to a mental representation that allows us to categorize experiences</term> that is significant for understanding his system somewhere, and makes it clear through the context in which he places it here and there, the passages important for the explanation of a concept are not always where the reader desires them to be. The included subject index is intended to assist the reader in this regard and, if possible, serve in this way as a commentary for the study of Kant's principal work. The selection of concepts, as well as the passages included, was made accordingly. Correspondingly, however, is the order that was established in the grouping of the cited explanations within the individual larger articles. In this process, the following scheme was used as far as possible: In the first place, what the respective concept generally means or designates in Kant's work was cited. This was followed by an example that served to explain the general meaning in detail or through a concrete case. In the third place, the psychological, logical, transcenden- <note>The text cuts off mid-word: "transscendentale" or "transcendental." In Kant's work, this refers to the conditions that make experience and knowledge possible.</note>

...detailed, metaphysical explanation that Kant provides somewhere for the concept. And finally, secondary characteristics or special applications of the concept were listed. Naturally, this scheme could only be followed to a limited extent. To ensure that the reader is spared the burdensome task of looking up cited passages, almost all of the more important passages have been reproduced in their full wording. In my opinion, most readers are better served by this than if a large number of parallel passages were merely listed numerically.

The well-known Vorländer index to Kant’s *Critique of Pure Reason* <note>original: "Kritik der reinen Vernunft"; this is Immanuel Kant's foundational 1781 work on the limits of human knowledge</note> provided me with valuable service in the preparation of the subject index; it will remain indispensable, particularly for philological <term>philological: the study of language in historical sources</term> Kant research.

Bremen, June 1913.

->Dr. Th. Valentiner.<-
