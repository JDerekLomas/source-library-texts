# Physiognomy
<note>original: "Physiognomonica"</note>

**F**rom which authors whose books I have at hand, it seems appropriate for me to speak. The authority of the Philosopher <note>likely referring to Aristotle</note>, of Polemon, and of Loxus <note>original: "Loxitatoris", a corruption of Loxus, an early Greek writer on physiognomy</note>, who wrote on physiognomy; I have chosen those things from them which pertain to the beautiful instruction of the human being. And so that they may be more easily understood, I shall set them down plainly, just as in official matters, even if the piping or the mystical style did not persuade them. First, therefore, it must be shown what physiognomy provides. Since the qualities of the soul are revealed by the quality of the body, for they are closely related. The fifth place of the middle flesh shows this to be moist. The body, however, which is especially clean, is in the nature of blood: pure, thin, and smooth, but more like dew or honey. Likewise, where they are pressed and narrow, here the purest things are contrary; thus they temper themselves above the rest, as is judged.

**S**ince the humor also shows the spirit of the mind from the breath of the soul, the pain of the soul <unclear>pain</unclear> is changed according to the male. Here, thus in sons, they have a likeness in the leg or the shin. To pour in benevolent spirits, he might bring in vices in the trumpet and, alas, the lips. First, that every offering of the mind shows this: it is so that he may beget another male, but also that there may be a female. Concerning this reasoning, accepting that the children of these places seem to have their own traits. And as the pleasing male in the female, and the female in the masculine, it reveals that the female is more than the masculine.

**P**hysiognomists seem to be formed by establishing the temperaments of the masculine and female soul before all else, so that both might avoid ignorance; and if not all causes, they are powerful in showing why. Since through faith, as in the mystical tongue, the clarity of things follows; but because they grasp the place in such a way, they can, through physiognomy, [know] of my sadness or specific obstruction. Therefore, let it stand in this: all say in the wildness of the song begun, as if to drop. Pure is whoever is strengthened.

# The Life and Passion of the Blessed Martyr

<column-break/>

->**The Trial of the Holy Virgin**<-

In those days, the blessed handmaid of God stood before the prefect, steadfast in her heart and unmoving in her faith. <note>The "prefect" likely refers to a Roman provincial governor, a common antagonist in early Christian martyr stories.</note> While the crowds gathered to witness her resolve, she remained as a pillar of light amidst the darkness of the heathens.

**The First Ordeal**

As it is written in the ancient chronicles: *The fire did not consume her, nor did the flames prevail against the spirit of the Lord.* <note>original: "Ignis non consumpsit eam, nec flammae praevaluerunt adversus spiritum Domini."</note> <margin>She is protected by the grace of the Holy Spirit</margin> Even as the wood was piled high and the pitch was poured, she looked toward the heavens and sang a psalm of praise. 

<insert>The king's heart was hardened like stone.</insert>

The executioners, moved by a cruel command, prepared the instruments of torment. <gloss>The iron combs and the gridiron</gloss> Yet, a small child—some say an angel in the guise of a youth—clung to her robes, signifying her spiritual purity and the divine protection that surrounded her. Even the beasts of the field, represented by the faithful hound at her feet, showed more mercy than the men who sought her life.

<column-break/>

### The Sentence of the Tyrant

| Instrument of Torture | Symbolic Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| The Burning Pyre | The purification of the soul through trial |
| The Crowned Judge | The earthly power that fails against the divine |
| The Vessel of Oil | The anointing of the martyr for her final journey |

The judge, seated upon his throne of pride, commanded that she be cast into the furnace. <note>This scene mirrors the biblical story of the three youths in the fiery furnace from the Book of Daniel.</note> But behold, the Lord sent a cooling dew to descend upon her, and the fire became as a refreshing breeze. <term>Refrigerium</term> <note>A Latin term meaning "refreshment" or "cooling," often used in early Christianity to describe the heavenly rest of the soul.</note>

*“O miserable men,”* she cried out, *“why do you labor in vain? My God is a consuming fire of love, while your flames are but shadows that pass away.”* <margin>The saint mocks the impotence of the idols</margin>

At this, the prefect was filled with a great rage and ordered that she be led away to the final place of execution, outside the city walls, where the crown of glory awaited her.
