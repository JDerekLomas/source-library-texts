Master Ruiheng <note>A courtesy name used by the author/publisher Cheng Dayue</note> says: That which transforms from the "One" into the "Changeable" is what we call the "Flow." How ancient and profound this concept is! 

Indeed, transformation is not merely a flow; it is the unity and the nobility of the source from which we compile these designs. Through the wisdom of constant change, one can fully comprehend the intricate interactions of the mind. This process is like the clarity of the sun and the moon, a brilliance passed down to worthy successors. Guanshi <note>original: 廣石 (Guǎngshí), likely a literary name of a collaborator or a specific location</note> offers a response in praise: "The rain, the dew, the frost, and the heavy mist—all are captured here." The seals and markings are firm and singular, utilizing the forms of mountain flora and the ancient characters for rain-prayers <note>original: 雩 (yú)</note>, all while seeking the pure essence of the azure sky.

# Garden of Ink of the Cheng Family, Scroll 1

## Mysterious Craftsmanship

The Historian of Ink says: "One *Yin* and one *Yang* constitute the *Dao*" <note>A famous quote from the *I Ching* (Book of Changes). It suggests that the interplay of opposites is the fundamental principle of the universe.</note>—this is a truth of the highest antiquity! Indeed, *Yin* and *Yang* are not the *Dao* itself; rather, the "Unity" <note>original: 一 (yī). In Daoist thought, the One is the first manifestation of the Dao from which all things arise.</note> that joins them is the means by which the *Dao* is formed. 

Between *Yin* and *Yang*, there exists the "ink" of brightness and darkness, of day and night. Are these not the great manifestations of Heaven and Earth? The sun and moon provide illumination; the wind and thunder drum and disperse; rain, dew, frost, and snow nourish and stabilize. Their utility is vast indeed! 

Clouds are dark and mysterious <note>original: 玄 (xuán). This word describes the color of high-quality ink and also signifies the "Profound Mystery" of Daoist philosophy.</note> while mists are azure; rosy clouds are brilliant, and mountain vapors are emerald. The Creator <term>Zhaowuzhe (The Fashioner of Things)</term> employs each of these in their own way. Yet, that which is never discarded is the work of the True Master <term>Zhenzai (The True Governor or Divine Power)</term>—is this not the ultimate pattern <note>original: 文 (wén). This term refers to both "writing" and the "natural patterns" found in the stars, animal furs, or landscape.</note> between Heaven and Earth? In my Garden of Ink...

# The Supreme Ultimate
<note>original: *Taiji*. In Chinese cosmology, this refers to the "Great Ultimate," the primordial source and governing principle of all existence.</note>

<margin>Ink Pond Refined Works</margin> <note>original: *Mochi Xiugong*, the title of this collection.</note>

<insert>
To the right is the **Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate**, transmitted by **Master Zhou Dunshi** (also known as **Maoshu**) to the **Two Cheng Brothers**. <note>Zhou Dunyi (1017–1073) was a foundational philosopher of Neo-Confucianism. The "Two Cheng Brothers" refers to his famous students, Cheng Hao and Cheng Yi.</note>

Master Maoshu said:
The **Infinite** is also the **Supreme Ultimate**. <note>original: *Wuji er Taiji*. This describes the universe's origin as both a limitless void and a supreme potential.</note> The Supreme Ultimate moves and generates **Yang**. When movement reaches its limit, it becomes still, and in stillness, it generates **Yin**. When stillness reaches its limit, movement returns. Movement and stillness alternate, each serving as the root of the other. As Yin and Yang are distinguished, the **Two Modes** are established. <note>The "Two Modes" are the fundamental pair of Yin and Yang.</note>

Through the transformation of Yang and the union with Yin, **Water, Fire, Wood, Metal, and Earth** are produced. These five phases of energy are distributed in harmony, and the **Four Seasons** proceed in their course. The Five Elements are essentially Yin and Yang; Yin and Yang are essentially the Supreme Ultimate; and the Supreme Ultimate is fundamentally the Infinite.

In the generation of the Five Elements, each gains its own nature. The truth of the Infinite and the essence of the Two (Yin and Yang) and the Five (Elements) combine in a mysterious union to produce form. The **Way of Qian** (Heaven) constitutes the male, and the **Way of Kun** (Earth) constitutes the female. The interaction of these two energies transforms and generates the **myriad things**. The myriad things produce and reproduce, and their changes and transformations are limitless.

I, Dayue, of humble standing, was a Confucian scholar in my youth. My name was registered at the Imperial Academy, and I served in the <term>Court of State Ceremonial: The "Honglu Si," an office responsible for imperial receptions and state rituals.</term>. Every day I waited at
