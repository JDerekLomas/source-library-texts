# That the Jesuit Doctrine Approves of and Justifies the Murder of Royal Persons and the Rebellion of Subjects.

## CHAPTER I.

**T**O remove all doubt and to ensure this dispute is sufficiently explained, necessity requires that the matter be traced back a bit further.
French chronicles and histories testify that on November 22, 1407, Louis, Duke of Orleans and the biological brother of King Charles VI, was murdered by certain men commissioned by John, Duke of Burgundy. The Duke of Burgundy had been in a quarrel with the aforementioned Duke of Orleans over the administration and management of the kingdom. Since the Duke of Burgundy could not deny the deed, he did not hesitate—in the presence of all the princes of the royal blood, councilors, and other servants—to defend it as a lawful act. This matter was justified by a Doctor of <term>Holy Scripture</term> <note>original: "H. Schrifft"</note> named Jean Petit, a native of Normandy. He asserted, through divine and human laws as well as spiritual and secular rights, that it is permissible for anyone to kill a tyrant by any means and in any way. Partly through force and partly through such justifications <note>original: "eynbildungen," literally "imaginings" or "fancies"</note>, the matter was pushed so far that the deed remained unpunished.

At that time, Jean Gerson, Chancellor of the University of Paris, was living. As a learned man (relative to those times), he set himself against this doctrine and the opinion of Doctor Jean Petit.
