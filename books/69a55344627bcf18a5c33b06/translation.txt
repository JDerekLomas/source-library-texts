# The Seven Valleys of Bahá’u’lláh

->In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.<-

Praise be to God Who has brought existence out of nothingness; engraved the secrets of pre-existence upon the tablet of man; taught him from the mysteries of divine speech that which he did not know; made him a Luminous Book to those who believed and surrendered themselves; caused him to witness the creation of all things <note>original Arabic: *Kullu Shay’*</note> in this dark and ruinous age, and to speak from the summit of eternity with a wondrous voice in the Excellent Temple <note>The "Excellent Temple" refers to the Manifestation of God—the human form through which God’s attributes are revealed to the world.</note>: to the end that every person may testify, in themselves and by themselves, in the station of the Revelation of their Lord, that truly there is no God but Him, and that every person may thereby reach the summit of realities, until no one observes anything without seeing God within it.

And I praise and glorify the first sea which has branched from the ocean of the Divine Essence, and the first dawn which has shone from the Horizon of Unity, and the first sun which has risen in the Heaven of Eternity, and the first fire which was lit from the Lamp of Pre-existence in the lantern of singleness: He who was Aḥmad in the kingdom of the exalted ones, and Muḥammad among the assembly of the near ones, and Maḥmúd <note>Aḥmad, Muḥammad, and Maḥmúd are all names and titles of the Prophet Muḥammad, derived from the Arabic root for "praise" or "exalt."</note> in the realm of the sincere ones. “...Invoke Him by whatever name you will: He has the most excellent names” <note>original: Qur’án 17:110</note> in the hearts of those who know. And may abundant, lasting, and eternal peace be upon His household and companions!

# The Valley of Search

The horse <note>original: "steed"—representing the quality or virtue required to move forward</note> of this Valley is patience; without patience, the traveler on this journey will reach nowhere and achieve no goal. He should never lose heart; even if he strives for a hundred thousand years and still fails to behold the beauty of the Friend <note>original: "the Friend"—a mystical term for God</note>, he should not waver. For those who seek the Sanctuary <note>original: "Ka’bih"—the holy shrine in Mecca; here used to mean the ultimate spiritual goal</note> of the verse "for Us" <note>A reference to the Quranic promise of guidance for those who struggle for God</note> rejoice in the good news: "In Our ways will We guide them." <gloss>9 Qur'an 29:69: "And whoever makes efforts for Us, in Our ways will We guide them."</gloss> In their search, they have firmly prepared themselves <note>original: "stoutly girded up the loins of service"—an idiom meaning to prepare for hard work or a journey</note> for service, and seek at every moment to journey from the state of neglect into the realm of true existence. No bond shall hold them back, and no advice shall turn them away.

It is necessary for these servants to cleanse the heart—which is the source of divine treasures—from every worldly trace, and to turn away from mere imitation, which is...

One must judge the intensity of a search by the standard of Majnún <note>original: "Majnún"—literally "insane," the title of the famous lover in Persian and Arabic literature whose beloved was Laylí</note>. Indeed, although wise people might think it shameful to look for the Lord of Lords in the dust, this shows the intense passion of the search. "Whoever seeks something with zeal shall find it" <note>An Arabic proverb</note>.

The true seeker hunts for nothing except the object of his search, and the lover has no desire other than union with his beloved. The seeker will never reach his goal unless he sacrifices everything. That is, everything he has seen, heard, and understood must be considered as nothing, so that he may enter the realm of the spirit, which is the City of God. Effort is needed if we are to seek Him; passion is...

## The Valley of Love

and be dissolved in the fire of love. In this city, the heaven of ecstasy is raised up, and the world-lighting sun of yearning shines, and the fire of love is ablaze. When the fire of love burns, it turns the harvest of reason to ashes.

Now the traveler is unaware of himself and of anything besides himself. He sees neither ignorance nor knowledge, neither doubt nor certainty; he does not know the morning of guidance from the night of error. He flees both from unbelief and faith, and deadly poison is like a healing balm to him. Therefore <note>original: "Wherewhere"—likely an OCR error or archaic form of "Wherefore"</note> Attár <note>original: "Attár"—Farídu’d-Dín Attár (ca. 1150–1230 A.D.), a famous Persian Sufi poet and author of "The Conference of the Birds"</note> says:

> For Attár’s heart, a single atom of Your pain.

In this station, the lover has no thought except for the Beloved, and seeks no refuge except for the Friend <note>The "Beloved" and the "Friend" are mystical titles for God</note>. O My Brother! > A lover is one who is cool in hellfire;
> A knower is one who is dry in the sea.<sup>15</sup>

Love accepts no existence and wishes for no life: It sees life in death, and in shame it seeks glory. Therefore, O friend, give up your self so that you may find the Peerless One; pass by this mortal earth so that you may seek a home in the nest of heaven. > Love does not seize upon a living soul,

# The Valley of Love

Love sets the whole world aflame at every turn, and it devastates every land where it carries its banner. Individual existence has no place in its kingdom; the wise hold no authority within its realm. The giant monster <note>original: "leviathan"—a massive sea creature, here representing the overwhelming and consuming power of love</note> of love swallows the master of reason and destroys the lord of knowledge. It drinks the seven seas, yet its heart’s thirst is still not satisfied, and it asks, “Is there yet any more?” <note>original: "Qur’án 50:29"—In the Quran, this is the cry of Hell, never satisfied and always seeking more; here it represents the infinite longing of love.</note> The lover turns away from himself and withdraws from everyone on earth.

->Love is a stranger to both earth and heaven;<-
->In it are seventy-two different forms of madness. <note>original: "seventy-and-two"—referring to a traditional number representing a great variety or the many sects of Islam. This quote is from Jalálu’d-Dín Rúmí (1207–1273 AD), the famous Persian Sufi poet and founder of the "whirling" dervish order.</note><-

Love has bound countless <note>original: "myriad"—meaning ten thousand, or any indefinitely large number</note> victims in its chains and wounded countless wise men with its arrows. Know that every red flush in the world comes from love's anger, and every pale cheek comes from its poison. It offers no remedy but death; it walks only in the valley of the shadow. Yet its venom is sweeter than honey on the lover’s lips, and its destruction is fairer in the seeker’s eyes than a hundred thousand lives.

Therefore, the veils of the selfish ego <note>original: "satanic self"—referring to the lower, insistent self that acts as a barrier between the soul and God</note> must be burned away in the fire of love, so that the spirit may be purified and cleansed, and thus may know the rank of the Lord of the Worlds.

# The Valley of Knowledge

In this valley, he emerges from doubt into certainty and turns from the darkness of illusion to the guiding light of the awe of God. <note>original: "fear of God"—In this spiritual context, this refers to a profound sense of reverence, mindfulness, and awe before the Divine, rather than literal terror.</note> His inner eyes will open, and he will converse privately with his Beloved; he will open the gate of truth and devotion and shut the doors of empty fantasies. In this stage, he is content with God's decree; he sees war as peace and finds the secrets of everlasting life within death itself. With both his inner and outer eyes, he witnesses the mysteries of resurrection in the world of creation and in the souls of men, and with a pure heart, he understands the divine wisdom in the endless Manifestations of God. <note>original: "Manifestations of God"—In the Bahá’í Faith, these are the Great Educators of humanity, such as Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad, and Bahá’u’lláh, who reflect God’s attributes to the world.</note> In the ocean, he finds a drop; in a drop, he beholds the secrets of the sea.

->Split the atom’s heart, and behold!<-

In ignorance, he finds many hidden truths, and in knowledge, a thousand manifest wisdoms. He breaks the cage of the body and the passions and associates with the people of the eternal realm. He climbs the ladders of inner truth and hastens toward the heaven of deeper meaning.
