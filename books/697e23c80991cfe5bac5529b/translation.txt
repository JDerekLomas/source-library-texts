# THE PRINTER TO
## THE CHRISTIAN READER.

Honorable dear Reader, since I have come into possession of the *Church History of Eusebius* <note>Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 260–339 AD), the first major historian of the Christian church.</note>, and also the *Ecclesiastical History* <note>original: "Historie Ecclesiastica"</note> which is called the *Tripartita* <term>Tripartita: A "three-part" history created by merging the works of three 5th-century historians: Socrates Scholasticus, Sozomen, and Theodoret.</term>, translated into Dutch by good men of the Church, and seeing that there are now many various chronicles and histories printed and sold that do not specifically recount the affairs of the Church as these do. Furthermore, because there is today a great question and struggle as to who stands closest to the ancient Christian church and remains in agreement with it, and since these historians specifically recount the beginning and progress of said church—from the revelation of Christ in the flesh until around the year 400—they present to us, as if in a chest, all the treasures of ecclesiastical matters. In our current time, this is highly useful for all those whom God has placed in any form of government, whether spiritual or worldly, as well as for those outside of it.

In view of this, I have spared no expense nor shirked any labor so that this work might come forth for honor and profit, and for the service of our Dutch Nation, having been printed and widely sold for so long in various other languages. I hope to have done the kind Reader a convenient service here; if you buy and read this History instead of other fictionalized histories <note>The printer is likely contrasting factual church history with popular medieval romances or unverified legends.</note>, you shall easily understand and find, through the struggle of the **Martyrs** who fought for the truth described herein (alongside other churchly matters), the footsteps that the **Apostles** and **Apostolic Men** have walked. And you shall neither be unaware nor ignorant of how, when, and for what reason every error made its incursion. I have also newly added here at the end, for the service of the Christian Reader, that which has happened to the Church of God in various Provinces, but principally in **France** <note>This refers to the contemporary struggles of the Huguenots in the French Wars of Religion.</note>, in order to have good knowledge of Church matters. This is most necessary for a Christian: to seek that which is Heavenly (as in Colossians 3) <note>Referring to Colossians 3:1-2: "Set your hearts on things above, where Christ is..."</note> and to align himself with the understanding of the first and oldest Christian Churches. May God preserve us therein, and let us always enjoy the comfort of said Church, the **Holy Spirit**, through Christ, the giver of the same Holy Spirit.

->AMEN,<-

Dated Dordrecht, 1613.

# PREFACE to the Christian
## Reader, on the usefulness of these Histories.

<margin>The argument for reading history</margin>
Just as all histories, chronicles, deeds, actions, and events, dear Reader, are always read with great desire and longing, and always to some use or profit: so much more will the reading of this *Ecclesiastical History* <note>original: "Historie Ecclesiastica"</note> of Eusebius, Ruffinus, and the other *Tripartite Ecclesiastical History* be of benefit to those who have found any discernment in the Holy Scriptures. Such a person will easily come to know what was done and handled in the Christian Church by the Apostles and Apostolic Men after the time of Christ.

<margin>Eusebius also wrote many other books.</margin>
For Eusebius, a prince and the foremost of all writers of Church History, acquitted and exerted himself singularly in presenting these things to us. In this, he lacked neither learning, wisdom, nor eloquence, nor even memory. For he does not merely bring forward the most ancient Fathers, one after another, along with the old stories and their deeds; he also tells us vividly—and allows us to judge—the things as they occurred and happened continuously, one after another, in the Church, drawing from the books, acts, letters, councils, and synods of the oldest Fathers. And although this History is disregarded and rejected by many—who would rather read lying, fictional things—they shall be corrected here, being reminded of the truth. For what is more necessary for a human to know than what has happened and occurred in the Church of Christ since the manifestation of our Lord?

<margin>The teaching of history is necessary for a good Christian life.</margin>
For if all histories bring us some benefit, how much more so this church history: which instructs people in the eternal, Divine Religion, and points us toward the first, oldest Fathers and how they served God, so that we might in some way be joined with them to God. And truly, if histories did not bring to our memory all worldly and spiritual dealings—and especially what concerns Religion—then the negligence and imagination of men would devise and darken all the glory and beauty of Divine and human laws. This happens especially among the Atheists <note>original: "Atheos"</note> and those commonly called Epicureans <note>In this period, "Epicurean" was a derogatory term for someone who lived for worldly pleasure and denied the immortality of the soul or divine providence.</note>, who hold no more regard for God than for themselves. Otherwise, what would we know of the creation of the World, of Adam, Seth, Noah, or the Deluge <note>original: "Diluvie," referring to the Great Flood.</note>, <margin>Genesis 7. Exodus 1. Acts 7. Psalm 105.</margin> of the Patriarchs, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and their seed? Or of King Pharaoh in Egypt, under whom the Hebrew people first began to multiply, and the leader Moses, the rising of other Kingdoms, and the planting of the promised land according to the Prophecies?

Further, what would we know of Alexander the Great, who greatly troubled <note>original catchword: "quelde," meaning to plague or torment.</note> this people?
