# CHAPTER I
## The Universality of Sex

Every ancient secret society, esoteric organization, and school of thought agreed on one central point: the universality of sex. They realized that the principle of sex was manifest throughout the entire cosmos and was active across the whole universe. *The Phallic Cross symbol* <note>original: "Phallic Cross"; this refers to ancient symbols like the Ankh or the equilateral cross that represent the union of masculine and feminine energies as the source of life.</note> is found in use in nearly every occult organization or body. It signifies the teaching that the male and female principles are inherent in each and every form of nature's activities and manifestations.

In many older writings, you will notice that the term "gender" is used instead of "sex." This is because the primary idea of sex, as understood in occultism, is that of begetting, bringing forth, and procreating. The term "gender" is particularly meaningful and expressive of this idea. Occultists realize that the work of procreation is manifest on every plane—physical, mental, and spiritual—and that the principle of "gender" is active everywhere, at all times, and in all ways. However, in this short book, we will use the term "sex" instead of "gender" for several reasons. It should be understood, of course, that the term is used in its widest and most universal sense, rather than referring only to the specific biological sex of living things or the human race in particular.

*The Arcane Teaching* <note>The "Arcane Teaching" is the name of the specific philosophical system presented by the author, William Walker Atkinson, which draws from Hermeticism and ancient esoteric traditions.</note> embraces the well-established occult doctrine of the universality of sex. It holds that sex is apparent not only on the physical plane, but also on the mental and spiritual planes. Furthermore, the principle of sex is evident not only in living, organic forms, but also in so-called "lifeless" and inorganic matter. The principle of sex is fundamentally tied to the Law of Opposites <note>term: Law of Opposites</note> <note>Also known as the Principle of Polarity, this law suggests that everything in the universe has two poles or opposites, like positive and negative, which are necessary for any kind of creation or movement.</note>, which we have discussed at length in our other works.

## Sex in Animal Life

In animal life, just as in plant life, we find male and female elements of reproduction. In animals, the male reproductive element is called the *spermatozoon*; the female reproductive element is called the *ovum* <note>original: "Spermatozoon" and "Ovum"; Latin terms for "seed-animal" and "egg"</note>.

The spermatozoa (the plural of spermatozoon) are the active, moving parts of the male reproductive element. They consist of a head, a rod-shaped middle section, and a long, hair-like tail. They move in a spiral pattern through the vibrating motion of this tail. These were formerly thought to be parasites living in the seminal fluid, but researchers eventually discovered they are actually the <term>fecundating cell</term> <note>original: "fecundating cell"; the cell responsible for fertilization</note> or male element of reproduction. They are found in all animals, including humans. 

The size of spermatozoa varies between different species. In humans, they are about one six-hundredth of an inch long. Their unique movements look like those of a tadpole, with the hair-like tail moving constantly from side to side to push the spermatozoon from one point to another. It is made of *protoplasm*, the substance that forms the basis of all physical life <note>In the 19th and early 20th centuries, protoplasm was considered the "physical basis of life," a term popularized by Thomas Henry Huxley</note>. Spermatozoa develop from a "mother-sperm-cell" through a process of <term>segmentation</term> <note>the division of a cell into smaller parts</note> or subdivision, in a manner similar to that of the simplest forms of animal life. They live in a jelly-like <note>original: "gelatinous"</note> fluid that mixes with other fluids produced by glands to make up the male seminal fluid.
