# Preface to the Precious Mirror of the Taiyi Ancestral Tradition

The <term>Taiyi</term> <note>The "Great One," referring to both a supreme celestial deity and a pivotal star near the North Star used in ancient divination</note> is the spirit of Heaven and Earth. Its star resides to the south of <term>Tianyi</term> <note>The "Heavenly One," another significant star in the ancient Chinese celestial hierarchy</note>. It governs sixteen spirits and possesses the knowledge of wind, rain, floods, droughts, <term>warfare</term> <note>literally "gold and leather," referring to weapons and armor</note>, and famine.

In ancient times, when the <term>Yellow Emperor</term> <note>The legendary ancestral sovereign of Chinese civilization, traditionally said to have reigned in the 3rd millennium BCE</note> fought <term>Chiyou</term> <note>A tribal leader and legendary figure credited with inventing bronze weapons and war</note> at the wilderness of <term>Zhuolu</term> <note>The site of a foundational battle in Chinese mythology</note>, the heavens produced a great fog so thick that the day became as dark as twilight. His advisor, Fenghou, surveyed the land and constructed the <term>South-Pointing Carriage</term> <note>A mechanical, non-magnetic vehicle that used differential gearing to ensure a figure always pointed south, used to navigate through the fog</note> to clarify their direction. This invention was entirely derived from the methods of Taiyi. These teachings have been passed down to the present for over three thousand years, categorized as a system of instructional arts.

Since antiquity, sage kings have revered the heavens and granted the seasons to the people <note>The "granting of time" or "imparting the calendar" was a primary duty of the Chinese Emperor to ensure agricultural success</note>. Their governance was prosperous and peaceful, bringing benefits to all under heaven by following and applying these methods.

This system contains the sequence of the <term>Nine Palaces</term> <note>A 3x3 mathematical grid used to track the movement of cosmic energies</note> and stellar patterns. It encompasses the signs of disaster or fortune within the <term>Twelve Fields</term> <note>The "Fenye" system, which maps celestial constellations to specific terrestrial regions of China</note>, clarifies the rise and fall of <term>host and guest</term> <note>Technical terms in divination and military strategy; the "host" is generally the defender or the established power, while the "guest" is the attacker or the newcomer</note>, and determines the victory or defeat of military strategists both near and far. Truly, this is a silent accord with the most refined and divine...

This work is categorized and indexed into a total of twenty volumes and is titled the **Precious Mirror of the Taiyi Ancestral Tradition**. The knowledge collected within these pages actually stems from the original intent of the <term>Methods of World Cycles</term> <note>original: 刧法 (jiefa). These are complex calculations used in Taiyi divination to predict major shifts in history, natural disasters, and the rise and fall of dynasties over vast "aeons" or cycles of time</note> established by the <term>Yellow Emperor</term> and his advisor, <term>Fenghou</term>.

If a ruler uses this system, he can lead his people to become like those of the golden age of <term>Yao and Shun</term> <note>The legendary "Sage Kings" of Chinese antiquity, regarded as the ultimate models of virtuous and harmonious governance</note>. If a minister uses it, he can guide his sovereign to become a ruler as wise as Yao and Shun themselves. Is this not a <unclear>significant</unclear> contribution to governance? 

The compilation of this book, in its broader scope, does not contradict the intentions of the ancient Sages; in its specific details, it does not deviate from the teachings of past worthies. It is named a "**Precious Mirror**" because, just as a mirror reflects one’s physical appearance, this book allows future generations to easily observe the true patterns of reality.

Respectfully prefaced by the **Elder of Dawn Mountain** <note>Xiaoshan Laoren, likely a pseudonym for the author or compiler</note> on an auspicious day in the first month of summer, in the seventh year of the <term>Dade</term> era <note>The "Great Virtue" era of the Yuan Dynasty, corresponding here to the year 1303 CE</note>, the year of the Water Rabbit.

# Explaining the Original Arts of the Taiyi Divination Device

In ancient times, <term>Suirenshi</term> <note>The mythological "Fire-Maker," one of the Three Sovereigns of ancient China</note> looked upward to observe the Big Dipper and the Pole Star to determine the names of the directions. <term>Baoxishi</term> <note>Commonly known as Fuxi, the legendary hero credited with creating the first symbols of the I Ching</note> followed this example to draw the **Eight Trigrams**.

This system was established when the **Yellow Emperor** received the Mandate of Heaven; his minister **Fenghou** created the <term>Taiyi Device</term> <note>The *Shi*, a physical divinatory board or astrolabe consisting of rotating plates</note>, and from this, the methods of <term>Taiyi</term> <note>The "Supreme One," a high-level form of divination focused on large-scale cosmic and political events</note> emerged. The physical form of the device consists of three layers: the upper layer is blue, modeling Heaven; the lower layer is yellow, modeling Earth; and the middle layer is red, symbolizing Humanity. Thus, the **Three Powers** <note>Heaven, Earth, and Humanity</note> are fully represented.

Heaven has the **Twelve Stations** <note>Divisions of the zodiac used to track the sun's position</note>; Earth has the **Twelve Branches** <note>The *Shi'er Chen*, used to mark hours, months, and directions</note>. Heaven has the four seasons; Earth has the four corners <note>The ordinal directions: northeast, southeast, northwest, and southwest</note>. # The Way of Heaven

The **Spirit of the Kun Trigram** <term>Kun: One of the eight trigrams, representing the Earth and the Southwest direction</term> is called **Great Martiality**. As autumn and summer are about to meet, the Yin energy begins to exert its influence over the Yang, causing things to wither and be struck down <note>original: "殺傷" (shashang). In Chinese cosmology, this refers to the "killing" or "withering" breath of the coming autumn that halts the growth of summer</note>. Therefore, it is called **Great Martiality**.

The **Spirit of the Monkey** <term>Shen: The ninth of the twelve Earthly Branches, corresponding to the 7th lunar month and the beginning of autumn</term> is called **Martial Virtue**. In the month governed by the Monkey, the energy of Metal <note>In the Five Elements system, Metal corresponds to autumn, sharpness, and the harvest</note> begins to grow robust. The power of "solemn execution" <note>original: "肅殺" (susha), the cold, atmospheric force that causes plants to dry and die back</note> takes command. Therefore, it is called **Martial Virtue**.

The **Spirit of the Rooster** <term>You: The tenth Earthly Branch, corresponding to the 8th lunar month and the peak of autumn</term> is called the **Grand Cluster** <note>original: "太簇" (Taicou). This is also the name of one of the twelve standard musical pitches in ancient China, associated here with the gathering of the harvest</note>. In the month governed by the Rooster, the myriad things reach full maturity, and there is a great abundance of gathered varieties. Therefore, it is called the **Grand Cluster**.

The **Spirit of the Dog** <term>Xu: The eleventh Earthly Branch, corresponding to the 9th lunar month and the transition toward winter</term> is called the **Ruler of Yin**. In the month governed by the Dog, the **Five Yins** <note>This refers to the hexagram *Bo* (Stripping Away), where five yin lines have risen to the top, signifying that the cooling, dark energy is nearly absolute</note> exert their true authority. It is a time of "yellow lower garments and supreme good fortune" <note>A reference to the *I Ching* (Book of Changes). The "yellow lower garment" represents a person who remains humble and correctly positioned despite holding great inner power, thus attaining luck</note>. Therefore, it is called the **Ruler of Yin**.

The **Spirit of the Qian Trigram** <term>Qian: One of the eight trigrams in the I Ching, representing Heaven and the Northwest direction</term> is called **Hidden Virtue** <note>original: "陰德" (Yinde). This refers to virtue or merit that is not displayed outwardly, reflecting the internal gathering of energy as the world cools</note>. As autumn and winter meet, the Yin energy reaches its completion and begins to give birth to the new Yang <note>According to the cycle of the seasons, as the darkness of winter peaks, the first spark of light and growth—Yang—is internally conceived</note>. Because it possesses great virtue in facilitating this transition, it is called **Hidden Virtue**.

The **Spirit of the Boar** <term>Hai: The twelfth of the Earthly Branches, corresponding to the 10th lunar month and the beginning of winter</term> is called **Great Justice** <note>original: "大義" (Dayi). In the month governed by the Boar, the "Six Yin" <note>Referring to the peak of Yin energy, symbolized in the I Ching by a hexagram consisting of six broken lines</note> are fully prepared. The Spirit of Water <note>In the Five Elements system, winter is the season of Water</note> takes command of power, and the myriad things are nourished and held in a state of internal gestation <note>original: "滋任" (ziren). This implies that while the surface world appears dead in winter, life is being nurtured deep within</note>. Therefore, it is called **Great Justice**.
