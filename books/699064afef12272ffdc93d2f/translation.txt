# COMPENDIUM OF REVELATIONS BY THE UNWORTHY SERVANT OF JESUS CHRIST, FRIAR GIROLAMO OF FERRARA, OF THE ORDER OF PREACHERS.

->JESUS. MARY.<-

**A**lthough for a long time and in many ways, through divine inspiration, I have predicted many future things: nevertheless, considering the sentence of our Savior Jesus Christ who says:
> Do not give what is holy to dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet and turn and tear you to pieces <note>original Latin: "Nolite sanctū dare canibus \ nec mittatis margaritas vestras ante porcos: ne forte conculcent eas pedibus & conuersi dirumpant uos"</note>

I have always been sparing in speech and have not expanded further than what seemed necessary for the salvation of men: so that our conclusions have been few, although there have been many proofs, exhortations, and persuasions to make them believed: always keeping secret the manner and the multitude of the visions and many other revelations: which I have never spoken of, not having been inspired to say them, and it not seeming to me necessary for salvation, nor men being yet disposed to believe them. Now, constrained by necessity, I have been moved to write down future things, especially the most principal and of greater importance, which I have publicly preached: both because many, having attempted to write while I was actually preaching, have not fully gathered the truth, but did so very disjointedly and even with many falsehoods: as they were not able to run as fast with the pen as I ran with my tongue: and also because some others, either for not having...

This, primarily, has moved me to write for the honor of God, so that His works do not fall into derision—especially since I have been told that these matters of ours, so flawed and full of errors, were to be put into print and published everywhere. And so that they cannot be corrupted or distorted in any way, I have published them in both Latin and the vernacular <note>original: "latine come vulgare"</note>, so that they may be more accessible to everyone.

I pray all the men and women who read them that, if they hear it said that I have foretold other things in the past up until the present day—the day this booklet was composed—they should not believe any that are not written herein. This is because our lukewarm <term>lukewarm: "tepidi," a term Savonarola used for his detractors or those indifferent to his reforms</term> friends and the "wise" of this world have attributed many false things to me, both within Florence and outside it—though more outside than within.

I know, however, that even these written words will be interpreted in different ways, according to that passage from Daniel 12:

And by many, they will even be derided. Nevertheless, whoever reads them with simplicity of heart, I believe will be enlightened by the truth and will produce some good fruit; for it is written:
> His conversation is with the simple. <note>original Latin: "Cum simplicibus sermocinatio eius" (Proverbs 3:32)</note>

¶ But before I enter into the narration of the things we have to say, it seems necessary to me, for the understanding of those things, to briefly declare the manner of prophetic revelations, so that everyone may understand how prophets learn from God that which they preach to the people. Because, therefore, as it is written in the first book of Kings in the ninth chapter <note>In modern Bibles, this is 1 Samuel 9:9.</note>: **He who is today called a prophet was formerly called a seer** <note>original: "Qui propheta dicitur hodie uocabatur olim uidens"</note>. A prophet is properly called one who sees things far removed from the natural knowledge of every creature; although the prophet also learns many things through the light of prophecy that are not far from human knowledge, because that light can extend to all things, both human and divine. 

Far from the natural knowledge of every creature are future contingent things <term>future contingent things: Events that may or may not happen, specifically those that are not determined by the laws of nature but by human choice</term>, especially those that proceed from free will. These cannot be known in themselves by men or by any other creature because they are not present except to eternity, which embraces all time. Nor can they yet be known by the rational creature, nor even by the intellectual creature in their causes; because, the causes being indifferent to producing them or not producing them, the created intellect cannot see toward which side those causes will incline. And therefore all the divinatory arts are rejected by the Scriptures and by the Canons <note>Canon law, the body of laws and regulations made by ecclesiastical authority (Church leadership).</note>, the head of which is judicial astrology <term>judicial astrology: The practice of predicting specific human actions or the fates of nations by the stars; Savonarola wrote a famous treatise against this practice</term>. For knowing future contingent things is a property of divine wisdom, in the presence of which every thing is past, present, and future, as it is written: **All things are naked and open to His eyes** <note>original: "Omnia sunt nuda & aperta oculis eius"; Hebrews 4:13</note>. Thus, future contingent things cannot be known by any natural light, but God alone is He who knows them in the eternity of His light: and

From Him alone do those to whom He deigns to reveal them learn these things. In this revelation, He does two things: one is that He infuses a **supernatural light** into the prophet/ which light is a certain participation in His eternity, through which the prophet judges two things regarding what is revealed to him/ namely, that they are true and that they are from God. And this light is of such efficacy that it makes the prophet as certain of these two things as **natural light** makes philosophers certain of the first principles of the sciences <note>In medieval philosophy, "first principles" are self-evident truths that do not require proof, such as "the whole is greater than the part."</note> and as it also makes every man certain that two and two make four. 

The other thing that God does in this revelation is that He distinctly proposes to the prophet what He wishes him to know and announce/ and this He does in many ways: as it is written in Hosea, chapter 12 <note>Hosea 12:10</note>: **I have spoken to the prophets: and I have multiplied visions/ and I have used similitudes by the hand of the prophets.** <note>original: "Locutus sum super prophetas : & ego uisionem multiplicaui/ et in manu prophetarum assimilatus sum."</note> 

Sometimes, that which the prophet must announce, He infuses into the intellect without any other <term>imaginary vision: A vision that appears to the internal faculty of the imagination rather than to the physical eyes.</term> in the way that He infused **wisdom** into Solomon/ and in this way the prophet David prophesied. Sometimes in the imagination He forms various figures and imaginary visions/ which signify what the prophet has to understand and announce: and he, through the aforementioned light, understands the entire meaning of the said visions: otherwise he could not be called a prophet. Wherefore it is written in Daniel, chapter 10 <note>Daniel 10:1</note>/ **There is need of understanding in a vision.** <note>original: "Intelligentia opus est in uisione."</note> 

And many times in those visions he hears various words pronounced within by different persons represented to his mind: which words, through the aforementioned light, he recognizes as proceeding from God through the ministry of the **Angels**. Sometimes God proposes to the external senses, especially to the eyes, things signifying that which must [be known...]
