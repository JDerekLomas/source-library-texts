# MOST SERENE
## PRINCE.

THE errors of nature, when she wanders into strange births <note>original: "alienos degenerantis in partus" — referring to biological anomalies or "monsters" of nature</note>, were once consecrated to the everlasting glory of Ferdinand II, Grand Duke of Tuscany; while they changed from the Medici spheres <note>A play on the "palle" or balls on the Medici coat of arms</note> into wondrous beauty and strength, why should I not place before your light, Most Serene Prince—most worthy nephew of so great an uncle—the precious flight of that same wandering nature through the depths of minerals? This was captured by that same Ulysses of Bologna <note>referring to Ulisse Aldrovandi (1522–1605), the famous naturalist</note> and drawn forth from the almost inaccessible hiding places of the concealed earth. These things, circumscribed by the orbit of your wisdom and enriched by the new brilliance of your patronage, will become famous for posterity. How beautifully, moreover, is everything that glows in gold and shines in gems devoted to a "Golden and Gem-like Prince," you who are truly the delight and the prize of our age! The lights of the subterranean firmament, which our Mineralogy investigates—and which nature, an Argus <note>In Greek mythology, Argus was a giant with a hundred eyes</note> with as many eyes as she has distinguished sparks of metals and stones—are dedicated to you, the sun of Italy! For truly, if all the power in metals comes from the influence of the wandering stars <note>the planets</note>, upon whose milk they grow and flourish, who does not know that the heaven of the Farnese lineage has transcribed into itself all the honors of other Princes, just as if they were planets? And since you have made for yourself a most rich compendium of them all, who can doubt that this work on Metals must be consecrated to you alone? For who does not marvel that there flows into you an extraordinary and portentous heap of splendors: the leaden gravity of Saturn from Ranuccio, your most excellent grandfather; the amber-like <note>original: "electrinam"</note> kindness of Jupiter from Alexander, Cardinal of the Vatican Court; the iron fortitude of Mars from Duke Alexander, once the lightning-bolt of Belgium; the golden profusion of the Sun’s Graces and Royal Majesty from Odoardo, your most auspicious father; the copper beauty of Venus from the distinguished Duke Ottavio; the quicksilver <note>mercurial</note> prudence of Mercury from Odoardo, Cardinal of the Holy Roman Church; and finally, the silvery wisdom of the Moon from the heroine Margaret, the arbiter of arms and peace? For even if I set aside Paul III, whom the voices of all ages praise, because with immoderate light...

# BARTHOLOMEW
# AMBROSINI

## Greetings to the Benevolent Reader.

In these times, nothing seems more praiseworthy to all than to leave behind a perpetual monument of one’s name for posterity. For this reason, men greedy for honor and glory despise not only wealth and riches, but even their own lives. Nevertheless, when the matter is considered more accurately, those who strive for everything to give birth to an eternal name for themselves should be judged more worthy of blame when they are driven by ambition rather than virtue; for they look not to what is honorable and useful to posterity, but only to what will be glorious for themselves. Therefore, only those are said to achieve true immortality of name who, led by the love of virtue and the desire to be helpful, favor the utility of all people with their whole effort. Among these, the most learned Ulisse Aldrovandi <note>1522–1605, a celebrated Italian naturalist and the father of modern natural history</note> must undoubtedly be counted. After publishing many volumes, he bequeathed a great many images carved on woodblocks to the Most Illustrious Senate of Bologna <note>original: "Bononiæ"</note>. With magnificent honors, the Senate invited the most learned professors so that, once a man skilled in these matters was chosen, these same images might finally be brought to light, adorned with their histories. Therefore, in order to obey the command of the Most Illustrious Senate and to gratify you, I have added the histories to the printed figures of clawed animals, Serpents, and Monsters; and now I commit this examination of Metallic subjects <note>referring to minerals and ores</note> with their images to the living breeze. Thus, God willing, the long-desired botanical work, adorned with the images of herbs, may be brought into public view. Farewell, honest Reader, and freely correct the errors; for they did not arise from my mind, but from the ignorance and carelessness of the printers.

->*Read through this volume of Fossils, studious Reader,*<-
->*Because it contains the riches of the world.*<-

# The Metallic Museum of the Great Ulisse Aldrovandi

I, the appointed Censor of books for the Most Eminent and Reverend Lord Cardinal Ludovisi, Archbishop and Prince of Bologna <note>Cardinal Ludovico Ludovisi (1595–1632) was a powerful patron of the arts and sciences in Bologna</note>, have examined this *Metallic Museum* of the great Ulisse Aldrovandi <note>original: "Vlyssis Aldrouandi"</note>, which was compiled by the most distinguished Bartholomew Ambrosini <note>Ambrosini was the custodian of the botanical garden and Aldrovandi's successor</note>, a diligent seeker of sublunary matters. Within this work, while the properties of minerals are narrated with subtlety and singular learning, the admirable secrets of Nature shine forth, explained with elegant erudition. Furthermore, because I have found nothing in it inconsistent with the Christian faith or good morals—indeed, I found many things written with wonderful piety for the cultivation of minds—I judge that it may be committed to the press and granted the light of publication with the greatest benefit and utility to students.

->*I, Francesco Natali of Bologna, Public Professor in the Ordinary Chair of Philosophy.*<-

Don Andreas Cuttica, Cleric Regular of St. Paul <note>The Barnabites</note>, Rector of the Penitentiary, acting on behalf of the Most Eminent and Reverend Lord Cardinal Archbishop.

I have reviewed this volume inscribed with the name of the extraordinary Ulisse Aldrovandi, titled *On Minerals and Stones*, which was elaborated through a course of most learned nightly vigils <note>original: "vigiliarum," referring to scholarly work done by lamplight</note> and reduced to a more abundant doctrine for those seeking knowledge by the most excellent Lord Bartholomew Ambrosini, by order of the Reverend Father Inquisitor of Bologna. It is truly an outstanding work that highly commends its author; for in it, through all-encompassing erudition and wonderful genius, the healthy and secret doctrine of minerals is penetrated and handed down. Therefore, having read this through and weighed it attentively, I have found nothing discordant with the faith or good morals. For this reason, for the greater glory of God, the utility of mankind, and the memory of such a great man, I deem it best that it be brought to light.

->*I, Fabiano de Fabiani, Public Doctor of Philosophy and Medicine.*<-

The aforementioned attestations standing, let it therefore be printed.
Friar Ludovico Maria Calcho, Master of Sacred Theology and Vicar General of the Holy Office <note>The Inquisition</note> of Bologna.
