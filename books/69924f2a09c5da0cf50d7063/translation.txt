## ECHOES FROM THE <term>GNOSIS</term><note>From the Greek word for "knowledge," referring to a direct, experiential insight into spiritual mysteries rather than mere intellectual learning.</note>.

Under this general title is now being published a series of small volumes, drawn from, or based upon, the <term>mystic</term><note>Relating to the pursuit of achieving communion with, or conscious awareness of, an ultimate reality or the Divine.</note>, <term>theosophic</term><note>In this context, it refers to "Divine Wisdom" (theosophia), a philosophical approach seeking to understand the nature of divinity through direct intuition and spiritual study.</note> and <term>gnostic</term> writings of the ancients, so as to make more easily audible for the ever-widening circle of those who love such things, some echoes of the <term>mystic experiences</term> and <term>initiatory lore</term><note>The secret knowledge or traditions passed down through "initiation" into ancient mystery schools.</note> of their spiritual ancestry. There are many who love the life of the spirit, and who long for the light of gnostic illumination, but who are not sufficiently equipped to study the writings of the ancients at first hand, or to follow unaided the labours of scholars. These little volumes are therefore intended to serve as introduction to the study of the more difficult literature of the subject; and it is hoped that at the same time they may become for some, who have as yet not even heard of the Gnosis, stepping-stones to higher things.

They were presumably thought of as Living Spheres, whirling out in every direction from the centre, and swirling in again to that same centre, once they had reached the limit of their periphery or surround. They were also, in all probability, conceived of as Winged Globes—a familiar figure in Babylonian and Egyptian art—thus symbolizing that they were powers of the Air, midway between Heaven, the Great Surround, and Earth, the fixed Centre. In other words, they were the Children of the **Æon** <note>original: "Æon." A Greek term for 'eternity' or 'age'; here it refers to a divine being representing the timeless source of the universe.</note>.

An anonymous ancient writer tells us (K. 39) <note>A reference to Fragment 39 in Wilhelm Kroll’s collection of the Chaldean Oracles.</note> that it is the blending of the intellectual <term>intellectual: In this philosophical context, often called "gnostic," referring to the divine Mind's capacity for active, self-conscious thought.</term> (or gnostic) and intelligible <term>intelligible: From the Greek *noetos*, referring to the realm of pure ideas or prototypes that can be understood by the mind but are not physical.</term> (or ideal) orders—that is, the union of the prototypes of what we distinguish as subject and object in the sense-world of diversity, or what we might call the self-reflective energy of the Mind on the plane of reality—that first "spirts forth" the One **Iynx** <note>original: "Iynx." ***This star-progression was not delivered from the womb of things because of thee.*** <note>The "womb of things" refers to the generative power of Nature (*Physis*); the fragment suggests the cosmos does not revolve simply to provide omens for human beings.</note>

<margin>K. 56.
C. 186.</margin>
***Bodies are allowed our self-revealed manifestations for your sakes.*** <note>The Greek term *autophania* refers to a god or spirit revealing their true form to a petitioner, rather than communicating through symbols or physical omens.</note>

And so we bring these two small volumes to a close in the hope that a few at least of the many riddles connected with these famous Oracles may have been made somewhat less puzzling.
