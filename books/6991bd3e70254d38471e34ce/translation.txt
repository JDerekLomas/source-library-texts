## CHAPTER I.

# VITALITY: ITS NATURE AND ORIGIN.

**W**HAT is Vitality?" This is the question that many of us find ourselves asking, after having heard the word used for many years. In a general way, we have understood that it is used to mean life, health, and strength; and yet, somehow, we feel that there is a deeper and more special meaning underlying the concept. And so there is, as we shall see as we proceed with our consideration of the subject.

"Vitality" is a word hard to define. Let us see what the dictionary makers have to say about it. Here are some of their attempts to express the meaning of the term: "Quality or state of being vital; vital force, etc." Not so very plain, is it?

Let us see what <term>Huxley</term> <note>Thomas Henry Huxley (1825–1895) was a celebrated English biologist and educator known as "Darwin's Bulldog" for his advocacy of the theory of evolution.</note> says about it in one of his scientific works: 
> "Considered apart from the phenomena of consciousness, the phenomena of life are all dependent upon the working of the same physical and chemical forces as those which are active in the rest of the world. It may be convenient to use the terms '*vitality*' and '*vital force*' to denote the causes of certain great groups of natural operations, as we employ the names of 'electricity' or 'electrical force' to denote others."

Another great authority, <term>Carpenter</term> <note>William Benjamin Carpenter (1813–1885) was an influential English physiologist and naturalist who studied the nervous system and biological organization.</note>, says that Vitality, or Vital-Principle, or Vital-Force, is: 
> "The principle which, in connection with matter, as in organized bodies, controls its manifestations and properties. Nothing is known of it, except as a force in connection with organization." 
Still a little cloudy, isn’t it?

But, let us not bother with the subtleties of definition. For our purpose, let us use the term "Vitality" in the sense of LIFE-FORCE, and leave the technicalities to the scientists and dictionary makers. We know that there IS such a thing as Life-Force—therefore, let us concern ourselves rather with the means by which it may be obtained, maintained, and regained if lost, rather than with the question of the ultimate meaning of Life itself. It is enough for us to know that Life *is*; and then learn how to secure and enjoy it.

### CHAPTER II.

## THE ENERGY OF THE AIR.

**I**f you turn to physics textbooks, you will see that the air we breathe is described as being composed of oxygen and nitrogen, with traces of several other less common gases. So far, so good; however, the atmospheric air also contains a certain force or energy—not a chemical substance, but rather a form of subtle energy—which in some ways serves to vitalize and energize the bodies of living things.

The truth of this is proven by experiments in which "artificial air" was manufactured from nitrogen and oxygen. When researchers attempted to keep animal or plant life alive in this mixture, the plants withered and the animals died after a short time. This demonstrates that something found in natural atmospheric air was lacking in the artificial version. It is as if natural air is charged with something similar to electricity, only far more subtle and refined, the purpose of which is to grant energy and vitality to living things.

Leaving aside for a moment the consideration of this <term>latent</term> <note>Existing but not yet developed or manifest; hidden or concealed.</note> energy in the air we breathe, let us take a quick look at the effect of oxygen when it is inhaled into the lungs. For you must remember this: there is a great vitalizing power in oxygen, which is shared with the entire body when it is breathed into the lungs.

You are most likely familiar with the process by which nature burns up waste matter in the lungs—waste carried in the blood as it returns to the heart. This impure, bluish blood flows back through the veins and is driven into the lungs by the heart. There, it is exposed to the oxygen just breathed into the lungs. The oxygen triggers a chemical combustion, which burns up the waste matter in the blood and expels it from the lungs in the form of <term>carbonic-acid gas</term> <note>original: "carbonic-acid gas"; now commonly known as carbon dioxide (CO2).</note>. Without this transformation process, the impure matter in the blood would poison the entire system in a very short time, resulting in death.

But this is not the only work performed by the oxygen breathed into the lungs. After the blood is cleansed of impurities in the lungs, it flows back through the arteries as rich, red, pure arterial blood, carrying minute particles of oxygen with it. These particles of oxygen are transported to every part of the body, where they serve to energize and vitalize every cell. This process is also critically important, and life cannot be maintained without it.

As for the mysterious energy in the air—which we may as well call "**vital energy**" from now on—its exact nature and how it operates are not very well-known. It is so subtle that it escapes detection by a <term>galvanometer</term> <note>An early scientific instrument used to detect and measure small amounts of electric current.</note> or the chemicals in a test tube. However, its existence seems to be proven by its effect upon living organisms. When a person absorbs a sufficient amount of it, they are vitalized, invigorated, and strengthened; when they fail to absorb enough of it, they become devitalized, weakened, and feeble.

Therefore, our first concern is to encourage you to take in a sufficient quantity of air during the process of breathing, for this is the primary way in which this atmospheric energy may be absorbed. Breathing is the most important of the three great bodily functions—eating, drinking, and breathing. While a person may live for days without food and hours without water, they can exist for only a few minutes without breathing. Truly, as the ancient writers were accustomed to saying: "**The Breath is the Life.**"

Let us, then, invite you to practice certain simple exercises designed to provide virility and life through the vitalizing components of the air. You may think that, because you have breathed your entire life, you already know how to breathe. However, wait until you have mastered these exercises; then you can decide for yourself whether you truly knew everything there was to know about breathing.

### CHAPTER III.

## BREATHING EXERCISES.

---

**HOW TO TAKE A FULL BREATH.** Stand erect, easily and naturally, with your hands hanging loosely at your sides.

Then, breathe a slow, full breath through your nostrils. At the beginning, let the air fill the lower part of the lungs. This is accomplished by "pressing down and out" on the abdomen by using the <term>diaphragm</term> <note>The large, flat muscle that separates the chest cavity from the abdominal cavity and acts as the primary muscle for breathing.</note>.

This abdominal breathing is the natural form of breathing for children and healthy, natural people. However, bending over desks, machines, or workbenches has caused the average person to lose the ability to perform it naturally, so a little practice is needed to regain it.

Secondly, fill the middle part of the lungs by pushing out the lower ribs and pushing the chest forward.

Third and finally, fill the upper part of the lungs, including the small cavities very high up. This last form of breathing is performed by slightly raising the chest and lifting up the <term>collarbone</term> <note>original: "collar bone."</note>.

Then, hold the breath for a second or two, and then exhale slowly, naturally, and fully until the lungs are completely emptied.

**HINTS REGARDING PRACTICE.** The movements described above must be practiced until you have mastered each one individually. Then, join them together into one continuous movement, one after the other, in an unbroken chain—not as a series of distinct, separate, or jerky movements. If you have difficulty at first with any of the breathing motions, it is helpful to practice before a mirror so you can see for yourself whether you are using the different parts of the body mentioned in the description. The abdominal form of breathing may also be practiced while you are lying down at full length. You will find it a wonderfully <term>vitalizing</term> <note>Giving strength and energy; life-giving.</note> exercise.

**HOW TO USE FULL-BREATHING.** You will find it is not easy to maintain this "full-breathing" throughout the entire day; it takes a long time to overcome old, imperfect breathing habits.

<note>The Elizabeth Towne Co.: A major American publisher of New Thought and self-help literature located in Holyoke, Massachusetts. Its founder, Elizabeth Towne, was a prominent figure in the movement.</note>

<note>L. N. Fowler & Co.: An established London publisher known for works on phrenology, health, and spiritual philosophy.</note>
