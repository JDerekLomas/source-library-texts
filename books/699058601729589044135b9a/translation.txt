# PREFACE

Although Prentice Mulford was one of the earliest pioneers of the "New Thought" <note>A spiritual movement originating in the 19th-century United States which emphasizes the power of positive thinking and the idea that "Infinite Intelligence" or God is everywhere.</note> teaching, he is still relatively little known or read in this country. This is chiefly because of the high price of the six volumes known as the "White Cross Library," the format in which his essays are published in America. It is hoped that this short selection of his essays will be the means of bringing his teaching within the reach of those, especially, who are able to purchase only a few or inexpensive books.

The complete series contains seventy-two essays treating man’s spiritual and physical life from various points of view. It has not been altogether easy to choose from such a wealth of original and suggestive thought those essays which will best appeal to the average reader. The selection has been made with the primary view of presenting those that seem likely to be especially practical and helpful. The text is printed as it was left by the author, except for the modernization of some American expressions and a few awkward sentences in which the meaning was obscure.

# THOUGHTS ARE THINGS

## THE MATERIAL MIND *versus* THE SPIRITUAL MIND

There belongs to every human being a higher self and a lower self—a self or mind of the spirit, which has been growing for ages, and a self of the body, which is but a thing of yesterday. The higher self is full of prompting ideas, suggestions, and aspirations. These it receives from the Supreme Power <note>The author's term for God or the Universal Intelligence</note>. All of this the lower or animal self regards as wild and visionary. The higher self argues for possibilities and powers for us greater than men and women now possess and enjoy. The lower self says we can only live and exist as men and women have lived and existed before us. The higher self craves freedom from the burden <note>original: "cumbrousness"</note>, the limitations, the pains, and the disabilities of the body. The lower self says that we are born to them, born to misfortune, born to suffer, and must suffer as so many have before us. The higher self wants its own standard for right and wrong. The lower self says we must accept a standard made for us by others—by general and long-held opinion, belief, and prejudice.

"To your own self be true," is an oft-uttered proverb <note>original: "adage"; a reference to Polonius's speech in Shakespeare's Hamlet</note>. But to which self? The higher or the lower?

# THE MATERIAL MIND

You have, in a sense, two minds—the mind of the body and the mind of the spirit.

Spirit is a force and a mystery. All we know, or may ever know of it, is that it exists and is constantly working and producing all results in physical things seen by the physical senses, as well as many more results not seen by them.

What is seen of any object—a tree, an animal, a stone, or a person—is only a part of that tree, animal, stone, or person. There is a force which, for a time, binds such objects together in the form you see them. That force is always acting on them to a greater or lesser degree. It builds up the flower to its fullest maturity. When it stops acting on the flower, it causes what we call decay. It is constantly changing the shape of all forms of what are called organized matter. An animal, a plant, or a human being is not the same physical shape this month or this year as they will be next month or next year.

This ever-acting, ever-varying force, which lies behind and, in a sense, creates all forms of matter, we call spirit.

To see, reason, and judge of life and things with the knowledge of this force is what is termed the "Spiritual Mind."

Through knowledge, we have the wonderful power of using or directing this force. When we recognize it and know that it exists, we can direct it to bring us health, happiness, and eternal peace of mind. Composed as we are of this force, we are always attracting more of it to ourselves and making it a part of our being.

With more of this force must come more and more knowledge. At first, in our physical existences, we allow it to work blindly. Then we are...

One result or reaction to this gloom born of hopelessness is a reckless spirit; people seek every possible gratification and pleasure regardless of right and justice, so long as the present body lasts.

It is difficult for the mind to give up its long-accustomed habits of thinking. In any case, it costs a struggle at first to admit that we have been mistaken and to abandon views we have held for a long time.

The material mind <note>The author uses "material mind" to describe a mindset focused purely on physical habits, tradition, and the visible world, as opposed to spiritual or intellectual growth.</note> wants to move in a rut of life and ideas, just as it always has done, and as thousands of people are doing now. It dislikes change more and more as the crust of old thoughts, maintained year after year, grows thicker over it. It wants to live on and on in the same house it has inhabited for years; to dress in the fashions of the past; and to go to business and return, year in and year out, at precisely the same hour. After a certain age, it rejects and despises the idea of learning any new accomplishments, such as painting or music. The greatest use of these arts is to divert the mind, rest it, and enable you to live in other departments of being. All of this is in addition to the pleasure given to you as the mind or spirit teaches the body more and more skill and expertise in the art you pursue.

The material mind sees the principal use of any art only as a means to make money. It does not see art as a means for giving variety to life, dispelling weariness, resting the portion of the mind devoted to business, improving health, or increasing the vigor of mind and body.

It holds to the idea of being "too old to learn." This is the condition of so many people who have arrived at or passed "middle age." They want to "settle down." They accept the idea of "growing old" as inevitable. Their material mind tells them that their bodies must gradually weaken, shrink from the fullness and proportions of youth, decay, and finally die.

When the mind has just begun to be awakened to these new truths, it will, in many cases, allow itself to be temporarily overpowered and ridiculed out of such ideas by its own **material mind**—the uneducated part of itself. In this struggle, it is very likely to be assisted by other material minds who have not woken up to these truths at all, and who are temporarily strengthened by the "positiveness of ignorance" <note>The confident certainty often displayed by those who lack knowledge.</note>. These people are like those who cannot see as far ahead as someone using a telescope, yet they may be perfectly honest in their disbelief regarding what the person with the telescope actually sees. Even if such people do not speak a word or argue against the beliefs of the partly awakened mind, their very thoughts act as a barrier or a blindfold against these glimpses of the truth.

But once the **spiritual mind** has begun to awaken, nothing can stop its further progress, even though the material mind may retard <note>original: "retard"; meaning to delay or slow down</note> it for a time.

### SOME LAWS OF HEALTH AND BEAUTY

Your thoughts shape your face and give it its peculiar expression. Your thoughts determine the attitude, carriage, and shape of your whole body.

The law for beauty and the law for perfect health are the same. Both depend entirely on the state of your mind; or, in other words, on the kind of thoughts you most frequently project and receive.

Ugliness of expression comes from the unconscious transgression of a law, whether that ugliness is in the young or the old. Any form of decay in a human body, any form of weakness, or anything in the personal appearance of a man or woman which makes them repulsive to you, exists because their prevailing mood of mind has made them so.
