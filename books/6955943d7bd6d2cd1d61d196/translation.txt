# THE EPISTLE

<margin>To the Honorable Lady, the Lady [Name omitted in original]</margin>

...appropriate the protection of this work to some one particular person. In choosing that person, none occurs to me as more proper than your virtuous self—not so much for my private obligations to you (which are indeed manifold, as I must publicly acknowledge) but for a peculiar interest, I might say, that you seem to have in this book. 

This book, I say, was so much esteemed by your late most worthy Lord and husband, that had his leisure been equal to his learning and his will, it would have been presented by a more noble *translator*. This book, I say—which though perhaps as *philosophical* in its theoretical points as to be above your understanding—yet as truly *theological* in its practical parts (the principal end of such theories), I am persuaded it is in accord with, and subject to, your will. 

And yet also (*Madam*, for I would rather you should wisely fear than I should foolishly flatter), look into it as a mirror—not so much to see if most parts are beautiful, but if any are less so. Weigh whether in all things and at all times you have truly preferred the truth of the goodness of God before the vanity of the vice of the world. If you have, continue in that path; so...

<summary>The author dedicates this translation to a noblewoman, noting that her late husband—a man of great learning—highly valued the text and might have translated it himself had he the time.</summary>
<keywords>dedication, patronage, widowhood, moral reflection, translation, piety vs. vanity</keywords>
