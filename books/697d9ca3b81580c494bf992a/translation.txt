There is a type of moral mysticism <note>The author uses the term "sittlicher Mysticismus" to describe a school of thought that relies on spiritual intuition rather than rational logic to determine right and wrong.</note> which insists that all the principles by which we must guide our actions be derived from a supposed supernatural experience. According to this view, the morality of an action is to be demonstrated through the revelation of the consequences that follow it, while the voice of reason is to be entirely excluded from the process. The origin of this way of thinking is as venerable as its consequences are harmful. It arises from men of the best intentions—individuals who feel deeply and vividly that human beings are elevated far above mere sensory pleasure, but who lack the intellectual cultivation and strength of spirit to find the ground <note>The text breaks off here at the end of the page.
