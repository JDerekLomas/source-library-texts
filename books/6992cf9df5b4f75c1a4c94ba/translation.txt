# The Western Ocean: The State of Germany

Initially, [Bavaria] seized several small districts along the **Rhine** <note>original: "臘引" (Layin)</note> as well as two small districts in **Prussia** <note>original: "魯社" (Loushe)</note> called **Ansbach** <note>original: "唵斯巴治" (Ansibazhi)</note> and **Sulzbach** <note>original: "粥魯剌" (Zhulula), likely referring to the Duchy of Neuburg-Sulzbach or a similar administrative region</note>. Because of these acquisitions, **Bavaria** <note>original: "麻注里阿" (Mazhulia), a phonetic variant of the "Mawalia" mentioned on the previous page</note> is still regarded as a powerful state today. 

In matters of governance, two **chambers** <note>original: "麻占" (Mazhan), likely a phonetic rendering of the German *Kammer* (chamber) or *Magistrat*</note> were established. One serves as the office for the high-ranking officials and **high prelates** <note>original: "大敎師" (Dajiaoshi), literally 'Great Teachers', a term often used in early Chinese texts to describe high-ranking Christian clergy who held political office</note> to conduct business. The other serves as the office for the presiding teachers. These presiding teachers also oversee literature and the technical arts. Furthermore, for every seven thousand households, one **representative** <note>original: "頭目" (Toumu), literally 'headman'</note> is appointed to participate in deliberations. 

Every three years, the King convenes a general assembly consisting of the members of the two chambers and the representatives from various regions. Excluding the local defense forces and the **militia** <note>original: "蘭威" (Lanwei), a phonetic rendering of the German *Landwehr*</note>, whose numbers are not recorded here, the official standing army consists of:

*   **Infantry:** 36,566 men
*   **Cavalry:** 6,408 men
*   **Artillerymen:** 3,357 men

The soldiers are recruited from among those aged nineteen to thirty. <gloss>The above is from the original text</gloss>
