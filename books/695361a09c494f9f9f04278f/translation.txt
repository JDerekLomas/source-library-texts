### THE
## TRANSLATOR
### TO THE
# READER.

The eminence and *worth* of this *author* need no apology; his *works* sufficiently commend *him*. As this present book clearly shows, he used no tautologies <note>original: "Tautologies"; the unnecessary repetition of the same idea in different words</note>, circumlocutions, or fruitless ambiguities <note>original: "Ambages"; roundabout or deceptive ways of speaking</note> in his *writings*. Instead, like a good *master* intending to inform rather than perplex the *minds* of his *disciples*, he speaks so succinctly of all things as is rarely seen in any other *author*. 

The *end* <note>purpose</note> why he *wrote* in his *time* was, as he himself declares, not only to *teach* and *direct* the *ingenious* <note>the talented or intellectually gifted</note>, but also to *detect* and *weaken* <note>original: "Enervate"</note> the fallacious descriptions of *sophisters* <note>original: "Sophiſters"; those who use clever but false arguments, particularly in alchemy</note>, whom he pronounces cursed. He even says he would be accursed himself if he did not discover <note>reveal</note> their frauds. 

For a similar *end*, I was incited by a worthy *friend* of mine to produce this *translation*, so that the *WORKS* of so ancient and venerable an *author*—comprising so many and various most needful *preparations*—might now at length be published in the *English tongue*. This is especially necessary because at this very *day*, there are so many persons publicly exposing their *chemical preparations* (as they call them). If these were truly what they are presented to be, or even in some competent measure corresponded to the specious *titles* with which they are marked <note>original: "inſignized"</note>, they would commend their *authors* rather than needing to be commended by them. For *chemistry* <note>original: "Chymiſtry"</note> is a true and real *art*, and when handled by prudent *artists*, it produces true and real *effects*.

> *But alas! It is found by experience,*
> *That our empty vessels make the loudest sound.*

And (what is more to be lamented) men who have scarcely seen the first entrances of *Chemistry*, or at most are but *beginners* <note>original: "Tyro's"</note> in that most necessary (though much abused) *science*, account themselves sufficiently accomplished if they are confident enough to boast at the rate of *masters*. Indeed, they boast more than good *masters* in that *art* dare to do. For every conscientious man practiced in *chemistry* sees *reason* <note>original: "cause"</note> enough to lay his *hand* upon his *mouth* <note>A gesture of humility or silence.</note>; and yet he does not neglect to do what *good* he can without blowing a *trumpet*. He who does otherwise may very well be suspected; for (according to the common *proverb*) *good wine needs no bush* <note>An old proverb meaning that a good product does not need an advertisement or a sign (the "bush") to attract customers.</note>, and every well-performed *work* commends its own *worker*. 

It is recorded of the famous *painter Apelles* that after he finished any eminent *piece*, he always exposed it to public *view* before he dared to commend it (as a perfect *work*) to the person for whom he made it. His *goal* in this, as *histories* amply relate, was that he might hear himself praised or dispraised from the *mouths* of others. From this, he would be able (prudently guessing by what he heard) to correct and amend his own *errors*. 

If *physicians* of our *time* did, in imitation of *Apelles*, expose their *works* for the same *end*, and would—upon just and undeniable *criticism* <note>original: "censures"</note>—endeavor to amend their *errors*, then their frequent *publications* would be highly commendable; but it is otherwise. Yet *charity* forbids me to attribute this to *greed* <note>original: "avarice"</note>, *fraud*, or *ambition* (*vices* abominable in all men, especially in *physicians*), but rather to other less injurious *causes*, such as *ignorance* and a want of certain *experience*. In amending these, this *book* may prove very serviceable: be—

because the *ignorant* and *unexpert* may possibly be informed by it, and from there learn true *experiences* <note>original: "Experiences"; here meaning practical experiments or observations</note>, by which (if *industrious*) they will in *time* be able to *correct* their own *errors*, just as well as if they had used the *policy* of *Apelles* <note>Apelles was a renowned ancient Greek painter known for his diligence and for inviting public criticism to improve his work.</note>. But of *covetous*, *deceitful*, and *ambitious men*, there is no such *hope*. Therefore, omitting these, I do not doubt but that to every *man* studious of *truth* <note>original: "Verity"</note>, the present *work* will be highly acceptable: because herein he will find *instructions* sufficient to inform his *judgment* in preparing medicines truly *chemical* <note>original: "Chymical"</note>. 

For this *author* (excelling others in this regard) has clearly and candidly, though briefly, taught the *methods* of purifying all *metals*, *minerals*, *salts*, *alums* <note>original: "Allomes"</note>, etc. In which true and perfect *purification*, the *virtue* <note>The inherent medicinal power or essential quality of a substance.</note> of each *subject* (its *vice* and *impurity* being separated) is rendered ten times more efficacious in *medicinal use* than the same *subject* could have been without such preceding *preparation*; as *experience* daily proves. 

But if any *man* objects and says, "This *author* taught these *purifications* only in *order* to find the great *Philosophers' Stone*"; to him I answer this: All *philosophers* (ancient and modern) unanimously affirm that *impurity* tends toward *corruption* and *death*, but *purity* toward *incorruption* and *life*. Therefore, if they—in order to amend imperfect *metals*, namely to heal their *diseases*—so strictly enjoined the *separation* of *heterogeneous* <note>original: "Heterogeneals"; substances of a different or foreign nature.</note> parts and the *purification* of *homogeneous* <note>original: "Homogenal"; substances of the same essential nature.</note> things; how much more ought every faithful *physician* labor in purifying the *subjects* of *medicine* for the *human body* (which is more precious than all *metals*)? Since the substances specified here are no small *part* of that medicine, I leave this to the *judgment* of all. And having introduced these points, I crave the *reader's patience* in the perusal of what follows.

When I had perfected this *translation* of *GEBER*, my *purpose* was to have concealed my *name*, as I did in the *translation* of *Royal and Practical Chemistry*...
