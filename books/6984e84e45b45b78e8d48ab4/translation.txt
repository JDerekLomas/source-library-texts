# Author’s Introduction
## Opening

**The Author says:** I have written this book, *Petahiah*, in my own language <note>The author likely refers to a more accessible style of Hebrew or a translation of concepts into a more modern idiom for his time.</note> to make known the one who awakens with awe. It is a work of majesty and merit, for it was my lot to have grace bestowed upon my heart. Who am I, and what is my strength, that I should be counted among those who have a portion in this? I have not lived in vain, and certainly have not caused harm, for here is this agreement <note>Referring to a formal rabbinic approval or *haskamah*</note> that my dear friend, the distinguished leader Rabbi Yohanan, son of Mordechai Hayyat of Poland, urged upon me. He is a diligent student who seeks the good of his people, a hero in the study of Torah and in the words of the *Tosafot* <note>Medieval commentaries on the Talmud</note> beyond all his peers. Now he has taken it upon himself to bring this composition to light, because of his love for this wisdom. He did not come from the ends of the earth or the farthest reaches of the north just to hear a word, but rather the word of the Lord and the depths of my inquiries. At the opening of his desire, it was a simple matter—he felt something was missing, and so he urged me to return to this work and fashion a crown for the sages of the Germanic lands <note>original: "Ashkenaz"</note>. May the Lord strengthen them in their days; may peace be abundant for them. For according to the depth of their wisdom in the Mishna, the Gemara <note>The two main components of the Talmud</note>, and the intricacies of their sharp pilpul <note>A method of talmudic study involving intense logical analysis</note> and their sharpness in holy knowledge and reasoning, so too do their doubts and riddles increase. Thus they shall bear fruit and see their small efforts grow into branches, building this heavenly work with all their heart and soul.

**Furthermore,** it is better for me to address those who preceded me, who printed matters by permission of what I have written. They produced the finest flour <note>A metaphor for refined scholarship</note> and transformed it, even if they followed a system where they brought in students who listened to my voice in learning. Indeed, many in the days of my youth cried out for it—a great group of wonder-workers and scribes who are the recorders. In the place of their greatness, they marveled at his grasp of the traditions. Therefore, all the leaders and elders humbled themselves to hear these lovely lessons. They turned their faces to read the Five Books of Moses <note>The Torah</note> for one hour every day, and the book *Pardes Rimmonim* <note>The "Pomegranate Orchard," the foundational work of the Kabbalist Moshe Cordovero</note> in a holy way and with clear language, as if it were flowing freely for them. This was done so that the law would not be without a reason. It was a common saying in their mouths that the elder rabbis would not allow the elders or our brothers to neglect the ethics of the Prophet. It was not clear to my palate or to others, including all the heads of these provinces among the sages and masters of charms. At that time, they all learned, and every man opened himself up like a flock to the praise of the Lord; otherwise, the earth would be filled with the knowledge of God.

**I,** the lowliest of those appointed, set my heart to sit and expound upon the Torah of Moses. The voice of the author of the book—he is the well-known sage of the book *Kadmoni* <note>Perhaps referring to a "primordial" or ancient source</note>—who bequeathed the Torah and performed wonders of wisdom in his commentary on the *Zohar* and the *Tikkunim* <note>Foundational texts of Jewish mysticism</note>. His friends established themselves through him, and those who peered into his book of understanding gained wisdom. There are many pamphlets from what was not printed of the *Zohar*, including the Song of Songs, Lamentations, and a commentary on *Sefer Yetzirah* <note>The Book of Formation, an early mystical text</note>, and a commentary on the prayers according to the tradition of every man. His soul is met by the book *Ohr Yakar* <note>"Precious Light," Moshe Cordovero's massive commentary on the Zohar</note>, which is mentioned many times in *Pardes Rimmonim* and in many other places. This is in addition to a great and glorious oxen <note>metaphor for a heavy/substantial work</note> of sermons following the literal meaning <note>term: Peshat (the plain or literal sense of scripture)</note>, the very flow of the life of the Torah. Now, see for the children of Israel, who produce arrangements to strengthen the small ones, to be given to children as sweet and soft snacks; one simply receives from them great voices like the voice of pure gold. His voice is that of the honored teacher and Rabbi, Moses <note>Referring to Moshe Cordovero, known as the Ramak</note>, the holy one, chosen to exalt and magnify his glory, like a commentary of holy blackness and depth. His hand was steady in the way of the "levies" and the "principle." It is written: *This is the book of Elijah*, and in it are twelve springs of water and tribes like mountains, according to the judgment of God, according to the traditions. I have already been observing: the *Pardes* is nothing less than a ruler over these times. To all these things are added and beautified pamphlets and books, large and small, books like a vision of perfume.

**Moreover,** he was a great sage in <term>Gematria</term> <note>The mystical practice of calculating the numerical value of Hebrew words to find deeper meanings</note>, a glory who spent all his days teaching students the living Torah. He was also a gatherer for the way, judging righteously between a man and his brother, between a stranger and his fellow. He was a distinguished authority, our teacher and Rabbi, Moses Cordovero, of blessed memory, the teacher of all the children of the exile, a pious man in his literal interpretations. Upon his grave, it is said that the Torah was given to him. Come and see what a great man has preceded us! After him, he sent a burden of glory, and they accompanied him before the bier, an intermediate man who could be included, whom man is commanded to follow, and from whom man draws understanding.

<margin>Merit</margin>
**My teacher,** Rabbi Azriel, of blessed memory, was a man of understanding whose old age was always dedicated to study. He brought me in, and for his sake, I was bound to this house of radiant wisdom. Through his blessing, I heard, and the soul of the mentioned sage shall rejoice here. The "orchard" <note>Referring again to the *Pardes*</note> is like a fool's rest, lying in peace. When I reached my portion, I stood to gaze with my soul, so that the book of the *Pardes* would not be hidden from me. Through the kindness of the supporters, I was fattened and I heard; and like Moses, my eyes were opened. I found rest in arranging my compositions—blessed is the one whose writing they have recorded in the Heavenly Academy. See, my work is the work of Heaven. Was it not a book? It was a scroll, and I have given it. I marvel at the mentioned bodies who support the elders and the youths alike. They became perfect through my words, and there is a day for this. Therefore, with these words, the Merciful One has not allowed me to forget all that is involved. And even so, by the name of God, I have again put my hand and doubled the wisdom and the taste of the core of the book and all these latter calls.

**At that time,** the sages desired to arrange the work in this manner. I saw the book *Pardes*—that in the first twenty-two "Gates," the matters of the World of Emanation <note>term: Atzilut (the highest of the four worlds in Kabbalah)</note> were not fully explained. I made it into a section of its own, for my voice—and afterwards my heart's reason—knew it well. I wrote in it that he had done the same in his precious introductions, which were already printed by his son, a young but wise man. Many things that were not explained there in detail come out perfectly in this book. In the fourth book, the flow of the *Pardes* is according to the values of the "Knorr" <note>Probably referring to the *Kabbala Denudata* by Knorr von Rosenroth, or a specific arrangement of the text</note>. The work is vast and deep in that part—a great sea, undoubtedly. I too, in my poverty, have approached it with inquiries and conclusions of the discourse, as the good hand of the Lord was upon me, with many explanations of verses from the Torah, Prophets, and Writings, and the *Zohar* of our Sages of blessed memory. It sits and is clarified and exact, containing a "taste for praise" in this perfect understanding. The second part I have called *One Language*. It was spoken on the day I stood; perhaps the Lord will be gracious and I will merit to do great things with it. For my heart has sought the depths of what I have brought near according to the deep logic in it, which is also mentioned in the book of *Elijah*. I turned and looked, and in the end, everything related to this work is gathered together—limitless waters with the appropriate family, several steps for the Babylonians in the sources of the Gemara. This book shall be for a remembrance, and they shall continue to add to it. This aforementioned book, at the end of the book of separate inquiries, has not yet been refined by my judgment from potential to actual. I am at the Gate of Wisdom, weighing the deeds of the *Pardes*. May the Blessed One, in His mercy and abundant kindness, grant me the merit to magnify the Torah of truth and to glorify the Holy One, according to what flows from the hand of the Lord, the Opener. For the companion is great and mighty, and the Torah is like a circle upon the soul of the pure one. When she approaches, her brother-in-law will understand us for days, and there shall be rest.

**However,** this is the day I hoped for, to prove to a lowly generation that one should distance himself from this illness. The merit of this book is great, for it has been hidden under my hand for more than thirteen years, as is fitting for it to be complete.

# Author’s Preface
## Opening

<margin>The Editor says</margin> I, the transcriber of the book *The Engravings of the Seal* <note>original: Pituchei Chotam</note>, have come forth to publish this magnificent pamphlet and to grant the public merit through it. For this was my intention: "If I am not for myself, who will be for me? And if not now, when?" <note>A famous quote from Ethics of the Fathers 1:14</note>. Especially since I have added love and affection for it, following the agreement of the leader who encouraged me, my dear friend, the leader Rabbi Isaac, son of Mordecai Chait <note>Chait literally means "tailor" in Hebrew/Yiddish</note> of Poland. He is a veteran student who seeks the good of his people and stands as a fence within the kingdom of the Torah. Like the words of the *Tosefot* <note>A group of medieval commentators on the Talmud</note> regarding all those of his age, he has now taken upon himself a complete "exile" <note>A practice of wandering or traveling for spiritual penance or study</note> to merit his wife and children through the comforts of salvation. He shall acquire the land to the far north to hear the word of God. After he showed compassion and did His will, there was nothing hidden that I did not read, for his power of study is recognized. He chose to do this to bring satisfaction to the holy sages and the rabbis of our neighborhood. May God strengthen them as their fortress and grant them peace. According to the depth of their wisdom in the **Mishna** and the **Gemara** <note>The two primary components of the Talmud</note> and their sharp analytical debates <term>Pilpul: a method of Talmudic study involving intense logical analysis</term>, they increase their labor and actions. Thus, their piety shall spread to perform their work for the sake of Heaven with all your heart and soul.

**Furthermore**, it occurred to me that no one preceded me in printing this with the permission of what you wrote and added from the first edition. It is equal in taste, for they used to include important matters and various collections. Many among them were built upon a great foundation of sages and scribes of the first generations <note>original: Rishonim</note>. In the place of their greatness, we find their humility before the eyes of all who see them. They merited to hear words of study and returned them to their mouths to read into the ears of others—one report every day from the book *The Orchard of Pomegranates* <note>original: Pardes Rimonim, the seminal work of Rabbi Moses Cordovero</note> in a short path and clear language. They are like food seasoned with salt and spices, so that the law does not remain without "taste." This is a common saying among our rabbis: "Wheat in small bundles" <note>An idiom meaning complex ideas condensed into small, manageable portions</note>. I did not turn back or turn away from our peace or the peace of others. All the leaders of these provinces in matters of law and practice, according to our knowledge, let no one be missing. Praise be to God, "the earth is full of the knowledge of the Lord."

**I**, a dweller of these surroundings, took it to heart. I sat and expounded upon the Torah of Moses—referring to the author of this book, the famous sage, the master of the "Vineyard" and the "Straightening" <note>Refers to Cordovero's works *Elim* and *Tomar Devorah*</note> who magnified the Torah. He performed wonders in his commentary on the *Zohar* <note>The primary text of Jewish mysticism</note>, the *Precious Light* <note>original: Or Yakar</note>. His companions, his strength, and the praises of Spain—our rabbis there were nurtured by him. Many of his pamphlets were printed with warning and brilliance, such as the *Song of Songs* and *Lamentations*, as well as his commentary on the *Book of Formation* <note>original: Sefer Yetzirah</note> and the commentary on the prayers of the entire year. All these were composed by the holy investigator at the time he wrote the precious book *Or Yakar*, which the mouth of the master will take many times into the *Orchard of Pomegranates* <note>Pardes Rimonim</note> and many corrections. This alone is a great and scattered treasure of sermons according to the literal meaning <term>Pshat: the plain or literal meaning of scripture</term>. The words of the Torah are the very life of the Children of Israel—sweet like honey, small droplets for infants and nurslings to suckle. It was received according to great voices, the voice of the mighty Lord. His glory grew in the holy commentary of the Torah, with depth and wide reach in the path of brevity. It is written: "This is the book of the generations of Israel," twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees in peace. I realized that the *Pardes* is nothing less than a sharp tool for those who study these compositions. Above all these are additions and sanctifications in many books, large and small, books without end.

**Moreover**, he was a great power in the **Gemara**, the master Yom Tov <note>Possibly referring to Yom Tov Lipmann Heller or a similar contemporary scholar</note>. All his days he arranged the students, and he was also fixed in the Torah to judge righteousness between a man and his brother. He was a distinct teacher of the Levites, a holy and pious man. In the eulogies at his grave, what did they say of our wisdom? "The end of the Torah." Come and see what a great man he was, upon whom the pillars of the world trembled. He found wisdom and brought forth understanding.

**The merit** of my teacher, Rabbi Ovadia <note>possibly 'Orel'</note> son of Isaac Chait of Mantua, and his righteousness shall stand forever. He brought me in and helped me. This sealed work was given in his "pomegranate" <note>A metaphor for the Pardes Rimonim</note>, and I acted. May the soul of the wise author rejoice with the pious in glory and rest on its bed in peace. For when I reached the age of wisdom, I was sent the book *The Pardes* by one of his students. I rejoiced and delved into it, and from there my eyes were opened. I found rest in the order of the compositions after I was in the Yeshiva <note>Academy for Torah study</note> of Velu. This is my work—heavenly work, not of the flesh. I revealed its hidden parts and gave it immediately to the designated friends to spread before them. Daily I spoke of it. Therefore, these things were first gathered without being broken or shattered. I added my hand a second time and doubled it into ten sections, and these are the words.

**At the time** the agreement was made to arrange the work in this manner, I saw the book *The Pardes*. In its first twenty-two branches, the words were famous throughout the world of thinkers. It was easy for the eyes to see on its own. My heart was amazed because I aimed for the knowledge of the elder author as well. Afterward, he made his introductions, which I had already printed. This is a precious part for the young and the old. Many of his words in his commentary there are sufficient. This book comes to be established in the order of the *Kavvanot* <term>Kavvanot: mystical intentions or meditations during prayer</term>, which is a great and wide sea without doubt. I too, among the first, joined in the inquiries with the author's difficulties, by the good hand of God upon me. There are also many fruits—verses from the Torah, the Prophets, and the Writings, and the teachings of our Sages from the Mishna, Gemara, and Midrash, which have a "taste of praise" <note>original: Ta'am l'shvach - a legal term for a significant improvement or addition</note> in this perfect wisdom. I called this section *The Language of the Living*. I hope to merit making a wise edition as is in my heart. The author was deep in his roots in the book *Ghalika* in two tracts. In my standing, all the merits of this work shall complete my composition. At the end, I added appropriate additions. My heart rejoices in the labor of this book. Its light shall be seen, and they will add to the splendor of the gate. At the end of the book are the "Burning Sermons" which I have not yet brought from potential to reality. This is in the *Gate of the Script*, which is one of the gates of the *Pardes*. May the Blessed One, in His mercy and great kindness, choose me to magnify the true Torah and glorify His holy name through me.

**However**, this is the day I have hoped for, to bring it to the light of the world. The account is sent, and in the book *Shelach HaRimon* <note>The Branch of the Pomegranate</note>, happy is he who has it now under my hand, covered in blessing more than them all, the crown of the soul, as is fitting to be.
