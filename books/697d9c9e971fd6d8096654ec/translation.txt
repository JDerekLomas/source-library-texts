# Front Cover of the Manuscript

-> [An illustration of the front cover] <-

This volume is bound in high-quality **red morocco** <term>morocco leather</term> <note>A fine-grained, soft leather made from goatskin, highly prized by 17th-century book collectors for its durability and its ability to take vibrant dyes.</note>. The exterior is decorated with a **gold-tooled** triple-border of thin lines, known as fillets, with small floral stamps at each of the four corners.

At the very center of the cover is an ornate, gold-stamped **armorial centerpiece** <note>A heraldic design indicating ownership of the book.</note>. This emblem consists of:

*   An oval wreath made of **laurel branches**.
*   A central shield featuring a **vertical serpent**. This is a punning reference—often called <term>canting arms</term>—to the owner’s name, as the Latin word for serpent is *coluber* <note>original: "coluber"</note>.
*   A **marquis’s coronet** resting atop the shield, indicating the owner's noble rank.

These are the arms of **Jean-Baptiste Colbert** <note>Colbert (1619–1683) was the powerful Minister of Finances under King Louis XIV of France and one of the most famous bibliophiles in history; his library was one of the greatest private collections of the 17th century.</note>. 

The spine of the book, visible at the left, features **raised bands** with gilded compartments. Each section between the bands is decorated with small, gold-stamped floral ornaments.
