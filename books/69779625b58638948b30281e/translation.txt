## For the LORD
# VREESWYCK.

T**HE West India Company.** <note>The French West India Company (*Compagnie des Indes Occidentales*) was a chartered company established in 1664 to manage French colonies in the Americas.</note> *To Lord Georges van Vreeswyck. Greetings.* Since the Country of Canada, or New France, is found to be abundant in Mines of various sorts, the tests of which have been performed giving hope of drawing a considerable profit <note>original: "proffit"</note> therefrom, if the management of it is entrusted to an Intelligent person well-versed in these kinds of works; We, the Directors General of the said Company, being fully informed of your capacity and experience in the knowledge of Mines, and that you know how to extract from them what useful products they can produce with all possible management <gloss>original: "mesnagement"; here meaning careful stewardship or economy of resources.</gloss>, trusting moreover in Your loyalty and affection, have by these presents Commissioned and do Commission you to have the principal management of the works of all the Mines of the said country of Canada, or New France, to oversee the opening of them and to place there the necessary people to smelt metals, and generally to act in everything concerning your said Commission for the good and advantage of the said Company according to the orders and instructions which shall be given to you by us. **M**oved by love for my neighbor, I have wished to bring this little handbook into the light; so that everyone who takes it in hand might see therein what a <term>Mining Master</term> <note>original Dutch: *Berg-meeſter*. This art is a gift of God; therefore, whoever begins with God overcomes everything that might come against him. One can read of this same subject in the Holy Scripture in Job 28, which has written very gloriously thereof; such as where the place is where one can find the <term>Minerals</term> and <term>Metals</term>, according to the will of God, and not according to our own will. # OF MINERALS, etc.

*Paracelsus*, *Agricola*, *Lazarus Erker* <note>Georgius Agricola (1494–1555) and Lazarus Ercker (c. 1530–1594) were the leading European experts on geology and metallurgy in the 16th century.</note> and others write of knowing the signs of the veins and paths <note>original: *aderen en gangen*. In mining, "veins" are smaller cracks filled with ore, while "paths" or "lodes" are the larger, primary mineral deposits.</note> beneath the earth; and yet, not everything is certain in our country, let alone in the lands where they have never been in their lives. For I have found this: that the stone there lies on its flat side, whereas in our Lands it lies mostly on its edge. In those countries, there are also places where the mountains are not much cracked; so that one must, with great effort, hew into or through a mountain, or burn it with <term>$\Delta$</term> <gloss>fire</gloss> <note>The triangle is the alchemical symbol for fire. "Fire-setting" was an ancient mining technique where rock was heated with fire and then quenched with water to make it crack.</note>, just as I myself had to do in Canada and other places.

Now then, to come to our purpose of proving that the descriptions of the <term>mountain-signs</term> <note>original: *Berg-teekens*. Surface indicators like soil color or vegetation that suggest minerals lie beneath.</note> are not certain in our Lands, and much less so in other Lands. When *Paracelsus* and others write that there would be minerals where no tall trees or grass grow, that is false: for I have seen myself that in the places where I found a mine, the most beautiful trees grew that anyone could wish for with heart and soul. However, everything they describe is mostly correct for the heights where they lived; but what is known to me regarding this, I shall now share with the world; and firstly, concerning the signs of the paths and veins, and in what manner they may be discovered according to the common style, and I have divided that into 15 Articles.
