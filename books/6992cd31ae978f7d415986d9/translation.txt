# Collected Illustrations of the Three Realms <note>original: "Sancai Tuhui"</note>

### Geography, Volume 7

Suzhou <note>original: "Wu Men," referring to the gateway to the ancient State of Wu</note> commands the scenic splendor of the "Three Rivers and Five Lakes" and is celebrated as the "deep interior" <note>original: "aoqu," a term for a secluded or profound region</note> of the southeast. The Great City of Wu was constructed by King Helü <note>A famous monarch of the Spring and Autumn period (r. 514–496 BCE) who established the foundations of the city.</note>. Its perimeter measures forty-seven *li*. It features eight land gates, symbolizing the eight celestial winds, and eight water gates, patterned after the eight trigrams <term>Bagua: a set of eight symbols used in Taoist cosmology to represent the fundamental principles of reality.</term> of the earth.

Within this region lie the scenic wonders of Mount Qionglong, Mount Xuanmu, Tiger Hill, Mount Lingyan, and Mount Dongting. Tiger Hill is located nine *li* to the northwest of the city; later, to observe a naming taboo <note>To avoid the personal name of the grandfather of the first Tang Emperor, Li Hu, the character for "Tiger" (Hu) was replaced.</note> during the Tang dynasty, the name "Tiger" was changed to "Valiant."

Mount Lingyan lies twenty-four *li* west of the city. The detached palace of the King of Wu once stood here, featuring the Palace of the Beautiful Maidens <note>original: "Guanwa Gong"</note>, the Lute Terrace, the Corridor of Echoing Clogs, and the Cave of Xi Shi <note>Xi Shi was a legendary beauty sent by the rival Kingdom of Yue to distract the King of Wu, leading to his eventual downfall.</note>. At the mountain's summit is a pond from which one can look down upon the Great Lake. Gazing toward the Western Mount Dongting, the dripping emerald of the peaks and the clustered greenery make one feel as if they are standing within a world of shimmering silver.

Mount Dongting is also known as Mount Bao, situated to the southwest of Wu County. Within it lies Linwu Cave <note>The "Forest Chamber Cave."</note>. The King of Wu once commanded the "Master of Spiritual Power" <note>original: "Weiling Zhangren"</note> to enter the cave; though he journeyed for seventeen days, he could not reach its end. During this expedition, he obtained the "Writings of Yu" <note>Ancient texts or charms attributed to the legendary Great Yu, who tamed the prehistoric floods of China.</note>. It is also said that Linwu Cave is one of the "Ten Great Grotto-Heavens" <term>Dongtian: sacred sites or "cavern-heavens" in Taoism believed to be inhabited by immortals.</term>. All of these sites are among the most peerless landscapes in the world.

Gusu Terrace, also known as Xu Terrace, is located on Mount Gusu. The *Records of the State of Yue* <note>original: "Yuejue Shu," a historical gazetteer of the Wu and Yue regions</note> states that King Helü <note>reigned c. 514–496 BCE; he was a famous monarch of the Wu state</note> built Gusu Terrace. It took three years of gathering timber before the project was completed. Its height was so immense that it could be seen from three hundred *li* <note>a traditional unit of distance; 300 li is approximately 150 kilometers</note> away. Tradition says that the phrase "deer roaming at Guxu" <note>a famous prophecy of ruin foretold by the official Wu Zixu</note> refers to this very location.

A poem by Liu Jia <note>a Tang Dynasty poet known for his descriptions of historical sites</note> says:
"While Goujian <note>the King of Yue and rival to Wu</note> spent his days tasting gall,
The King of Wu kept his wine cup full.
As the music of pipes and songs rose into the sea-clouds,
The sounds drifted down from Gusu Terrace."
<note>Goujian famously "slept on brushwood and tasted gall" to remind himself of the bitterness of his previous defeat while he secretly planned to overthrow the King of Wu.</note>

A poem by Li Bai <note>one of the most celebrated poets in Chinese history</note> says:
"When the crows come to roost on Gusu Terrace,
The King of Wu is drunken with Xi Shi in his palace."
<note>Xi Shi was a legendary beauty sent by the King of Yue to distract the King of Wu, leading to the eventual downfall of his kingdom.</note>
This was written as a lament for those past events.

**Tiger Hill** <note>Huqiu</note> is situated only a few miles from the prefectural government seat. Small streams and winding ravines weave through the landscape, encircling the hill like a crescent moon. Its most secluded and beautiful spot is the **Hejing Shrine** <note>Dedicated to the Song dynasty poet Lin Bu, also known as Hejing, famous for his reclusive life</note>. The scenery here seems to breathe out green foliage and gather white mists where the land meets the sky. 

Atop the hill stands a **pagoda** <term>pagoda<note>The Yunyan Pagoda, a famous "leaning" pagoda built in the 10th century</note></term> that pierces the high clouds. Looking down from its heights, the city of **Gusu** <note>An ancient name for Suzhou</note> appears so small it could fit in the palm of one's hand. Turning south from the pagoda, one finds the **Lecture Hall of Master Sheng** and the **Pavilion of the Realized Stones**. 

Beside the pavilion lies the **Sword Pool**. Its cliffs stand thousands of feet high, the twin rock faces so steep and sheer they look as if they were split by a divine blade. The water within is clear and cold, making a trickling sound as it flows. Below the cliffs is a massive, open rock, so vast and grand that it can accommodate more than a dozen <term>folding chairs<note>original: "Hu-beds," a style of portable seating introduced to China from Central Asia</note></term>. Within the area of the **Thousand People Rock** is the **White Lotus Pond**, where the lotuses stand tall and upright, their blossoms as brilliant as vermilion and jade.

Descending slightly further along a winding stone path, one encounters unusual spring basins and strangely shaped rocks. Suddenly, the path turns through a lush grove of pines and bamboo; this is the place where **Hejing** used to sit and read. 

This hill is the burial site of **Helü, the King of Wu** <note>A famous ruler from the Spring and Autumn period, 5th century BCE</note>. The tomb is said to contain many treasures: golden ducks, jade geese, bronze camels, crystals, pearls, and cinnabar. Legend tells that a **white tiger** once crouched upon the summit, which is how the hill earned its name. During the Jin dynasty, the high official **Wang Xun** and his brother **Wang Min** both established their residences here.

According to the local geographical records, the mountain is situated sixty *li* <note>approximately 30 kilometers or 18 miles</note> west of **Wu County** <note>modern-day Suzhou</note>. Ascending from the steep ridges, halfway up the mountain, one finds a pool. It spans more than several dozen *zhang* <note>a *zhang* is about 3.3 meters or 11 feet</note> across; for this reason, it is named the **Heavenly Pool** <note>original: *Tianchi* (天池)</note>.

During the Taikong era of the Jin dynasty <note>280–289 AD</note>, this pool frequently produced "thousand-petaled lotuses," which led to the mountain also being called **Mount Hua** <note>original: *Huashan* (華山); not to be confused with the Great Western Peak in Shaanxi province</note>. 

Upon the mountain, one can find the **Stone Drum** and the **Stone House**. There are also several other notable features: 
*   The **Tortoise Nest Rock**
*   The **Elegant Screen**
*   The **Tiger Gallop Spring** 
*   The **Dark Jade Cave**

During the Shaoxing era of the Song dynasty <note>1131–1162 AD</note>, the scholar Zhang Hanqing once resided here and gave it the name **Mount Jiuyin** <note>original: *Jiuyin Shan* (就隱山), meaning "The Mountain for Taking Up Seclusion"</note>.

Among the mountains of the Wu region <note>The cultural area surrounding Suzhou</note>, Mount Xuanmu <term>Black Tomb|Xuanmu</term> is the most secluded, yet it is also the most extraordinary. It faces the lake with steep precipices; when viewed alongside its red cliffs and emerald pavilions, it resembles a decorative screen. It is also known as Mount Dengwei. 

Mount Fahua <note>The Dharma Flower Mountain</note> serves as a protective screen in front of it. The "Bronze Pit" and "Green Branch" ridges meander to its left, while the "Wandering Dragon" ridge marks its right boundary. Its ridges and peaks connect in strange shapes and wondrous sights. Tradition holds that the immortal Yu Taixuan was buried here, which is why the mountain received its name <note>Xuanmu literally means "The Tomb of [Yu Tai]xuan"</note>. 

Furthermore, because the monk Wanfeng <note>Meaning "Ten Thousand Peaks"</note> practiced the Buddhist path beneath it, the mountain is also called Wanfeng. On its southern slope stands the Wanfeng Pagoda Cloister. The mountain is lush with fine arrow-bamboo <note>A species of slender, straight bamboo used for making arrows</note>. After ascending stone steps carved into the craggy cliffs, one reaches the Lake Pavilion of Master Hao’s Quarters. Beneath this pavilion grow many tall pines; when the wind rises, the valleys echo and the waters surge with a sound that can be heard for dozens of miles. 

Its most remarkable feature is found at the very summit: once you have climbed to the top, the various peaks of the Dongting range <note>The islands of East and West Dongting in Lake Tai</note> all appear to float upon the surface of the lake. At the foot of the mountain are strange stones of great beauty and bizarre form. From Green Stream to Tiger Mountain Bridge—a bridge also nestled among the rugged hills—six streams wind out from beneath it. Gazing out over the waves of Lake Zhenze <note>An ancient name for Lake Tai</note>, the water reflects and wraps around...

Mount Lingyan, known in ancient times as Mount Yanshi (Inkstone Stone Mountain), is located twenty <term>li</term><note>A traditional Chinese unit of distance; twenty li is approximately 10 kilometers or 6 miles.</note> west of Wu County. It is also known as Mount Shigu (Stone Drum Mountain).

It was here that Helü, the King of Wu, <note>Helü (r. 514–496 BCE) was a famous monarch of the Wu state during the Spring and Autumn period.</note> kept the beauties from the state of Yue. Upon the summit are two lakes. The *Continued Illustrated Classic* states that the peak of Mount Yanshi contains three ponds: the Sun-Moon Pond, the Inkstone Pond, and the Wanhua Pond <note>original: 翫華, meaning "Enjoying the Flowers."</note>. These do not run dry even in times of drought, as they were reportedly carved out during the era of the Wu state.

On the mountain, there used to be the Corridor of Resonant Clogs <note>The "Xiangxie" Corridor was famously built with hollow earthenware jars buried beneath the floorboards so that the sound of Xishi's wooden shoes would echo musically as she walked.</note>. Below it is a stone chamber which folk tradition identifies as the place where the King of Wu imprisoned Fan Li <note>A famous politician and strategist from the rival Yue state.</note>. There is also Yao Village; the stone produced by this mountain can be used to craft inkstones. <gloss>The name "Inkstone Stone" originates from this fact.</gloss>

The *Annals of Wu Prefecture* notes that when one climbs the mountain, the various islands of Lake Tai appear as if nestled within silver waves; it is a peerless landscape within the universe. The ruins of the Guanwa Palace <note>The "Palace of the Beautiful Lady," constructed by the King of Wu for his favorite consort, Xishi.</note>, the Cave of Xishi, the Corridor of Resonant Clogs, Fragrant Water Stream, and the King of Wu’s Well are all historical traces of this legacy.

## Page Nine

**Mount Tianping**

The *Illustrated Canon of Mount Tianping* states: The mountain is located twenty *li* <note>approximately 10 kilometers or 6 miles</note> west of Wu County. It is lofty and exceptionally high, with the surrounding peaks bowing toward it in a gesture of respect. It is the <term>guardian mountain</term> <note>a "Zhenshan" is a primary mountain believed to provide spiritual protection to a specific region</note> of the Wu territory.

The *Records of Wu Prefecture* state: This mountain is the highest in the Wu region. Its ancient clusters of peaks rise tall and upright, standing in solitary grandeur. The mountain is composed entirely of fantastic rock formations. Halfway up the mountain is the **White Cloud Spring**, which is also considered the finest in the Wu region. The summit is perfectly flat, and upon it sits the **Lake-Viewing Terrace**. Below the mountain is the **White Cloud Temple**. 

During the Song Dynasty, Fan Zhongyan <note>original: "Fan Weigong," a renowned statesman, philosopher, and poet (989–1052 CE)</note> petitioned the Emperor to bestow a name upon the temple, and his ancestral burial grounds are located here.

The *Records of Gusu* state: Located five *li* south of Zhixing <note>Zhixing is another notable hill in the Suzhou area</note>, this mountain contains the greatest number of fantastic rocks and miraculous sites. These include:
*   **The Hermitage of Master Yuan** <note>likely referring to the monk Huiyuan</note>
*   **The Ascetic’s Crag**
*   **The Dragon Gate**
*   **The Large and Small Stone Houses**
*   **The Table-Brush Peak** <note>a peak shaped like an upright calligraphy brush</note>
*   **The "Fly-Hither" Peak** <note>a common name for peaks traditionally said to have flown through the air from India to China</note>
*   **The Lake-Viewing Stone**
*   **The Smoke-Reflecting Stone**
