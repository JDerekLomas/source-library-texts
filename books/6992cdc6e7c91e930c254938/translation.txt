# Treatise on Armament Technology

<note>original: 武備志 (Wubei Zhi)</note>

This compendium addresses the <term>Art of War<note>original: 兵法 (Bingfa); refers to the traditional Chinese study of military strategy, tactics, and logistics.</note></term> as established during the **Ming Dynasty**. It was meticulously compiled by the scholar-official **Mao Yuanyi**. 

<note>Historical context: Completed around 1621, this work is the most comprehensive military encyclopedia in Chinese history, spanning 240 volumes and covering subjects from weaponry and troop formations to coastal defense and astronomy.</note>

The posture of the <term>Primal Unity Formation</term> <note>original: 混元陣 - *Hunyuan Zhen*; a formation named after the Daoist concept of the undivided universe</note> is like the state of <term>Primordial Chaos</term> <note>original: 混沌 - *Hundun*</note> before it was divided; it is blended into a single mass, swirling together without scattering. It is like the celestial phenomena within the <term>Sphere of the Heavens</term>, where the inner and outer positions of the stars are not yet distinct; it circles and revolves like a whirlwind, taking on the appearance of a long, coiled snake.

When it does disperse, it does so without falling into disorder, much as when the <term>Great Ultimate</term> <note>original: 太極 - *Taiji*; the supreme ultimate or the source of all existence</note> has already divided into its parts. The <term>Twenty-eight Lunar Mansions</term> <note>original: 二十八宿 - *Ershiba Xiu*; a system of constellations used in ancient Chinese astronomy to mark the moon's progress</note> each belong to their own specific quarter: East, West, South, and North. Represented by the <term>Dragon, Tiger, Bird, and Snake</term> <note>original: 龍虎鳥蛇 - *Long Hu Niao She*; refers to the Four Symbols or cardinal guardians: the Azure Dragon of the East, the White Tiger of the West, the Vermilion Bird of the South, and the Black Tortoise/Snake of the North</note>, the soldiers return to their respective units without a single error. They advance like wind and rain, and they retreat like a mountain being moved. This formation is well-suited for being stationed in mountains and is advantageous for fighting in dangerous or difficult terrain.

It is essential to practice this formation during times of leisure. Soldiers must recognize the sentry signals, be thoroughly familiar with the commands, watch the flags and banners, keep an eye on the signal fires, know when to advance or retreat, and understand the subtle art of dividing and merging. To encounter the enemy and achieve victory, one must employ a "single mind" <note>This implies total unity of purpose and concentration between the commander and the troops</note>.

In the midst of battle, one must look in all four directions. When encountering danger, deploy "extraordinary" <note>original: 奇 - *Qi*; a reference to Sun Tzu’s concept of "extraordinary" or unorthodox tactics</note> maneuvers. When facing steep heights, set ambushes. In the open wilderness, use "deception" to sow doubt in the enemy. In all matters of attacking, besieging, battling, or defending, the transformations of the **Primal Unity Formation** are sufficient to prepare one for any aspect of war.

# Treatise on Armament Technology, Volume 67: Military Formations 19

The **Three Talents Formation** is distributed into front, middle, and rear ranks to correspond with Heaven, Earth, and Man. Although the design of such formations is a legacy of the ancients, their variable application depends entirely on the commander.

Creating this formation is extremely convenient for establishing a camp on the fly; the distribution is well-proportioned. The front and rear are prepared for charges, while the center possesses two wings. If one unit engages in combat, the other two branches can "await the weary at ease" <note>original: 以逸待勞 — *yǐ yì dài láo*; a classic stratagem of resting one's troops while the enemy exhausts themselves through movement or attack.</note>. # Methods for Offensive Combat with the **Great Assault Formation**

Moving suddenly forward and then back, following the regulations of the drill, troops are deployed to the left and to the right. The inner and outer <term>sentry units</term> <note>original: 哨伍 (shaowu); small, mobile tactical squads used for both scouting and combat.</note> are arranged according to a <term>five-square pattern</term> <note>original: 五方形 (wufangxing); likely a grid-like arrangement of five units.</note> resembling the shape of the character for "Sun" <note>original: 日 (ri). Whether advancing or retreating in haste or at a measured pace, the troops remain fearless and without <gloss>slackening, moving like a line of fish; this constitutes the "unorthodox"</gloss> and <term>"orthodox"</term> <note>original: 奇正 (qizheng); a fundamental concept from Sun Tzu’s *Art of War* where "orthodox" (zheng) forces engage the enemy directly, while "unorthodox" (qi) forces deliver the decisive, unexpected blow.</note> troops of the Great Assault Formation.

Suppose the enemy comes to assault from both the front and the rear, or splits their wings to challenge us. In that case, our soldiers immediately retract and arrange themselves into a square camp to face the enemy on all four sides. If the enemy assaults from only one side, our formation immediately extends its two wings and transforms into the **Long Snake** <note>a linear, flexible formation discussed on the previous page</note> to seize victory.

If you suddenly encounter mountain forests or dangerous, narrow paths, it will certainly be difficult for the troops to advance in a synchronized line. In such cases, you must change the posture of the squads and units, sending scouts to explore the path as you proceed. Set <term>ambushes</term> <note>original: 設伏 (shefu)</note> along the road as you go; where you see a hazard, set an ambush, and where you encounter steep terrain, employ "unorthodox" tactics. If you estimate the enemy is <note>likely 弱 (weak)</note> and fragile, they can be captured; if you follow the natural flow of the terrain, victory can be taken. 

The training of elite troops in these formation maneuvers, and the ability to perceive a tactical opportunity in the space of a single breath—this is the complete essence of the **Great Assault Formation**.

# Strategy for Raiding the Enemy Nest

In the northern regions, water and grass are scarce during the colder months. Consequently, the **barbarians' horses** <note>original: 虜馬, luma. Referring to the mounts of the nomadic Mongol or Jurchen tribes.</note> are **weak and emaciated** <note>original: 羸弱, leiruo. A state of physical exhaustion common in steppe herds before the spring grass grows.</note> throughout the spring and winter. By taking advantage of their exhaustion and fatigue, our troops should **raid their camps**.

The plan for **eliminating the nest** <term>Eliminating the Nest</term> <note>original: 勦巢, jiaochao. A tactical term for a preemptive strike on a home base to destroy the enemy's ability to wage war.</note> should be executed during the second or third months of spring. At that time, we must accurately scout the number of tents to ensure our army can **attack with a ratio of three-to-one**.

### Logistics and Movement
*   Prepare **five days' worth of dry rations** <term>Dry Rations</term> <note>original: 餱糧, houliang. Dried or parched grains that could be eaten on the move without the need for cooking fires.</note>.
*   The operation should cover approximately a three-day round trip.
*   Strike when they least expect it, marching by both day and night.
*   The military orders must be strict and clear.

### Division of Duties
The army is divided into **three specific tasks**:
-> 1. One group to prepare for combat and defense <-
-> 2. One group to drive away the captured livestock <-
-> 3. One group to behead the occupants of the enemy tents <-
<note>Dividing the force ensured that while some soldiers focused on the objective—killing the enemy and seizing wealth—others remained vigilant against counter-attacks.</note>

### Execution and Withdrawal
One must wait for the first light of dawn, then **unleash the horses** <note>original: 撒馬, sama. To release the horses into a full gallop for a shock charge.</note> to charge and hack at the enemy tents. Once the objective is achieved, return with speed. **It is vital not to let greed for gain cause the troops to linger or scatter.**

After the raid, the **main army** should move forward to meet the vanguard. If the enemy pursues from behind, those soldiers who have already secured heads or captured livestock should proceed to the front to secure their prizes. The main body of the army will then act as a rearguard, **fighting while retreating**. This is the secret to achieving a total victory when raiding the nest.
