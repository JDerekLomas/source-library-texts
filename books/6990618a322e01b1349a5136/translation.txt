I answer that if anyone can show that the Gospel frees us from sin more than the Law freed our ancestors, then it might be so. But the Gospel does not free us [from the presence of sin], and therefore we are as likely to have such villains <note>original: "miscreants," which in this period referred to wicked people or "misbelievers."</note> among us as our ancestors did. For we are just as sinful as people were in the time of the Law. And Satan still remains among us. We are just as impatient, profane, and lacking in conscience as ever—having uncontrolled passions, violent emotions, a tendency toward bad company, and vain curiosities, and lacking respect for religion. Through these opportunities, the Devil takes advantage and works to have his way; for he goes where he is either certain or hopeful of a welcome (*Matthew 12:44*). 

He therefore watches for the time when he may best offer his service to those in whom he finds even the slightest preparation—such as when someone falls into a passionate sorrow accompanied by loneliness due to a loss, such as a husband, wife, child, or the like. The Devil offers himself to "comfort" such people in their sorrowful, melancholy state. Similarly, in a time of famine <note>original: "dearth," meaning a period of extreme scarcity or lack of food.</note>, extreme poverty often causes many to become desperately impatient. Or, they are so impatient because of poverty that they insist on being rich, even against God’s providence <note>God's divine guidance and care for the world.</note>, falling into such an uncontrolled passion that they want their needs met and desires fulfilled by any means possible—right or wrong, as they say. 

This also happens when someone is enraged with anger, plotting revenge, or is closely acquainted with those who are witches. Likewise, it occurs when anyone is addicted to reading and studying dangerous books that entice them to practice hidden mysteries, magic, and enchantments. Thus, through these and similar means (as can be gathered from the confessions of witches), they prepare themselves for Satan's temptations to draw them into witchcraft. I could cite innumerable examples of this, as you shall read later in more detail when I discuss the types of people most addicted to witchcraft. Therefore, do you not realize that such people still remain among us? Yes, certainly they do—and witches as well.
