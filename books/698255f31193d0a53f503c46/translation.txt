# Of the Life and Conduct of JACOB BÖHME,
->centered text<- named *Teutonicus* <note>Latin: "The German." Böhme was known as the *Philosophus Teutonicus* because he wrote his profound mystical philosophy in his native German rather than Latin.</note> by the scholars hereafter.

Jacob Böhme was born in the year 1575 in Old Seidenburg, approximately two miles from Görlitz—a city located in <term>Upper Lusatia</term> <note>A historical region in what is now eastern Germany and western Poland.</note> that is highly renowned among learned men. He was fathered by Jacob and born to his mother Ursula, both of whom were poor peasant folk. In his youth, he herded cattle and was finally, upon the advice of his family, sent to school. There, alongside the "fear of God," he grasped the basics of reading and writing. Afterward, he was apprenticed to the shoemaking trade; in which, once he became a master in the year 1594, he also entered into marriage with the maiden Catharina, the legitimate daughter of Johann Kunschman, a butcher in Görlitz. He lived well and peacefully with her for 30 years and fathered four sons, whom he saw taught various trades.

This man, having been devoted to the "fear of God" <note>A phrase common in the 17th century describing a life of deep piety and reverence.</note> from his youth and having very gladly attended sermons, was finally awakened through the saying and promise of our Savior in **Luke 11, verse 13**: *The Father in heaven will give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him for it.* At the same time, he was moved by the diversity and conflict of religions—into which he could not fit himself—to pray, seek, and knock fervently and incessantly in simplicity of spirit in order to recognize the Truth. By this means, according to the Divine pull and will, he was translated in spirit into the **Holy SABBATH**; where, according to his own confession, he remained for seven whole days in the highest state of joy. ### Explanation of the Life and Conduct of J. B.

...he was driven by Divine zeal, such that he very vehemently rebuked shameful and blasphemous speech, and earnestly turned away from all dishonorable behavior out of a love for virtue. Because this way of life and his actions were so contrary to the ways of the world, he became only an object of their mockery and laughter. Meanwhile, he supported himself by the labor of his hands in the sweat of his brow, until, at the beginning of the sixth century <note>original: *VI. Seculi* (Latin). This refers to a millenarian or chronological period in Böhme’s mystical timeline.</note>—namely in the **Year 1600**—he was for a second time seized by Divine Light. Through a sudden glimpse of a pewter vessel, he was introduced into the innermost ground or <term>center</term> <note>original: *Centro* (Latin). In Böhme's philosophy, the "center" is the essential point from which all nature and spirit originate.</note> of secret nature. At this, because he did not trust his vision enough, he went out into the open fields and saw the wonder-works of the Creator revealed quite purely and clearly in the <term>signatures</term> <note>The "Doctrine of Signatures" is the belief that the outward appearance of things in nature reveals their hidden spiritual essence or "inner soul."</note> of all created things. He was overwhelmed with great joy at this, but remained silent, praised God, and for the time being let this be enough for him.

But according to God’s holy counsel, which conducts His work in secret, ten years later—namely in the **Year 1610**—he was touched and renewed by God for a third time through the overshadowing of the Holy Spirit. Consequently, because he had been so enlightened, and so that he would not let such great grace bestowed upon him slip from his memory, nor resist his God, he wrote at home for himself. He did this with few resources and was provided with no books at all, except for the Holy Scripture.

**Anno 1612.** He wrote his first book, *AURORA*, which means the <term>Day-Spring</term> <note>original: *AURORAM*, a Latin title meaning the dawn or the rising of the sun.</note>. When the author was accused because of it, this book was taken into custody at the town hall by the magistrate of Görlitz. They gave him an order that he, as an <term>idiot</term> <note>In the 17th century, "idiot" (from the Latin *idiota*) was not an insult to intelligence, but a technical term for a "layman"—someone without a formal university education or clerical rank.</note>, should henceforth abstain from such book-writing, as it did not befit his station. Following this, he paused for seven years; but afterward, when he was as if awakened again by the promptings of the Holy Spirit of God, and also urged and requested by several God-fearing people, he took up the pen again, continued writing, and produced his works with good leisure and deliberation.
