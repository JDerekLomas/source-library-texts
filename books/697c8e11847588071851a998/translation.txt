Weishaupt, acting as a lawyer <term>jurist (Jurist): a scholar or professional with deep knowledge of the law and legal theory</term> and advocate for his own cause, begins by appealing to his consistently good reputation <note>original: "Leumund"; a person’s social standing or moral character as perceived by the public.</note> (p. 23). He then seeks to prove that his intentions in this matter could not have been evil: because he sought neither comfort and rest, nor lust and sensual pleasure, nor fame and honor, nor power—except only for the "good cause," for the sake of truth and virtue (from p. 26 onward). He only sought to elevate the power of reason and virtue (p. 35).
