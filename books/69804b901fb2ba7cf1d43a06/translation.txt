# Preface

...the **history of heretics** has been presented in all its particulars, even the smallest details; I do not believe the accounts of religious errors and heresies in those large volumes appear at all the same as they were initially drafted in this small book.

I felt it necessary to mention this here as a reminder, so that no one assumes they will find in the present work merely a brief extract of what is more extensively detailed in those two <term>folio volumes</term> <note>Large, prestigious books printed on full-sized sheets of paper; the author refers here to his massive two-volume *Impartial History of the Church and Heretics*.</note>. Rather, this book is primarily composed for those who desire a general and summary understanding of all church affairs, especially for students who love such knowledge, and most of all for the youth. The history of heretics, however, encompasses countless other subjects and assumes <note>original Latin: "præsupponiret"</note> that the reader already possesses some chronological understanding <note>original Latin: "connexion"</note> of historical eras and events in their proper sequence.

Moreover, one finds here a sufficient, thorough, and clear introduction to the history of the Old Testament, which is not to be found elsewhere. Thus, one can easily see once more that both books belong almost inseparably together, and that each may suitably compensate for the other’s omissions.

# Preface

And this is precisely why I have not wished to undertake any expansion or continuation of the present *edition*. A discerning reader can find more than enough information in the large book; however, providing a *continuation* up until the end of this *century* <note>original Latin: "seculi"</note> would give me pause for the reasons I cited in the third part of the **History of Heretics** <note>A reference to his massive work, the *Impartial History of the Church and Heretics* (1699), which revolutionized the field by treating "heretics" with sympathy.</note>.

My wish in this regard is that God would awaken many sincere souls and make them capable of investigating these necessary and important matters in a truly thorough and <term>impartial</term> <note>The concept of being "impartial" (*unparteyiſch*) was the author's core philosophy; it meant looking for Christian truth outside of official church dogmas and being fair to those labeled as "heretics."</note> manner, and also to uncover even the most recent **church history** wisely and without a misguided love for the parties and sects of this world. Not only will I rejoice at this, but I will also show myself indebted to anyone who sincerely and modestly points out the errors I have made in such a *study* <note>original Latin: "studio"</note> and corrects them for the common good. For such souls, and no others, are fit to perform this kind of work, which will hopefully prove all the more important in *practice* <note>original Latin: "praxi"</note> in the future, given how poorly and perversely it has been handled until now by all the religious parties.

> May God increase the power of His light in
> and among us all, to His great glory!

# Preface

...the **history of heretics** was laid out before one's eyes in all, even the most minute circumstances; I need not even mention how the accounts of **religious errors** and **heresies** in those volumes have taken on an entirely different appearance throughout than what was initially drafted in this small book.

This seemed necessary for me to mention here, so that no one might think that he would find in the present writing a mere brief extract of what was only more extensively detailed in those two <term>folio volumes</term> <note>original: "Folianten". Folios were the largest book format of the time, printed on large sheets of paper folded only once, signifying a massive and expensive scholarly work.</note>.

Rather, this book is primarily composed for those who desire a general and summary understanding of all **church affairs**, and especially for students who are enthusiasts of such knowledge, but most of all for the youth. The **history of heretics**, however, extends across countless other subjects and <term>presupposes</term> <note>original Latin-derived: "præsupponiret"</note>, as it were, that the reader already possesses some **chronological connection** <note>original: "chronologische connexion", meaning a basic understanding of how historical events and time periods link together.</note> of periods and stories in their proper order.

Furthermore, one finds here a sufficient, thorough, and clear introduction to the history of the **Old Testament**, which is not to be found elsewhere. For this reason, one can again easily see that both books belong together almost inseparably, and that one can fittingly compensate for the shortcomings of the other.
