# Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms
<note>original: 海國圖志 (Hǎiguó Túzhì)</note>

**Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms** <note>original: 海國圖志</note>, written by the scholar **Wei Yuan** during the **Qing Dynasty**. This work focuses on **maritime geography** and is presented here in the format of a traditional **thread-bound book**.

The front cover of the volume features a calligraphic title slip <note>original: 題簽 (tiqian)</note>—a strip of paper pasted onto the dark blue cover—which is a classic hallmark of Qing Dynasty bookbinding. This work is historically significant as the most comprehensive study of Western geography and military technology available in China at the time, written in response to the First Opium War to help the empire understand and defend against foreign powers.

The Romans <note>original: 丹 (Dān)</note> attacked and seized the land of Portugal; the inhabitants sued for peace, but soon the Romans raised troops again, destroyed the Alans <note>original: 阿丹 (Ādān)</note>, and retook the territory.

In the year 1200 <gloss>the second year of the Qingyuan era of Emperor Ningzong of the Song Dynasty</gloss>, the Castile <note>original: 加色代爾 (Jiāsèdài'ěr)</note> tribe of Spain gave a daughter in marriage to Henry of Burgundy <note>original: 佛蘭西之律與厘 (Fólánxī zhī Lǜ-yǔ-lí)</note> <gloss>The text notes that "Lǜ" is a title of office and "Lí" is the personal name, referring to Count Henri</gloss>. As part of the dowry, several districts in the northern corner of the land of Portugal were gifted.

Thereupon, Henry <note>original: 興厘 (Xīnglí)</note> moved his residence to these northern districts, and his power grew stronger day by day. He raised an army once more to reclaim the southern territories, including Lisbon <note>original: 義斯門 (Yìsīmén)</note> and Santarém <note>original: 西彌里 (Xīmǐlǐ)</note>. He subsequently established his capital at Lisbon, founded a state, and declared himself King.

After the year 1500 <gloss>during the Zhengde era of Emperor Wuzong of the Ming Dynasty</gloss>, Portugal dispatched naval fleets to the east to seize coastal regions and to the west to take the land of Brazil <note>original: 摩那濟爾 (Mónàjì'ěr)</note> in the continent of South America.

More than a hundred years later, King Sebastian <note>original: 塞麻斯田 (Sāimáshīsìtián)</note> went to lead an invasion <note>This refers to the disastrous Battle of Alcácer Quibir in North Africa</note>. His military ranks fell into disorder, and through his own tactical errors, his troops were cut off and the army was utterly defeated. The King died, and his various successors <note>literally "sons," though the historical succession was complex</note> fought to take the throne. They begged for assistance from King Philip <note>original: 菲里 (Fēilǐ)</note> of Spain <note>referred to as Great Luzon</note>, who eventually settled the royal succession. From that time on, the affairs of the nation were subject to the control of Spain.

# Geography and Culture of Portugal

The **Tagus River** <note>original: 辣日哥 (Làrìgē); likely referring to the Tagus or the Lisbon region</note> and the prominent European city of **Coimbra** <note>original: 高士多 (Gāoshìduō)</note> are located here. Among their people was a member of the **Society of Jesus** <term>Jesuit Society: a Catholic religious order known for its scholars and missionaries who often served in the Chinese imperial court</term> named **Sousa** <note>original: 蘇氏 (Sū shì); likely referring to the historian Manuel de Faria e Sousa</note> who wrote the *Geography of Portugal* <note>original: 陟祿日亞書 (Zhìlùrìyà Shū); a transliteration of "Lusitania," the ancient name for Portugal</note>, which is exceptionally precise.

Their local products include fruits, silk, and cotton. They have an abundance of aquatic life and are highly skilled at brewing **grape wine**, which remains unspoiled even after the long sea voyage to China. Their estates and gardens can span dozens of *li* <note>a Chinese mile, approximately 500 meters</note> in circumference, teeming with birds and beasts; famous kings from foreign lands often travel to these grounds to engage in hunting.

### Social Customs and Charity

It is their custom to maintain **Benevolent Societies** <note>original: 仁會 (Rénhuì); referring to the *Santa Casa da Misericórdia*, a Portuguese charity foundation</note> to provide relief for orphans, widows, and the destitute. When merchant ships arrive, if a traveler dies without an heir, the society collects their luggage, tracks down their relatives, and returns the belongings to them. The King dispatches officials to every district to manage the estates of orphaned children; once the children reach adulthood, the officials return all the property to them, often with added value.

### Diplomatic History with the Qing Court

Prior to the **Ming Dynasty**, there was no formal communication with China. In the 5th year of the **Yongzheng** reign <note>1727</note>, they presented a memorial and local products as tribute. In the 18th year of the **Qianlong** reign <note>1753</note>, they sent a tribute mission again.

The Emperor ordered the Director of the **Imperial Astronomical Bureau**, **Liu Songling** <note>original: 劉松齡; the Chinese name of the Jesuit missionary Ferdinand Augustin Hallerstein</note>, to lead the delegation on their journey to the capital <note>Beijing</note>.
