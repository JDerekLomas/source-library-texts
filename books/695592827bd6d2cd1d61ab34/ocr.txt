<meta>Engraved frontispiece by Pierre Miotte (Burgundus) for Athanasius Kircher's "Ars Magna Lucis et Umbrae", published in Rome by Hermann Scheus, 1646.</meta>

<image-desc>
A complex allegorical engraving. At the top center, the Hebrew Tetragrammaton (יהוה) radiates light from within a break in the clouds. This light forms a large triangle that dominates the composition. 

On the left side of the triangle, a radiant male figure representing the Sun (Apollo/Sol) sits atop a double-headed eagle; he holds a caduceus and is surrounded by astrological symbols. On the right side, a dark-skinned female figure representing the Moon (Diana/Luna) is adorned with stars and a crescent moon, sitting upon two peacocks and holding a reflective shield.

The four corners represent the four ways of knowing:
- Top Left: **AVCTORITAS SACRA** (Sacred Authority), depicted with an open Bible and cherubim.
- Top Right: **RATIO** (Reason), depicted with a divine eye in a triangle, a hand writing on a tablet, and an owl (wisdom).
- Bottom Left: **AVCTORITAS PROFANA** (Profane Authority), showing an open book near a landscape featuring a large villa with formal gardens and an elaborate sundial.
- Bottom Right: **SENSVS** (Sense), showing a hand pointing to a mirror reflecting a solar image into a dark cave, representing the camera obscura and Plato's Allegory of the Cave.

In the center of the triangle is a large circular medallion containing the main title and dedication. Below this circle, at the bottom center, is an oval portrait of Archduke Ferdinand.
</image-desc>
