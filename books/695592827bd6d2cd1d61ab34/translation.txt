# THE HARMONY OF THE TEN-STRINGED INSTRUMENT,
## Or the Concept of the *Work in Ten Parts*.

I. The Physiology of Light, the Wisdom of Shadows, and the Art of Color. <note>original: "Physiologia Lucis, Sciasophia, Ars Chromatica." Kircher uses "Physiology" in the classical sense of "the study of nature."</note>
II. The Radiation of Nature; the Wisdom of Echoes, and Optics. <note>original: "Actinobolismus Naturæ; Echosophia." Actinobolismus refers to the emission of rays.</note>
III. Apparatus for Curious Gnomonics, and the Doctrine of Conic Sections. <term>Gnomonics: The art of measuring time using a sundial or gnomon.</term>
IV. Various Horography, to be established on given planes. <term>Horography: The art of constructing instruments for telling time, such as clocks or dials.</term>
V. The Drawing of Astrolabes, and Gnomonic Geography.
VI. The Sciatheric Proteus, and Physico-Astrological Gnomonics. <note>original: "Proteus Sciathericus." Named after the shape-shifting god Proteus, this refers to the "ever-changing" nature of shadow-casting instruments.</note>
VII. The Reflective Art, or Astronomy by Reflection. <note>original: "Ars Anacamptica." This refers to catoptrics, or the study of mirrors.</note>
VIII. The Refractive Art, or Astronomy by Refraction. <note>original: "Ars Anaclastica." This refers to dioptrics, or the study of light passing through lenses.</note>
IX. Gnomonic Cosmometry, that is, the measurement of the world through light and shadow.
X. The Magic of Light and Shadow: Horographic, Parastatic, and Catoptric. <note>Parastatic refers to the representation or "standing forth" of images, often through optical illusions.</note>

->Epilogue, *or the Metaphysics of Light and Shadow*.<-

> *Upon a ten-stringed harp I will sing praises to thee. Psalm 143, verse 9.* <note>original: "In decachordo psalterio psallam tibi." This biblical reference justifies the ten-part structure of the book as a form of divine praise.</note>
