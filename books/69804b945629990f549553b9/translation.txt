# To the Christian Reader.

Favorable Christian Reader, we give you herewith the following well-grounded open letter by Mr. Caspar Schwenckfeld—as stated in the title above—to read and to consider with an impartial mind and judgment. We are also of good confidence that you will not regret the effort, but rather that in this difficult and manifold division concerning the <term>Sacrament of the Lord’s Supper</term>, it will serve you for the very best. 

Beyond his books already released in print, the mentioned man has written many more heartfelt and comforting open letters to various places at the request and desire of good lords, friends, and God-fearing people, in order to serve them toward the knowledge of the truth. In these, he not only testifies of the Lord Christ and His divine glory, but also explains several difficult passages of the Holy Scriptures quite clearly and understandably. 

May the kind and merciful God grant that such work—and whatever serves toward the saving knowledge of Jesus Christ, toward Christian love, peace, and godliness—may come forth through the press more and more. We would hope that many errors, which have now shown themselves again, might thereby be remedied, and that many troubled consciences might be served and helped in Christ. 

Therefore, we will pray and entreat that the Lord Jesus might bring His light and His pure divine truth to light ever more brightly for the driving away of darkness and for the salvation of our souls. May He also give the good-hearted a Christian judgment to find the right <term>middle way</term> in Christ between the <term>Papacy</term> and <term>Lutheranism</term>, <note>Schwenckfeld sought a third path that avoided both Roman Catholic transubstantiation and the literalism of the Lutheran view, while also distancing himself from what he saw as the "empty" symbolism of the radical reformers.</note> and that they take not the chaff for the kernel, nor the scum for the silver. Rather, in divine matters, according to the teaching of St. Paul, they should test everything well and keep that which is good, so that they also learn more and more to recognize our Lord Jesus Christ <term>according to the Spirit</term>. <note>"According to the Spirit" (nach dem Geist) is a key Schwenckfeldian phrase emphasizing the internal, spiritual experience of Christ over external rituals or literalist interpretations.</note> May God grant it, Amen.
