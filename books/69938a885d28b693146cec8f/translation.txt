# FROM THE SUCCESSOR ON THE [FIRST] ALCIBIADES

Of the Platonic dialogues and of the entire philosophical contemplation derived from them, we consider the most sovereign and stable starting point to be the recognition of our own essence. <note>original: "τὴν τῆς ἑαυτῶν οὐσίας διάγνωσιν" (tēn tēs heautōn ousias diagnōsin). This refers to the Delphic maxim "Know Thyself."</note> For once this is correctly established, we shall certainly be able to learn more accurately both the good that belongs to us and the evil that opposes it. For it is the nature of each being that, just as its existence is different, so too its perfection differs—to some one kind and to others another—according to its underlying reality. <term>hypostasis: the fundamental substance or "standing under" that gives a thing its individual existence.</term>

For whether existence and the good both proceed from the same cause and source, as Aristotle says, <note>Aristotle often explored the relationship between being (on) and the good (agathon) in his *Metaphysics*.</note> it must surely be that the measures of essence and the intellectual character of each perfection are presupposed; or whether the good comes from another cause, more ancient and more divine, while essence and existence come to things from another source, each thing participates in being either more dimly or more clearly, according to how it has received its share: those beings of the first rank share in a greater and more perfect degree; those stationed in the middle share in a secondary degree according to their own rank; and the lowest of beings share in the lowest degree.

And how indeed could it be otherwise for beings, with gods and providence leading them, unless they remained within the Good according to their worth? For if Intellect <term>Nous: the divine Mind or intuitive reason that governs the cosmos.</term> guides beings as though they were incomplete and provides to each the appropriate measure of existence, would then the Good—which is itself even more ancient than Intellect—make its own distributions to beings and its outpourings of goodness in a disordered way? Not where there is a need for differentiation according to essence.

Instead, they lead the dialogue back to its **syllogistic** <note>original: "συλλογιστικόν" (syllogistikon). A syllogism is a logical argument where a conclusion is reached from two premises.</note> and demonstrative character, especially when they deem it right to handle the art of dialectic. And indeed, they enumerate the arguments: 

First, one **syllogism** which demonstrates that **Alcibiades** does not know what is just. 

Second, the one showing that the "many" <note>The "many" (hoi polloi) refers to the general public or uneducated masses who Socrates argues cannot teach virtue.</note> are not serious teachers of justice. 

Third, again, the one concluding that it is the respondent, and not the questioner, who is the author of the conclusion in cases where questioning and answering occur. 

Fourth, the one where it is again shown that both speaking and acting are the work of the same **science** <note>original: "ἐπιστήμης" (epistēmēs), meaning certain, organized knowledge or expertise.</note>. 

Fifth, the one reasoning that the just is, after all, the advantageous. 

Sixth, again, one intertwined with this, which concludes that only the beautiful is good. 

Seventh, the one establishing that according to **double ignorance** <note>original: "διπλῆν ἀμαθίαν" (diplēn amathian). A key Platonic concept where a person does not know, but mistakenly believes that they do know.</note>, **Alcibiades** is ignorant of himself and of affairs. 

Eighth, the one refuting him by showing he has not even considered who his true rivals are. 

Ninth, indeed, which shows him to be completely ignorant of the manner of correct science. 

Tenth, in addition to these, the argument through which our **essence** <term>Essence (Ousia): The fundamental "whatness" or being of a thing. In this Neoplatonic context, it refers to the soul's true nature as distinct from the body.</term> is **purified** <note>original: "ἀνακαθαίρεται" (anakathairetai). This refers to the process of "clearing away" false beliefs and bodily attachments to see the soul clearly.</note>, along with the three ways of identifying it and the character of the care fitting for it. 

Therefore, we shall not only praise those who say these things...
