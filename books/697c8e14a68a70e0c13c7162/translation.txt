...knew how to be modest in things that are, by their nature, inaccessible to human beings; who recognized everything in a light of his own and could—even with that which cannot be conceived or measured by man—make it almost more endearing through his order and perspective, and could refine it for human memory—at least for his own time. Who finally sought God with focused energies: such a man can bear witness to what is within and for humanity. And his testimony, as that of such a man, deserves in this case or that to be heard as a voice. (*)

The cultivation of his being cost him much labor and effort; in his strength, he possessed too much loyalty and <term>humility</term> <note>original: *Demuth*</note> to be mistreated by critical <term>omnipotence</term> <note>original: *Omnipotenz*; here referring to an arrogant, all-knowing critical spirit</note>, or by self-satisfied comfort, or by a frivolity that plays with the wind...

---

(*) To avoid annoying the reader with respect to P. <note>Blaise Pascal</note> or myself, I remark that I do not say this out of *submission* <note>original: *Submission*; meaning subservience or blind obedience</note>; rather, I write everything that might touch upon his praise not *very humbly* <note>original French: *treshumblement*</note>, but as one who bears witness.
