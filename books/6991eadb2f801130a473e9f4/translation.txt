# PREFACE

The manuscripts of Leonardo da Vinci, which have remained for so long in the shadows of the <term>Royal Library of Windsor</term> <note>The primary residence of the British Monarchy, housing a vast collection of Leonardo's drawings acquired by King Charles II.</note>, constitute the most formidable monument ever raised to the glory of human genius. Among these treasures, the studies relating to the **Canals** and the management of waters hold a place of the first order.

It is not merely a matter of simple sketches; these are the profound meditations of a master who was simultaneously a painter, an architect, and a preeminent engineer. <margin>Leonardo as "Master of Waters"</margin> Through these pages, we see the birth of the modern science of <term>Hydraulics</term> <note>The branch of science and technology concerned with the mechanical properties of liquids.</note>.

<column-break/>

Leonardo sought to tame the flow of the Arno and to connect the seas. <insert>He envisioned the irrigation of the Lombardy plains.</insert> Every stroke of the pen, every note written in his famous *mirror writing* <note>Leonardo wrote from right to left, a technique that requires a mirror to read easily.</note>, reveals a constant concern for utility joined with beauty.

As the <gloss>Great Master</gloss> himself wrote:
"Water is the driving force of all nature." <note>original: "L'acqua è il vetturale della natura." This famous aphorism reflects Leonardo's belief that water is the vital fluid of the Earth, similar to blood in the human body.</note>

We offer these reproductions to the public with the respect due to such a legacy, ensuring that the technical precision of the originals is preserved for the benefit of modern science and art history alike.
