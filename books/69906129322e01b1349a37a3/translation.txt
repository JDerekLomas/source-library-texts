# MARSILIO FICINO

->The Florentine humbly commends himself<-
->to the Most Reverend Father in Christ,<-
->Lord Giovanni de' Medici, Cardinal<-
->of the Holy Roman Church.<-

---

->*<-

When I had determined to write a letter to you to offer my congratulations on the Cardinal’s dignity recently bestowed upon you, and as I was considering to whom I might best entrust it to be delivered—someone who might make our congratulation significantly more pleasing—I suddenly betook myself to the Academy <note>The Platonic Academy in Florence, revived by the Medici and led by Ficino</note>, hoping that at least within its inner chambers some greeter most acceptable to you would not be lacking. There, by a certain divine fate, the first to meet me among the Academics was Iamblichus, surnamed "the Divine," a great priest in that place. To him indeed I said: "Greetings, Iamblichus, great priest. At present (as you see) I am about to congratulate Giovanni de' Medici, the new prelate of the Christian religion. I pray you, if my request is just, that you will make our congratulation—otherwise a meager thing—more full by means of your divine words and mysteries." He assented to my prayers, and so, with this sentiment, that great priest comes to you, Giovanni, great priest. Receive, therefore, such a great greeter with joy; and after the first greeting, after the letter has been read, listen more attentively, if you please, to what that divine man says. For he has promised that, in order to speak things equally worthy of his persona and yours, he will express to you in brief what the Egyptian and Assyrian priests <note>Ficino refers here to the "Ancient Theology" (Prisca Theologia), a lineage of wisdom he believed flowed from the East to Plato</note> thought concerning religion and divine matters.

<summary>Marsilio Ficino dedicates his translation of Iamblichus to the young Cardinal Giovanni de' Medici (the future Pope Leo X), framing the ancient philosopher as a "divine priest" greeting a modern Christian prelate. He introduces the work as a summary of Egyptian and Assyrian wisdom regarding divine mysteries.</summary>
