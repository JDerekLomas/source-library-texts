# THE ESSENCE OF TAJIKA

Having bowed to the pair of lotus feet of Lord Rama, then to the Goddess of Speech <note>Saraswati</note>, to Ganesha, to the group of planets beginning with the Sun, to Shiva, and to the son of Yashoda <note>Krishna</note>, I shall speak the **Essence of Tajika** <term>Tajikasara: A treatise on annual horoscopy</term>. It is concise, easy to understand, beautiful, and provides clear knowledge; it was composed after examining various **Tajika** <term>Tajika: A system of Sanskrit astrology influenced by Persian and Arabic traditions</term> texts to bring joy to the astrologers. || 1 ||

Salutations to the holy Ganesha. Having prostrated to the planets—the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn—who are worthy of worship; and having placed in my heart my Guru, who is like the Sun in awakening the golden lotus of understanding within the mind; || 1 || and having placed in my heart the Goddess Sharada <note>Saraswati</note>, who is as bright as the autumnal moon and is the sole lamp for the destruction of the darkness of ignorance, I now explain the easy meanings of the systems within the **Essence of Tajika**. || 2 || Having first composed a commentary on the **Great Garland of Festivals** <term>Brihat-Parvamala: Likely a previous work by the author regarding the calendar</term>, I am now prepared to create these verses for the instruction of my students. || 3 ||

In this world, "the wise, when embarking upon a desired task, first perform a salutation to their chosen deity." Thus, to follow the tradition of the wise, and because of the saying: "Many obstacles arise in the path of the auspicious, even for the great; but for those engaged in the inauspicious, the obstacles disappear," the author of this book, at the commencement of this science, performs a **Benediction** <term>Mangalacarana: An auspicious opening prayer to ensure the successful completion of a task</term> by bowing to his chosen deity and states the subject matter.

Regarding the phrase "Of Sri Rama": The connection is that I, an **Astrologer** <term>Ganaka: A calculator or astronomer-astrologer</term> named **Haribhadra**, shall speak the Essence of Tajika. The name for books concerning the results of the solar return year is **Tajika**.

->The terminology of the ancient teachers.<-

It is said in the *Tajika Nibandha* <note>A foundational compendium on Tajika astrology</note>: This knowledge was told by Brahma to the Sun; by the Sun, it was told to the Greek <note>original: "Yavana"; refers to the Ionian/Greek transmission of astronomical knowledge to India</note>; and by the Greek, that knowledge was revealed to the Tajika <note>original: "Tajikasya"; refers to the Perso-Arabic astrological tradition</note>. Therefore, this is the essential pillar of the Tajika scriptures.

What has been done here? Having bowed with mind, speech, and body—making oneself humble—to the pair of lotus feet of Lord Rama; to Vagishvari <gloss>the Goddess of Speech, Bharati</gloss>; then to Heramba <gloss>Ganesha</gloss>; to the Sun and the other <gloss>group of planets</gloss>; to Rudra <gloss>the Lord Ishvara</gloss>; and to the son of Yashoda, Shri Krishna.

What are the qualities of this *Tajika-Sara* <note>The "Essence of Tajika"</note>? It is "brief and accessible," meaning it is small in word count but easy to understand in meaning. Furthermore, it is "pleasing," meaning it is charming due to the beauty of its verse, and thus it provides clear understanding. Furthermore, it was "composed after observing various Tajika texts," meaning it was created after reviewing many different scriptures of this tradition. <insert>The author uses the suffix "tam" here as a universal grammatical marker.</insert> Furthermore, it is "a giver of joy to the astrologers," meaning it causes great happiness to those who calculate the stars.

One might ask: in the scriptures, the praise of three types of deities is possible—the "appropriate" deity, the "beloved" deity, or the "appropriately-beloved" deity. 
1. Praise of an **appropriate** deity is like praising a King at the start of a treatise on Ethics, or the God of Love at the start of a treatise on Erotics. 
2. Praise of a **beloved** deity is like the praise of Shiva and Gauri at the start of the *Raghuvamsha* epic. 
3. Praise of an **appropriately-beloved** deity is like the praise of Mahavira, the Lord of Yoga, at the start of a treatise on Yoga. 

In this text, by using the words "Shri Rama" and so on, the author performs praise for his **beloved** deity. By mentioning "the Sun and others," the author performs praise for the **appropriate** deities <note>appropriate because the Sun is the primary deity of astrology</note>. || 1 ||

**Whatever destiny—whether auspicious or inauspicious—was earned in previous lives and produced by one's own body, all of that surely reaches its fruition at a fixed time, such as a specific year. This is agreed upon by all the wise scholars regarding a living being's current life; therefore, after reflecting on that, I shall speak of the brilliant results produced by the solar return year.** || 2 ||

Now, the author states the purpose of the book—*That which was previously earned*—because of the eternal connection between the relative pronouns "that" and "which." For this reason, whatever was earned in a previous life, produced by the self and the body <note>original: "ātmadehajanitam"</note>, whether good or bad, is called Fate <term>Daiva: the accumulated fruit of past actions</term>. In this life, in this present existence, when the determination of the year, month, and day occurs, a living being surely attains the ultimate ripening <note>original: "vipāka"</note> or result; this is the meaning. Therefore, having reflected and deliberated upon this, I shall speak of the results produced by the year. What kind of results? Those agreed upon by all the wise scholars <note>original: "sūribhiḥ"</note>, and furthermore, those which are clear, manifest, and inspiring.

Now he explains the method for calculating the "heap of days" <term>Ahargana: the total number of days elapsed from a fixed epoch to a specific date</term> and other factors—*The number of years elapsed*—The group of years that have passed since the time of birth should be set down in four places. When the birth era <note>The Shaka era year of birth</note> is subtracted from the desired current era year, the remainder is the elapsed years. As it is said: "Having ascertained the birth era, subtract it from the desired era; the remainder found there shall be the group of elapsed years." || 1 ||

Then, in sequence, the elapsed years are multiplied by 375, 15, 31, and 30. Below these, one should place sixty, and above, they should be joined with the results of the birth day-count and the minutes <note>original: "nāḍikā"</note>—that is, the day-count at the time of birth and the time elapsed from sunrise. This becomes the elapsed collection of days for the start of the desired year. What kind? Exactly as it was calculated from whichever astronomical manual <note>original: "granthataḥ"</note> was used on the day of birth; that is the meaning. This is what is being expressed: the calculation must remain consistent with the original manual used at the time of birth.

### Now, the calculation of the desired year from the total heap of days (Ahargana)

The total count of days <note>original: "dyugana" or "ahargana," the number of days elapsed since a fixed epoch</note> multiplied by **eleven** (11) <note>represented by the word "Rudra," the eleven forms of Shiva</note> and divided by **six hundred and ninety-two** (692) <note>represented by the phrase "days, teeth, and flavors"</note> gives a quotient. This quotient is added to the original day-count. The result is then divided by **thirty** (30) <note>represented by "void and qualities"</note>; the resulting quotient is the total number of months, and the remainder is the lunar day <term>Tithi</term>.

Then, that total number of months is increased by **fifteen** (15) and divided by **five hundred and three** (503) <note>represented by "qualities, senses, and arrows"</note>. The quotient obtained, plus one, represents the **intercalary months** <term>Adhimasa</term> <note>extra months added to the lunar calendar to keep it in sync with the solar year</note>. These intercalary months must be subtracted from the total heap of months.

The remaining months are divided by **twelve** (12) <note>represented by "Suryas," the twelve solar deities</note> to find the elapsed years. These years are added to the **Epoch Era** <term>Shaka</term> <note>the reference year from which the calculation began</note>. The remainder from the division by twelve indicates the number of elapsed months starting from Chaitra <note>the first month of the Hindu year</note>. In this way, the time of the year is described by the wise. From this day-count, the positions of the planets and the houses should be calculated accurately, and their strengths should be determined as if through direct observation. || 5-6 ||

Now, the explanation of the calculation of the desired time from the day-count: "The day-count multiplied by Rudra..."—The previously calculated day-count, including its fractions, is multiplied by **eleven** (11) and divided by **six hundred and ninety-two** (692). The day-count is increased by this quotient and then divided by **thirty** (30). The quotient is the total months, and the remainder is the lunar day. Then, that month-count is increased by **fifteen** (15) and divided by **five hundred and three** (503). The result, plus one, is the number of **intercalary months**. These <term>Adhimasa</term> are to be subtracted from the total heap of months. Then, dividing by twelve gives the elapsed years. These elapsed years are added to the **Epoch Era** <term>Shaka</term> from which the day-count was originally calculated; this becomes the **Desired Era** <term>Ishta-shaka</term>. The remainder from the division by twelve should be known as the elapsed months, such as Chaitra and so on. Thus, the time of the year is described by the wise. The rest is clear.

# Now, the Calculation of the Lunar Day (Tithi)
The number of elapsed years multiplied by 343 <note>original: "gunavedarama," a numerical code where guna=3, veda=4, rama=3</note> and divided by 31 <note>original: "kurama," where ku=1, rama=3</note> gives the days and fractions. This, combined with the days elapsed since the start of the month at birth and divided by 30 <note>original: "kharama," where kha=0, rama=3</note>, yields the remaining lunar days for the current year. || 11 ||

Regarding the calculation of the lunar day: "The number of elapsed years..."—For example, the elapsed years 59 multiplied by 343 equals 20,237. Divided by 31, the result is 652;48. Adding the 5 days and 35 ghatis <note>A ghati is a unit of time equal to 24 minutes</note> elapsed since the first day of the bright fortnight at birth, we get 658;23. Dividing this by 30, the remainder is 28 lunar days in the bright fortnight. In this calculation of the lunar day, one must accept the lunar day on which the previously determined weekday falls, as the weekday is the primary regulator. || 11 ||

# Now, the Calculation of the Lunar Mansion (Nakshatra) and Yoga
The elapsed years multiplied by 10 <note>original: "vyomendu," where vyoma=0, indu=1</note> and subtracted by the quotient obtained by dividing the years by 240 <note>original: "shunyavedashvi," where shunya=0, veda=4, ashvi=2</note>. This result, placed in two positions and added to the birth mansion and birth yoga respectively, then divided by 27 <note>The number of lunar mansions</note>, gives the current mansion and yoga. || 12 ||

Regarding the calculation of the mansion and yoga: "Divided by 27..."—the remainder is the clear result. For example, the elapsed years 59 multiplied by ten is 590. Set this aside. 590 divided by 240 gives a quotient of 2. Subtracting this from the 590 above gives 588. Adding the birth mansion (the 8th, Pushya), we get 596. Dividing by 27, the remainder is 2; this indicates the Bharani mansion has passed. In the other position, 588 added to the birth yoga (the 13th) equals 601. Dividing by 27, the remainder is 7, which is the yoga named Shobhana. || 12 ||

## Now, the Calculation of the Weekday of the Solar Ingress

> Subtract the number 1445 <note>original: "bāṇa-yuga-abdhi-candra," using the Katapayadi system where arrows=5, ages=4, oceans=4, moon=1</note> from the current Shaka year. > Add the elapsed years and divide by seven; counting from Sunday, this constant is known as the Ingress Constant (*Sankranti-Dhruva*). ### Explanation of the Ingress Calculation

Regarding the verse "Shaka...": This refers to the number of years that have passed since the beginning of this specific astronomical manual. One should count from Sunday; the rest is clear. 

For example: The Shaka year is 1516 <note>approx. 1594 CE</note>. Subtract 1445 <note>the epoch of the manual</note> from this. These are the years elapsed since the manual's start: 71. Multiply this by 207, which equals 14,697. Divide this by 800, yielding 18 days, 22 ghatis, and 16 palas <note>18|22|16; a ghati is 24 minutes, a pala is 24 seconds</note>. Add the 71 elapsed years to this, resulting in 89|22|16. Divide by 7 <note>to find the weekday remainder</note>, and the remainder is 5|22|16. This is the constant known as the **Ingress Constant** (*Sankranti-Dhruva*). 

By adding the specific additive factors (*kshepakas*) for each of the twelve zodiac signs to this constant, the weekday and time of that specific Ingress is found. 

For example: The Constant is 5|22|16. To find the **Aries Ingress** (*Mesha-Sankranti*), add its specific additive factor of 6|58|30. The sum is 12|22|46 <note>corrected from 136 in transcription</note>. Divide by 7, and the remainder is 5|20|46. Thus, in the almanac, the Aries Ingress occurs on Thursday <note>the 5th day counting from Sunday</note> at 20 ghatis and 46 palas. Other Ingresses should be understood in the same way. || 13 ||

This doubled value of thirty-six is subtracted from its own movement-correction <note>original: "svachara-drikphala"</note>. Thus, having observed this, if the visible body is greater than one <note>original: "rupadhika"</note>—meaning greater than one zodiac sign <term>Rashi: a sign of the zodiac consisting of 30 degrees</term>—then it is diminished by one. The remaining degrees are halved to find the "aspect" <term>Drishti: the astrological "glance" or influence one planet casts upon another</term>. This process should be performed for up to four nights.

If the value is greater than the "Vedas" <note>original: "vedadhika"; the number 4 represents the four Vedas</note>—meaning more than four signs—then those four signs are subtracted. The remaining degrees, combined with their own half, are subtracted from forty-five <note>original: "panchayuga"; literally 'five-yugas' representing 45</note> to find the aspect. If it is greater than five, then five is the additive. In the remainder, the aspect is doubled. If it is greater than six, then six signs are the additive. The remainder is multiplied by five <note>original: "sharahatam"</note> and doubled. This, subtracted from sixty, is the aspect. If it is greater than the "horses" <note>original: "ashvadhika"; the number 7 represents the seven horses of the Sun's chariot</note>—meaning more than seven—then seven is discarded. The remainder, joined with its own half, is the aspect. If it is greater than the "elephants and teachers" <note>original: "gajaguru"; the number 8</note>—meaning more than eight—then eight is discarded. Half of the remainder is subtracted from forty-five <note>original: "banabdhi"; literally 'arrows-oceans' representing 45</note> to find the aspect. This continues up to eleven signs. If it is greater than "Shiva" <note>original: "shiva"; the number 11 representing the eleven forms of Rudra</note>—meaning more than eleven signs—then eleven is discarded. The remainder is doubled to find the aspect in minutes <note>original: "kaladya"</note>. This method of calculating aspects was created by the author of this text using his own intellect and proportions. || 55 || 56 ||
