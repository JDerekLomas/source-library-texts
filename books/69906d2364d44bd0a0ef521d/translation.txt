# Salutation to Lord Ganesha
## The Procedure for the Planetary Sacrifice as Stated in the Supplementary Texts

Now begins the **procedure for the planetary sacrifice** <term>Graha-makha-prayoga: a ritual performed to appease or honor the nine celestial bodies in Hindu astrology</term> according to the supplementary traditions. This rite is of three types, distinguished by their purpose: **obligatory** <term>Nitya: rituals performed daily or at fixed cosmic intervals</term>, **occasional** <term>Naimittika: rituals performed for specific life events or milestones</term>, and **desire-based** <term>Kamya: rituals performed to achieve a specific goal or ward off a specific evil</term>. 

The **obligatory** rites are performed during the solstices, equinoxes, and eclipses. The **occasional** rites are performed during auspicious life-cycle ceremonies such as conception. The **desire-based** rites are performed for the removal of calamities or the acquisition of wealth. 

In this matter, the original source-texts must be understood. One must perform the ritual only after knowing the "touch" <note>likely referring to the planetary alignments or the physical contact with ritual implements</note> and other specifics of the planets; otherwise, the rite is fruitless. Regarding this, the teacher **Ashvalayana** <note>A famous ancient sage and author of ritual manuals (Sutras)</note> says:

> "One must know the form, color, weapons, station, the sacred circle, the mantras, the vehicles, the sacrificial wood, the fire-pit, the offerings, as well as the flowers, gems, and shapes. One must know the **Gayatri** <term>a highly sacred Vedic meter and mantra</term>, the root mantras, the name-mantras, the 'armor' <note>Kavacha: protective mantras</note>, and the mantras for the **presiding and co-presiding deities** <term>Adhidevata and Pratyadhidevata: the secondary gods associated with each planet</term> along with their 'touch' and other details. A wise man should know all these regarding the nine planetary carriers. He who is ignorant and merely relies on faith does not obtain the fruit of the rite. Such a man, despite his efforts, encounters great sorrow and the loss of his life-force."

Thus, the application of the Gayatri and the Planetary Gayatri will be described. The colors and other details, as well as the pavilion and fire-pit, are as follows: <margin>Shiva

The **desire-based** rite should be performed according to one's own needs. Aside from the obligatory and occasional rites, all others should be done in a **ceremonial pavilion** <term>Mandapa</term>, as stated in the **Matsya Purana** <note>One of the eighteen major Puranas (ancient encyclopedic scriptures), often cited for architectural and ritual dimensions</note>. Regarding the fire-pit, the teacher **Shivabhatta** <note>A ritual authority; the OCR 'pivabha' is likely a transcription error for 'Shivabha'</note> also provides instructions. 

The characteristics of the pavilion are described by **Hemadri** <note>A 13th-century polymath and high official who wrote the Chaturvarga-chintamani, a massive compendium on ritual law</note> and in the **Matsya Purana**:
A wise man should cause the pavilion to be built to the **northeast of the house**. It should be equal to a temple of Rudra (Shiva), square, and on leveled ground. It should be **ten cubits** <note>A 'hasta' or cubit is the length from the elbow to the tip of the middle finger</note> or perhaps sixteen cubits in size. The learned man should ensure it has four doors.

As for the **fire-pit** <term>Kunda</term> and the **sacred circle** <term>Chakra</term>: for the sacrifice to the nine planets, the fire-pit should be **one cubit** in size on all sides. It should be square, and its exterior should be purified and bright. The **rims** <term>Mekhala: the tiered borders or steps around the fire-pit</term> should be four fingers in width and four fingers in height. These should be constructed according to the measurements of one's own wealth <note>meaning the scale of the ritual can be adjusted based on the patron's means</note>. 

The center should be raised like the **back of a tortoise** <term>Kurma-prishtha</term> and marked with lines on the sides. It should be equipped with an **opening** <term>Yoni: the 'womb' or lip of the fire-pit from which the energy or fire is said to emerge</term> that is shaped like the trunk of an elephant or a pipal leaf. There is an option to make it resemble an elephant or a pipal leaf. That **opening** <gloss>yoni</gloss> must be placed to the West.

## The Procedure for the Planetary Sacrifice

The **patron** <term>Yajamana: the person who sponsors and benefits from the ritual</term>, having bathed and performed his daily morning duties, should state the specific time—the solstice and other calendrical markers—and formulate the **declaration of intent** <term>Sankalpa: a formal vow made at the start of a ritual to state its purpose</term>: 

"For the purpose of pleasing the Supreme Lord through the attainment of planetary favor, I shall perform the planetary sacrifice." 

In the case of a desire to remove calamities, the patron should say: 
"For the purpose of pleasing the Supreme Lord through the fulfillment of a specific desire [naming the desire]..." 

In the case of life-cycle rites like the **conception ritual** <term>Garbhadhana: the first of the sixteen traditional sacraments (Samskaras)</term>, the performer of that rite should say: 
"For the purpose of pleasing the Supreme Lord through the attainment of favor from the Great Sun and other planets, I shall perform the planetary sacrifice."

Having spoken the intent, "I shall perform," the materials should then be gathered. For the daily and occasional versions of the rite, one should obtain the items specified in the primary texts; in their absence, one should procure what is available according to one's capacity. In some cases, because no substitute is allowed, only the items specified in the texts must be used.

Regarding the **selection of the Preceptor** <term>Acharya-varana</term>: one should choose a Brahmin who is of good character and knows the ritual procedures. Touching the priest's hand with his own, the patron should select him to act as the High Priest <term>Brahma</term> and Preceptor for this planetary sacrifice. He then selects the other **officiating priests** <term>Ritvijs</term>, choosing each one individually.

<margin>Selection</margin>

The patron addresses the priest: "I select you, a Brahmin of such-and-such lineage, born of such-and-such clan, student of such-and-such branch of the Vedas, named so-and-so, to be the Preceptor in this planetary sacrifice ritual." 

Once the Preceptor is selected, the priest responds with verses such as "Jupiter is the Preceptor of the gods..." <note>original: "bṛhaspatiḥ"; referring to the planet Jupiter as the divine priest</note>. Then, following the path of the Sun, the patron should request the priest, saying: "I shall perform the sixteen-fold service. Having been requested by me, may you be successful in this sacrifice." The priests, being pleased, should then perform the work.

### Planetary Procedure

...perform the pacification ritual according to the rules. This includes the honoring of the **priests** <term>Ritvij: the officiating priests invited to perform specific roles in a sacrifice</term> with perfumes, clothing, and other gifts according to one's financial capacity. 

There, the **Preceptor** <term>Acharya: the head priest who oversees the entire ceremony</term> should perform the **Agnyuttarana** <term>lit. "lifting out of the fire"; a ritual purification of new metal or stone idols to remove the "heat" or impurities of the crafting process</term> of the planetary images. The procedure for this is found in the **Appendix** <note>Parishishta: supplementary texts to the Kalpa Sutras providing ritual details</note>. 

The nine materials for the images are:
1. Copper
2. Crystal
3. Red Sandalwood
4. Gold
5. Silver
6. Iron
7. Lead
8. Bronze
9. Gold (again, as a substitute for others)

Alternatively, gold alone may be used for all of them. Regarding this, the sage Brihaspati also suggests that the image of Mercury should be made of gold. 

Then, the Preceptor, having purified his hands, states the place and the time. With the permission of the Patron, he makes the declaration: "I shall perform the duties of the Preceptor." 

He then recites:
"Whatever spirits are stationed here, having always relied on this place, let them leave this location and go wherever they belong. Let them depart!"

While reciting this, he should scatter mustard seeds in the directions starting from the East, moving clockwise. 

> "May the Maruts, pure and purifying, come to us with their offerings. They who are the source of truth, born of purity, the cleansers. Agni, the most observant of pure vows, the pure sage, the pure poet, shines when the oblation is offered. In his rising, the pure ones..."

> "...let them come. May the demons depart! Let it be known that this is a place for the sacrifice to the gods, and let it be protected from the Rakshasas." 

With this, he should mark the ground in the various directions. 

Then, he should sprinkle the area with **Panchagavya** <term>the five products of the cow: milk, curd, ghee, urine, and dung; used for extreme ritual purification</term> while reciting the three verses beginning with *"Vashukadya..."*. Similarly, he should sprinkle the area with pure water while reciting the three verses: *"O Waters, you are the source of bliss..."*

Then, with palms joined in reverence, he recites the prayer for auspiciousness:
"May we mount the great ship of the gods, which provides safety and well-being, and which is free from injury. O Lord of the Word, release us from distress... I take refuge with a concentrated mind. May there be well-being in our hardships, may there be fearlessness for us."

After praying to the gods with these two mantras, he should perform the **Agnyuttarana** of the nine planetary images. 

**The Procedure for Cooling the Images:**
For the mantra *"Agni, I bring the sacrifice..."*, the sage is Ghrina, the deity is Agni, and the meter is **Trishtubh** <note>A Vedic meter consisting of four padas of eleven syllables each</note>. This mantra is used for the Agnyuttarana. After bathing the image with the verse containing the word "Agni," one should bathe it again with the verse containing the seven-fold Fire. Thus concludes the Agnyuttarana. 

Thereafter, the Preceptor, after making a formal vow of action, begins the preparation in the North-East (Ishanya) corner of the courtyard. He marks a square area measuring one hand-length <term>hasta: approx. 18 inches</term> in each of the four directions, or thirty-four fingers wide...

## Procedure for Adding Bark
<margin>Planetary Procedure</margin>

<note>This section describes the "Sarvau-shadhi" or the gathering of various sacred earths and barks to be placed inside the ritual water pot.</note>

Gather earth from a horse-stable, an elephant-gate, an anthill, a cowshed, and from the path of a bull. Recite the prayer: "O Earth, who was lifted up by the hundred-armed Krishna in the form of a Boar, take away the sin I have committed." 

Place these in the pot. Then recite the "Roaring Frightfully" hymn <note>original: Ruvanti Bhima; Rigveda 9.72.1</note>, which belongs to the sage Renujyamadagni, dedicated to the Purifying Soma in the Jagati meter: "The roar of the mighty bulls... Soma comes to sit in his rightful place."

Following this, add the barks of the **Five Sacred Trees** <term>Panchavalkala: a group of five barks used for purification, typically including the Banyan and Pipul</term>, specifically the Nyagrodha (Banyan), Ashvattha (Sacred Fig), Plaksha (White Fig), and others. Recite the hymn by the sage Vashistha: "May the herbs that grow and go... bless this ground."

Then, place the leaves of those same trees into the pot. Recite the "Come to us, O Soma" hymn <note>original: Apyayasva; Rigveda 1.91.16</note> by the sage Gautama, dedicated to the Moon: "May Soma grow from all sides... be the source of our strength." With this, add **cow's milk** and **curd** while reciting the Dadhikravna hymn.

"With four and four, with two and five; and then again with two, the offerings are made. May that Lord Vishnu <note>The verse refers to the "Yajnamurtis" or the structure of Vedic offerings through different numbers of oblations</note> be pleased with me."

Then, after honoring the Brahmins with salutations, unbroken rice, flowers, cloth, betel leaves, and ritual fees <term>Dakshina</term>, and having joined his palms in prayer, he should say: "Whatever ritual of the Nine Planet Sacrifice I have performed—which may be deficient in its timing, deficient in devotion, deficient in strength, or deficient in faith—let it all be made complete and flawless by the word of you Brahmins." 

Then all the priests should respond: "Let it be complete. Let it be flawless." 

He should receive these and other blessings. Having prayed to them and bowed down, he should recite: "I salute that Infallible Lord <note>original: *Achyuta*</note>, by whose remembrance and the utterance of whose name every deficiency in penance, sacrifice, and ritual action immediately reaches completeness. If those performing a ritual should fall from the correct path through negligence, everything becomes complete by the mere remembrance of Vishnu"—so says the revealed scripture <term>Shruti</term>. 

Having spoken this, to remedy any excesses or deficiencies in the ritual, he should chant the three names: "Achyuta, Ananta, Govinda." According to the text *Parijata*, after dedicating the fruit of the household sacrifice to Lord Vishnu, he himself, with a joyful heart, should eat a meal along with his kinsmen.

This is the Nine Planet Sacrifice as described in the *Ashvalayana Grihya Parishishta* <note>An appendix to the household ritual manuals of the Ashvalayana school of the Rigveda</note>. It is specifically for those of the Ashvalayana school following the Madri lineage. For those of the 'Many-Hymned' school <note>original: *Bahvrichas*, referring to the Rigveda</note>, it is said in their own household manuals: "Whatever ritual is prescribed for a person, if they perform it according to the requirements of that scripture, then everything is considered fully accomplished." 

This principle applies to others as well. Whatever is mentioned in one's own household manual <term>Grihya Sutra</term>, that should be followed. If a specific ritual is not mentioned in the manuals of sages like Bharadvaja and others, then it should be performed as stated in the *Matsya Purana* <note>One of the eighteen major Puranas, containing extensive ritual instructions</note>, because the legal codes <term>Smritis</term> are common to all. This is the general direction.
