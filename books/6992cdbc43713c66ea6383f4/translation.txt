# The Invincible Array for Subduing the Rong

The **Invincible Array for Subduing the Rong** <note>original: 平戎萬全之陣 (Píngróng Wànquán zhī Zhèn). "Rong" was a historical term for non-Han peoples to the north and west; this formation was specifically designed by the Song to counter nomadic cavalry.</note> was personally devised and authored by **Emperor Taizong of the Song**. <note>Reigned 976–997 CE.</note> The formation utilizes 4,320 chariots <term>車 (chē): in this context, these are heavy war-wagons or supply carts used as mobile fortifications</term>, 30,650 cavalry horses, and 140,930 soldiers. These are deployed into a Great Array consisting of five divisions: Front, Rear, Center, Left, and Right.

### Historical Context and Critique

During the **Yongxi era** <note>984–987 CE</note>, when the **Khitan** <note>The Liao Dynasty, a powerful nomadic empire to the north of the Song</note> invaded our borders, **Emperor Taizong** devised this formation to defend against the sudden charges and breakthroughs of the barbarian cavalry. The **Female Formation** <note>original: "牝陣" (Pinzhen). The formation is executed when the **black flag** <note>Black is the symbolic color for the element of Water and the North.</note> is raised and the drums are sounded. In the **Female Formation**, the two wings are spread forward, making it easy to "swallow and envelop" the opponent. By enticing the enemy to charge into the center, they become surrounded on three sides, which is sufficient to defeat the **Male Formation** <term>Male Formation (Muzhen)</term><note>A wedge-shaped formation designed for a heavy frontal breakthrough.</note>.

The classics state: "When the enemy is sharp and pointed, attack them from the flanks." Therefore, the "Female" overcomes the "Male."

# Treatise on Military Preparations, Volume 62

To the right is the **Male Formation** <term>Mu Zhen: An offensive, proactive military deployment characterized by its "Yang" or aggressive energy</term>, also known as the **Fire Formation** of the **Yellow Emperor's Five Elements** <note>original: "Huangdi Wuxing zhi Huo Zhen" (黃帝五行之火陣). The Yellow Emperor is the legendary ancestor of the Chinese people; the Fire element is associated with the South, summer, and rapid, consuming movement</note>. It is identical to the **Male Formation** of **Sun Tzu** <note>The famous author of *The Art of War*</note>, the **Elite Sharp Formation** of **Wu Qi** <note>A renowned Warring States period strategist; his "銳陣" (Ruizhen) focused on concentrated, elite shock troops</note>, and the **Soaring Bird Formation** of **Zhuge Liang** <note>The chancellor of Shu Han during the Three Kingdoms period. The "Soaring Bird" is one of his "Eight Formations" (Bagua Zhen), designed for flexibility and rapid strikes</note>.

When the **vermilion flag** <note>Red flags were used to signal the Southern/Fire position and orders to advance</note> is raised and the drums are sounded, the army advances. By utilizing a position that is lower in the front and higher in the rear, the army can take advantage of gaps in the enemy's lines and create disorder. Depending on the specific terrain, it may also be advantageous to employ the **Female Formation** <term>Pin Zhen: A receptive or defensive formation designed to absorb an enemy's momentum</term>; this approach can successfully overcome the **Charging Square** formation.

In the **Charging Square** <note>original: "Chong Fang" (衝方). A dense, rectangular formation intended for a heavy frontal breakthrough</note>, the troops are crowded in the front but sparse in the rear. While the front-line combatants are tightly packed together, there are excessive reserves left idle. As the military classics state: "Those who gain the assistance of the terrain will prosper; those who lose the assistance of the terrain will perish." Therefore, the **Male Formation** is the superior counter to the **Charging Square**.

# The Wheel Formation
<note>original: *Chulunzhen*. A dynamic formation designed for mobility, resembling a revolving wheel that can engage the enemy from any direction.</note>

The **Wheel Formation** described to the right is synonymous with the <term>Earth Formation of the Three Talents</term> <note>The "Three Talents" refers to the cosmic trio of Heaven, Earth, and Humanity.</note> established by **Grand Duke Jiang Ziya**; the **Wheel Formation** of **Sun Tzu**; the **Assault Formation** of **Wu Qi**; and the **Mastery of the Mechanism** formation used by **Zhuge Liang**.

On flat plains and wide-reaching fields, where the army must fight while on the move, the **Wheel Formation** is most suitable. The advantage of the **Wheel** lies in its ability to advance, allowing it to overcome the <term>Hare Net Formation</term> <note>original: *Fuju*. A defensive formation named after a net used to catch rabbits; it is designed to entangle an attacker but lacks lateral mobility.</note>.

# Zhao Benxue’s Critique of Xu Dong’s Four Formation Diagrams

The four formation diagrams in the **Tiger Seal Manual**<note>Original: Hǔqián Jīng (虎鈐經). A significant military treatise from the Song Dynasty, though the author here associates it with the Yuan.</note> were expounded by Xu Dong of the Yuan dynasty. Taking a standard force of 100,000 soldiers as the example: seven parts consist of infantry to serve as the **orthodox** force<note>Original: zhèng (正). The "orthodox" or "upright" force refers to the main body of troops that engages the enemy directly.</note>, and three parts consist of cavalry to serve as the **extraordinary** force<note>Original: qí (奇). The "extraordinary" or "singular" force refers to units used for surprise attacks, ambushes, or flanking maneuvers.</note>.

The formation called the **Flying Goose** possesses a heavy striking power; the **Mist** formation possesses an expansive, enshrouding power; the **Rainbow** formation possesses a curved, embracing power; and the **Eight Trigrams** formation possesses a power for responding in all four directions. These formations transform into one another to serve as the tactical method.

How does one critique these? Generally, **Holding the Extraordinary**<term>Wòqí (握奇): An ancient tactical concept where a commander keeps a reserve force "in hand" to strike at the decisive moment.</term> and the **Rule of Five**<term>Wǔfǎ (伍法): The traditional system of organizing soldiers into basic squads of five, which serves as the building block for all larger military maneuvers.</term> are the two great essentials of constructing a military formation.

**Holding the Extraordinary** is the means by which the "body" of the formation is established. Once the body is established, the "four limbs" <note>The "four limbs" refers to the flanking divisions or vanguard and rear units that move in coordination with the main body.</note> can perform their functions, and the defense remains solid. The **Rule of Five** is the means by which the placement of units is determined. When placement is systematic, then advancing and retreating, entering and exiting, and the opening and closing of "extraordinary" and "orthodox" maneuvers remain orderly and not chaotic. Through this, the few can withstand the many, and the weak can become strong.

These four formations by Xu Dong all utilize a single front as their primary stance, with the central officers aligned in a straight line to form the **Central Army**.

# Wang Ji’s Critique of Linear Formations

**Wang Ji**<note>original: 王驥 (Wáng Jì). Multiplying this by five again results in one hundred and twenty-five men. Doubling that again results in two hundred..."
