# Preface.

Until today, various Sanskrit and vernacular texts regarding the science of astronomy and astrology <note>original: "Jyotish Shastra"</note> have been printed and circulated among the people. However, among them, no text has been observed that clearly explains the results of the planets placed in the birth chart <note>original: "Lagna Kundali"</note> across the twelve houses, in accordance with the principles of the entire branch of horoscopic astrology <note>original: "Hora Skandha"</note> and house-division <note>original: "Bhava Chalita"</note>. It is a widely held belief that events occur exactly according to whether the planets were favorable or unfavorable at the time of birth. All people desire to understand these future results with little effort through the birth chart. However, the branch of astrology containing this subject is difficult; observing it and predicting or understanding future results is not a task for an ordinary scholar. Regarding the qualities such a person must possess, a verse <note>original: "Shloka"</note> has been stated in the *Jataka Bharanam* <note>A classical Sanskrit treatise on natal astrology</note>.

### Verse.

> One who has reached the opposite shore of the vast ocean of horoscopic astrology, who is profoundly proficient in both arithmetic and algebra <note>original: "Patya" and "Bija"</note>, and who is skilled in the science of spheres <note>astronomy/cosmography</note>—he alone is capable of declaring the fruits of destiny. || 1 ||

### Meaning.

Only that learned astrologer <note>original: "Joshi"</note> who has mastered the vast branch of horoscopic astrology, understands all forms of mathematics including algebra, and is skilled in the science of the celestial spheres, will be capable of predicting results.

As described in the verse above, in this current age <note>original: "Manvantara"</note>, learned astrologers are scarce, yet the desire to know the future is held by many. For this reason, a concise and delightful Sanskrit work was found, composed in the *Bhujanga Prayata* meter <note>A poetic meter characterized by a "snake-like" rhythmic movement</note>. This work was prepared after thorough consideration and logical reasoning by a famous great scholar named Narayan Bhatt, of the Bhatt family from the holy city of Kashi <note>Modern-day Varanasi</note>, who extracted the essential essence of the horoscopic branch of astrology to bring joy to scholars and to help in understanding future results.

# Definitions of the Text.

<note>The text begins mid-sentence, likely continuing from the previous page's definition of a birth chart.</note> ...is called the Birth Chart <term>Janma Kundali; a map of the heavens at the exact moment of birth</term>.

The Bhava Chalit Chart <note>original: "Bhava Chalit Kundali"; a dynamic house chart that adjusts for the Earth's tilt</note> is created by calculating the twelve houses <term>Bhavas; the twelve divisions of a horoscope representing different life areas</term> such as the Ascendant, according to the methods taught in the *Graha Laghava*, the *Keshavi*, or the *Muhurta Martanda* <note>These are classical Sanskrit astronomical and astrological treatises widely used in India</note>. After calculating these, one must determine their junctions <term>Sandhis; the border points between two houses</term>. If a planet's position is less than the house's starting point, it should be written in the preceding sign. If it is equal to or greater than the house's starting point but within the junction, it remains in that house. If it exceeds the junction, it must be placed in the following house. A chart in which the nine planets <term>Navagraha; the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, and the two lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu</term> are arranged according to this deliberation is called a Bhava Chalit Chart.

### The Author’s Auspicious Invocation and the Vow of the Text
### Verse.

> Salutations to the Lord Ganesha. <note>original: "Shri Ganeshaya Namah"</note> Bowing down to the Cloud-like One <note>Lord Krishna</note>, who wears shining yellow silken garments, and who is embraced by the Moon-faced one <note>Radha</note> just as a dark cloud is embraced by a flash of lightning; I, Narayana, shall now proclaim the Chamatkara Chintamani. || 1 ||

Commentary: He who has donned yellow and excellent <unclear>garments</unclear> that are beautifully adorned—that Krishna-like Moon is embraced by Radha in the form of a lightning bolt. In another sense, Krishna is called the Moon because he is pleasing, and he is called the Cloud <note>original: "Ghana"</note> because he grants the water of mercy to the Chataka-bird-like devotees. Having bowed to such a God who is embraced by the aforementioned Radha, the author named Narayana speaks this book titled Chamatkara Chintamani.

### Verse.

> I worship Mukunda, whose complexion is dark like a rain-cloud, who at times is filled with the tinkling sound of many small bells, who wears a shimmering yellow garment, and who wanders in the courtyard of Yashoda—He who is unattainable even to the great masters of Yoga. || 2 ||

Commentary: At a certain time, I worship the Lord Mukunda <note>a name for Krishna meaning 'Giver of Liberation'</note>, whose feet are ringing with small bells, who has donned a beautiful yellow silk garment <term>Pitambara: a sacred yellow silk cloth worn by deities</term>, and who moves about in the courtyard of the home of Queen Yashoda. He is the Supreme Soul, endowed with the six divine opulences, unattainable even to the greatest of Yogis, and whose color is like that of a dark cloud.

### Verse

> Having thoroughly churned the four hundred thousand verses of the Great Awakening of Light <note>original: "Jyotirmāhabodha," likely referring to a vast compendium of astronomical knowledge</note> for the delight of the learned, I now compose this work. It is beautiful in its logic, concise in its wording, and written in the "Gait of the Serpent" meter <note>original: "Bhujangaprayāta," a poetic meter characterized by a rhythmic, curving flow</note>. ॥3॥

**Commentary —** The author states that after researching four hundred thousand texts of the Horā section <note>the branch of Jyotish dealing with predictive astrology</note> of the Science of Light <term>Jyotish: the traditional Indian system of astronomy and astrology</term>, and after fully considering them, he is creating this work titled *Chamatkāra Chintāmaṇi* <note>The "Wish-fulfilling Gem of Wonders"</note>. He intends it to be logically pleasing, concise in language, delightful to scholars, and composed in the *Bhujangaprayāta* poetic meter.

### Verse

> What is the use of planets placed in the sky if one does not know their movements? What is the use of the lords of the planets if their positions in the eight-fold divisions are not calculated? What is the purpose of a clarity that lacks a foundation? It is only through the results <note>the actual lived effects</note> that the truth is understood, not by mere positions. ॥4॥

**Commentary —** If one does not understand the positions and movements of the planets in the sky, and fails to calculate the precise "clear" <note>mathematically corrected</note> planetary positions at the moment of birth, and fails to arrange the twelve houses <note>the divisions of the zodiac relative to the horizon</note> and the dynamic birth chart <term>Kundali: a horoscope or birth chart</term> according to the rules—then what is the basis for predicting the results of the planets? People only care about actual experience and results. Without following the prescribed methods to ensure accuracy, that experience will not be validated. Therefore, I speak of the fruits of these planets in accordance with those methods.
Having thus stated the vow and the methodology of the book, I shall now speak of the results of each planet in the twelve houses.
