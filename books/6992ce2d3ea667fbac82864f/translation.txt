### Regulations for Internal Security and Communication Control

Whenever the enemy reaches the outskirts of the city, it is strictly forbidden for anyone within the city to recklessly raise tall objects—such as poles, signposts, or similar items—or to play musical instruments. This measure is taken for fear that internal traitors might use these as signals to coordinate with the enemy outside.

Whenever a messenger arrives at the city gate, they must be escorted directly to the **Commanding General** <term>Commanding General (*zhujiang*): the supreme military leader in charge of the city's defense</term>. **Subordinate officers** <term>subordinate officers (*bijiao*): mid-to-low ranking military officers acting as assistants to the general</term>, commoners, and civil officials are strictly forbidden from meeting with them privately. If anyone finds an **anonymous missive** <term>anonymous missive (*feishu*): literally "flying letters," these were leaflets or messages thrown over the walls by the enemy or left by spies to incite rebellion or spread rumors</term> within the city, they must not read it. Instead, they must immediately take it to their respective military camp and, in the presence of witnesses, seal the document and deliver it to the Commanding General.

All individuals skilled in **astrology and divination** <term>astrology and divination (*xingqi shushu*): the study of celestial omens, atmospheric signs, and mathematical occult arts used to predict the outcome of events</term> must be brought under the direct supervision of the government authorities. They are forbidden from speaking privately with others or discussing strange or supernatural omens, as such talk might **delude the masses** <note>original: "惑衆" (*huozhong*). In a siege, rumors of bad omens were considered as dangerous as a physical breach of the walls.</note> and incite panic.

Once a command has been issued, no officer—from the Commanding General and the Deputy General on down—is permitted to give **conflicting orders** <term>conflicting orders (*zhuanyi zhihui*): unauthorized or contradictory instructions that deviate from the established military plan</term>. All actions must strictly adhere to the established regulations.

# Calculating Grain and Food Provisions

Defending a city depends entirely on the **inhabitants**; the inhabitants depend entirely on the **soldiers**. To provide food, one must first calculate for the civilians, then calculate for the soldiers, and thus calculate the total food needed.

### Throwing Bricks and Stones

One should stockpile many bricks and stones atop the city walls. It is vital to avoid recklessly discharging **arrows, stones, or firearms** <term>矢石火器 (*shishi huoqi*)</term> when the enemy is more than ten paces <note>approximately 15 meters</note> away. Not only will you fail to hit the rebels, but you will also waste useful supplies. Generally speaking, defensive equipment should all be used within a ten-pace range. Only when every action produces a visible result can the defense be considered appropriate.

I have often said: Defending a *li* is not as effective as defending a *zhang*; defending a *zhang* is not as effective as defending a *chi*. <gloss>Defending a distance of 500 meters is less effective than defending 3 meters; defending 3 meters is less effective than defending 1 foot.</gloss> The further the range, the more effort is wasted; the closer the range, the more effective the strength. 

The fundamental purpose of **city defense** <term>守城 (*shoucheng*)</term> is simply the fear of the rebels scaling the wall. The method of resisting the rebels is simply to prevent them from scaling the wall. If long-range attacks do not hit their mark, yet exhaust your strength and damage your equipment, what is the point of them?

### Recruiting the Brave

Whenever the enemy surrounds the city, one must select and recruit brave warriors for specialized tasks. Therefore, it is essential to order the soldiers to **take shifts** <term>輪班 (*lunban*)</term> to rest during the day [to ensure they are fully alert under the cover of darkness].

# Deceptive Night Drumming

During the night, the drums should be beaten once or twice as if the army is preparing for a **sortie** <term>出師 (*chushi*)</term> <note>a sudden attack launched from a besieged place</note>. This tactic is designed to ensure the enemy gets no sleep, while our own soldiers actually remain resting peacefully. It is essential to inform the guards atop the walls beforehand so that they are not thrown into a panic when the drumming begins.

Throughout the day, all waste and excrement should be thrown over the wall to the base of the fortifications outside.

If the city is short-staffed and the situation becomes critical, able-bodied women should disguise themselves in men’s hats and robes to assist in the defense atop the walls. During the morning and evening ritual shouting, men, women, and children must all cry out in unison. This is done to create the illusion of a massive population and to project a **vibrant spirit** <term>精采 (*jingcai*)</term> <note>in this context, it refers to a display of high morale and intimidating vigor intended to discourage the besiegers</note>.

Families—both men and women—should remain gathered together in their designated areas. However, anyone found guilty of **harassment or lewd behavior** <term>調戲褻狎 (*tiaoxi xiexia*)</term> shall be **beheaded and their head displayed to the public** <term>斬首示眾 (*zhanshou shizhong*)</term> as a warning.

## Using Long Hooks

When the enemy attacks the city using **high-scaling towers** <note>original: *dengaoche* — mobile siege towers designed to allow soldiers to fight at the same height as the wall's defenders</note>, the towers stand level with the city walls. The enemy uses ropes to tether large, sturdy timbers, and groups of five or six men swing these logs to ram the **parapets** <term>女墻 (*nüqiang*)</term> <note>literally "woman’s wall"; these are the smaller, protective crenelated walls built atop the main rampart</note>, much like the heavy rammers used in traditional oil-pressing. 

The parapets are naturally thin and are highly susceptible to the shock of such vibrations; in an instant, the wall will collapse, and the enemy will enter. At this critical juncture, one must use **long iron hooks** <term>提鉤 (*tigou*)</term>. Wait for the tethered timber to reach the edge of the parapet, then use three or four hooks to snag and pull the ropes. Using sharp blades, cut the ropes, and the timber will fall to the ground below. 

In years past, when the **Wokou** <note>original: *wo* — maritime raiders, commonly referred to as "Japanese pirates," who were a major security threat during the Ming Dynasty</note> attacked **Tongxiang** <note>a county in Zhejiang province, China</note>, the local people used these hooks to destroy their siege engines.
