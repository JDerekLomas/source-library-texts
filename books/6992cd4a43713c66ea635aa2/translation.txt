### Korea
<note>original: "朝鮮" (Chao-xian/Joseon). The name literally translates to "Morning Radiance" or "Land of the Morning Calm."</note>

Korea was originally the land enfeoffed to Jizi <note>A semi-legendary royal from the Chinese Shang Dynasty who is traditionally credited with bringing civilization to the Korean peninsula.</note>. During the Qin Dynasty, it fell within the outer borders of the Liaodong region. Later, Emperor Wu of the Han Dynasty conquered Korea and organized the territory into the four commanderies of Zhenfan, Lintun, Lelang, and Xuantu.

Towards the end of the Han Dynasty, the region was occupied by Gongsun Du <note>A warlord who established a semi-independent state in Liaodong.</note>. At the close of the Yongjia era of the Jin Dynasty <note>roughly 313 CE</note>, the territory fell under the control of the Kingdom of Goryeo <note>referred to here as Gaoli</note>. 

During the Tang Dynasty, imperial forces captured Pyongyang and established the Andong Protectorate-General <note>A military administrative office used by the Tang to manage frontier regions</note>. The center of the kingdom subsequently shifted eastward, located over a thousand *li* <note>A traditional unit of distance; 1,000 li is approximately 500 kilometers</note> southeast of the Yalu River. 

During the time of the Later Tang in the Five Dynasties period <note>923–937 CE</note>, Wang Jian <note>The founder of the Goryeo Dynasty</note> expanded the territory significantly, unifying the former lands of Silla and Baekje into a single state. He moved the capital to Songak <note>modern-day Kaesong</note> and designated Pyongyang as the "Western Capital." During the Zhiyuan era of the Yuan Dynasty <note>1264–1294 CE</note>, the Western Capital was brought under the direct administration of the Mongol Empire, which established the Dongning Route General Administration there.

At the beginning of the Hongwu era of our current Dynasty <note>the Ming Dynasty, starting in 1368 CE</note>, the ruler Wang Zhuan <note>King Gongmin of Goryeo</note> sent a memorial to the Emperor to offer his congratulations on the Imperial succession. He was initially enfeoffed as the King of Goryeo, but the kingdom’s name was later changed to Joseon.

The kingdom is divided into eight provinces:
* **The Central Province** is called Gyeonggi.
* **The Eastern Province** is called Gangwon; this was originally the land of the Yemaek people.
* **The Western Province** is called Hwanghae; this is the old territory of Ancient Joseon and the Mahan confederacy.
* **The Southern Province** is called Jeolla, which was originally the land of the Byeonhan.
* **The Southeastern Province** is called Gyeongsang, formerly the land of the Jinhan.
* **The Southwestern Province** is called Chungcheong, which, like the west, falls within the ancient territory of the Mahan.
* **The Northeastern Province** is called Hamgyeong...

### Daeheung Valley
Located between the two mountains of **Cheonma** and **Seonggeo**. The trees are lush and dense, and the springs and stones are bright and pure. In summer, a canopy of green shade covers the ground, and water lilies bloom, filling the entire valley with a clear fragrance. In autumn, the red maples and yellow leaves are reflected in the depths of the water; it is truly a magnificent landscape.

...Directly beneath the base of the rock, the opening is ten feet <note>original: 丈 (zhang). A traditional unit of length approximately equal to 3 meters.</note> wide. At the point where its depth can still be measured, it is only ten feet, but beyond that, the passage winds and curves so that its true depth is impossible to gauge. **The Moving Stone**
Below the **Peak of Nine Wells** <note>original: 九井峰 (Gujeong-bong).</note>, two stones stand uniquely atop the layered cliffs. They are over ten feet high and roughly ten spans in circumference. To the west, they lean against the mountain summit; to the east, they overlook a sheer precipice. Although their weight is such that even a thousand people cannot move them, if a single person shakes them, they appear as if they are about to fall, yet they never do. Thus, they are also called the **Spirit Stones** <note>original: 靈石 (Yeongseok/Lingshi).</note>. The name of the prefecture <note>Yeongam-gun (Spirit Rock County) in modern-day Jeollanam-do province.</note> is derived from this landmark.

**The Blue Crane Grotto**
Located deep within **Mount Jiri** <note>original: 智異山 (Jiri-san). Historically regarded as one of the most sacred mountains in Korea and a site of many legends.</note>. The mountain path is extremely narrow, opening only as wide as a single thread, so that travelers must bow their bodies to proceed. After traveling for several miles, one reaches a vast and open realm. In every direction, the fields are fertile and the soil is rich, ideal for sowing and planting. Because only blue cranes <note>In East Asian mythology, the blue crane is an auspicious bird associated with immortals and hidden paradises.</note> dwell there, the place earned its name. It appears to be a site where those who **withdrew from the world** <note>original: 遁世 (dunshi). Refers to the practice of seeking a hidden utopia to escape political turmoil or war.</note> lived in ancient times, as there are still remains of crumbling walls and old embankments to be found.

## Port Mouth Mountain
Located nine *li* <note>original: 里. A traditional unit of distance; nine *li* is approximately 4.5 kilometers or 2.8 miles.</note> east of **Goseong County** in **Gangwon Province**. At Goseong Port, there is a rock that rises abruptly in layers, resembling a set of stairs. On its summit, more than a hundred people can sit. To the north of this rock, there is another peak composed entirely of stone. Looking east into the sea about five *li* away, there are stone peaks standing like a folding screen. Below these peaks are rocks shaped like "clutching dragons and seizing tigers," <note>An idiomatic expression used to describe jagged, powerful, and dynamic rock formations.</note> which are extraordinarily strange. There are also two rocks facing each other as if they were people whispering in pairs. These stones are all white, reflecting brilliantly against the blue sea; looking upon the scene is like gazing at a painting.

## Three-Day Inlet
Located seven or eight *li* north of **Goseong**. It is surrounded by a ring of overlapping peaks and ridges, containing thirty-six peaks within. The valleys are clear and secluded, while the pines and rocks are ancient and strangely formed. In the middle of the water is a small island with flat, winding dark rocks <note>original: 盤陀 (pantuo). Refers to large, weathered stones with uneven but walkable surfaces.</note>. Atop the island, gnarled pines <note>original: 虬松 (qiusong). Literally "dragon-pines," referring to old trees with twisting, scale-like bark.</note> cast deep shade, and pear blossoms bloom in wild profusion. The lake water is so bright and translucent that it could serve as a mirror. This is not a place for those who eat the food of the hearth <note>original: 烟火食者 (yanhuo shizhe). ### The Flute to Calm Ten Thousand Waves
<note>original: 萬波息笛 (Manpasikjeok). A legendary musical instrument of the Silla Kingdom, symbolizing divine protection and national harmony.</note>

During the reign of King Sinmun of Silla <note>original: 新羅神文王 (Silla Sinmun-wang). The 31st monarch of Silla, who ruled from 681 to 692 AD.</note>, a small mountain appeared floating in the East Sea, drifting back and forth with the waves. When this flute was played:
*   Enemy armies would retreat.
*   Illnesses were healed.
*   Rain would fall during droughts, and the skies would clear during floods.
*   The winds would die down, and the waves would become calm. 

It was thus named the **Flute to Calm Ten Thousand Waves**. It was treasured as a state heirloom for generations. When King Hyoso <note>The 32nd King of Silla, r. 692–702 AD.</note> ascended the throne, he added to its title, calling it the **Grand Flute to Calm Ten Thousand Waves**. <note>original: 萬萬波息笛 (Man-manpasikjeok).</note> It is now lost.

### Annam

The kingdom of **Annam** <note>original: "安南" (Annam). Historically "the Pacified South," a Chinese name for the territory of northern Vietnam.</note> was originally the ancient land of **Southern Jiaozhi** <term>Jiaozhi (Jiaozhi): an ancient name for northern Vietnam, which functioned variously as a Chinese province or a vassal state</term>. During the Song dynasty, the court enfeoffed **Đinh Liễn** <note>The son of Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, the first emperor of Vietnam after independence from China.</note> as the Prince of Jiaozhi Commandery. Later, **Lê Hoàn**, **Lý Công Uẩn**, and **Trần Nhật Cảnh** <note>The founders of the Early Lê, Lý, and Trần dynasties respectively.</note> took the throne through successive usurpations.

After five generations [of the Trần dynasty], the throne was usurped by **Lê Quý Ly** <note>Also known as Hồ Quý Ly, who founded the short-lived Hồ dynasty.</note>. In the fourth year of the **Yongle** era [1406], the Emperor dispatched troops to pacify the region. We established the **Jiaozhi Provincial Administration Commission**, which governed 17 prefectures, 5 sub-prefectures, 41 independent sub-prefectures, and 157 counties.

In the second year of the **Xuande** era [1427], **Lê Lợi** <note>The founder of the Later Lê dynasty and a national hero in Vietnam for resisting Ming rule.</note> rebelled once more. Troops were sent to suppress him. Lê Lợi, acting out of fear, submitted a memorial to the throne begging for the restoration of a descendant of the Trần clan to the throne. The **Imperial Court** <note>The original text uses a space before "朝廷" (Imperial Court) as a mark of respect.</note> granted this request and abolished the system of provincial administration. However, shortly thereafter, Lê Lợi usurped the throne from the Trần heir and established his own rule.

In the sixth year of the **Jiajing** era [1527], the Commander **Mạc Đăng Dung** took advantage of the chaos within the Lê dynasty. He assassinated **Lê Húi** <note>The Emperor Lê Cung Hoàng.</note> and established himself as ruler. He illegitimately claimed the national title of **Great Việt** <note>original: "大越" (Dayue/Đại Việt).</note> and changed the era name to **Mingde**.

His son **Mạc Đăng Doanh** succeeded him and changed the era name to **Dazheng**. Furthermore, they encroached upon our domestic territory. In the eighteenth year of the Jiajing era [1539], the Emperor dispatched troops to punish them. Mạc Đăng Dung submitted a formal surrender, apologized for his crimes, and expressed his willingness to return the encroached lands. Consequently, the **Imperial Court** pardoned Mạc Đăng Dung’s crimes and appointed his son, Mạc Đăng Doanh, as the **Commander-in-Chief of Annam** <term>Commander-in-Chief (Dutongshi): a lower-ranking title than "King," used by the Ming to signal that Annam was a subordinate territory rather than a fully independent vassal kingdom</term>. The military campaign was subsequently halted, though the guilt of their autocratic usurpation remains as it was.

To this day, they bring tribute twice every year, with over a thousand people arriving each time to request rewards. Because it is impossible to distinguish between genuine and fraudulent claims of succession, they all refer to themselves as "Sheren" <note>A title for the sons of officials or administrative aides</note>. However, they intermarry with the northern nomads and even serve as their guides. Though they are nominally called "Outer Guards," they are in truth a hidden danger close at hand; the Crisis of the Gengxu Year <note>1550 CE, when Mongol forces under Altan Khan raided the outskirts of Beijing</note> serves as a warning of this.</gloss>

The Emperor appointed their leaders as Commanders and Vice-Commanders, each leading their respective tribes. From then on, they have brought tribute every year. Their mountain is called Mount Mameng. <gloss>It spans a thousand li, and its central peak is shaped like a horse's crest <note>original: 'Mameng' (馬孟); likely a geographic descriptor for the shape of the ridge</note>.</gloss> Their tribute consists of camels and horses. Their arrival for tribute occurs twice annually: once for the Imperial Birthday and once on New Year's Day. Each guard is permitted a contingent of one hundred people.

## The Land of Ghosts <note>original: "Guiguo" (鬼國); a historical term often used for the territory of the Luoluo or Yi people in modern-day Yunnan and Guizhou</note>
During the Zhiyuan era <note>1264–1294 CE</note>, Emperor Shizu <note>Kublai Khan, founder of the Mongol-led Yuan Dynasty</note> issued an imperial decree to launch a campaign against the Wei-shi clan in the Land of Ghosts. These people were originally known as the Huihe <note>An earlier transcription of "Uighur"</note>. ## The Xi <note>A nomadic people of Northeast China, closely related to the Khitans</note>
Taizu of the Liao Dynasty <note>Abaoji, the founder of the Khitan Empire</note> once campaigned against the Xi and pacified them, establishing the Duogui tribe. During the reign of Emperor Shengzong, the annual tribute of musk deer and other local goods from the five tribes of the Xi was discontinued.

## The Mubugu <note>A tribal group located in the northern frontier during the Liao period</note>
They first sent tribute during the reign of Emperor Taizong of the Liao Dynasty. By the reign of Emperor Shengzong, however, all their tribes had rebelled. It was not until the reign of Emperor Xingzong that their chieftain returned to offer tribute once again.

# The Five Nations <note>original: "Wuguo" (五國); a confederation of five Jurchen tribes—the Merkit, Yuexi, Huifa, Qin, and more—settled along the lower Songhua and Amur Rivers</note>

## The Kingdom of Ryukyu

The Kingdom of Ryukyu <note>Modern-day Okinawa, Japan.</note> is located to the east of Quanzhou, Fujian. From the time of the Han and Wei dynasties <note>Approx. 206 BCE – 265 CE</note>, there was no communication or <term>tribute missions</term> <note>A system where foreign states provided gifts to the Chinese Emperor to acknowledge his ritual primacy in exchange for trade rights and protection.</note> from this land. However, during our current dynasty, they were the first to submit and declare allegiance. Their ruler led his sons and younger brothers to the imperial court. **Taizu** <note>The "Grand Ancestor" and founding emperor of the Ming Dynasty, the Hongwu Emperor (r. 1368–1398).</note> praised their loyalty and obedience, bestowing upon them imperial tallies, seals, ceremonial headwear, and robes. 

Furthermore, the Emperor sent thirty-six families from Fujian who were skilled in navigation <note>original: "Minren zhi shan cao zhou zhe sanshiliu xing" (閩人之善操舟者三十六姓). This was a famous diplomatic act to provide the Ryukyu Kingdom with the technical expertise needed for international trade and seafaring.</note> to assist them. He also granted permission for the King’s sons and the sons of his high officials to study at the **National Academy** <note>original: "Guoxue" (國學), the central institution of higher learning in the imperial capital.</note>. 

Originally, the country was divided into three entities: the King of **Zhongshan** (Central Mountain), the King of **Shannan** (Southern Mountain), and the King of **Shanbei** (Northern Mountain). Since that time, only the King of Zhongshan has continued to send tribute without interruption; the other two kingdoms were eventually annexed by him. The local products they offer as tribute are mostly purchased from other nations, as their own land produces very little.

### The Kingdom of Burma
<note>original: "Mian Guo." Historically known to the Chinese as Mian, referring to the various kingdoms in the region of modern-day Myanmar, particularly the Pagan and Ava periods.</note>

The **Kingdom of Burma** is among the "Southwestern Barbarians." It is not known what tribe they originate from. Their land borders **Dali** <note>A kingdom in modern Yunnan province known for its Buddhist culture.</note> and in some parts is not far from **Chengdu**. The exact dimensions of their territory in leagues are unknown. To the east it reaches **Mubang** <note>Hsenwi, in the northern Shan State of Myanmar.</note>; to the south it reaches the **South Sea** <note>likely referring to the Bay of Bengal or the Andaman Sea</note>; to the west is **Gali**; and to the north is **Longchuan**. 

Within its borders are the five cities of the Burmese King: **Jiangtou** <note>Kaungsin</note>, **Taigong** <note>Tagaung, an ancient capital</note>, **Mala’an**, **Zhengguo**, and **Pugan** <note>Bagan, the famous capital known for its thousands of Buddhist temples.</note>. The people have walled cities, houses, and cottages in which they dwell. They ride elephants and horses on land and use boats and rafts to cross waters. 

For their writing, documents presented to superiors are inscribed on **leaves of gold**; for the next rank, **paper** is used; and for others, they use **palm leaves** <note>original: "kulang ye." These are Ola leaves, traditionally used throughout Southeast Asia for manuscripts.</note>. Their language requires translation to be understood.

In the 8th year of the **Zhiyuan** era <note>1271 CE, during the reign of Kublai Khan</note>, the Dali Regional Pacification Office and the Marshal's Household sent envoys to summon the King to submit, but he did not come. In the 19th year <note>1282 CE</note>, the Yuan Emperor sent Prince **Sangudar** <note>original: "Xiangdawuer"</note> to attack Burma, successfully conquering it. In the 29th year of the **Hongwu** era of the **Imperial Ming** <note>1396 CE</note>, the kingdom first submitted to our dynasty. The **Burma Military and Civilian Pacification Commission** and other offices were then established.

## Siam <gloss>This land is located in the sea at the southernmost reaches of Champa</gloss>

The kingdom was originally two separate countries: **Siam** <note>Syam</note> and **Louvo** <note>original: "Luohu"; the Lavo Kingdom centered on modern-day Lopburi</note>. The people of Siam are the descendants of the **Red Eyebrows** <note>term: "Chimei"; a famous peasant rebel group from the Han Dynasty (approx. 17–23 CE) whose remnants allegedly fled south</note>. Their soil is barren and unsuitable for farming. In contrast, the land of **Louvo** is flat and expansive, yielding abundant harvests. Consequently, the people of Siam rely on Louvo for their food supply. Later, during the **Zhizheng** era <note>1341–1368 CE</note> of the **Yuan Dynasty**, the two countries merged into a single state.

At the beginning of the **Hongwu** era <note>1368–1398 CE</note> of our current **Imperial Dynasty**, they presented a **memorial written on gold leaves** <note>term: "Jinye biao"; an official diplomatic document engraved on thin sheets of gold to show high status and respect</note> and offered local products as tribute. An imperial decree was issued to grant them an official seal and investiture, and they were provided with **tallies** <note>term: "Kanhe"; divided certificates used to verify the identity of official tribute missions</note>. At their request, the Emperor also granted them standard weights and measures.

The kingdom is located at the extreme south of **Champa**. Their tribute includes:

*   **Animals and animal products:** Elephants, ivory, rhinoceros horns, peacock tails, kingfisher feathers, tortoise shells, and six-legged tortoises.
*   **Gemstones and valuables:** Precious stones, coral, gold rings.
*   **Camphor varieties:** **Flake camphor** <note>term: "Piannao"</note>, **plum-blossom camphor** <note>term: "Meinao"</note>, **bran camphor** <note>term: "Kangnao"</note>, camphor oil, and camphor wood.
*   **Aromatics and incense:** Sandalwood, **sapanwood incense** <note>term: "Suxiang"</note>, **benzoin** <note>term: "Anxi-xiang"; an aromatic balsamic resin</note>, **"yellow-ripened" incense** <note>term: "Huangshu-xiang"</note>, **lakawood** <note>term: "Jiangzhen-xiang"; used for incense and traditional medicine</note>, Louvo incense, **frankincense** <note>term: "Ruxiang"</note>, tree incense, **aucklandia** <note>term: "Muxiang"</note>, **"dark incense"** <note>term: "Wuxiang"</note>, and **cloves** <note>term: "Dingxiang"</note>.
*   **Medicinals and resins:** **Asafoetida** <note>term: "Awei"; a pungent dried latex used in medicine</note>, rose water, clove bark, **wanshi** <note>a type of stone or possibly an ornamental dish</note>, plant stems, **dragon's blood** <note>term: "Tengjie"; a bright red resin from the Calamus tree</note>, **gamboge** <note>term: "Tenghuang"; a saffron-colored pigment resin</note>, sulfur, and **myrrh** <note>term: "Moyao"</note>.
*   **Miscellaneous spices and dyes:** **Catechu** <note>term: "Wudieni" or Terra Japonica; an extract used for tanning and dyeing</note>, nutmeg, pepper, white cardamom, and **long pepper** <note>term: "Bibo"</note>.

To the southeast is a great mountain; to the northwest, the land reaches the sea.

**Huamian** <note>original: 花面 (Huamian), literally "Flowered Faces"; historically associated with the tattooed inhabitants of the Sumatran interior, such as the Battak people</note> borders the territory of **Samudera** <note>original: 蘇門答刺 (Sumenda-la); an important sultanate on the north coast of Sumatra</note>. Regarding the "Flowered Faces," the men all use ink to tattoo their faces with the shapes of flowers and beasts, which is why they are given this name. <gloss>They brought tribute to the August Ming dynasty</gloss>

---

**Luzon** <note>original: 呂宋 (Lüsong); refers to the largest island in the Philippines</note> is a small country in the middle of the sea. <gloss>They brought tribute to the August Ming dynasty</gloss>

---

**Kochi** <note>original: 柯枝 (Kezhi); modern Cochin/Kochi in Kerala, India</note> is situated directly opposite **Mount Ceylon** <note>original: 錫蘭山 (Xilanshan); modern Sri Lanka</note>. Its villages sit beside the sea, and the fields are barren, yielding poor harvests. <gloss>They came to the August Ming dynasty to present tribute</gloss>

---

The land of **Ganbali** <note>original: 甘巴里 (Ganbali); likely referring to Comari/Cape Comorin at the southern tip of India, or perhaps Quilon</note> is located on a large island within the Southern Ocean. <gloss>They presented tribute to the August Ming dynasty</gloss>

---

The land of **Boluo** <note>original: 婆羅 (Boluo); referring to Borneo/Brunei</note> is backed by mountains and faces the Western Sea. <gloss>They presented tribute to the August Ming dynasty</gloss>

---

**Adan** <note>original: 阿丹 (Adan); modern Aden in Yemen</note> borders the sea and is situated near the country of **Calicut** <note>original: 古里 (Guli); though geographically distant, Aden and Calicut were the two major hubs of the Indian Ocean spice trade</note>. They pile stones to form city walls and use <term>Luoban stones<note>original: 羅般石 (luobanshi); likely a transcription of a foreign term for marble or specifically dressed masonry</note></term> to construct houses. The fields there are fertile and beautiful.

# Sancai Tuhui: Geography, Volume 13

**Timor** <note>original: 吉里地悶 (Jilidimen); the island of Timor in the southeastern Malay Archipelago, known in Ming times as a source of sandalwood</note> is located to the east of **Lianjialuo** <note>original: 連迦羅 (Lianjialuo); likely referring to the Lingga Islands or a nearby region</note>. Its land is filled with <term>miasma</term> <note>original: 瘴氣 (zhangqi); in pre-modern medicine, this refers to "unhealthy vapors" rising from tropical forests or swamps, believed to cause illnesses like malaria</note>.

**Kalimantan** <note>original: 假里馬丁 (Jialimading); the island of Borneo</note> faces **Jiaolan Mountain** <note>original: 交欄山 (Jiaolanshan); Gelam Island, a landmark for Ming sailors off the southwest coast of Borneo</note> across the ocean. In this land, the mountains are arranged like emerald-green screens, and the climate is constantly hot.

**Aru** <note>original: 啞魯 (Yalu); a kingdom on the northeastern coast of Sumatra</note>. To the south of this kingdom are great mountains; to the north is the great sea. To the west lies the border of **Samudera** <note>original: 西門答剌 (Ximendala); referring to the Samudera-Pasai Sultanate on northern Sumatra, which was a primary hub for Ming voyages</note>. To the east, there are flat lands suitable for planting rice and grain.

**Naha** <note>original: 那哈 (Naha); likely a transcription for a region near Nakur</note> is the same as the kingdoms of **Sumatra**, **Shigusü**, and **Yuanna**. The southern part of the country has deep, great mountains; the north faces the great sea, and the east also consists of great mountains.

**Lide** <note>original: 黎代 (Lidai); a small polity on the northern coast of Sumatra</note> is located to the west of the territory of the **King of Nakur** <note>original: 那孤兒王 (Naguer Wang); famously known in Ming records as the "King of the Tattooed Faces"</note>. To its south are great mountains, and to the north, it overlooks the vast sea. To the west, it connects with the **Lambri** <note>original: 淳里國 (Shunliguo); a transcription of Lamuri or Lambri, a kingdom at the northwestern tip of Sumatra</note> border.
