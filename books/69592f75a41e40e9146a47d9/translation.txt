<note>This page features a classic "snail" or "spiral" marbling pattern, often referred to in historical bookbinding as *coquille*. This technique was achieved by dropping pigments onto a tray of thickened water (size) and using a stylus or comb to swirl the colors into rhythmic, shell-like spirals before transferring the design to the paper.</note>

Such papers were highly prized in the <term>early modern period</term> <note>roughly 1500–1800</note> for their beauty and their ability to hide wear and tear on the inner covers of a book.
