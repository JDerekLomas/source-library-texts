Let common urine evaporate <note>original: "verriechen"; literally to "smell away" or exhale into the air</note> in a large glass flask over the fire until a black matter remains at the bottom <note>original: "in fundo"</note>. This same matter should be sealed <note>original: "verlutiren"; to seal a vessel airtight using "lute," a paste of clay, dung, or cloth</note> in an unglazed earthenware pot or jar, and calcined <term>calcined; heated to high temperatures to reduce a substance to ash or powder</term> as strongly as possible for three or four hours. Whatever then remains in the pot or jar, leach it through a linen or woolen bag with pure filtered rainwater until all the sharpness is removed from the matter. Then let the moisture evaporate and coagulate <note>original: "eincoaguliren"; to thicken or solidify from a liquid into a solid salt</note>; thus one obtains a snow-white salt, which some physicians use in many illnesses. Its dose is four grains <term>grain; a very small unit of weight, roughly 65 milligrams</term> at a time, administered in suitable liquids.
