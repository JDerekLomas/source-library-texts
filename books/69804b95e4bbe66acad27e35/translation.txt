Now, I do not wish to speak here of the definition of the **Philosopher’s and Universal Stone** <note>original: "Lapidis Philosophici & Universalis."</note> / for such things are found everywhere in the writings and books of the Philosophers; instead, I only wish to instruct the common practitioners here who are unlearned in Latin / and who understand the philosophical **terms** <note>original: "Terminos." These are the specialized vocabulary of alchemy.</note> not according to their internal nature and property / but only **by name** <note>original: "nominetenus."</note> and superficially; for the **Philosopher’s Stone** <note>original: "Lapis Philosophorum."</note> and the **Universal** are not always one and the same thing / and they do not always stand as **interchangeable terms.** <note>original: "termino convertibili." In logic, this refers to two terms that mean exactly the same thing and can be swapped; Starycius argues they are distinct concepts.</note>

<margin>~</margin> Therefore, know / that **Raymundus Lullius** <note>Ramon Llull (c. 1232–1315), a Majorcan philosopher and polymath whose name was frequently attached to alchemical works in the Renaissance.</note> made twelve **Universal Stones**, each one distinguished from the other; but otherwise, the **Philosophers** generally have three **Universal Stones**, and beyond these yet one **Most Universal Stone** <note>original: "Lapidem Universalissimum."</note> / and this is first the true **Philosopher’s Stone** and blessed Stone of the Wise.

The first **General or Subordinate Universal Stone** <note>original: "Lapis Universalis Generalis oder Subalternus."</note> is **Vegetable** <note>original: "Vegetabilis." This refers to an alchemical medicine or stone derived from plant matter rather than minerals or metals.</note> / this must now be sought **materially** in the **Vegetable kingdom** / its **solvent** <note>original: "Menstruum."
