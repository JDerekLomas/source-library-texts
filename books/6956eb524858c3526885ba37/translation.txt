The said earths must not be mixed but joined together, and this is what the art of **Arnaldus de Villa Nova** <note>Arnaldus de Villa Nova (c. 1240–1311) was a famous physician and alchemist; the "Rosary" (Rosarius Philosophorum) mentioned here is a seminal alchemical text attributed to him.</note> says in the *Rosary*, and **Master Ramon Llull** <note>Ramon Llull (c. 1232–1315) was a Majorcan philosopher; many alchemical works were pseudepigraphically attributed to him in this period.</note> speaks of it in his book the *Rosary* and elsewhere concerning the said stone: **mineral, vegetable, and animal**, which is body, soul, and spirit. And the spirit is mild and has little sulfur and dry earth, and at the end of its nature it is able to transform and transmute itself. It can become metal, for all movement reduces itself to its own honey <note>original: "mel"; likely referring to the "sweetness" or pure essence of the substance</note>, and this belief and multiplication in its entirety has made a true metallic body. Just as the leavened dough makes the bread—for when it is cooked with the leaven, the said leaven no longer has power over it—so especially do many authorities agree, though their words seem strange. For few will understand that the shining thing will do well by staying within the principles of nature, but it will produce from the one the **rust of antimony** <term>erugo d'antimonj: verdigris or copper-rust of antimony, a specific chemical byproduct in the Great Work</term> and from the iron the ingenious movements near the practice of gold.

Ramon says of the seven operations that in the whole year:
- Two are of the spirit of the **Fifth Essence** <note>The "Quintessence," the purest fifth element beyond earth, air, fire, and water.</note>
- Two are of the sulfur
- Two are of the ferment
- And the seventh and final is of the **multiplication**

For when the stone has reached all perfection, it can be multiplied by whatever body one wishes, and by whatever spirit one wishes to prepare and verify. For it has the nature of leaven, and so the element and the spirit of **Hermes** <note>Hermes Trismegistus, the legendary Hellenistic figure to whom the Hermetic corpus and the foundations of alchemy are attributed.</note> which is called "gold" everywhere by Arnaldus de Villa Nova in the published earth... <note>The text here becomes somewhat obscure, likely referring to the "white sulfur" or the "philosophical earth."</note> let no one laugh at his own [work] for purifying, and it would have a published base. And this is published as his white sulfur, not burning, and because it submits to the bottom it makes a certain sulfur that is better than the juice of the stone. This sulfur makes tinctures and makes the waters airy and silicious, and so it is mild to raise <unclear>vstit</unclear> earth. Amen.
