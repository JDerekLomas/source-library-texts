->**SENECA, Lucius Annaeus**<-

Born at Corduba <note>Modern-day Cordova, Spain</note> around 5 or 4 B.C. to a noble and wealthy family. After a childhood and youth marked by illness in Rome under the care of an aunt, he suffered from lifelong nervous disorders but achieved great fame in the fields of rhetoric <note>The art of persuasive speaking and writing</note>, philosophy, finance, and imperial service. Following a period of disgrace during the reign of Emperor Claudius, he was appointed tutor and later, in 54 A.D., became the primary advising minister to Nero. He failed to prevent some of Nero's most heinous crimes. Though his actual involvement remains questionable, he was implicated in a conspiracy and committed suicide by imperial order in 65 A.D. 

A man of contradictions, he was wealthy yet preached indifference to riches; he feared pain and death yet taught others to scorn both; and there were further discrepancies between his personal conduct and his moral principles. However, he was not a wicked man. 

Of his surviving works, we possess ten so-called "Dialogues" <note>original: "Dialogi"</note>, seven of which are philosophical inquiries—covering subjects such as divine providence, steadfastness, the happy life, anger, leisure, tranquility of mind, and the brevity of life. We also have three other treatises (concerning money, benefits, and natural phenomena); 124 "Moral Letters" <note>original: "Epistulae morales"</note> all addressed to a single recipient; a satirical skit regarding the official deification of the Emperor Claudius; and nine rhetorical tragedies (intended for reading rather than stage performance) based on ancient Greek themes. Many of his "Letters" and all of his public speeches have been lost to time. Much of his thought is characterized as clever rather than profound, and his writing style is noted for being sharp and concise rather than expansive.
