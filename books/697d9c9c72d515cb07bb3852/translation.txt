One is sufficiently assured that there already exist far too many books dealing with <term>Hermetic philosophy</term> <note>A tradition of esoteric knowledge named after Hermes Trismegistus, focusing here on the "Great Work" of alchemy.</note>; and that, since no one wished to write with total clarity—without ambiguous meanings and without allegory—concerning this science (which, indeed, no wise man will ever do), it would be better to remain silent altogether rather than to fill the world with new writings. Such works serve only to further confuse the minds of those who strive to fathom the <term>Philosophical secrets</term> <note>In the 17th and 18th centuries, "philosophy" often referred specifically to alchemy and the study of nature's hidden laws.</note>, rather than leading them onto the right path that would bring them to the desired place they intend to go.
