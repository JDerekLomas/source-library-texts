# Preface

Although Paracelsus explains many more magnificent <term>mysteries<note>original: "mysteria"; refers to hidden spiritual or natural truths</note></term> in this little book—concerning the four <term>elements<note>Earth, Air, Fire, and Water</note></term>, concerning mankind, and concerning good and evil <term>spirits</term>, etc.—I shall nevertheless leave them all uncounted here, for the little book is clear and intelligible enough on its own.

However, since I have never forgotten the friendship and inclined goodwill that Your Excellence showed me in Strasbourg several years ago, I have allowed this little book to be released in public print under Your Excellence's name. Firstly, so that I may show myself grateful toward Your Excellence; and also, because this subject matter will, by virtue of your station, undoubtedly be more dear and pleasant to Your Excellence than other <term>labors<note>original: "labores"; refers to academic or professional works</note></term>.

# Preface

It is well worth considering that **Theophrastus** <note>Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim, better known as Paracelsus (1493–1541), the famous physician and alchemist.</note> bids us to learn wisdom in this little book, and that he speaks no word in it in vain.

It is therefore my most humble request to Your Excellencies <note>original: "E. E." or Euer Excellenz, a formal address to the authorities or patrons to whom the book is dedicated.</note> that you might accept my good intentions in the best possible light, and allow your Theophrastus to be commended to your care. May Your Excellencies' noble spirit and the love which you have shown toward all good arts until now also be felt toward the *Writings* <note>original: "Scriptis"</note> of Theophrastus. Dated at Haguenau <note>A city in Alsace, then a significant center for printing.</note> on New Year's Day, in the year 1575.
