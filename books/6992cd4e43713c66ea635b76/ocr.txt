# 禹 貢 五 服 圖

<image-desc>
This is a schematic diagram from the "Sancai Tuhui" illustrating the "Five Domains" (Wufu) system attributed to the Great Yu in the "Yu Gong" chapter of the Book of Documents. The diagram features a central vertical axis that serves as a spatial model of the empire. At the center is "土城" (the Royal Capital). Above and below this center, the zones of Dian (甸), Hou (侯), Sui (綏), Yao (要), and Huang (荒) are listed, interspersed with the term "Bi" (弼), indicating transitional or auxiliary areas. The lower portion of the axis also maps these to the "Nine Domains" system of the later Zhou dynasty, including Nan (男), Cai (采), Wei (衛), Yi (夷), Zhen (鎮), and Fan (藩). Vertical columns of explanatory text on either side detail the distances (each zone being 500 li wide) and the conceptual correspondences between the Xia and Zhou systems of land division.
</image-desc>

# 禹貢總圖

<image-desc>
A traditional Chinese woodblock print map titled "General Map of the Tribute of Yu" (禹貢總圖). The map illustrates the geographical worldview of the "Yu Gong" chapter of the Classic of History (Shangshu), depicting the Nine Provinces (九州) established by the Great Yu. 

Major features include:
- The winding courses of the Yellow River (黄河) in the north and the Yangtze River (江) in the south.
- The Nine Provinces labeled throughout: Jizhou (冀州), Yanzhou (兖州), Qingzhou (青州), Xuzhou (徐州), Yangzhou (揚州), Jingzhou (荆州), Yuzhou (豫州), Liangzhou (梁州), and Yongzhou (雍州).
- Stylized mountain ranges represented by clusters of peaks, including the Five Sacred Mountains (泰山, 華山, 衡山, 嵩山 are visible).
- Significant water bodies such as Lake Dongting (洞庭), Lake Poyang (彭蠡), and the Huai River (淮).
- Labels identifying ancient tribal groups (e.g., 萊夷, 嵎夷) and specific landmarks like the Taihang Mountains (太行) and Mount Min (岷山).
- The map uses traditional cartographic symbols: undulating lines for water and relief-style drawings for mountains.
</image-desc>
