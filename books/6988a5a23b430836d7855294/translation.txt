Whoever wishes to hold me for a fool
For believing that any dream comes true,
May do so; but as for me, I have faith
That a dream is a signification <note>original: *signifiance*; a term from medieval semiotics meaning a sign that points to a deeper truth or a foreshadowing of future events.</note>

The reason being, that at night one dreams
Of many things in a hidden way
Which one later sees quite clearly.

In the twentieth year of my life,
At the point when Love takes his toll <note>original: *prẽst le peage*; "taking the toll" refers to the inevitable moment in youth when one falls under the power of Desire, treated here like a tax or passage fee one must pay to enter adulthood.</note>
From young people, I went to lie down
One night, as was my custom,
And indeed, I fell fast asleep.
While sleeping, a dream occurred to me
Which was very beautiful to behold,
As you will hear me describe;
For in the seeing of it, it pleased me much,
And there was nothing in the dream
Which did not later happen in full,
Exactly as the dream recounted.
I wish to set this down in rhyme,
The better to lead you with pleasure;
Love requests and commands it of me.
And if, by chance, anyone asks
What I wish this romance
To be called, let the lover know
That it is the **Romance of the Rose**,
In which the whole **Art of Love** is enclosed.
The subject matter is beautiful and praiseworthy.
