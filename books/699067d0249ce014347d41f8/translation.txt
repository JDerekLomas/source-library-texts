**I**n the previous sections, therefore, it has been most clearly proven that, with the two fundamental elements established—namely Earth and Fire—it was necessary for the two aforementioned means <note>The "means" refer to the mathematical proportions of Air and Water that bridge the extremes of Fire and Earth.</note> to be bound together. For as much as there is between Water and Air by reason of density and weight, there is just as much between Air and Fire. And again, what exists between Air and Water by reason of rarity and lightness, the same exists between Earth and Water. Likewise, what exists between Earth and Water by reason of density and weight, this exists between Water and Air. And what is between Water and Air, this is between Air and Fire. And conversely: what is between Air and Fire by reason of thinness and lightness, this is between Air and Water. And what is between Water and Air is understood to be between Water and Earth. <gloss>the same</gloss> These things are compared only to their neighbors and those coherent to them; but toward the extremes, an equality is maintained by a healthy balance. For what Earth is in weight to Air, so is Water to Fire. As it is with the sun, so it is with that which is equally divided and associated among themselves. These things were said so that it might be established by clear reason that neither a plane without depth, nor a solid <unclear>depth</unclear> without others, can be bound together. And so it clearly stands that from these aforementioned materials, this most beautiful visible machine <note>original: "machinam visibilem"; a common metaphor for the structured universe or cosmos.</note> was fabricated, a friend to its parts by reason of equilibrium, associated with those things which are whole and without any loss. Furthermore, so that it might be a perfect living being <note>original: "animal"; in the Platonic sense of the 'World Soul' or the universe as a living organism.</note> and possessed of eternal safety in its composition. Moreover, He gave to it a fitting form. Indeed, for a living being containing all other living beings within itself, and destined to rule itself, He gave it a spherical shape.

# On the Shape and Soul of the World

And it was made round and spherical, so that from the center it would be equally distant from the edges in every direction. Finally, he polished this globe to be smooth on the outside all around, and for good reason. For it had no need for sight, nor hearing, nor any organ for breathing, nor were any limbs attached to it through which food might flow in or waste flow out. It had no need for hands to grasp, nor feet for movement; for it, no local movement <note>movement from place to place</note> was suitable, but only that rational movement which belongs to the purest things. Therefore, it is carried in a circle, and of necessity it revolves, turning upon its own axis, because its motion is fixed. Since it contains all things within itself, there is no other place for it to go; rather, by a perpetual rotation, it is moved within its own space.

Having thus explained these parts, he placed the **Soul** in the center of it and ordered it to be extended equally through the entire globe; and so he willed the smooth sphere to be turned and moved within its own circumference. The Soul itself is conceived in this way: taking fitting parts from **indivisible** and **divisible** substance, and likewise from the **same** and **diverse** nature, and mixing them together, he blended that nature.

Two from the east and likewise two from the west, of which one in each pair flows back toward the north and the other toward the south. Thus, meeting one another in a certain immense collision, they intermingle; and from this collision of waters, many have estimated the ebbing and flowing of the ocean <note>The author is explaining the tides as the result of these massive oceanic currents crashing into one another at the cardinal points.</note>. When these currents reach our sea—that is, the narrower straits—it must be understood that this occurs from the aforementioned outflows, which the common people are accustomed to call "the ocean." ### THE DIVISION OF THE OCEAN.

The ocean is said to be that which encircles the entire world. It is called the Eastern, Southern, Western, and Northern Ocean. We divide the ocean by different names because all seas proceed from it. Namely: our sea <note>The Mediterranean</note> below Egypt and Syria, which is generally called the "Great Sea"; the Red Sea; the Euxine Sea <note>The Black Sea</note>; the Tyrrhenian Sea; the Persian Gulf; the Arabian Gulf; the Cimmerian Sea; elsewhere the Propontis <note>Sea of Marmara</note>; the Hellespont; the Caspian Sea; the Frozen Sea <note>original: "saucum mare," likely a corruption of "sarmaticum" or "sacrum"</note>; the Aegean Sea; the African Sea; the Macedonian Gulf; the Adriatic Gulf; the Myrtian Sea; the Cretan; the Ionian; the Corinthian; and the Liburnian Gulf.

# CONCERNING THE THREE PARTS OF THE HABITABLE WORLD.

Let us explain how our ancestors divided this part inhabited by us, and what those parts contain. Therefore, they established that the entire circle of the earth, circumscribed by the border of the Ocean, is divided into four <note>The author refers to the four cardinal directions or quarters, though only three continents were known to them</note>; and they called its three parts Asia, Europe, and Africa. However, some have thought there were only two parts—that is, Asia, and then Africa should be included within Europe. Let the figure placed below <note>Referring to the T-O map diagram</note> clear up any obscurity here. We shall follow each of these parts individually.
