# ON THE CORRUPTIONS
## OF PROPHECIES: CHAPTER I.

**Moreover**, those vain oracles of certain fanatics <note>The author uses the term 'fanatici' (fanatics) to describe religious enthusiasts who claimed direct inspiration or secret knowledge outside of established church authority</note> consist almost entirely of **three** main points.

**First**: they claim that the **Antichristian Kingdom** in this world, before the Coming of Christ, must be completely and utterly cut off, so that it no longer exists at all. They say that the time for this destruction has arrived in this very century of ours, in which—once the Antichrist has been totally overthrown—the Gospel must be preached throughout the entire world, and the souls and consciences held under his yoke must be fully restored to liberty. Furthermore, they argue that all the property of the Antichrist, with all his gold, silver, and treasures, ought to be given over to plunder. By this foolish prophecy <term>fatuicinio (a play on 'vaticinio' or prophecy, suggesting it is 'fatuous' or foolish)</term>, it seems to me that they wish to drive and incite men (especially those greedy for rich booty) to cast down all who are devoted to the Popish <note>original: 'Pontificiæ'; referring to the Roman Catholic Church</note> Religion. For it is well known that there is hardly a greater incentive for war among the common people than the certain hope of plunder.

They add **secondly** concerning the **Roman Empire** <note>In this context, the author refers to the Holy Roman Empire, which many 17th-century radicals believed would fall to make way for a thousand-year reign of saints</note>: that its form is not only to be changed, but that it is to be overthrown and a new kingdom and form established. They claim that the era of the Roman Empire is now completed and finished, and therefore nothing but total ruin should be expected before the glorious coming of Christ. From this, it follows that the highest **Magistrates** <term>Magistratus (civil authorities and governing officials)</term> are brought into contempt, public peace falters, and the obedience of subjects due to their lawful magistrates is, among some people, suspended.
