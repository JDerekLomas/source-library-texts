# PREFACE.

I wrote this Treatise during my stay in France twelve years ago; and I communicated it in the year 1678 to the learned persons who then composed the <term>Royal Academy of Sciences</term> <note>The primary scientific institution of France, established by Louis XIV in 1666</note>, to which the King had done me the honor of calling me. Several members of this body, who are still alive, will be able to remember having been present when I gave the reading of it, and better than the others, those among them who applied themselves particularly to the study of <term>Mathematics</term>; of whom I can now only cite the celebrated Gentlemen, <term>Cassini</term> <note>Giovanni Domenico Cassini, known for discovering the division in Saturn's rings</note>, <term>Romer</term> <note>Ole Rømer, the first to measure the speed of light</note>, and <term>De la Hire</term> <note>Philippe de La Hire, a prominent astronomer and mathematician</note>. Considering this, I finally judged that it was better to let this writing appear as it is, than to let it run the risk, by waiting longer, of remaining lost.
