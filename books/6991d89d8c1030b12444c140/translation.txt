# The Map of the Subtle Body: The Seven Chakras

This manuscript page features a detailed illustration of a yogi in a state of deep meditation, serving as a visual manual for understanding the <term>Sukshma Sharira<note>original: "सूक्ष्म शरीर", refers to the "subtle body" or energetic double of the physical form</note></term>. 

The figure is seated within a decorative architectural arch, signifying the sacred nature of the body as a temple. Along the central axis of the spine—the <term>Sushumna Nadi<note>The central channel through which spiritual energy flows</note></term>—are the seven primary energy centers known as <term>Chakras<note>literally "wheels" or "vortices" of energy</note></term>.

### The Celestial Centers
At the very top of the head is the **Sahasrara**, depicted as a thousand-petaled lotus representing pure consciousness. Just below, on the forehead, is the **Ajna** or "Third Eye," the seat of intuition and command.

### The Elemental Centers
Moving down the torso, five additional floral centers are depicted, each containing specific symbolic figures and colors:
*   **The Throat:** Associated with space and purification.
*   **The Heart:** Representing the element of air and the unstruck sound.
*   **The Navel:** The center of fire and transformative heat.
*   **The Sacrum:** Associated with the element of water.
*   **The Base:** The root center, associated with the earth element.

<note>While the original inscriptions within these circles are stylistic representations of seed syllables (bija mantras), they serve as focal points for the practitioner to vibrate specific sounds during meditation.</note>

### Symbolic Guardians and Ritual Objects
Flanking the yogi's torso are a **ritual flame** and a **serpent**. The serpent represents the <term>Kundalini<note>The dormant spiritual energy coiled at the base of the spine, visualized as a snake</note></term>, which the practitioner seeks to awaken and draw upward through the centers.

At the very base of the illumination:
*   A **turtle** and a **fish** are depicted, representing grounding, stability, and the origin of life in the primordial waters.
*   In the bottom corners sit two ritual vessels: a **Kamandalu** <note>an ascetic's water pot</note> and a highly decorated ceremonial jar, symbolizing the preservation of sacred nectar.
