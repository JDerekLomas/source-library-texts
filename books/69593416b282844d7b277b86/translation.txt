# DEDICATORY EPISTLE.

...once brought to light and celebrated characters <note>original: "characteres"; here meaning distinguished qualities or reputations</note> who were by no means different from one another in nature and condition; namely, one of whom rendered that famous and renowned University of Cambridge richer in the instruments of study and science, more ornate with magnificent buildings, and more pleasing and accessible to those eager for learning. Furthermore, he established that magnificent sanctuary of select books, a vast work of architecture destined for the use of a Library—a gift welcome to all the literate, profitable to anyone seeking ample utility, and like sweet milk from the breasts of Minerva <note>The Roman goddess of wisdom and weaving</note>, abundantly pouring forth and liberally showering upon the thirsty. The other offspring or character of your impregnated charity is that excellent Library in the Monastery most famous among Londoners for the monuments and tombs of Kings, called Westminster. It was erected at no small expense to your Dignity and fortified and adorned with every necessary refinement, and especially with rare books of the highest estimation. This library, celebrated and heralded throughout almost all of Britain as much for the beauty of its structure and arrangement as for the excellence of the books contained within it, is accustomed daily to depict and engrave the praises of its illustrious founder and patron upon the eternal monuments of fame. Thus, it is clearly declared by this sweet and voluntary goodness of yours that your Honor and Dignity were born not for yourself alone, but for others, being inclined and devoted to doing good by a natural disposition of the soul. Finally, the latter order and the reason closer to me, by which a cautious respect has induced me and mature consideration has urged me to celebrate your name and honor through this dedication of my works, is that close proximity of our spirits and the firm bond in consanguinity <note>blood relation</note>, by virtue of which bodies otherwise separated are nonetheless joined, bound, and united together by the firmest chains and by a certain magnetic glue of nature, as it were. Therefore, those things which are offered to your Lordship and Dignity with a sincere heart and grateful mind, may you (Reverend Father, a Presiding Officer most worthy of such a place and office) deign for your part to accept with a serene brow and a placid countenance. I implore you to pursue me—who am most devoted to your Lordship in every duty of diligence as well as obedience—with favor and benevolence, while I, for my part, humbly and from the very center of my heart pray to God, the Best and Greatest, that you may live long and happily, be directed by a favorable and propitious divine power in your life, and finally remain forever in the office of holy goodness toward all.

HOW varied and mutually disagreeing are the opinions of philosophers, both ancient and modern, concerning the essence, origin, and effects of meteors; how unstable their assertions are, cannot be hidden from those who have devoted their labor somewhat more accurately to reading the books and volumes of philosophy. The reason for this matter is usually readily apparent to men endowed with sharpness of intellect; for where the foundations of the palace of philosophy are found to be based on fables, there a weak structure—hardly different from the shadows of a Chimera <note>A mythical creature; here meaning a wild, groundless fancy</note>—is to be expected, and consequently, one that is stuffed and swelling with the propped-up wings of doubtful probability. What of indubitable truth is to be drawn from a spurious philosophy, produced by the foolish labors of the Pagans? How great, then, is the madness in us Christians to venerate those pagan authors of philosophy and Medicine like deities? To read their writings with such diligence and confidence? To hold their inventions and arts in such immense esteem? To defend their opinions in public Schools with such obstinacy? Indeed, to cite their authorities so steadfastly and firmly in writings and disputes, as if they had been poured out and distilled onto the earth divinely from the heavens and immediately from GOD himself? Since the sacred writings testify that Christ is the only truth, and all truth of things is from Him—the power and wisdom of GOD teaching all things.
