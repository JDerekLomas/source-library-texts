*   **Grand Square Flag**: A large, square banner representing the central authority of the unit.

## Un-notched Arrow

->**Un-notched Arrow**<-

<note>The **Un-notched Arrow** (original: 無扣箭, *wúkòu jiàn*) is a projectile characterized by the absence of a standard nock (the groove that fits onto a bowstring). In traditional Chinese military contexts, "un-notched" arrows were often designed for use with <term>arrow tubes</term> (筒, *tǒng*) or specialized mechanical launchers. Because the projectile is pushed from the base by a piston or a heavy cord rather than being pulled by a thin string, the complex notched tail was unnecessary, making these arrows faster and cheaper to manufacture for high-volume barrages.</note>

## Buckwheat-Edged Arrow

->**Buckwheat-Edged Arrow**<-

<note>The **Buckwheat-Edged Arrow** (original: 蕎麥稜箭, *qiáomài léng jiàn*) is named for the specific geometry of its <term>arrowhead</term>. Buckwheat seeds are known for their distinctive three-sided, pyramidal shape with sharp longitudinal ridges. By mimicking this "buckwheat" shape, the arrowhead becomes a three-edged armor-piercing tool. This design was significantly more effective than flat blades at puncturing the metal links of chainmail or the toughened leather plates of <term>lamellar armor</term>, as the ridges concentrated the force of impact onto three sharp points that could split the defensive material.</note>

## Half-side Notched Arrow

->**Half-side Notched Arrow**<-

<note>The **Half-side Notched Arrow** (original: 半邊扣箭, *bànbiān kòu jiàn*) is distinguished by the unique construction of its <term>nock</term> (the structural groove at the rear of the arrow that connects to the bowstring). In traditional Chinese archery, most nocks were deep, U-shaped grooves carved into the shaft or an insert. The "half-side" design features a shallower or asymmetrical notch. This was a tactical modification designed for speed; it allowed the archer to "snap" the arrow onto the string more quickly and with less precision than a deep nock required, which was particularly advantageous for rapid-fire volleys or for cavalrymen who needed to reload while in motion.</note>
