### NOTICE.

I offer here to enthusiasts of Historical Research those findings which I have made regarding a period in the **History of the Knights Templar** <note>The Knights Templar were a powerful Catholic military order founded in the 12th century; their sudden downfall and the subsequent trials in the 14th century have long been a subject of historical debate and mystery.</note>, which until now has remained buried in a deep obscurity; not because we have lacked the materials necessary to clarify it, but because the prejudices, or the laziness of historians, have prevented them from making a proper use of them. This is a new proof of the labor that history still requires, and how much its form would change if the documents it provides us were examined with care and brought fully into the light.

I flatter myself that I have clarified this matter, so obscure until now, in a manner suited to making the truth appear distinctly. I know that one could push these investigations even further, and I believe that the way in which I have treated this point of history would be capable of yielding several very interesting results. I leave this task to <term>Scholars</term> <note>original: "Savans." Referring to the "Republic of Letters," the international community of intellectuals and academics during the 17th and 18th centuries.</note> who will have more knowledge and leisure, and as much love for the truth as I.

### NOTICE.

learned member of the Academy of Sciences <note>Likely referring to the Royal Academy of Sciences and Belles-Lettres in Berlin, an institution central to the Enlightenment, of which Nicolai was a prominent member.</note> to which I dedicate this *Essay*, will perhaps take pleasure in continuing the examination of this matter; one has all the more reason to hope for this, as this Academy has particularly dedicated itself to history, and we already have fine monuments <note>In this context, "monuments" refers to the enduring records, published memoirs, and scholarly achievements of the Academy.</note> of its labors.

The subject matter that I treat at the end of this work has only a very distant relationship with my principal object; I nevertheless wished to take advantage of this opportunity to make known—so that they are not lost—certain discoveries that chance led me to make some time ago.

Berlin, this 4th of
March 1782.
