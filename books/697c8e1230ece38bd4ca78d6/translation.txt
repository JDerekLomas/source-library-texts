# Preface to the Impartial Reader.

Favorable Reader / this German discourse, which has been compiled by a **chemist** <note>original: "Chymico"; in 1636, the roles of "chemist" and "alchemist" were not yet distinct.</note> who has been highly practiced for a very long time / and has been observed through manifold **laboring** <note>original: "laboriren"; referring to practical laboratory work and experimentation rather than just theoretical study.</note> / is given and dedicated to you in print for your use and benefit / provided you yourself have a desire for the truth / not so that the God- and truth-loving **Author** might seek any worldly honor or a great name thereby / No / his heart / which has been well known to me for several years now / shows otherwise / nor so that he might bring great gain and wealth to himself through the **publication** of such **secrets** <note>original: "arcano"; refers to hidden alchemical knowledge or "arcana."</note> / which are unjustly composed by almost all the inexperienced and unlearned / but his goal is directed primarily to the glory of God / and / as mentioned above / to the benefit of his neighbor.

Although the said **Author** is unknown in **verbal colleges** <note>original: "Collegiis verbalibus"; a derogatory reference to academic institutions that focused on rhetoric and logic rather than practical science.</note> / and where one only holds much regard for **Aristotelian whimsies** <note>original: "Aristotelischen Grillen"; a common critique by early modern scientists against the traditional philosophical curriculum based on Aristotle, which they viewed as outdated and impractical.</note> / for **it is better to live for God and be unknown to the world,** <note>original: "melius est vivere Deo & ignotum esse mundo." This phrase reflects a common sentiment in mystical and alchemical circles that true wisdom is found in quiet devotion rather than public fame.</note>

<summary>The author introduces a "German discourse" on alchemy, emphasizing that the work is based on years of laboratory experience and intended for the reader's benefit and God's glory rather than personal fame.</summary>
