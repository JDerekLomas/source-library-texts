# <insert>Collected Illustrations of the Three Realms</insert>

<term>Sancai Tuhui<note>Original Chinese: 三才圖會. The "Three Realms" or "Three Powers" (Sancai) refer to the traditional Chinese cosmological triad of Heaven, Earth, and Humanity.</note></term>

This work is a renowned <term>leishu<note>A traditional Chinese encyclopedia format where information is categorized by topic rather than alphabetically.</note></term> from the Ming Dynasty, compiled by the scholar Wang Qi <note>1529–1612</note> and his son Wang Siyi. Published in 1609, it is famous for its extensive use of illustrations to document everything from astronomical phenomena and geographical maps to human portraits and flora and fauna.

The text presented here appears on a vertical title slip <note>known as a "shumu"</note> common on traditional <term>string-bound books<note>A classic East Asian binding style where a paper cover is stitched to the pages using silk or cotton thread.</note></term>. The layout reflects the aesthetic standards of 17th-century Chinese publishing, emphasizing a comprehensive synthesis of knowledge across the three spheres of existence.

<summary>This is the title page for the "Collected Illustrations of the Three Realms," a monumental illustrated encyclopedia from the Ming Dynasty. It highlights the work's intent to catalog all known phenomena in the heavens, on earth, and within human society.</summary>
