<note>The title shown here is for the **Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms** (original: *Haiguo Tuzhi* 海國圖志). Compiled by the scholar <term>Wei Yuan<note>A prominent Qing Dynasty scholar and advisor (1794–1857) who advocated for practical statecraft and modernizing China's defenses.</note></term>, this work was one of the first Chinese publications to provide a detailed and systematic account of the history, geography, and political systems of Western countries.</note>

<term>Maritime Kingdoms<note>original: "Haiguo" 海國</note></term> literally translates to "Sea Kingdoms," referring to the various nations across the oceans, particularly the Western powers like Britain, France, and the United States, which were largely unknown to the general Chinese public at the time.

<term>Illustrated Treatise<note>original: "Tuzhi" 圖志</note></term> indicates a format that blends visual maps and diagrams with historical and descriptive text. Wei Yuan’s goal was to help Chinese officials and intellectuals "learn the superior techniques of the foreigners to control them."

Use one or two small iron chains, each five feet in length, secured with iron rings. One end of the chain should be attached to a large iron nail, seven or eight inches long, with a tip that must be forged to an extreme sharpness. On each boat, place two large iron hammers.

In the inner reaches, a survey of the provincial river <note>The Pearl River</note> reveals that twenty *li* <note>Approximately 10 kilometers</note> to the east, there is a strategic pass called **Liede**.
