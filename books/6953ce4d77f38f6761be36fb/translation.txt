## NOTE ON ILLUSTRATION

This is one of twelve panels from the Triumphal Arch of Marcus Aurelius, erected on the Capitoline Hill in 176 AD in honor of the double conquest of Germanic and Sarmatian nations <note>The Marcomannic Wars (166–180 AD) against tribes along the Danube</note>. It represents two German captives brought in by the Praetorian Guard <note>The elite unit of the Imperial Roman army that served as personal bodyguards to the emperors</note>. They are begging for the Emperor's mercy, which the protective gesture of his hand shows is being granted. The face of Marcus is grave and sad. Behind him appears his Praetorian Prefect <note>The commander of the Praetorian Guard</note>, Bassaeus Rufus. The composition of the work is fine and effective. Three other panels were taken from the Church of Santa Martina on the Capitoline in 1525 and are now preserved on the first landing of the Palace of the Conservators in Rome.
