# ON THE MOVEMENT AND MEASUREMENT OF WATER

<margin>The weight of the water is the cause of its movement.</margin>

When the water is in its natural state, it does not move unless it is provoked by a lack of balance <note>Leonardo uses the concept of "equilibrium" to describe why water remains still or begins to flow</note>. If the surface of the water is perfectly level, it remains at rest; but if one part is lower than another, the water will seek to fill that void, creating a current.

### OF THE EDDIES AND VORTICES

<column-break/>

The water that falls perpendicularly into other water creates a circular motion. This motion is not merely on the surface, but penetrates deep into the body of the pool. 

**Observation:**
Observe the movement of the surface of the water, which resembles that of hair, which has two motions, of which one responds to the weight of the strands, the other to the direction of the curls; thus the water has its eddying motions, one part of which responds to the principal current, the other to the random and reverse motion. <note>Leonardo frequently compared the patterns of nature, such as flowing water and human hair, to find universal laws of physics.</note>

| TYPE OF MOTION | DESCRIPTION OF FORCE |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct** | Following the slope of the riverbed |
| **Reflected** | Bouncing back from an obstacle or bank |
| **Circular** | Created by the meeting of two opposing currents |

<insert>
The air also moves in this way, though it is invisible to the eye.
</insert>

<gloss>Water is the driver of nature.</gloss> <note>original: "vetturale di natura"</note>

### On the Foundations of Buildings

The earth that is most suitable for supporting the weight of a great structure is that which is composed of <term>tufa (tufo)</term> <note>A type of porous rock formed from volcanic ash, common in Italian architecture</note> or of very hard and compact gravel. If one must build upon sandy soil, it is necessary to dig until one finds the solid bed of the earth, or to use <term>piles (pali)</term> <note>Wooden stakes driven into the ground to provide a stable foundation in soft soil</note> made of oak or alder, driven deep and close together.

<margin>Water is the enemy of foundations</margin>

One must observe with great care the movement of the waters in the vicinity of the building. For if a stream or a hidden vein of water should pass beneath the foundations, it will, over time, carry away the finer parts of the earth, leaving the structure to lean or to crack. 

->**Of the Proportions of the Walls**<-

The thickness of the wall at its base should be determined by the height it must reach and the weight of the roof it must sustain. <insert>See the rule of the tenth part</insert> <note>Leonardo often referenced the Vitruvian principle that the thickness of a foundation should be greater than the wall above it</note>.

### Observations on the Flow of Rivers

When a river strikes the bank, it does not remain there, but rebounds like a ball and strikes the opposite bank with equal force. 

| Type of Bank | Effect of Water | Necessary Remedy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sandy | Rapid erosion | Stone reinforcement |
| Clay | Slow softening | Drainage channels |
| Rock | Minimal change | None required |

<note>original: "l'acqua che percuote la ripa"</note>

The water that falls from a height into a basin will dig a deep hole at the point of its impact, and the earth that is removed from that hole will be deposited further down the stream, forming a <unclear>sandbank or shoal</unclear>. This must be considered when building bridges, lest the central <term>piers (pile)</term> be undermined by the very force of the water they are meant to divide.
