### Salutation to the Illustrious Ganesha. Now begin the results of the Sun’s Transit. <note>original: "Śrīgaṇeśāya namaḥ. Atha saṅkrāntiphalāni." *Sankranti* refers to the moment the Sun moves from one zodiac sign to another, an event of great astrological significance in India.</note>

The astrologer Naga <note>original: "Nāga Jyotirvid"</note>, a supreme treasury of knowledge, provides this description of the sevenfold results of the Sun’s transit for the benefit of the virtuous. || 1 ||

When the Sun resides in the sign of the Lion <note>original: "Simha"</note>, there is great destruction. Those dwelling in the mountains, forts, and forest lands face pain and suffering on the battlefield. The rulers of the Himalayan regions <note>original: "Himavat-deshadhipa"</note> face death or the loss of their kingdoms, and other kings experience the symptoms of a fading dawn.

When the Tiger <note>original: "Vyaghra"</note> is the "vehicle" <note>In this branch of astrology, each transit is assigned a "vehicle" or animal mount that dictates the flavor of the prediction.</note> of the transit, there is a fierce and trembling energy. Blood flows like the red water-lily, and many set out on journeys. While some find an abundance of life, joy, victory, and strength, those associated with the Deer <note>original: "Mriga"</note> and other such groups find no such gain. || 2 ||

Time, the lord of the lotus-thicket, determines who shall possess the fierce and difficult-to-attain pearls of success. In an instant, various groups of elephants and horses belonging to the Yadava lineage <note>A reference to a specific historical or mythological dynasty</note> may face their end. Even those who protect the Earth face pain, and if the Sun enters the sign of the Water-Pot <note>original: "Kumbha"; Aquarius</note>, it acts as a cause of distress. Those dwelling in the treasuries and the sons of the elite are cast down in an instant. The people fall into pain, struck by a fainting stupor, even as they are accompanied by brothers and kin in new and various states of movement. || 3 ||

The lord of the lotus-forest, moving like an intoxicated elephant, brings war to the Kalinga region... <note>The text breaks off here; Kalinga refers to the coastal region of modern-day Odisha.</note>

If the Maker of the Day <note>original: "Dinakara"</note> appears here as if holding Bilva leaves <note>leaves of the wood-apple tree, sacred to Shiva</note>, it brings about the death of temple priests <note>original: "Devalaka," those who subsist on temple service</note>.
