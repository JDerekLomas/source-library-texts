—ing a path not attempted by others, which is sometimes so enveloped in the clouds of **antiquity** that in certain matters it does not quite reach the monuments and secrets of our **Ancestors**; in others, it fails to recover the strokes of characters that are worn away and escape the sharpness of the eyes. It is a joy to have advanced this far, even if it is not granted to go further. It will be enough for me to have rescued the remaining trophies of our fathers from destruction and to have left certain matters to be refined by a grateful posterity, so that the evidence of noble deeds which still remain with us do not perish entirely. There will one day be someone who, kindled by love for his country, will add more and not neglect these things. Because of the thorny difficulty of our antiquities, the greater part of our countrymen have been deterred from their duty to the fatherland; despising what is at home, they have devoted themselves to foreign things. But to neglect what is near, to look up to the remote, to adore the external, and to loathe the domestic is not a virtue, but a vice. For this reason, the noble deeds of our ancestors—their rituals, customs, institutions, decrees, victories, triumphs, monuments, and infinite other trophies of **Danish** virtue—lie buried in darkness and are overwhelmed by eternal oblivion. I felt that something had to be attempted, so that I might either stir up noble talents born for our country by my example, or, through this work, pave a smoother path toward our **Antiquities**. Encouraged by the favor and kindness of my superiors, I have already presented preliminary efforts in the <term>Fasti</term><note>original: "Fastis." Since I see that these were kindly received by fair-minded readers, I decided that these <term>specimens</term><note>original: "παραδείγματα" (paradeigmata). In doing so, I aim to ensure that our **Danish Antiquities** receive no less honor than those of other nations, and that they regain their own majesty to be emulated by future generations. 

I humbly ask that you, **Magnificent Chancellors**, do not disdain to look upon these efforts with a kind countenance and to hold them recommended under your protection and patronage. If I wished to list the reasons that impelled me to this task, I would have to compose a lengthy account of your merits across all ranks of dignity—service rendered to the **King**, the **Prince**, the **Fatherland**, the **Church**, and this **Academy**.
