### The Rule for Calculating Sub-periods

> Multiply the duration of the period by itself, and divide that product by the total sum of all periods. The resulting quotient should be known as the **days**. Multiply the remainder by sixty, and divide again by the total sum of all periods; the result is the **time-units** <term>ghatika</term> <note>A traditional unit of time; 60 ghatikas make one full day and night (24 hours).</note> of the sub-period. || 1 ||

#### A Mathematical Example
The meaning of this rule is as follows: 
Suppose the **Major Period** <term>Dasha</term> is 20. 
Multiply the period by itself (20 multiplied by 20) = 400.
Divide this by the **Total Sum of Periods** <term>Dashapinda</term> <note>In this specific calculation, the sum of all planetary influences equals 36.</note>, which is 36.
The quotient obtained is **11 days**. 
The remainder is 4.
Multiply the remainder by sixty = 240.
Divide this by the **Total Sum of Periods** (36) which results in approximately 7. 
Because the difference is small <note>original: alpanantara; referring to rounding up the fractional result</note>, the obtained **time-units** <term>ghatika</term> are **7**.
