# PREFACE
## TO THE READER

<image-desc>Decorative woodcut initial 'A' featuring foliate and floral motifs.</image-desc>

*There was brought to me not long ago, Curious Reader—whether from India, or perhaps drawn from some other unknown storehouse of the world, I know not what title to call this treasure <note>original: "κειμήλιον" (keimelion) — a precious thing or heirloom.</note>—a new and truly exotic portent of nature. Yet what I assert is certain, and has been clearly, indeed many times, proven by the personal observation <note>original: "αὐτοψίᾳ" (autopsia)</note> of most expert and trustworthy <note>original: "ἀξιοπίστων" (axiopiston)</note> men. It is a thing that, in an earthly garment, mimics a truly heavenly nature: in its little body, it expresses the archetype <note>original: "ἀρχέτυπον" (archetypon) — the original model or pattern.</note> of the whole world, engraved by the singular industry of wise nature. At first glance, the thing indeed appears quite cheap and despicable; however, he alone who has weighed its inner parts more deeply will find that nothing worthier of admiration has been produced in the majesty of all nature. It is of such value that it can in no way be compared to gold, silver, or gems, being as it is the only way, guide, and key to the treasures of the whole world; and although it is devoid of all motion, as well as a soul, yet nothing is more mobile than this motionless thing; nothing more lively than this life-less thing.*

*It consists of certain arms so thin that no sight can be so lynx-eyed <note>A common 17th-century reference to the "Lynceus," a mythological figure or animal noted for superhumanly sharp sight.</note> as to reach them; yet they are so powerful that by their extension it seems to embrace not only the sky but all the climates of the world. Nor is any nation so barbarian or remote that it has not subdued and brought it under its yoke. Furthermore, it is endowed with such subtlety that no enclosure is so fortified, no prison so shut, that it does not penetrate it in a moment, with no one existing who can elude its force. It loves and hates one and the same thing at the same time so stubbornly that it seems it cannot be separated from the loved one by any arts, nor can it be joined to the same because of a certain natural contrariety and repugnance.
