## Paracelsian Writings.

1. The Golden Fleece. <note>A common alchemical title referring to the quest for the Philosopher's Stone.</note>
2. Elixir and Tincture; cures leprosy, gout, dropsy, paralysis, and other bodily contagia. <note>original Latin: "curiat lepram, podagram, hydropisin, paralisin & alia corporis contagia"</note>
3. Paracelsian Tinctures.
	1. The manner of sowing the seed into the earth. <note>An allegorical description of placing alchemical matter into a medium for growth or fermentation.</note>
	2. Coagulation of Mercury <note>symbol: ☿</note> into Gold <note>symbol: ☉</note> through the salts of metals.
	3. Fixation through the morning star. <note>The "Morning Star" often refers to the metal Antimony or the planet Venus.</note>
	4. Tincture of Mercury <note>symbol: ☿</note> into Gold <note>symbol: ☉</note>.
	5. Of the Green Lion. <term>Green Lion: a vital alchemical symbol often representing "raw" matter, such as vitriol or unrefined antimony, capable of dissolving gold.</term>
	6. Augmentation <term>Augmentation: the process of increasing the quantity of a metal or the potency of a tincture.</term> with the Mercury of Lead <note>symbol: ♄</note> by Johannes Trithemius. <note>Trithemius (1462–1516) was a famous abbot, cryptographer, and teacher of Paracelsus.</note>
	7. Precipitated Mercury <note>symbol: ☿</note> through the Oil of Vitriol. <note>Oil of Vitriol is a historical name for concentrated sulfuric acid.</note>
	8. Fixative water. <note>original: "Aqua fixatiua"; a solvent used to make volatile substances stable under heat.</note>
	9. Fixation of Lead <note>symbol: ♄</note> into Gold <note>symbol: ☉</note>.
4. The Testament of Theophrastus. <note>Refers to Theophrastus von Hohenheim, better known as Paracelsus.</note>
