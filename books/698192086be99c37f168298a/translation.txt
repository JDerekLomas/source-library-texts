### *To the Sincere Reader.*
<note>original: "Candido Lectori." A common Latin greeting to a fair-minded or "bright" reader.</note>

I must entreat you, kind Reader, to grant some indulgence to my affection in these *Memorials* <note>original: "Memorialibus." These are the commemorative essays and funeral orations listed in the contents.</note>. For I am well aware that I was gripped by either love or grief when I wrote these things: nor am I so made of iron that I feel no leaning toward my benefactors, or look upon public disaster <note>original: "cladem." This likely refers to the "Fire of Vaihingen" mentioned on the previous page, which devastated Andreae's parish.</note> unmoved—something, however, which a person who does not know these friends of mine, or these works of mine, might easily do. Yet I would dare to profess that no injury has been done to the truth <note>original: "veritati." Andreae is defending his objectivity; though he feels deep emotion, he claims the historical facts remain accurate.</note>, and I can promise many witnesses. For what benefit to others these works should see the light of day <note>A common idiom for being published.</note>, it is not clear; I certainly, with my well-wishers...

<summary>In this preface, Johann Valentin Andreae asks the reader for indulgence, explaining that his writings are born of deep personal grief and affection for his patrons and his community, yet he maintains they are truthful accounts.</summary>
