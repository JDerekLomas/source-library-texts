## A Declaration of egregious Popish Impostures
## &c. by Samuel Harsnet, successively
## Bishop of Chichester and Norwich,
## and Archbishop of york.

->Besides the curiosity and<-
rarity of this work, it is doubly in-
teristing from the use which Shakspeare
has made of it in King Lear, and
that from it we are enabled to fix
with tolerable accuracy the chronology
of that play. See King Lear, act III.
scenes 4 & 6. and notes. See also
Boswell's Edit. of **Shakspeare**, vol. 11.
p. 404, &c. On the subject of **Shakspeare's**
**religion**, consult also the annexed
note, communicated by Mr. Thomas
Rodd, bookseller.

Sold, Roxburghe, no 272, 8s. Worth,
pt 3, no 691. 14s. The present copy,
which is a fine one, cost me £ 1. 5s.

B.

This volume is of considerable rarity and
curiosity. It furnished Shakespeare
with the names of some of his
Spirits Mahu, Flibbertigibbet &c those
who vexed poor Tom (see Lear III, 4.)
& is of importance as settling the con-
troverted point of his religion indeed.
It is conclusive as to his being a Protestant,
since no Catholic would have alluded to
an imposture calculated to bring odium and
ridicule upon his religion

<margin>Note of Mr. Thomas Rodd.</margin>
