A full-page woodcut illustration depicting the fabulist Aesop. He is portrayed as an elderly man with a pronounced hunchback, large facial features, and disheveled hair, following the traditional medieval and Renaissance iconographic description of his appearance. He wears a simple, knee-length tunic with a belt and is barefoot.

Aesop is surrounded by numerous small vignettes and symbolic objects that represent his various fables. These include:
- A bed and a figure resting at the top left.
- A fortified tower and a bird (possibly an eagle).
- Various animals including a dog, a sheep, and several birds.
- A human skull.
- Objects such as a basket of bread, a bundle of grain, a ring, a bone, and a pitcher.
- A man seated at a desk writing or reading in the lower left.

At the top center of the image, the name "ESOPVS" is printed, with the initial letter "E" appearing in reverse (a common occurrence in early woodblock printing). The entire illustration is enclosed in a simple rectangular border.
