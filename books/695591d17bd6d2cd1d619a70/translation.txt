# Shelach
<note>The Torah portion "Shelach Lecha" (Numbers 13:1–15:41), which tells the story of the twelve spies sent to scout the Land of Canaan.</note>

"Send for yourself men, that they may scout the land of Canaan, which I give..." (Numbers 13:2). Behold, one must examine this closely, for it is known that the commandments of the Torah are truth and justice for every living being. Yet Rashi <note>The primary medieval French commentator</note>, of blessed memory, wrote as follows: "'Send for yourself'—according to your own judgment; I do not command you; if you wish, send." Is it conceivable that the Blessed Name would say to him regarding a matter from which evil would result, "If you wish, send"? For the Blessed Name desires only good in His world!

Rather, the simple excuse <note>or "resolution"</note> is that in truth, their souls were very great, even above the level of Moses our Teacher, peace be upon him. This is the intent of Rashi's specific wording, "according to your judgment"—meaning that all their knowledge, wisdom, and attributes were above your own judgment and intellect. Therefore, the Holy One, Blessed be He, said, "Send for yourself according to your judgment," for if *He* had sent them, an immeasurably great evil would have resulted. However, Moses our Teacher, peace be upon him, saw in his prophecy that among these men were wicked individuals, etc. For this reason, he did not want to send them until he consulted with the Divine Presence <note>original: "Shekhinah"</note>, and the Holy One, Blessed be He, said to him, "Send for yourself according to your judgment."

Rashi, of blessed memory, explained: "According to your judgment," etc. We must understand what Rashi's intention was here. Why would it matter to the Holy One, Blessed be He, whether he sends them or not? Just because the people requested it, was Moses forced to send them? Could the Holy One, Blessed be He, not have prevented this thing from them? Rather, certainly the intention is as written in the holy book <term>Or HaChayim</term> <note>A famous 18th-century Torah commentary by Rabbi Chaim ibn Attar</note>: "And Moses sent them..." It is proper to understand how we find, etc. The matter is that the souls of these spies were very, very high in their level; only later did they become corrupted through pride, etc. This is why the Holy One, Blessed be He, said to him, "Send for yourself," for in truth there was a great good in this. And even though there was great suffering for Israel, nevertheless, it was all for the good, as it is written. And with this, it will be understood...
