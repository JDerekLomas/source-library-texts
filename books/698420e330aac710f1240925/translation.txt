That Orpheus, and the Theban <note>Amphion</note>, and the Methymnaean <note>Arion</note>, were certain men—or rather, not men at all, but deceivers. Under the pretext of music, they brought ruin to human life. Through certain artful illusions, they demonically incited men to corruption, celebrating shameful acts in their rites <note>original: "orgiis"</note>, and turning mourning into songs for the Gods. They were the first to lead men toward idols; indeed, they used stones and wood—that is, images and sketches—to rouse the savage customs of the people. Through their chants and poems, they reduced that truly beautiful freedom of those who lived under heaven to the lowliest slavery. But my singer is not like that; he came shortly after to dissolve the bitter slavery of the demons who held tyrannical power. Leading us toward the sweet and kind yoke of religion and piety toward God, he calls back to heaven those who had been cast down to the earth. Surely he alone, out of all who have ever existed, has tamed those most difficult of wild beasts: human beings. The "birds" he tamed were the flighty among them; the "serpents" were the deceivers; the "lions" were the high-spirited and those easily moved to anger; the "swine" were those devoted to pleasure; and the "wolves" were the rapacious. As for the "stones and wood," these are the foolish. Furthermore, a person steeped in ignorance is even more senseless than a stone. Let the prophetic voice come forward as our witness, harmonizing with the truth, which takes pity on those who are worn down in ignorance and madness.

He has adorned this entire universe for you in a rhythmic and fitting way, and He reduced the discord of the elements into an orderly consonance, so that the whole world might become a harmony for Him.
