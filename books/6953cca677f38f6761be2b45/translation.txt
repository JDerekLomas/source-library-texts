<note>This chapter contains the secret names of the guardians of the seven gated mansions of the underworld. To pass, the deceased must address each guardian by their secret name, proving they have the divine knowledge required for eternal life.</note>

->SECOND MANSION<-
<note>original: "Ārit." Hieroglyphic columns 1-5.</note>

In the second hall, the gateway is guarded by three formidable beings. To enter, Ani must acknowledge:
1.  **The Doorkeeper**: "He who opens his face." <note>original: "Un-hat." Likely a reference to the guardian’s vigilance.</note>
2.  **The Watchman**: "He who looks behind him." <note>original: "Seqed-her." A guardian who sees in all directions.</note>
3.  **The Herald**: "He of the loud voice." <note>original: "Kan-uti."</note>

->THIRD MANSION<-
<note>original: "Ārit." Hieroglyphic columns 1-8.</note>

The challenges intensify at the third gate. Ani must correctly identify these guardians:
1.  **The Doorkeeper**: "Eater of the foulness of his own hind-parts." <note>A terrifying image meant to represent a self-sustaining, chaotic force that must be tamed by the soul's words.</note>
2.  **The Watchman**: "The vigilant one."
3.  **The Herald**: "The proclaimer."

->FOURTH MANSION<-
<note>original: "Ārit." Hieroglyphic columns 1-9.</note>

At the fourth gate, the guardians are:
1.  **The Doorkeeper**: "He who repels the weak."
2.  **The Watchman**: "He who is alert of face."
3.  **The Herald**: "He who drives back the attacker."

Ani is shown approaching these gates with his hands raised in a gesture of adoration, his wife **Thuthu** following closely behind him. They carry offerings and recite the prayers inscribed in the columns above them.

### CHAPTER 146: THE PYLONS OF THE HOUSE OF OSIRIS

<note>While the mansions (Ārit) represent rooms or stages, these "Pylons" represent the massive, fortified gateways of the divine palace. Each Pylon is a goddess in her own right, possessing a name and a specific guardian.</note>

->THE THIRD PYLON<-
<note>Hieroglyphic columns 1-4</note>
The third gateway is named **"Mistress of the Altar-Slabs."** It is guarded by a green-skinned deity who watches over the offerings made to the gods.

->THE FOURTH PYLON<-
<note>Hieroglyphic columns 1-6</note>
The fourth gateway is named **"She who prevails over the Two Lands."** <note>The "Two Lands" refers to Upper and Lower Egypt.</note> Its guardian is depicted with the head of a jackal, reminiscent of Anubis, the god of embalming.

->THE FIFTH PYLON<-
<note>Hieroglyphic columns 1-6</note>
The fifth gateway is named **"Mistress of the Heat."** This pylon is guarded by a hawk-headed deity, representing the scorching power of the sun and the protective fire that consumes the enemies of Osiris.

->THE SIXTH PYLON<-
<note>Hieroglyphic columns 1-6</note>
The sixth gateway is named **"Lady of Light."** Within this portal sits a human-headed guardian holding a ceremonial sash. This gateway represents the illumination that the soul receives as it draws closer to the presence of the Great God.
