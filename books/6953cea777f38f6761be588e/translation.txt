# PREFACE

THE Greek text of the *Statesman* and the *Philebus* is based upon the Clark Manuscript (B) and the Venice Manuscript (T). Deviations from the text as given in one or other of these <term>manuscripts</term> <note>original: "MSS."</note> are noted in the margin at the foot of the page. In most instances disagreement between these two <term>manuscripts</term> and occasionally readings found in inferior <term>manuscripts</term> or in ancient quotations, as well as emendations offered by modern scholars, are noted, even when they have not affected the text chosen. The following abbreviations are employed:—

B = Clark or Bodleian Manuscript, written <term>in the year of our Lord</term> <note>original: "A.D."</note> 895.
T = Venice Manuscript, Appendix class 4, codex 1; twelfth century.
W = Vienna Manuscript 54, Greek Supplement 7.
D = Venice Manuscript 185.
G = Venice Manuscript, Appendix class 4, codex 54.
btw = later hands of the B, T, and W manuscripts.

# PREFACE

The brief introductions aim merely at supplying such information as may in some measure aid the reader to appreciate these particular dialogues.

HAROLD N. FOWLER.

The text here given of the *Ion* <note>A dialogue by Plato concerning poetic inspiration and the skill of the rhapsode (a professional performer of epic poetry).</note> is based on the recension <term>recension: a critical revision of a text based on a careful study of sources</term> of Schanz <note>Martin von Schanz (1842–1914), a German classical scholar known for his editions of Plato.</note>. Two modern corrections are adopted and noted at 533 D and 539 E <note>These numbers refer to "Stephanus pagination," the standard system used to cite the works of Plato.</note>. The introduction and notes are intended to give only immediate help in understanding the dialogue.

W. R. M. LAMB.
