The goal of this modest book is to provide a clear and accessible account in English of the life and writings of an extraordinary Flemish mystic <note>A mystic is someone who seeks a direct, personal experience or union with the Divine through prayer and contemplation.</note> from the fourteenth century, who was a contemporary of our own Walter Hilton <note>Walter Hilton (c. 1340–1396) was an influential English Augustinian mystic and the author of *The Scale of Perfection*.</note>. ### EARLY YEARS AND EDUCATION

Blessed John Ruysbroeck, known as "the Admirable" and "the Divine Doctor," is widely considered the greatest mystic the Low Countries <note>The "Low Countries" historically refers to the coastal region in northwestern Europe, including modern-day Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg.</note> have ever produced. He was born in 1293 in Ruysbroeck, a village several miles south of Brussels, located between that city and Hal. 

Following the custom of the time, particularly among members of religious orders <note>original: "Religious"</note>, he was named after his birthplace: John van Ruysbroeck, or simply John Ruysbroeck. The Venerable à Kempis—the Latinized form of van Kempen—is a similar example; Thomas was named after his hometown of Kempen, even though his patronymic <note>The text cuts off at "patro-", which is clearly the start of "patronymic," a name derived from one's father.</note>

### Early Years and Education

He also was eager and dissatisfied; but the larger world for which he sighed was to be found along the unfamiliar paths leading to the sublime heights and the refined <note>original: "rarified"</note> atmosphere of mystical contemplation.

The pious runaway made his way to Brussels to visit an uncle of his, one John Hinckaert, a senior Canon <note>A Canon is a member of the clergy who belongs to the staff of a cathedral or collegiate church.</note> of St. Gudule’s. However, one day divine grace reached him while he was listening to a sermon, drawing him gently and strongly toward a life of extreme simplicity and self-denial <note>original: "mortification"</note>. His example was soon followed by a fellow Canon named Francis van Coudenberg, a Master of Arts who possessed considerable wealth and was a man of great reputation among the people.
