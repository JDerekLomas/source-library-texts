->HERMETIC PHILOSOPHY<-
<note>original Latin: "PHILOSOPHIA HERMETICA." Hermetic philosophy is the study of the "Great Work" of alchemy, astrology, and spiritual magic, attributed to the legendary figure Hermes Trismegistus. In this context, it represents the pursuit of the divine hidden within nature.</note>

<note>The emblem described here—a pelican feeding its young with its own blood—is known as the "Pelican in her Piety." In alchemical thought, this represents the "multiplication" stage of the work, where the substance is nourished and strengthened. The four roses below suggest the four elements (Earth, Air, Fire, and Water) brought into harmony, a hallmark of Rosicrucian influence.</note>

<term>Hermetic Philosophy</term> <note>A tradition of ancient wisdom that seeks to understand the relationship between the human soul (the microcosm) and the universe (the macrocosm).</note>
<term>Pelican in her Piety</term> <note>A traditional Christian symbol of sacrifice adopted by alchemists to represent the "feeding" of the alchemical stone with the "blood" of the mercury.</note>
<term>alchemy</term> <note>The medieval forerunner of chemistry, focused on the transmutation of matter and the attainment of spiritual perfection.</note>
<term>roses</term> <note>Symbolic of the unfolding of the soul and the completion of the alchemical work.</note>
