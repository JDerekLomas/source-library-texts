## To the Reader.

Reader,

Take notice, that in this edition, published September 5th, 1653, I have made very many additions to every sheet in the book. Also, be aware that those books of mine printed with the small typeface used for Bibles are very falsely printed; there are usually twenty or thirty gross mistakes in every sheet. Many of these errors are exceedingly dangerous to those who shall venture to use them. Therefore, I do hereby warn you of them; I can do no more at present. Only take notice of these three directions by which you shall be sure to know the true edition from the false.

**The first Direction:** The true edition has this title at the top of every page in the book: [*The English Physician Enlarged*]. The small counterfeit one has only this title: [*The English Physician*].

**The second Direction:** The true edition has these words, [**Government and Virtues**] <note>original: "Government" refers to the astrological ruler of the plant</note>, following the description of the time the plants flower, etc. The counterfeit small one has these words, [**Virtues and Use**], following the time of the plant's flowering.

**The third Direction:** The true edition is in Octavo <note>a book size resulting from folding a sheet into eight leaves</note>, with a larger typeface than the counterfeit one, which is in Twelves <note>Duodecimo; a smaller book size where the sheet is folded into twelve leaves</note>, using the small typeface typically used for Bibles. I shall now speak something regarding the book itself.

All other authors who have written of the nature of herbs gave not a bit of a reason why a specific herb was assigned to a specific part of the body, nor why it cured a specific disease. Truly, my own body being sickly made it easy for me to understand that health was the greatest of all earthly blessings; and truly, he was never sick who does not believe it. Then I considered that all medicines were compounded of herbs, roots, flowers, seeds, etc. This first set me to work studying the nature of simples <note>original: "Simples" are medicinal plants or herbs used on their own, rather than in a compound mixture</note>, most of which I knew by sight before. Indeed, all the authors I could read gave me but little satisfaction in this particular, or none at all. 

I cannot build my faith upon authors' words, nor believe a thing just because they say it. I could wish everyone were of my mind in this: to labor to be able to give a reason for everything they say or do. They say reason makes a man differ from a beast; if that be true, pray, what are they who, instead of providing a reason for their judgment, quote old authors? Perhaps their authors knew a reason for what they wrote, perhaps they did not—what is that to us? Do we know it? 

Truly, in writing this work, first to satisfy myself, I drew out all the virtues of common herbs, plants, and trees, etc., from the best or most approved authors I had or could get. Having done so, I set myself to study the reason for them. I knew well enough that the whole world, and everything in it, was formed of a composition of contrary elements, and in such a harmony as must surely show the wisdom and power of a great God. I knew just as well that this creation, though thus composed of contraries, was one united...

# TO THE READER.

*...united Body, and* **Man** *an epitome of it; I knew those various affections in* **Man** *in respect of* **Sickness** *and* **Health** *were caused* **Naturally** *(though* **God** *may have other ends best known to himself) by the various operations of the* **Microcosm** <note>original: "Microcosm"; the idea that a human being is a "little world" reflecting the entire universe.</note>; *and I could not be ignorant, that as the Cause is, so must the Cure be. Therefore, he that would know the reason of the operation of Herbs must look up as high as the* **Stars**. *I always found the* **Disease** *vary according to the various motion of the* **Stars**; *and this is enough, one would think, to teach a* **Man** *by the* **Effect** *where the* **Cause** *lay. Then, to find out the reason of the operation of Herbs, Plants, etc., by the* **Stars** *went I; and herein I could find but few* **Authors**, *and those as full of nonsense and contradictions as an egg is full of meat. This being little pleasing and less profitable to me, I consulted with my two brothers,* **Dr. Reason** *and* **Dr. Experience**, *and took a* **Voyage** *to visit my* **Mother Nature**. *By her advice, together with the help of* **Dr. Diligence**, *I at last obtained my desires; and being warned by* **Mr. Honesty**—*a stranger in our days—to publish it to the world, I have done it.*

*But you will say, "Why did I need to have written on this subject, seeing so many famous and learned men have written so much of it in the* **English Tongue** <note>The English language.</note>, *nay, much more than I have done?"*

*To this I* **Answer**:

1. *All those who have written of Herbs, either in the* **English** *or not in the* **English Tongue**, *have in no way answered my intentions in this book. For they have intermixed many—nay, very many—foreign herbs, and many which are hard—nay, impossible—to be obtained; and I am very sensible of what harm this may do. Once a student in* **Medicine** <note>original: "Physick"</note> *in* **Sussex** *sent up to* **London** *to me to buy certain medicines for him and send them down. When I viewed the list, they were medicines quoted by* **Authors** *living in another nation and were not to be had in* **London** *for love nor money. So the poor man had spent much effort and brainpower studying medicines for a disease that were not to be had. Likewise, a man reading* **Gerard's** *or* **Parkinson's Herbal** <note>John Gerard (1545–1612) and John Parkinson (1567–1650) were the authors of the most famous English herbals of the time.</note> *for the cure of a disease may, as likely as not, happen upon an herb that is not to be had here, or not without great difficulty, if at all. But in my book, all the herbs grow near him.*

2. *My last, though not the least of my reasons, is that neither* **Gerard** *nor* **Parkinson**, *nor any who ever wrote in a similar manner, ever gave one wise* **Reason** *for what they wrote. Thus, they did nothing else but train up young novices in* **Medicine** *in the* **School of Tradition**, *teaching them just as a* **Parrot** *is taught to speak: "An author says so, therefore it is true." And if all that* **Authors** *say is true, why do they contradict one another? But in my book, if you view it with the* **Eye of Reason**, *you shall see a* **Reason** *for everything that is written. By this, you may find the very ground and foundation of* **Medicine**; *you may know what you do and why you do it. This work shall call me* **Father**, *as it has (to my knowledge) never been done in the world before.*

*I have now but two things more to write, and then I am finished:*
1. *What the profit and benefit of this work is.*
2. *Instructions in the use of it.*

# TO THE READER

The reasons that draw man to this study are many—so many that should I sum up all the particulars, this *Epistle* <note>original: "Epistle"; here meaning the introductory letter or preface</note> would be as large as the *Book* itself. I shall only quote a few general headings.

First, the admirable harmony of the creation is seen herein: in the influence of stars upon herbs and the body of man; how one part of the creation is subservient to another, and all for the use of man. By this, the infinite power and wisdom of God in the creation appears. If I do not marvel at the simplicity of the *Ranters* <note>A radical 17th-century religious sect often accused of pantheism or denying a creator God</note>, never trust me. Who, when viewing the creation, can hold such a foolish opinion as to think it existed from eternity, when its mysteries are so clear to every eye? But that *Scripture* shall be verified to them, *Romans 1:20*: "For the invisible things of Him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even His eternal power and Godhead, so that they are without excuse." *And a poet could teach them a better lesson:*

->*Lest the Author of the Divine gift should slip from your mind,*<-
->*Every herb reveals the present God.*<-
<note>original Latin: "Excideret ne tibi Divini muneris Author / Præsentem monstrat, quælibet Herba Deum."</note>

->*Because out of your thoughts* God *should not pass,*<-
->*His* image *stamped is on every* grass.<-

*This indeed is true:* God *has stamped his* image *upon every* creature, *and therefore the abuse of the* creature *is a great sin. But how much more does the* wisdom *and* excellency *of* God *appear if we consider the* harmony *of the* creation *in the* virtue *and* operation *of every* herb? *This is the first reason.*

*Secondly, by this you may know what infinite knowledge* Adam *had in his* innocence <note>original: "Innocency"; referring to the state of man before the Fall in Eden</note>, *that by looking upon a* creature, *he was able to give it a name according to its* nature. *By knowing that, you may know how great your* fall *was, and be humbled for it even in this respect: because by it you are now so ignorant.*

*Thirdly, here is the right way for you to begin the study of* medicine <note>original: "Physick"</note> *if you intend to begin at the right end, for here you have the reason of the whole* art. *I wrote before in certain* astrological lectures *which I read and printed, entitled* Semeiotica Uranica <note>A Greek-titled work by Culpeper meaning "Uranic Signs" or "Astrological Judgment of Diseases"</note>, *explaining what* planet *caused (as a secondary cause) every* disease, *and how it might be found out which* planet *caused it. # TO THE READER

*By this method, you may strengthen a specific part of the body with its corresponding element; for example, the brain by herbs of* Mercury, *the breast and liver by herbs of* Jupiter, *the heart and vital organs by herbs of the* Sun, *and so forth.*

*Fifthly, you may oppose diseases by using herbs of the planet opposite to the planet that causes the ailment: such as treating diseases of* Jupiter *with herbs of* Mercury, *and vice versa; diseases of the Luminaries <note>The Sun and the Moon</note> by herbs of* Saturn, *and vice versa; and diseases of* Mars *by herbs of* Venus, *and vice versa.*

*Sixthly, there is a way to cure diseases sometimes by sympathy, where every planet cures its own disease. For instance, the* Sun *and* Moon *cure the eyes with their herbs,* Saturn *cures the spleen,* Jupiter *the liver,* Mars *the gallbladder and diseases of choler <note>original: "choller"; one of the four bodily humors, associated with anger and digestive bile</note>, and* Venus *cures diseases in the reproductive organs.*

*Seventhly, there was a small treatise of mine regarding* Human Virtues <note>original: "Humane Vertues"</note>, *printed at the end of my* Ephemeris <note>An almanac or table of planetary positions</note> *for the year 1651. I believe it would greatly benefit young students to study that alongside this book.*

*Eighthly, young students would help themselves and benefit exceedingly in the study of* Medicine <note>original: "Physick"</note> *if they would take the pains to examine the virtues of the herbs in this book and compare them to these rules. They shall find, to their great satisfaction, that they are all in agreement; thereby, they shall see the reason why a specific herb leads to the cure of a specific disease.*

*Ninthly, I gave you the key to all of this in the entry for the herb* Wormwood. *If anyone thinks that key does not fit the lock because of the complexity of the language there, I have provided it to you again—plainly and without distraction—under another herb of the same planet in this book.*

*That herb is* Carduus Benedictus <note>The Blessed Thistle</note>, *which is located in alphabetical order in the book; I refer you to that place.*

*If you choose to use these rules and apply them to the entry for* Carduus *in this book, you shall find them true throughout the entire work. By paying attention to them, you may be able to provide a reasoned explanation for your judgment to anyone who asks you. I assure you it gave much satisfaction to me, and I wrote it for your benefit. But I must conclude, as my* Letter <note>original: "Epistle"</note> *has already exceeded its* limits. *By this, you see what* logic *can be provided for* medicines, *and the necessity for every* physician *to be an* astrologer. *I suppose you have heard this before, but now you know it. What remains but for you to labor to glorify* God *in your various positions? Do good to yourselves first by increasing your knowledge, and to your* neighbors *afterward by helping their illnesses. I hope this* nation *is worthy of such people, and to such people I shall remain a* friend. *When* publishers <note>original: "Purchaser"</note> *can enjoy their rightful* property *in their* copyrights <note>original: "Copies"</note> *without fear of* thieving rogues, *I shall not fail to produce many more* books *for the* common good *in the* English language *for the benefit of all my* countrymen, *poor or rich.*

***

Spitalfields, next door
to the Red Lion.
September 5, 1653.

->**Nicholas Culpeper.**<-
