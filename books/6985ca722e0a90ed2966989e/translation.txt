### To the Very Learned
## EVERHARDUS SCHUTTENIUS, <note>Latin: "EVERHARDO SCHUTTENIO"</note>
### In honor.

He who does not know God rightly, cannot honor Him rightly,
Who also knows neither himself, nor his own nature.
He does not turn his inner spirit toward any grace,
And alas! day after day causes his sins to increase.

[He] who wishes to turn everyone diligently to this <term>Exercise</term> <note>A reference to the book's title, "The Practice [Exercise] of Piety."</note>,
A holy and very godly gift,
Now brought into print by EVERHARDUS:
Here one can learn to know God and also ourselves.

Christ-loving [reader], this book, this precious pledge,
Accept then with delight, and read with understanding;
You shall find your heart, mind, and spirit to be at rest.

This Schuttenius has felt; therefore he places it into our

# Notice to the Reader. <note>original: "Waerſchouwinge" — in 17th-century Dutch, this term functioned as a "notice" or "admonition" to set the reader's expectations.</note>

Should anyone be pleased to compare this edition of ours with the English, they will find that, just as we have sought to stay closer to the original in various passages, we also depart further from it in many others. Indeed, by leaving out or adding certain words or phrases, we seek to help or improve the sense and the expression of the Author; or at least to express it more clearly and plainly in our own language. In the beginning of this work, I had not intended to pay such close attention to every detail; however, as the work progressed, I found it necessary for the better edification of our countrymen to take the liberty of changing not only the style of the words, but also the subject matter itself in many passages. This was done especially so that I might omit many <term>annotations</term> <note>Schuttenius suggests that by weaving explanations directly into the text, he could avoid cluttering the margins with scholarly notes.</note> which I otherwise would have had to include as necessary observations and warnings. We have done our best to build the **Practice of Piety** <note>Dutch: "Practijcke der Godtſaligheyt." This was the most famous work of Lewis Bayly, a staple of Puritan devotional literature.</note> upon the firm foundations of a **Scriptural Theology**, and not upon any loose or uncertain concepts.

->THE END.<-

<summary>In this preface, the translator Schuttenius explains his "free" translation style, admitting to altering the original English text to better suit the spiritual needs and language of his Dutch readers.</summary>

## To the Christian Reader.

<image-desc>A large decorative woodcut initial 'D' containing an illustration of a landscape with trees and a building in the background.</image-desc> Since the Printer had requested that in this edition the Texts of Holy Scripture should be set according to the <term>new translation</term> <note>This refers to the *Statenvertaling* (States Translation), the first official Dutch Bible translation from the original languages, completed in 1637, just five years before this printing.</note>; I did not find it inadvisable that, at the same time, the translation should be compared anew with the English here and there by one of our Students, who is well-versed in the English language. For the greater clarity of the <term>simple</term> <note>original: "eenvoudigen" — refers to the common laypeople or those without formal theological training.</note>, he has slightly softened the phrasing and the word order, which previously followed the English manner and idioms quite closely. I have also cursorily overseen this myself, and furthermore, I have occasionally changed or omitted a small word in the Author’s text; I have also added <margin>marginal notes</margin> to certain passages for better understanding, observation, and sometimes as a warning to the simple-hearted. I did not wish to <term>over-refine</term> <note>original: "subtilisere" — to engage in overly subtle or academic hair-splitting.</note> everything, but only touched upon some passages with a brief word where it was necessary. The citations of the [scriptural] texts placed in the margin have also been changed here and there: though I have not had the time to oversee all of them and sift through them with the greatest precision. I trust that this labor of mine shall not be in vain: remain herewith commended to God and the word of His grace. At Utrecht, this **1st of February, 1642.**

->*Your ever-serviceable*<-
->*friend and brother,*<-
