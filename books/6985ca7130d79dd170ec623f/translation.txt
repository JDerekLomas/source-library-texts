## SECOND TREATISE,
### Concerning the
# PRETERNATU-
# RAL HISTORY OF
# BOTH WORLDS. <note>original: "Utriusque Mundi." Fludd’s central philosophy was that the universe (Macrocosm) and the human being (Microcosm) mirror each other in every detail.</note>

->*Divided into three Sections,*<-

In Which
> The First deals with the causes of Meteors <note>In the 17th century, "meteors" referred to any atmospheric phenomena, including rain, wind, and lightning, as well as their biological equivalents within the human body.</note>, both Macrocosmic and Microcosmic, and their effects in general.
> The Second treats the particular impressions of Meteors, regarding both favorable and adverse health; and the signs that foretell them in the past, present, and future.
> The Third explains with great precision the method for averting the worst and most unhealthy future outcomes of Meteors, for overcoming their current attacks, and finally for restoring former health that has been lost.

# History of the Preternatural Microcosm

<note>The "Preternatural Microcosm" refers to the human body when it is in an abnormal or diseased state, or influenced by forces beyond the ordinary course of nature.</note>

The second Treatise of the Microcosm <note>The "Little World," a common Renaissance concept where the human body reflects the structure of the universe</note>, which concerns the art of Medicine, is divided into 3 Sections, of which:

- **The First treats of the**
    - { Theosophical <note>Divine wisdom and its relation to the body</note>,
    - { Cabalistic <note>Mystical traditions, often involving the Hebrew alphabet, applied to healing</note>,
    - { Physiological,
    - **Portions of Medicine, which we have treated extensively in this present Volume.**

- **The Second is divided into three portions; of which**
    <margin>All of these are reduced into a single Section, occupying the space of one volume.</margin>
    - **The First concerns the healthy or healing part of medicine (which is called <term>hygieinón</term> <note>original Greek: "υγιεινόν"; the study of health preservation</note>), showing the method for maintaining health, either:**
        - **Super-celestial** <note>Influences from the divine realm, beyond the stars</note>
            - By the divine influence moving downwards.
            - By the prayers of the good penetrating upwards.
            - By a life of piety resisting the assaults of wicked Demons.
        - **Celestial** <note>Influences from the stars and planets</note>
            - By the observation of malevolent aspects <note>The geometric angles between planets</note> and caution in avoiding their effects.
            - By the science of repelling the misfortunes of both the Planets and the fixed stars, and preventing their malice.
        - **Elemental:**
            - **By the external quality of the elements. In which consists:**
                - The climate of the location.
                - The four quarters of the year: { Hot, Cold, Moist, Dry. }
                - The disposition of the weather.
            - **By the internal respect and factors peculiar to the body: under which are contained:**
                - Dietary regimen.
                - Complexion <note>The unique balance of "humors"—blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile—in an individual</note>.
                - Age.
                - Motion and Rest.
                - Exercises.
                - Affects of the mind <note>Emotions and passions</note>.
    - **The Second concerns preternatural "meteors" <note>In Renaissance medicine, "meteors" refer to vapors, winds, or disturbances inside the body that mimic atmospheric phenomena like storms</note> or unhealthy and morbid things. See A.**

- **The Third narrates the cunning skill for discussing and dispelling those "meteors" that behave preternaturally. See B.**
