The true character of our soul is to help one another. Imparting knowledge to others leads to the influx of **virtuous deeds** <note>original: "karmas." Giving up external and internal attachments shall liberate our soul.

All my blessings to this great work, which is deep in thought and simple in expression.

# PREFACE

**Teacher Umāsvāmi** <note>original: "Āchārya Umāsvāmi." He is the celebrated author of this text, recognized by all Jain sects.</note> was the Chief Disciple of **Teacher Kundkund** <note>original: "Āchārya Kundkund," the most prominent philosopher-monk of the Digambara Jain tradition.</note> – the most revered Teacher in the **sorrowful** <note>original: "duṣṣama," referring to the fifth era of the current time cycle in Jain cosmology, characterized by a decline in spiritual purity and height.</note> division of the present descending cycle of time, whose works have been possible to document and preserve. Teacher Umāsvāmi lived in the second century AD.¹

The above cited work states that a special feature of Teacher Umāsvāmi’s *Aphorisms on the Nature of Reality* <note>original: "Tattvārthsūtra."</note> is that it is the first Jaina scripture written in the **Sanskrit** language <note>Earlier Jain scriptures were primarily written in Prakrit, the common vernacular.</note>. Most learned and venerable Teachers including **Venerable Pujyapāda** <note>original: "Shri Pujyapādasvami."</note>, **Teacher Akalanka** <note>original: "Shri Akalankasvami."</note>, and **Teacher Vidhyanandi** <note>original: "Shri Vidhyanandisvami."</note> have written extensive commentaries on the *Aphorisms on the Nature of Reality*. *The Attainment of All Meanings* <note>original: "Sarvārthasiddhi."</note>, *The Royal Commentary* <note>original: "Rājavārtika."</note>, *The Verse Commentary* <note>original: "Ślokavārtika."</note>, and *The Illumination of Meanings* <note>original: "Arthaprakāṣika."</note> are all commentaries on this scripture. The work is of great value for the beginner as well as for the learned. Its composition has great charm. Each **aphorism** <note>original: "Sūtra," a concise rule or statement intended to be memorized.</note> is composed in the fewest possible words and can easily be memorized. Many Jains recite these aphorisms.

In his Foreword to *Reality* Professor A. Chakravarti writes:

> *The Attainment of All Meanings* by the venerable Pujyapāda is the oldest extant
> commentary on the *Aphorisms for Understanding the Nature of Reality* <note>original: "Tattvārthādhigama Sūtra."</note> by **Teacher**
> **Umāsvāmi**.
> It is noted for its depth of thought and simplicity of
> expression. This famous work is a compendium of all
> aspects of Jaina Religion and Philosophy. The author’s
> literary skill is evident in the charm and beauty of his style.
> His mastery of philosophic thought is clear from his lucid
> exposition of the complex reality. The greatness of the work
> can be realized from the fact that the great logician, **Teacher Akalanka** <note>original: "Shri Akalanka Deva."</note>, thought it fit to base his well-known
> commentary, *The Royal Commentary*, on *The Attainment of All Meanings*.²

# THE ULTIMATE CONTRIBUTION

As the present work was nearing completion, an apprehension started to kindle within me: given my status as a mundane householder, and limited knowledge – like a drop in the ocean – of the issues covered in the *Aphorisms on the Nature of Reality* <note>original: "Tattvārthsūtra," the foundational scripture of Jain philosophy composed by Umaswati.</note>, many learned individuals may not find themselves sufficiently enthused to go through the work with right earnest.

I thought of showing the final proofs to the Most Learned, Teacher of Teachers, <term>Āchārya</term> <note>A high-ranking monk who leads a group of ascetics and serves as a spiritual preceptor.</note> 108 <note>The number 108 is sacred in Indian traditions; in the Jain context, it refers to the 108 virtues of a supreme teacher.</note> Vidyanandji <term>Muniraj</term> <note>Literally "King of Monks," a title of deep respect for a Digambara Jain ascetic.</note> at Kundkund Bharti, New Delhi. On a cold December afternoon, I reached Kundkund Bharti and waited on its sprawling lawns for the Muniraj to come out after his routine <term>Samayika</term> <note>original: "Sāmāyika," a period of 48 minutes spent in meditation and equanimity, detaching from all worldly thoughts.</note>. Just before 3.0 p.m., the Muniraj walked out majestically with a white <term>pichchi</term> <note>A ceremonial whisk made of naturally shed peacock feathers, used by monks to gently move insects without harming them, embodying the principle of non-violence (Ahimsa).</note> in his hand. While the others were beset by shivering cold wind, the Muniraj seemed to have conquered the nature by dwelling in the warm house of inner knowledge.

I touched the worshipful feet of the Muniraj, he took seat on a wooden stool, and with great nervousness I handed over to him the folder containing the final proofs of the book. His first observation was that the presentation seemed to be attractive and it immediately allayed partially my anxiety. He then read aloud the <term>Mangalacharan</term> <note>An auspicious opening prayer or invocation intended to remove obstacles.</note>, followed by the first <term>Sūtra</term> <note>An aphorism or concise rule.</note> –

->Right faith, knowledge, and conduct (together) constitute the path to liberation. <note>original Sanskrit: "Samyag-darśana-jñāna-cāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ."</note><-

The Āchārya guided that a corollary of this Sūtra –

->Wrong faith, knowledge, and conduct (together) constitute the path to the cycle of rebirth. <note>original Sanskrit: "Mithyā-darśana-jñāna-cāritrāṇi saṃsāramārgaḥ."</note><-

> Wrong belief, knowledge, and conduct (together) constitute
> the path to eternal transmigration.

should also be reflected upon by all.

[ **The senses and the non-sense** ] The five physical senses and the mind [ **that** ] for that sensory knowledge <term>mati-jñāna</term> [ **cause** ] are the instrumental causes.

<note>In Jain philosophy, the "non-sense" (anindriya) refers specifically to the mind (*manas*). While the five senses perceive external objects like color or sound, the mind is the internal faculty that processes this information.</note>

## Apprehension, speculation, judgment, and retention. (15)
<note>original Sanskrit: "Avagrahehāvāyadhāraṇāḥ"</note>

[ **Apprehension, speculation, judgment, and retention** ] These are the four stages or divisions of sensory perception.

The four divisions of sensory knowledge are apprehension, speculation, perceptual judgment, and retention.
<note>These four stages describe the cognitive process: **Apprehension** (avagraha) is the first awareness of an object; **Speculation** (īhā) is the desire to know more or clear doubts; **Judgment** (avāya) is the determination of the object's true nature; and **Retention** (dhāraṇā) is the internalizing of that knowledge so it can be remembered later.</note>

## Of the many, the many kinds, the quick, the hidden, the unuttered, and the lasting, as well as their opposites. (16)

<note>This sūtra explains that the mind and senses can perceive objects in twelve different ways (six qualities and their six opposites). ## Clairvoyance based on the lifting of veils is of six kinds and belongs to the rest. (22)
<note>original Sanskrit: "Kṣayopaśamanimittaḥ ṣadvikalpaḥ śeṣāṇām"</note>

<gloss>[ Arising from destruction-cum-subsidence ]</gloss> <term>Clairvoyance</term> that arises from the partial removal and partial suppression of karmic veils <gloss>[ is of six kinds ]</gloss>: following, non-following, increasing, decreasing, steady, and unsteady. These six variations <gloss>[ belong to the rest ]</gloss>—meaning they occur in human beings and animals.

<note>Unlike the "birth-based" clairvoyance of gods and hell-beings mentioned on the previous page, this type of clairvoyance is earned through spiritual merit. The six types describe how the ability behaves: whether it follows the person to a new location, grows stronger, or remains unstable.</note>

Clairvoyance arising from the lifting of the veil is of six kinds. It is acquired by the rest (namely human beings and animals).

---

## Telepathy is of two kinds: simple and expansive. (23)
<note>original Sanskrit: "Ṛjuvipulamatī manaḥparyayaḥ"</note>

<gloss>[ Telepathy ]</gloss> The knowledge of the thoughts of others <gloss>[ is of two types: simple and expansive ]</gloss>. <term>Rjumati</term> (straight/simple) and <term>Vipulmati</term> (complex/expansive) are its two forms.

*Rjumati* and *vipulmati* are the two kinds of telepathy (*manahparyaya*).

---

## The distinction between them is based on purity and non-decline. (24)
<note>original Sanskrit: "Viśuddhyapratipātābhyāṃ tadviseṣaḥ"</note>

<gloss>[ Through purity and non-decline ]</gloss> Based on the purity of the soul's internal state and <term>non-decline</term>—meaning the knowledge is not lost before attaining <term>Omniscience</term>—<gloss>[ is the distinction between them ]</gloss>. These two factors create the difference between the "simple" and "expansive" forms of telepathy.

<note>Vipulmati is considered superior because it is "purer" (more detailed) and "non-declining," meaning once a monk attains this level of telepathy, he will certainly progress to full enlightenment without losing this ability.</note>

### Technical Terms

*   **Destruction-cum-subsidence (Kshayopashama):** A state where some karma is destroyed, some is suppressed, and some remains active, allowing certain innate abilities of the soul to manifest.
*   **Clairvoyance (Avadhijnana):** Direct knowledge of material objects limited by space and time, without the use of senses.
*   **Telepathy (Manahparyaya):** Direct knowledge of the thoughts and mental states of others.
*   **Simple Telepathy (Rjumati):** A form of telepathy that perceives simple thoughts of the mind, speech, or body.
*   **Expansive Telepathy (Vipulmati):** A more refined and permanent form of telepathy that perceives subtle and complex mental states.
*   **Non-decline (Apratipata):** The quality of being permanent; once attained, it does not vanish.
*   **Omniscience (Kevalajnana):** The highest form of knowledge; absolute, infinite, and all-encompassing.

->**The distinction between clairvoyance and telepathy is based on purity, range, ownership, and scope. (25)**<-

2. **Range:** Clairvoyance can see across the whole universe, whereas telepathy is limited to the region where human beings live.

## Active consciousness is the defining characteristic. [Sutra 8]

**[Defining Characteristic]** The defining characteristic of the soul **[Active Consciousness]** is consciousness <note>original: upayoga; the functional manifestation of the soul's innate power of knowing and perceiving</note>.

*Consciousness is the differentia (distinctive characteristic) of the soul.*

---

## It is of two kinds, with eight and four subtypes respectively. [Sutra 9]

**[That]** That active consciousness **[is of two kinds]** is of two types: cognitive application <term>Jnanopayoga</term> and perceptive application <term>Darshanopayoga</term>. And they respectively **[consist of eight and four subtypes]**; that is, cognitive application has eight subtypes: sensory knowledge, scriptural knowledge, clairvoyance, telepathy <note>original: manahparyaya; direct knowledge of the thoughts of others</note>, and omniscience (these five constitute **Right Knowledge**); and false sensory knowledge, false scriptural knowledge, and false clairvoyance (these three constitute **Wrong Knowledge**), making eight subtypes in total. 

Additionally, perceptive application has four subtypes: ocular (visual), non-ocular (through other senses or the mind), clairvoyant, and omniscient perception. In this way, by combining the eight types of knowledge and the four types of perception, there are twelve total subtypes of active consciousness.

**[Vocabulary Definitions]**

## In the center of these is Jambudvipa; it is circular like a disc, one hundred thousand yojanas in diameter, with Mount Meru at its center like a navel. [Sutra 9]
<note>original Sanskrit: "तन्मध्ये मेरुनाभिर्वृत्तो योजनशतसहस्त्रविष्कम्भो जम्बूद्वीपः ॥९॥"</note>

At the very center of all these alternating continents and oceans lies <term>Jambudvipa</term> (<note>the "Rose-Apple Island" where humans reside</note>). It is perfectly circular, shaped like a flat plate or disc. At its exact center stands the <term>Sudarshana Meru</term> (<note>Mount Meru</note>), serving as the "navel" or central axis of the world. The total diameter of Jambudvipa is one hundred thousand <term>yojanas</term> (<note>a yojana is a classical Indian measurement of distance, often calculated as roughly 8 to 9 miles in this cosmological context</note>).

## They are composed of gold, silver, purified gold, lapis lazuli, silver, and gold respectively. ||12||
<note>original Sanskrit: "हेमार्जुनतपनीयवैडूर्यरजतहेममयाः"</note>

The mountains mentioned previously are, in order, composed of or colored like: 1. Gold, 2. Silver (or white), 3. Purified/Heated Gold, 4. <term>Vaidūrya</term> (<note>often identified as beryl or lapis lazuli; a precious blue gem</note>), 5. Silver, and 6. Gold.

## Their sides are variegated with various jewels, and they are of equal width at the top, the base, and the middle. ||13||
