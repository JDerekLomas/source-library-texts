### Here begins the life of Saint Paula, a most noble widow and senator of the city of Rome, published by the glorious Saint Jerome, holy doctor of the church.

**S** <note>The original manuscript features a large, decorated red initial 'S' at the start of the word "Si" (If).</note> If all the members of my body were turned into tongues, and all my limbs resounded with a human voice, they would still be able to say nothing worthy of the virtues of the holy and venerable Paula. Noble in her lineage, but much nobler in her holiness. Once powerful in her riches, but now more distinguished by the poverty of Christ. Of Greek stock, an offspring of the Scipios <note>The Scipios were one of the most famous and powerful families in ancient Rome, known for their military victories.</note>, the heir of [Aemilius] Paulus—whose name she bears—and the true and direct descendant of her mother Marcia Papiria, daughter of Africanus. 

She preferred Bethlehem to Rome, and exchanged the glittering roofs of gold for the lowliness of shapeless mud. Let us not grieve that we have lost such a woman, but rather give thanks that we had her—or rather, that we still have her. For to God all things live, and whatever is returned to the Lord is still counted as part of the family, because the loss of her to us is her habitation in the heavenly house. 

For as long as she was in the body, she was a pilgrim wandering away from the Lord, and with a constantly tearful voice she sought Him, saying: "Woe is me, for my sojourn is prolonged; I have dwelt with the inhabitants of Kedar <note>original: "cedar". Wherefore she also frequently inserted that saying: "I am a stranger and a pilgrim, just as all my fathers were." And again: "I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ." Whenever, however, by some infirmity...
