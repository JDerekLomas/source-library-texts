# TO THE FAIR-MINDED READER.

*...considered it enough to have explained it with impunity. From this it follows that the labors of those most ingenious philosophers were in vain, by which they wished to have the heavens, the lands, the seas, and man himself recalled to and examined by the standard of truth and goodness. Great volumes were composed for this purpose, yet they always struggled against the vertigo* <note>original: "vertigine". In this context, it refers to a sense of dizziness or lack of stability in human reasoning.</note> *of the human brain, the slipperiness of changeable time, and the nobility of this machine of ours* <note>original: "machinæ huius noſtræ". This likely refers to the "world-machine" (the universe) or the human body as a complex divine creation.</note>, *until our Christ—Truth and Goodness itself and the salvation of the world—walking among us and calling out to the world in vain, openly showed us that there is no reason for us to hope for anything further from it, nor to expend our labors except in vain. He showed us, clearly, that this is a kingdom of darkness and of storms, where no matter what you attempt, "Ethiopians never turn white,"* <note>original: "æthiopes nūquam albeſcant". A common Latin proverb (Aethiopem lavare) meaning to labor at an impossible task or to try to change the unchangeable.</note> *and the blind cannot be delighted by the light. Yet, the world must still be rebuked so that when He is about to decree judgment concerning each individual at the Final Judgment, the conscience may be tormented by the memory of the truth it heard. From that time, when every good man has been buffeted by the hatred of Satan* <note>original: "Sathanæ odio". The author views the opposition to truth as a spiritual conflict.</note>, *he has sought to provide for his own people through the confession of truth; and for this one name alone he has always been mocked and accused, frequently [enriching] the fields of the Church with fi-* <meta>catchword: deli</meta>

<summary>The author argues that human philosophy failed to find absolute truth until Christ revealed the world to be a "kingdom of darkness" where effort is often futile.
