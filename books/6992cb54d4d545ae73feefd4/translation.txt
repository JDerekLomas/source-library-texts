# The Complete Library of the Four Treasuries
<term>Siku Quanshu: The largest collection of books in Chinese history, commissioned by the Qianlong Emperor of the Qing dynasty in 1772. It was intended to categorize and preserve all significant literature in the empire.</term>

## Classics Division
<term>Jingbu: The first of the four branches of the imperial library. This section is dedicated to the foundational Confucian texts, such as the Book of Changes and the Analects, which formed the moral and political core of Chinese society.</term>

<summary>This title page identifies the volume as part of the monumental Siku Quanshu imperial collection, specifically categorized under the "Classics Division."</summary>

# Imperially Commissioned Complete Library of the Four Treasuries
<term>Imperially Commissioned (Qinding): This indicates the work was produced under the direct order and authority of the Emperor, in this case, the Qianlong Emperor of the Qing dynasty.</term>

## Classics Division
<term>Classics Division (Jingbu): The most prestigious of the four categories of the imperial library, containing the foundational texts of Confucianism and their various commentaries.</term>

## Cleansing the Heart through the Book of Changes, Volume 1
<note>original: "Zhouyi Xixin" (周易洗心). This is a commentary on the I Ching (Book of Changes) written by the scholar Ren Qiyun. The title suggests that the study of the ancient hexagrams is a process of internal moral purification.</note>

Detailed Proofreading Official and Imperial Censor, <gloss>Your Majesty's servant</gloss> Sheng Jiayou
<term>Imperial Censor (Jiancha Yushi): A high-ranking official responsible for maintaining internal discipline and ensuring the accuracy of government records and imperial publications.</term>

Vice Commissioner of the Office of Transmission, <gloss>Your Majesty's servant</gloss> Mo Zhanlu, responsible for Final Review
<term>Office of Transmission (Tongzheng Shisi): The government bureau that handled the flow of official reports and memorials between the provinces and the Emperor.</term>

Chief Proofreading Official and Demoted Compiler, <gloss>Your Majesty's servant</gloss> Weng Shengmai
<note>The term "Demoted Compiler" indicates that Weng Shengmai held the academic rank of a Hanlin scholar but had been recently lowered in rank, perhaps due to a bureaucratic error or political shift, though he remained vital to the project's scholarly labor.</note>

Proofreading Official and Secretary of the Grand Secretariat, <gloss>Your Majesty's servant</gloss> Gao Zhong

Transcriber and Student of the Imperial Academy, <gloss>Your Majesty's servant</gloss> Zhu Kun
<term>Student of the Imperial Academy (Jiansheng): A student at the Guozijian, the empire's highest educational institution. These students were often recruited for their excellent calligraphy to hand-copy the final versions of these books.</term>
